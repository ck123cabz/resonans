# Block 02: segments and time budget

The arc puts the problem first and the product after it. The viewer learns the map in the problem segment, then watches the product fill it in.

## The six segments, for a 2-minute video

| Segment | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | about 6% | Name the problem in one scene; the product is not named |
| 2 Problem, broken down | about 34% | A mini explainer: the problem split into 3 to 5 named pieces, one per screen |
| 3 The turn | about 8% | Why the pieces cost the viewer; the product is named here |
| 4 The product | about 31% | The same pieces in the same order, now done by the product |
| 5 Proof | about 6% | One result, one case |
| 6 The viewer's part and the step | about 15% | What the viewer now does, then one action |

Scale the shares for other lengths; keep segments 2 and 4 near two thirds of the run time together. For 90 seconds: hook 0:00 to 0:05, problem 0:05 to 0:36, turn 0:36 to 0:43, product 0:43 to 1:11, proof 1:11 to 1:16, step 1:16 to 1:30.

When there is no public result to show, segment 5 goes and its seconds move to segment 4.

When there is no page after the click, the step names the one place to reach a person, such as a Facebook page, and what to send.

## Rules

- Work backwards from the outcome the viewer wants. Segment 1 names how the viewer gets it today and why that is unreliable, in their own insider words (a client's line: "most firms base it on referral"). Segment 2 breaks that way into its pieces, and segment 4 shows the product as the repeatable system that replaces it. A cut that opened with the outcome already in hand drew the client's "starts assuming you already have a deal" at 5:09 am on 9 Oct.
- Segment 4 follows segment 2's pieces in the same order. Words may differ; the pieces may not.
- The product's name first appears in segment 3, at about 40 to 45% of the run time.
- One proof case. Tripling social proof halved organic conversion in Ryan Mathews's 200 experiments (`knowledge/corpus/ryan-mathews/analysis/core-knowledge.md` §7). Christian ran four unnamed proofs in a client's VSL 1 on 8 Oct, right before the step, against this finding. Until a test settles it, ask which one before writing segment 5.
- The voice stops for 2 to 3 seconds before the product is named, and the picture fills that silence by turning the anchor into the brand. In VSL 1 the four boxes became the four parts of the brand mark (`[pause 2.6]` in its SCRIPT.md). A brand clip pasted into the gap read to Christian as "slapped there".
- The step names what happens after the click: a form, then a call, and how long the call is.
- Above $2,000 the video names no price (Ryan Mathews §1.1).

- Set the premise before the question. A question line ("Sino ang may power na humarang?") works only when the
  viewer already holds the idea it rests on; asked before power was introduced, it came across to Christian as "assuming"
  (WNF Ep 1, 13 Oct). Say what the segment is about first, then ask.
- A line that points back ("humarang", "maraming dahilan") needs its referent on screen or in the same line. Two
  seconds after the referent, a viewer who blinked asks "humarang what?" Restate it on screen as a headline.
- Give each segment one job for the viewer's mind, and build its visuals to that job. Christian's frame, 13 Oct,
  from the cognitive-function model: the lived story makes the viewer feel (it is their own country's life); the
  borrowed authority (a book, a Nobel, sources) makes them trust, with proof and no emotion; a dry line like
  "papayag ka ba?" gets satire. Write the job into the segment table.
- The visuals say what a neutral script cannot. A flat line ("300 hectares") gets a picture that shows how unfair
  it is; the voice stays factual and the picture carries the opinion.
- End on the loop you opened, then the step. "Bakit ≈9× ang agwat?" plus "check the pinned comment" read to
  Christian as abrupt and unclear: the outro has to close the question in words he can retell.

## Put the table in the script file

Columns: segment, time range, share, job, what holds attention. The person approves this table before the voice lines are written.
