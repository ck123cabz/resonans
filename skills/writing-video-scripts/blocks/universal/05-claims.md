# Block 05: the claims gate

A video is public, so every name and number in it has to be public first.

## Steps

1. List every name, number and claim in the voice lines and the on-screen labels.
2. Fetch the product's live site, not a saved capture, and find each one. Record the page and the date fetched.
3. Mark each claim: on the live site; in a private source only; nowhere.
4. A claim inferred from a word in the brief, such as a payout from "money game", counts as "nowhere".
5. "On the live site" may stay. "Private only" goes, or is said in general terms with the private figure behind it ("hundreds of qualified owners a month"). "Nowhere" goes.
6. Names of clients appear only if the person who owns the video says so, even when the site names them.
7. Keep a "left out on purpose" list in the script folder, so the next draft does not put a cut claim back.
8. Keep a confidential list of prices, client terms and internal rates, and keep all of it out of every script.

## The worked example

Keep the example deal, customer or case in one fact-sheet file: its name, place, industry, figures and process stage. Generate every voice line and on-screen value from that file. Blind readers caught a chart point at 8.5x labelled 7.2x, and an industry on one card that the app screen contradicted. The final cut passed once every cell multiplied out from one source figure.

- When sources disagree, pick one value per fact, then sweep every asset for the old one.
- Have someone in the trade check that the steps run in the trade's order and the stage matches the activity. Readers flagged a teaser sent before the NDA ("would worry me") and a lead marked "New" with 12 buyers contacted.
- No placeholder a viewer knows is fake. One reader saw a 555 phone number and said it "looks staged".

## Claims that need a named person

Some lines are true only if someone confirms them: what a product promises, who a customer thinks they are talking to, any guarantee. Write these into the script's "Still open" section with the person's name. Never fill the gap with a plausible line.

A figure a skeptical viewer will doubt goes the same way: put its basis on screen, or send it to its owner as a named question before the video ships. Every blind reader stopped at a "75% of all sell-side supply" line: "Nobody controls 75% of supply, and nothing on screen backs it." One concrete named result at the viewer's own scale beats a market-share total; one reader said a single close in their size range would change their answer.
