# Ranking the material

Interactional expertise is picked up from people who do the work, and it degrades each time it is
passed on (Collins and Evans 2007). So the sweep ranks sources by how close they sit to practice.

| Rank | Material | Where it is usually found |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Practitioners thinking aloud while they work | streams, live teardowns, screen recordings, pair sessions, recorded reviews |
| 2 | First-person accounts of an incident | postmortems, "what went wrong" threads, AMAs, "why I quit" posts, interviews |
| 3 | Insiders arguing during a disruption | the thread after a patch, a rule change, an outage, a tool launch |
| 4 | The historical record | primary documents, standards, the first versions of a practice |
| 5 | Practitioner teaching | courses, books by working practitioners |
| 6 | Outsider summaries | listicles, vendor blogs, encyclopedia entries |

Rank 6 is used only to find ranks 1 to 5. A claim that rests on rank 6 alone is marked `[M]` and
never `[D]`.

For each source, the sweep agent records: the URL or path, the rank, the role of the person
speaking, and the date. Undated material is marked undated; a field's practice drifts, and a 2015
answer may be the old side of a current fight.

**Comments are material.** The replies under a practitioner's post are where other insiders agree,
correct and joke, and they carry most of a field's culture. Read them.
