# Pack format

Seven sections, one file each, under `knowledge/learning/<field-slug>/`. Each file opens with
frontmatter (`type: immersion`, `field:`, `section:`, `date:`) and a one-line purpose. Each section is
built from the sweep; the headings below are the frame, and what goes under them is never written in
advance. Markers on every claim: `[D]` a practitioner said it in the cited source, `[M]` someone else
reports it, `[I]` the pack infers it, `[A]` the sources are silent. Cite as `(author or handle, year,
where)`.

## map.md

How the field came to be what it is. Ordered by the crises that changed it, not by topic: for each,
the date, what broke, who answered, what the answer left behind in today's practice. Ends with the
layers a newcomer has to cross, each as one observable thing someone at that layer can do.
Source: Bourdieu (1990), where habitus is argued out loud during crises.

## roles.md

At least five roles: a beginner, a pro, someone the field serves, a critic, someone who left. For
each: what they want, what they fear, what they notice that the others miss, and their words for
it. Then one real event from `incidents.md`, read once from every role. Every role reading quotes
that role's own first-person material; a role with none is marked `[A]` and listed in `gaps.md`.
Source: Lave and Wenger (1991), learning as moving from the edge of a community to its centre.

## culture.md

The insiders' own vocabulary, each term with its tone (praise, insult, in-joke, warning) and an
example of use. Status markers: what earns respect, what marks a tourist. Rituals and recurring
events. Where insiders gather, ranked by how much real practice gets discussed there. Who matters,
each with their character and what they are known for. Source: Pike's emic categories and
Spradley (1980).

## incidents.md

Critical moments, told first-hand where possible. Each one: the situation, the cues the person read,
their goal, what they expected, the call they made, what happened, and what it felt like in their
words. Include failures and near-misses; a field's bad days teach more than its wins. At least three
incidents must be usable as incident stops (see `checks.md`): the source records the call made at a
clear decision point. Source: Klein (1998), the Critical Decision Method.

## fights.md

Each live disagreement as: the question, each side's best case in its own words, who holds it, and
the older war the fight re-runs. Never resolved by the pack. A fight with only one side found is
marked `[A]` for the missing side.

## judgment.md

The calls insiders make that no rule covers. Each as: the situation, the call, the cue that triggers
it, what a novice does instead, and where the call goes wrong. These become SOP steps and their pass
conditions. Sources: Dreyfus (1980) on situational aspects; Ericsson and Pool (2016) on mental
representations.

## gaps.md

What the sources could not give. First, the body's knowing: what has to be felt by doing it
(Collins 2010). Then roles, incidents or fights the sweep found no first-hand material for. Ends
with **His part**: the spaces to join, the reps only a body can do, and who witnesses them.

## README.md

It holds the field in one line, why now with a link to the note or SOP it feeds, the roles, and the
named judge. It has no status line, because done-ness is read from `checks.md`.

Packs are for Christian first and agents second (his call, 20 Oct 2026). So the README ends with
**Your reading path**, the sections in the order he should read them with judgment first, and **Ten
things to be able to say**, one fact each, every one already in a section. The list form comes from
the mathematics lore (13 Aug). Write both after the sections are assembled, and never from the
held-back test files.
