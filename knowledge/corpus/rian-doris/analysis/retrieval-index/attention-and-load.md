## Cognitive Load Dump

**What it is** `[D]` A protocol that clears mental RAM by externalizing every open loop onto
paper, deciding the fate of each one, then physically hiding the paper.

**When to use it** Before a flow block, when the mind is cluttered enough that you cannot start.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. **The Brain Dump.** Grab a physical piece of paper and a pen, never a digital device.
   Frantically write down absolutely everything occupying space in your mind: tasks, worries,
   random thoughts, open loops, conversations you need to have. Do not attempt to organize the
   list, just dump. Write for 10 minutes. Even when you feel empty and think you have written
   everything down, force yourself to keep writing for an additional 3 to 5 minutes so the
   deepest layers of mental clutter surface.
2. **Organize Your RAM.** Decide the fate of everything dumped, filtering into four strict
   categories: (a) Do it now, if the task takes under 2 minutes, complete it immediately;
   (b) Schedule it, add it to your calendar for a specific time; (c) Delete it, cross it out
   completely if you realize it is not actually important; (d) Delegate it, note exactly who you
   are going to hand the task off to. This snips the open loops so the brain can release them.
3. **Release and Reset.** Take the paper, fold it in half, and put it completely out of sight, in
   a drawer or folder. Then take three deep breaths on the 3-2-10 pattern: inhale 3 seconds, hold
   2 seconds, slowly exhale 10 seconds.
4. **Immediate Action.** Do not check your phone or email. Use the freshly cleared state to dive
   directly into your highest-priority goal-directed task.

**Every number** `[D]` Dump duration 10 minutes, plus a forced 3-to-5-minute overrun. Do-it-now
threshold under 2 minutes. Three breaths at 3 seconds in, 2 seconds hold, 10 seconds out. Working
memory holds 4 to 7 distinct pieces of information. Outcome: mental RAM drops from an overloaded
100% down to a primed 20% to 30% capacity (q17).

**Mechanism** `[D]` Exceeding the working-memory limit causes a neural traffic jam in which the
brain tries to fire the default mode network (internal planning and mind-wandering) and the task
positive network (external focus) at the same time. High cognitive load physically prevents
transient hypofrontality, the temporary quieting of the prefrontal cortex, which is an absolute
biological prerequisite for flow. Writing by hand forces the visual and motor cortex to work
together, creating a cognitive offloading effect that a digital device does not. Folding and
hiding the paper creates a hard psychological boundary, signaling to the brain that the thoughts
have been handled. The three breaths activate the parasympathetic nervous system and clear the
stress hormones generated by high cognitive load.

**Equipment** `[D]` The Flow Dojo build calls for an analog dump station: a physical Moleskine
notebook and pen kept on the desk at all times, so the dump has no setup friction (q20).

**Science under it** `[M:Sweller]` cognitive load theory, coined by John Sweller. q22 dates it to
1988 and q59 dates it to the 1980s and places him at the University of New South Wales. Learning
and performance crash when the mental effort placed on working memory exceeds the 4-to-7-item
limit. `[M:Miller]` George Miller (Harvard) gives short-term memory as 7 plus or minus 2 items.
q59 carries both figures in the same file and never reconciles them: 4 to 7 under Sweller, 7±2
under Miller. Both carried. `[M:Fox]` Michael Fox, credited to UBC in q59, for the anti-correlated
relationship between the default mode network and the task positive network. That attribution
looks wrong against outside knowledge and is a verification task.

**Sources:** q22 (authoritative protocol), q17 (the machinery and the 100% to 20-30% figure),
q20 (the equipment), q21 (the Sweller citation), q59.

## Salience Swap (the Pull-Work Method)

**What it is** `[D]` A neurological protocol for breaking out of a paralyzing preoccupation loop by
switching the kind of work rather than fighting the thoughts.

**When to use it** When you catch yourself mentally looping. The named diagnostic symptoms: reading
the same sentence three times without comprehending it, opening the same email twice, and feeling
unable to generate internal discipline to complete a task.

**Preoccupation defined** `[D]` An involuntary engrossment in repetitive thoughts, caused by a
stressful event or looming threat, that steals focus from the present moment.

**Push work against pull work** `[D]`

| | Push work (low salience) | Pull work (high salience) |
|---|---|---|
| Attention system | Goal-directed: you generate focus from within | Stimulus-driven: external forces capture your focus |
| Qualifies when | Working alone without external accountability, abstract thinking, long-term planning, sustained internal motivation required | Real-time interaction with others, concrete problems with visible results, urgent deadlines, waiting stakeholders, high stimulus level |
| Examples | Writing a report alone at your desk | Handling customer emergencies, leading a meeting, high-stakes negotiations |

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. **Accept the Preoccupation.** Stop fighting your compromised attention networks. Take one breath
   and explicitly acknowledge to yourself, verbatim: "My brain is preoccupied, I need to swap".
2. **Execute the Swap.** Do not deliberate. Switch immediately and aggressively from solo push work
   to a high-salience pull work task. If you were writing alone, instantly jump on a call with a
   colleague or dive into a crisis management task.
3. **Ride the Salience Decay.** Work exclusively on pull tasks for 30 to 90 minutes, then test
   whether you can return to your original push work. If the preoccupation still grips you, continue
   with the pull work.

**Every number** `[D]` Diagnostic counts: same sentence read three times, same email opened twice.
Pull-work window 30 to 90 minutes before re-testing.

**Warnings** `[WRN][D]` Using sheer willpower to suppress the thoughts intensifies them. During the
pull-work window you must maintain forward motion, because stasis feeds the negative spiral.

**Mechanism** `[D]` When you are preoccupied your ability to push attention is completely
compromised, while your ability to be pulled by external stimuli remains intact. That asymmetry is
the whole basis of the swap. The salience network, primarily the anterior insula and anterior
cingulate cortex, acts as an attention switchboard. When a crisis hits it tags the crisis as a
"Level 10 threat", overriding your standard work tasks. Salience decays on its own because the
amygdala cannot maintain peak activation indefinitely, so high-stimulus pull work waits out the
neurological half-life of the panic while the prefrontal cortex comes back online.

**Missing terminating condition** `[A]` Step 3 says test after 30 to 90 minutes and continue with
pull work if preoccupation persists, with no cap on cycles and no fallback if pull work is
unavailable. The protocol assumes a job that always has a crisis or a colleague to call.

**Sources:** q23, q60.

## Temporary Overengagement

**What it is** `[D]` An extreme protocol against preoccupation: increase your work output enough to
saturate your attention networks, making it neurologically impossible for an intrusive thought to
colonize your consciousness.

**When to use it** When a preoccupation has outlasted what the Salience Swap can clear.

**Every number** `[D]` 14 days. Increase work output by 50% to 100%.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Saturating the attention networks leaves no spare capacity for the preoccupation
to occupy.

**Note on coverage** This protocol appears only in the q60 sweep, which lists it in one line. No
dedicated source file expands it, so the step list above is the whole of what the corpus supplies.

**Sources:** q60.

## Attention Recovery and the Boomerang Brain

**What it is** `[D]` Training that shrinks the time between being distracted and being back on task,
so an interruption barely registers.

**When to use it** Continuously, as reps rather than as a session.

**The target it replaces** `[M:Mark]` Gloria Mark (UC Irvine) monitored office workers in 2004 and
found it takes an average of 23 minutes and 15 seconds to refocus after a single interruption. That
figure is what the whole protocol is aimed at replacing.

**The trained targets** `[D]` Detect that your attention has drifted within 3 seconds. Fully recover
focus within 30 seconds.

**The complete training protocol, three parts** `[D]`

1. **Treat work as meditation.** In traditional meditation the breath is the anchor. At work you use
   a wildly specific clear goal as the anchor. Write the goal down before starting the task so there
   is zero ambiguity. As you work you train detection speed, catching the mind wandering, and
   recovery speed, pulling it back to the clear goal.
2. **Train for work using formal meditation.** Sitting meditation provides highly concentrated reps.
   Every time the mind wanders from the breath and you pull it back you execute a rep of attention
   recovery that directly strengthens the prefrontal control networks for work.
3. **The Flow State Breath Reset.** On catching yourself distracted, immediately execute the 3-2-10
   breathing pattern: inhale 3 seconds, hold 2 seconds, exhale slowly for 10 seconds. q33 adds that
   the inhale goes through the nose, the exhale through the mouth, and the cycle repeats three times.
   Immediately after exhaling, physically rewrite a fresh specific clear goal, for example "write
   three sentences explaining the proposal", to manually re-engage the prefrontal cortex.

**Every number** `[D]` 23 minutes 15 seconds is the population baseline. 3 seconds to detect, 30
seconds to recover. A single 10-minute meditation session might contain 30 to 50 reps of attention
recovery. Breath ratio 3 seconds in, 2 seconds hold, 10 seconds out, repeated 3 times.

**The stated end state** `[D]` Over time, what used to be a 10-minute YouTube rabbit hole naturally
shrinks into a mere 10-second glance.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Building a Boomerang Brain physically creates enhanced connectivity between the
dorsal prefrontal cortex and the anterior cingulate cortex. When an irrelevant distraction appears,
these executive control networks override the brain's automatic threat and salience detection,
allowing rapid disengagement. The 3-2-10 breath activates the vagus nerve and creates a clean
neurological break from the distraction.

**The contrast** `[D]` The pinball brain bounces uncontrollably from distraction to distraction,
which is why the 23-minute loss occurs. The Boomerang Brain, like a thrown boomerang, curves right
back to its starting point almost instantly.

**Boundary** `[A]` The sources never specify how many days or weeks the training takes to reach full
effect. The corpus only says "over time". That gap sits directly under a protocol whose whole pitch
is replacing 23 minutes and 15 seconds with 3 seconds.

**Note on the merged source.** Pass 2 merges q39 and q41 into this protocol. q41 was asked for an
11-minute flow entry; the transcript never states 11 minutes anywhere, verified by re-query on
2026-07-28. The only durations q41 supplies are 5 to 10 minutes for the Release phase and up to 23
minutes as the refocus cost. Do not prescribe 11 minutes.

**Science under it** `[M:Mark]` the 23 minutes 15 seconds. `[M:Barros]` Marcelo Barros (Federal
University of Sao Carlos, 2018) showed flow massively engages attention networks and sends blood
flow to focus regions. q55 spells the name "Baros" and q59 spells it "Barros"; both readings carried.
`[M:Benson]` Herbert Benson (Harvard, 1970s) discovered the relaxation response, which underwrites
the breath. `[M:Peifer]` Karina Peifer (Journal of Happiness Studies) showed flow occurs at a perfect
balance of sympathetic and parasympathetic activation.

**Sources:** q24 (authoritative), q30, q33, q39, q41, q55, q59.

## The Simple Math Method (the Life Maintenance Audit)

**What it is** `[D]` A mathematical procedure for forcing your brain out of a rut by calculating how
many waking hours go to life maintenance and swapping them for high-value activities.

**When to use it** When you are in a rut, and then on a repeating cycle.

**The fixed equation** `[D]` 24 hours in a day, minus about 8 hours of sleep, leaves exactly 16 hours
of waking life.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. **List.** Write out every daily, weekly and monthly task that consumes your time and energy.
2. **Time it.** Write down exactly how much time each activity takes.
3. **Score it.** Rate every single activity on an autotelic value scale from 1 to 10, based on how
   intrinsically enjoyable it is to do for its own sake.
4. **Cut against the threshold.** The strict cutoff is 7 out of 10. Any activity scoring less than 7
   is immediately flagged for elimination.
5. **Work the list in order.** Start at the top with the tasks that consume the most time and yield
   the lowest autotelic scores. For each item under the 7/10 threshold, ask whether you can eliminate
   it entirely using cash or creativity. If yes, remove it from your life immediately. If you cannot
   remove it completely, automate it, delegate it, or hack it down to a manageable size.
6. **Repeat every six months,** to catch new maintenance tasks that have crept back into the schedule.

**Every number** `[D]` 24 hours minus 8 hours sleep equals 16 waking hours. Autotelic scale 1 to 10.
Threshold 7. Audit cycle every 6 months.

**Definitions** `[D]` Life maintenance load: the everyday chores, errands, administrative upkeep and
commutes that steal time from meaningful goals. Exotelic activities, such as meal prep, cleaning and
paying bills, are performed merely to meet a requirement or avoid a negative consequence. Autotelic
activities are done purely for their own enjoyment and intrinsic purpose.

**Mechanism** `[D]` A high volume of exotelic tasks constantly fragments your day, forcing the brain
to repeatedly switch gears and destroying the deep focus flow requires. Mundane exotelic tasks also
keep the default mode network buzzing with self-consciousness, which inhibits your ability to lose
yourself in work. Autotelic activities are intrinsically rewarding, so they capture attention, block
peripheral distractions, and trigger a dopamine release. The sustained focus plus neurochemical
reward decreases internal chatter, allowing loss of the sense of time and self.

**The stated outcome** `[D]` The method restructures the heat map of your day, shifting hours from
exotelic toward autotelic, which creates large uninterrupted blocks of time for peak performance.

**Population assumption** `[I]` The remedy "eliminate it using cash" presumes cash, and treating every
sub-7 activity as removable cannot hold for caregiving. The corpus offers no version of the audit for
someone without discretionary income or with non-delegable domestic obligations.

**Sources:** q25, q36 (the same audit under the laziness argument), q60.

## The DMN Method for Decisions

**What it is** `[D]` Flow-based decision making. A three-step protocol that bypasses stressful, slow,
effortful analytical thinking and hands the problem to the intuitive automatic subconscious.

**When to use it** On any decision that does not have to be made in the next 48 hours. His stated rule
of thumb: 90% of decisions do not need to be made immediately.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. **Give your subconscious a target.** Separate the surface-level symptoms from the actual root
   problem, digging down until you find the core root. Formulate it into a precise question. Then
   physically write the question down with pen and paper.
2. **Recruit the silent operator.** Step away from the problem entirely and surrender control to
   incubation. Engage in lightly stimulating physical activities that run on automaticity.
3. **Extract the juice.** After the incubation period, sit back down with your paper and begin
   actively writing out solutions to the question you posed.

**Incubation activities, the full permitted list** `[D]` Taking a shower, driving, taking a quick walk,
gardening, cleaning, playing pool. q26 gives four of these (walk, shower, gardening, cleaning) and q28
gives all six.

**Prohibited during incubation, in full** `[WRN][D]` Watching TV, playing video games, scrolling social
media. These require far too much mental processing and will block the subconscious from doing its job.

**Every number** `[D]` The incubation period scales with the magnitude of the decision and can range
from a single hour, to multiple days, or even weeks. Rule of thumb: 90% of decisions do not need to be
made immediately, and you should ideally give your subconscious 48 hours or more. If no epiphany
arrives, extract anyway: after just a minute or two of writing, the decision pours out rapidly. The
conscious mind processes about 40 to 50 bits of information per second; the subconscious processes
roughly 400 billion bits per second.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Intense focus on a task runs the Task Positive Network. Stepping away into lightly
stimulating automatic activity switches the brain to the Default Mode Network, which governs
mind-wandering and internal reflection, and unleashes the silent operator to run high-level simulations
at astronomical speeds by recognizing patterns across billions of bits of exformation. The permitted
activities induce transient hypofrontality, the quieting of the conscious prefrontal cortex. Writing the
question by hand moves the information from short-term to long-term memory and triggers the Reticular
Activating System to prioritize finding a solution.

**How you know it is ready** `[D]` The decision often arrives spontaneously as a sudden clear gut
instinct, the silent operator knocking on your door before you sit down to extract it. He calls the
result the "cognitive bias high": calm confidence from making a flawlessly right decision without
second-guessing or a stressful pros-and-cons list.

**Evidence flag** The entire protocol rests on the 400-billion-bit figure, which arrives with no study,
sample or design. So do the 40-to-50-bits figure and the 64% waking-hours figure used alongside it.

**Science under it** `[M:Kahneman]` System 1 and System 2. q28 names Kahneman alone; q59 names
Kahneman and Tversky. Tversky appears nowhere else in the corpus, so a lens should say Kahneman.
`[M:Wilson]` Timothy Wilson named the adaptive subconscious and calls it the silent operator.
`[M:Nørretranders]` Tor Nørretranders coined exformation. `[M:UPenn]` q59 credits the University of
Pennsylvania with the 400 billion bits against 40 bits comparison. `[M:Eagleman]` David Eagleman
(Stanford) theorized that the conscious mind processes the headline while the subconscious processes
the entire newspaper. `[M:Jarvilehto]` Lauri Järvilehto (Aalto University) concluded that flow can be
thought of as intuitive action, and intuition as cognition in flow.

**Sources:** q26 (authoritative on the three steps), q28 (fuller on the activities and the sources), q59.

## Decision Recycling

**What it is** `[D]` Automating medium-sized recurring "should I or shouldn't I" decisions on a macro
level across your life. Instead of making unique decisions to reach the same predictable outcomes, you
make one optimal decision upfront and reuse it endlessly.

**When to use it** On any decision you have already made more than once and will make again.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. Identify five common scenarios where you repeatedly make unique decisions.
2. Make the choice right now, once.
3. Apply it as a strict rule from that point forward.

**Every number** `[D]` Five scenarios at setup.

**His own examples, as given** `[D]` Always fly direct regardless of the price. Set your alarm for
exactly 7:30 am every single day. Only socialize on Wednesday nights.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Decision-making depletes cognitive energy, increases cognitive load, and interrupts
the divergent thinking flow needs. Eliminating the jagged, halting moments of trivial decision-making
lowers cognitive load and frees the mind to diverge, dream and access continuous flow. He calls the
result being your own "Puppet Master", and calls the rules "liberating constraints".

**Population assumption** `[I]` The examples presume discretionary air travel and full control of your
own alarm.

**Sources:** q27, q60.

## Decision Autopilot

**What it is** `[D]` Automating the micro-level "what's next" decisions inside a known workflow or
routine. An explicit script for your daily habits so you move through the process mindlessly, without
your brain stopping to converge on inconsequential choices.

**When to use it** On routines you already perform: a morning ritual, an evening wind-down.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. Take a routine you already perform.
2. Determine the exact sequence of events.
3. Write the details down, mapping every micro-decision so any "should I" choice is completely removed.

**His own examples, as given** `[D]` Deciding in advance whether you get water before or after you
meditate, or whether you shower before you stretch. A full "learning plus coffee" routine mapped out:
grab headphones, switch to airplane mode, pull up Audible, press play, heat water, grind coffee, sip,
and walk.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Removing the "what's next" question removes a class of micro-decisions that would
otherwise interrupt attention, lowering cognitive load and letting rumination run uninterrupted on
something worth ruminating on.

**Science under it** `[M:Kant]` Immanuel Kant is offered as the historical example. His strict daily
routine, waking at 5:00 am, drinking tea, smoking exactly one pipe, then writing and lecturing, was
completely preset so he would not waste cognitive energy on recurrent decisions.

**Sources:** q27, q60.

## Upgraded Rumination

**What it is** `[D]` Deliberately transforming the mind's natural tendency to wander into a 24/7
problem-solving supercomputer by redirecting idle cognition onto your top professional priorities. The
commute becomes a brainstorming session; game-changing proposals get conceptualized while washing dishes.

**When to use it** As the standing use of idle time, once Decision Recycling and Decision Autopilot have
freed the bandwidth.

**Every number** `[D]` For roughly 64% of your waking hours the default mode network is active, typically
ruminating on trivial micro-concerns: social interactions, commuting options, outfit choices.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Radically reducing the number of decisions you make frees the bandwidth the default
mode network would otherwise spend on trivia. Attention then expands to the far edges of consciousness,
which is where the creative breakthrough insights are waiting.

**Science under it** `[M:Hamilton]` J. Paul Hamilton (Laureate Institute for Brain Research) is credited
in q59 with the 64% figure, from a meta-analysis showing that high trait rumination strongly connects
the subgenual prefrontal cortex to the default mode network, wiring the brain to reward endless social
worrying. q27 states the 64% figure with no researcher named. The figure arrives with no study design or
sample.

**Do not collapse with the Salience Swap.** Preoccupation (q23) and rumination (q27) run on the same
substrate and get opposite prescriptions: preoccupation is a hijack to firewall with pull work, rumination
is an idle resource to upgrade. The corpus never draws the line between them.

**Sources:** q27, q59.

## System 1, System 2, and the Silent Operator

**What it is** `[M:Kahneman]` The dual-process split the whole DMN decision protocol is built on.

**When to use the distinction** When deciding which mode should own which phase of a decision.

**The two systems** `[M:Kahneman]`

- **System 1:** intuitive, automatic, fast and effortless thinking, relying on pattern recognition and
  instincts. q28 locates it in lower brain regions, the basal ganglia and amygdala, asserted in the same
  breath as the Kahneman attribution although the neuroanatomy is not part of what Kahneman is credited
  with.
- **System 2:** conscious, logical, deliberate, slow and effortful thinking, the mode you use when
  carefully weighing options or making pros-and-cons lists.

**The correct allocation** `[D]` System 2 for the preparation phase, to rigorously and diligently define
the exact root problem you are trying to solve. System 1 for the decision phase, once the problem is
clearly defined, to select the solution by flow-based intuition. System 1 is the cognition that powers
flow, allowing rapid, flawless, split-second decisions without conscious interference.

**The named mistake** `[WRN][D]` Over-indexing on System 2 to make the actual decision, like agonizing
over a pros-and-cons list. Relying heavily on System 2 for decision-making slows you down, increases
stress, drains cognitive energy, and traps you in a loop of self-doubt.

**The Silent Operator** `[M:Wilson]` Psychologist Timothy Wilson named the Adaptive Subconscious, an
evolved high-speed information processing system he calls the silent operator: a mental search engine
that rapidly scans your environment, memories and social cues to instantly recognize patterns and deliver
intuitive gut feelings. This is the entity the DMN Method hands the problem to.

**Exformation** `[M:Nørretranders]` Tor Nørretranders coined exformation: the ambient data and details
from your environment that the conscious mind actively filters out and discards to avoid overload, such
as the hum of a coffee shop or a fleeting facial expression. Though you are not consciously aware of it,
it still enters and feeds the subconscious, supplying the contextual database the brain uses to generate
intuitive decisions. Exformation is what makes the 400-billion-bit claim coherent, and it underwrites the
incubation step.

**Every number** `[D]` The conscious mind processes about 40 to 50 bits of information per second. The
adaptive subconscious processes 400 billion bits per second. Both figures arrive with no study, sample or
design.

**Handing a problem over, the complete three steps** `[D]`

1. Separate the symptom from the root problem, digging down until you find the core root.
2. Formulate a precise question for the subconscious to chew on.
3. Physically write the question down with pen and paper, which moves the information from short-term to
   long-term memory and activates the Reticular Activating System.

**Sources:** q28 (authoritative), q26, q59.

## The Flow Dojo

**What it is** `[D]` A workspace designed with absolute intentionality, solely dedicated to activating
flow states with maximum reliability.

**When to use it** As a one-time build, then defended by the single-use rule.

**The six design principles, complete** `[D]`

1. **Suppression is an active process.** The brain craves new information like it craves food, and
   filtering out irrelevant stimuli is a highly effortful, battery-draining process. The Dojo minimizes
   what the brain has to filter in advance.
2. **Be conscious of anchoring bias.** Your environment sets your psychological baseline. A disciplined,
   orderly workspace anchors you to excellence and precision; a messy one anchors you to sloppiness and
   error. A desk covered in unwashed plates and tangled cords produces sloppy emails, poor formatting and
   reduced output.
3. **Make the space conducive to the physiology of focus.** Avoid grey-zone environments such as sofas or
   coffee shops, which send mixed messages about whether it is time to work or relax. The space must be
   alluring enough to draw you in and equipped for positional variance, alternating between sitting,
   standing and walking.
4. **Eliminate the invisible enemy, friction.** Friction is micro-moments of frustration, such as finding
   a pen or untangling a cord, that spike cortisol and chip away at cognitive resources.
5. **Deter disruptions like mosquitoes.** Disruptions force the brain into resource-draining context
   switching, which can devour up to 40% of your productive time. Engineer the space to make you
   inaccessible and to make interrupting you feel like a burden to others. q60 states this as sitting with
   your back to the room, wearing ear muffs, and looking intense.
6. **Condition your workspace for flow.** Only ever use the Dojo for focused work, so simply entering the
   room triggers the release of dopamine and norepinephrine. q60 adds: if you procrastinate, leave the
   room immediately.

**Every number** `[D]` Temperature maintained between 21 and 25 degrees Celsius (69.8 to 77 degrees
Fahrenheit). Context switching devours up to 40% of productive time, stated with no source, study or
sample. Positional variance ratio for flow: 50% standing, 25% walking, 25% sitting, also stated with no
source (q60).

**Removed from the room, complete list** `[D]` Smartphones, kept completely out of sight and out of reach
in a cupboard, another room, or your car. Excess gadgets: tablets, TVs and video games. Visual clutter:
stacks of paper, dirty coffee mugs and trinkets. Temptation: mini-fridges loaded with soda or donuts.

**Added to the room, complete list** `[D]` Plants for air purification and natural sunlight to combat
fatigue and regulate circadian rhythm. An inspiring view: horizon lines, emerald fields or mountains.
Optimal temperature 21 to 25 degrees Celsius. Conditioning stimuli: rhythmic instrumental music, binaural
beats, a burning incense stick. An office library of physical books you enjoy.

**Named equipment, complete list** `[D]` Automatic adjustable standing desk with preset height
adjustments. Motion board for use while standing, letting you rock side-to-side to offset pressure on each
leg. Inexpensive under-desk treadmill, for walking meetings and to trigger exercise-induced transient
hypofrontality. Seating: an ergonomic chair, a backless chair or saddle seat, a stability or exercise ball,
a kneeling chair, and a comfy couch or hammock for brainstorming. A single laptop plugged in and shut, plus
a single large monitor on adjustable arms. Wireless keyboard, trackpad and ergonomic mouse. Big
"shotgunner" earmuffs or noise-canceling headphones. Retro wired Apple headphones for walking meetings,
because the microphone on voice isolation mode blocks background noise. Cable tags and management systems.

**Arrangement rules, complete** `[D]` Analog dump station: a physical Moleskine notebook and pen on the
desk at all times. Screen height: the neutral middle of the monitor aligned with your natural eye level.
Inaccessibility positioning: desk placed so your back faces others, minimizing eye contact. Location
boundary: the office far enough from where you sleep for environmental separation, close enough to avoid a
draining commute. Feng Shui applied to the furniture arrangement.

**Friction elimination in practice** `[D]` Keep the computer always on with a screensaver so all you have
to do is tap one key to begin. Keep a pen handy so you never have to search for one. Adopt a strict policy
of "never putting things down, always putting them away".

**Boundary** `[A]` No prices, brands or model numbers are given for the standing desk, motion board,
under-desk treadmill, monitor or monitor arms. Moleskine and Apple are the only two consumer brands named
in the whole build.

**Science under it** `[M:Wheal]` Jamie Wheal, co-author of *Stealing Fire*, coined the term Flow Dojo.
`[M:Pavlov]` classical conditioning, given as the basis for the single-use rule. `[M:Gazzaley]` Adam
Gazzaley defined distractions as goal-irrelevant information. `[M:Ekman]` Paul Ekman on micro-expressions,
that the face reflects internal state, used for deterring disruptions.

**Sources:** q20 (authoritative), q09 (build it the night before a One-Month Day), q60, q59.

## Phone and Device Setup

**What it is** `[D]` The executable four-phase checklist that implements the phone rules. q18 is the rule
and the neuroscience; this is the configuration.

**When to use it** Once, as a setup pass, then re-run when apps creep back.

**Phase 1, the device itself** `[D]`

- Turn off every notification, pop-up and red dot for all apps, including essential ones like phone,
  messaging and calendar.
- Disable "Raise to Wake" and "Always On" display so the phone stays completely dark and silent until you
  intentionally interact with it.
- Activate greyscale or red-tint mode to kill the colour and reduce visual allure. Set up automations to
  activate these filters at specific times.

**Phase 2, the apps** `[D]`

- Delete all social media, games, news and distracting apps.
- Value test: remove all of them for a few days and see which ones you actually miss by necessity.
- Clear the home screen. Remove all app icons, folders and widgets from the primary home screen, leaving
  only between 0 and 4 essential apps visible.
- Create an isolated "Inbox" folder: gather all remaining communication and messaging apps into one folder
  named Inbox and move it to the very last page, to break the habit of mindless checking.

**Phase 3, the blocker** `[D]`

- Download and install the Freedom app on your phone.
- Configure permissions: allow Screen Time access and VPN setup, and do not allow the app to send you
  notifications.
- Build the block list: add all distracting apps, messaging apps, streaming apps, news sites, and specific
  personal guilty-pleasure websites. The named example is IMDb.
- Schedule recurring sessions on the Session screen: select your block list and create a recurring session
  for your daily flow block (for example 5:00 am to noon), for 90 minutes before bed, or run it 24 hours a
  day, 7 days a week for total restriction.
- Enable lock modes: in the settings menu, the gear icon at the bottom right, turn on Locked Mode and
  Uninstall Protection.

**Phase 4, desktop and analog** `[D]`

- Install Freedom on your work computer and apply the exact same block list and recurring schedule.
- Delegate phone functions to desktop: take calls, check your calendar, use desktop versions of social
  media, and use the desktop app "Step Two" for two-factor authentication.
- Swap for analog tools: buy a real alarm clock, wear a watch, carry a physical notebook.
- Relocate the physical device during flow blocks: power the phone off and put it completely out of reach
  and sight, locked in a cupboard, left in another room, or in your car.

**Every number** `[D]` 0 to 4 apps visible on the home screen. Recurring block session examples: 5:00 am to
noon, 90 minutes before bed, or 24 hours a day 7 days a week.

**Warning with no escape hatch** `[WRN][D]` Locked Mode plus Uninstall Protection makes the block completely
irrevocable. You cannot end the session early, delete the app, or log out to regain access. The corpus
supplies no counter-indication and no escape hatch for that state.

**Named software** Freedom (phone and desktop) · Step Two (desktop two-factor authentication) · Self-Control
(desktop blocker, named in q60) · WhatsApp (named as a violation example) · IMDb (named as a guilty-pleasure
block-list example).

**Sources:** q19, q18, q60.

## Flow Before Phone

**What it is** `[D]` Complete productive flow on your absolute highest-priority task in the morning before
you turn on, or even touch, your smartphone.

**When to use it** Every morning, as the entry-level phone rule.

**The threshold** `[D]` Two to three hours of morning flow before the phone (q18, q08). Competing value: the
morning flow block is given as 1 to 3 hours in q14 and q15. Both carried.

**Mechanism** `[D]` The smartphone is the primary 21st-century flow blocker because it disrupts focus with
distraction and uncertainty. Three named routes. First, variable reward novelty: unpredictable hits of
dopamine train the brain to constantly scan for new effortless stimuli, biasing attention toward rapid task
switching rather than sustained focus. Second, evaluation mode: looking at messages forces the brain into
decision-making ("Should I reply to this? Is it urgent?"), activating the prefrontal cortex, which is the
opposite of the transient hypofrontality flow requires. Third, the cortisol spike: news apps or stressful
messages activate threat-detection circuits, elevating cortisol and norepinephrine and shifting the nervous
system into a hypervigilant fight-or-flight state, when flow requires a calm balanced autonomic system.

**Escalation, three incompatible thresholds** All three are stated flatly in the corpus and none supersedes
the others: Flow Before Phone (2 to 3 hours), Phone Off Till Noon, and Phone Off Till Finish Line, which
locks the phone away the entire workday.

**Sources:** q18, q08, q60.

## Phone Off Till Noon

**What it is** `[D]` An advanced phone-fasting protocol, absolute in its rule: no phone use until 12:00 pm
every day, no matter what happens.

**When to use it** As the escalation from Flow Before Phone.

**What counts as a violation** `[D]` A violation is any interaction with or proximity to the device, not
just endlessly scrolling. Named "just a glance" violations: checking WhatsApp for a minute, reading a single
news headline, sending a quick text. Each shatters the focus tunnel. Keeping the phone in the room is itself
a violation: face down on your desk counts, and even powered off counts.

**Mechanism** `[D]` The mere presence of the phone is a distraction because the brain must expend cognitive
energy just to resist the temptation of picking it up, draining the battery of your brain.

**The complete replacement set** `[D]`

- **Physical placement.** Keep the phone completely out of sight and out of reach, ideally locked in a
  cupboard or in another room entirely.
- **Analog replacements.** A real physical alarm clock to wake up, a watch to check the time, a physical
  notebook on the desk to jot down thoughts.
- **Delegate to desktop.** Check your calendar, take calls, use desktop apps for two-factor authentication,
  and use desktop versions of social media if absolutely necessary. Desktop interfaces lack the highly
  addictive slot-machine user experience designed into mobile apps.
- **Batch your communication.** Tell colleagues, friends and family about your new communication schedule so
  they know when to expect a reply, and batch all message checking into a dedicated window later in the day.
  Schedule that batch during the afternoon biological trough, when energy and cognition naturally dip.
- **Set up an emergency channel.** Designate a specific separate channel and ask key people to use it
  exclusively. If you must stay reachable, for example meeting someone for an early flight, leave the ringer
  on and ask them to call you directly, bypassing the need to open addictive apps.

**Boundary** `[A]` No reset or recovery rule is given for what to do after a violation. The protocol defines
violations exhaustively and says nothing about how to resume.

**Science under it** `[M:Asurion]` a survey found people check their phones 96 times a day. `[M:Basex]`
unnecessary interruptions cost US businesses $650 billion a year. Both are cited in q59 as the support for
this protocol, with no study design in either case. `[M:unnamed]` q33 states that simply having your phone
visible, even powered off, measurably drains working memory and cognitive performance, with no researcher,
study or sample named.

**Sources:** q18, q33, q59, q60.

## The Possession Purge

**What it is** `[D]` A one-day physical minimalism protocol that reduces cognitive load by removing
possessions, on the argument that each item you own captures a piece of your attention.

**When to use it** As a single scheduled event, not spread over weeks.

**The complete step list** `[D]`

1. Dedicate one full day and schedule it as a singular event, so it does not sprawl out exhaustingly over
   weeks.
2. Take absolutely everything you own from every room and pile it onto the floor of your most spacious room,
   forcing you to confront the true volume of your possessions.
3. Pick up every single item and assign it to one of two binary piles, Keep or Cut.
4. Take everything in the Cut pile and immediately remove it from your life by selling, donating, recycling
   or trashing it.

**The filtering question changes by tier** `[D]` Three tiers of minimalism are named, and which question you
use depends on how extreme you want your minimalism to be.

| Tier | Who it is for | Filtering question, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 1, Aggressive Minimalists | Those whose singular priority is professional purpose and accessing flow | "Is this a tool that advances my craft and professional pursuit directly?" If yes, keep it; if no, cut it |
| 2 and 3, Tempered or Mild Minimalists | Those balancing ambition with a household or family | "What am I assuming I'll get from this, and is it worth the cost of ownership?" |
| 2 and 3, alternative framing | Same | "Do I love this more than the potential cost of my attention and professional progress?" |

q53 gives a shorter pair of the same filters, verbatim: "Is this a tool that advances my craft and
professional pursuit directly?" and "Is this worth the cost of ownership?"

**Boundary** `[A]` Three tiers are named but only two filtering questions are supplied. Tier 2 and Tier 3 are
never distinguished from each other.

**Mechanism** `[D]` Physical possessions actively increase cognitive load because each item you own captures
a piece of your attention, requiring mental resources for recognition, categorization, maintenance and
decision-making. q53 states the same as a cost of ownership: possessions must be managed, maintained, stored,
cleaned and worried about, draining the mental RAM required to access flow. Flow requires all of your
attention on the present moment, which is impossible when the environment forces the brain to constantly
filter out irrelevant physical stimuli.

**Warnings and principles** `[WRN][D]` Clutter strongly predicts procrastination, fatigue and negative
emotions, and actively blocks flow. `[D]` "The possessions you possess possess the mind": the owner becomes a
slave to their belongings. `[D]` Every single item removed reclaims cognitive capacity that can be channelled
directly into your work.

**Science under it** `[M:Sweller]` cognitive load theory, the amount of information working memory can hold at
any given time, given as the justifying science for the whole protocol. `[M:Hook]` Joshua Hook (University of
North Texas) ran a systematic review proving minimalism strongly increases intrinsic motivation, autonomy and
mastery. `[M:Csikszentmihalyi]` noted the cognitive cost of possessions draining psychic energy.

**Note on the merge.** Pass 2 names q53 as the authoritative file for this protocol, with q21, q56 and q59
merging in. q53 sits in the leverage lane. The four-step procedure and the three-tier filter above come from
q21, which is the only file carrying them.

**Sources:** q21, q53, q56, q59.
