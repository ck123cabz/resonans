## Hedonic Prioritization

**Type:** [MEC] / [WRN] · **Provenance:** [D]; the science behind it is [M:Berridge], credited only in q59 · **Sources:** q34, q59

**What it is.** The trap of choosing to work on tasks that trigger psychological pleasure, such as
high-level strategizing, rather than the painful, low-flow tasks the business actually needs to grow.

**When to use it.** As a diagnostic when the calendar is full, the days feel productive, and the one
constraint choking the business has not moved in weeks.

**Mechanism.** Kent Berridge (University of Michigan) found that the brain's "liking" and "wanting"
systems are separate, and that dopamine is the molecule of craving rather than of pleasure. Doris
names nobody in q34. q59 is the single file in the corpus that credits Berridge, and it names
hedonic prioritization as the thing his work explains.

**The correction.** The 8-Minute Method, below.

## Getting Addicted to Discipline (the 8-Minute Method)

**Type:** [PRO] · **Provenance:** [D]; step 2 rests on [M:Grossmann] · **Sources:** q34, q59

**What it is.** A three-step sequence that splits your mind into the part that sets strategy and the
part that does the work, so the schedule is written by the strategist and executed by the worker.

**When to use it.** When you keep picking the enjoyable task over the necessary one, and when the
work you avoid is the work that would move the constraint.

**The complete sequence, three steps:**

1. **Decide what you want.** Consciously choose between activity-based pleasure, which runs on
   dopamine and craving, and growth-based progression, which triggers serotonin, the signal of
   safety and status in the evolutionary brain.
2. **Set your strategy as if someone else will execute it.** Pretend you have a robotic clone who
   will do whatever is required with zero complaints. Use cold cognition to map out exactly what the
   business needs that clone to do to grow maximally, free of the psychological contamination of
   your own preferences.
3. **Let the business-first strategy dictate your schedule.** When you sit down and hot cognition
   tempts you toward a fun task, stop and ask: "Is this what the business needs, or is this what I
   want to work on?" Then follow the cold-cognition schedule you laid out for your clone and ignore
   the in-the-moment impulse.

**Mechanism.** Activity-based pleasure runs on dopamine and craving. Growth-based progression
triggers serotonin, which signals safety and status. Step 2 works by self-distancing (see the
Solomon Paradox section below), which dampens the brain's egocentric networks.

**The name carries no number.** [A] The video is titled "How To Get Addicted To Discipline In 8
Minutes" and no step in the sequence is timed. The total is never stated as eight minutes anywhere
in the file. Pass 2 §3.6 excludes 8 minutes from the numbers index as a video-title figure. Do not
prescribe it as a duration.

## The Solomon Paradox and Self-Distancing

**Type:** [MEC] / [CIT] · **Provenance:** [M:Grossmann], confirmed by q59; presented unattributed in q34 · **Sources:** q34, q59

**What it is.** The Solomon Paradox, named after the biblical King Solomon, who gave incredibly wise
advice to others while his own personal life was a disaster. Igor Grossmann (University of Waterloo)
is the source q59 credits for discovering it, and for showing that a third-person perspective
rapidly improves problem-solving. q34 names no researcher, no study and no publication.

**When to use it.** Any decision about your own work where you can predict you will rationalize.

**The cause.** Egocentric bias. Thinking about your own problems zooms you in, and your emotions
completely cloud your logic.

**The move.** Evaluate the problem from a third-person perspective, as if it is happening to someone
else. This bypasses the emotional circuitry, dampens the brain's egocentric networks, and unlocks
intellectual humility and faster problem-solving.

**Where it sits in a protocol.** Step 2 of the 8-Minute Method operationalizes it as the robotic
clone: write the strategy for a person who is not you.

## Hot Cognition and Cold Cognition

**Type:** [MEC] · **Provenance:** [D], unattributed in the corpus · **Sources:** q34

**Cold cognition.** How you think when calm and planning, when you can see clearly what needs to be
done.

**Hot cognition.** How you think in the moment: pulled toward what feels rewarding, pushed away from
what feels uncomfortable.

**Why the split matters operationally.** Every protocol in this file that separates planning from
doing rests on it. You write the schedule in cold cognition and execute it in hot cognition, which
means the schedule must be written to survive an executor who will want to renegotiate it.

**The test to run in the moment.** "Is this what the business needs, or is this what I want to work
on?"

## The Domino Habit

**Type:** [PRO] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q35 (authoritative), q34, q60

**What it is.** The single daily behaviour that, when executed, automatically corrects the rest of
the day and makes peak-performance habits fall into place naturally. It acts as the lead domino:
once flicked, it starts a cascading chain of productivity and alignment.

**When to use it.** When your days are inconsistent and you are trying to fix them one habit at a
time. Find the one that carries the others instead.

**Identification question, verbatim.** Look back at the periods in your life when you were the most
happy, fulfilled and productive, and ask: "What is the one habit that has been a constant for me
during those periods?"

**Qualification test.** A habit qualifies if nailing it practically guarantees your day will be an
**8 or 9 out of 10**.

**Qualifying examples as given.** An early morning workout · a meditation session · reading for **60
minutes** · diving straight into a focused work block immediately on waking. His own worked example
throughout is waking at **5:30 am** and going straight into flow. The specific habit varies from
person to person.

**Forgiveness rule.** Miss the Domino Habit and you wait for the next alarm, pivot on it, and put the
next 24-hour cycle back on track.

## Hinges and Temporal Landmarks

**Type:** [PRO] / [NUM] · **Provenance:** [D]; the temporal-landmark research is [M:UPenn] · **Sources:** q35 (authoritative), q34 (holds the attribution), q60

**What they are.** Temporal landmarks are markers in time that separate your past self from your
future self and produce a "fresh start effect." Hinges are the physical phone alarms you set for each
one. A door pivots on its hinges; the alarms are the pivotal turning points signalling the shift from
one activity to the next.

**Doris's own move on top of the research.** Place the landmarks *inside* the 24-hour day, so instead
of one fresh start per year or per week you get a continuous fresh-start effect driving behavioural
consistency.

**Attribution.** q34 credits the University of Pennsylvania for the temporal-landmark research and
gives no researcher, paper or year. q35 covers the identical claim with only "researchers have
found" and drops the attribution entirely. q59 lists UPenn against "behavioral consistency via
Hinges."

**Setup, complete:**

1. Identify your Domino Habit (see the section above).
2. Reverse-engineer the 24 hours leading up to it and list every essential milestone you must hit to
   guarantee the habit happens.
3. Set phone alarms for a **maximum of 6** of those landmarks.

**Why 6 is the cap.** More than six makes the system too complex, disrupts natural flexibility, and
drastically lowers your likelihood of adhering to them.

**Full worked example, q35's version, all six with their stated purposes, for a 5:30 am Domino Habit:**

| Time | Hinge | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 5:00 pm | Work finishes | Hard stop for the professional day |
| 6:00 pm | Finish eating | Food cut-off so digestion does not ruin sleep |
| 8:00 **am** | Reboot routine | Start of recovery |
| 8:30 pm | Ready for bed | Start of wind-down |
| 9:00 pm | Be in bed ready to sleep | Lights out |
| 5:30 am | Wake up and flow | The Domino Habit itself |

**Two defects in that example, both preserved rather than corrected.** The third row reads "8:00 AM"
inside an otherwise pm-ordered evening sequence and is near-certainly a typo for 8:00 pm. Separately,
q34 gives a different chain for the same example: finish eating by 6:00 pm, start winding down by
8:00 pm, be in bed by 8:30 pm. q35 puts ready-for-bed at 8:30 pm and in-bed at 9:00 pm. Both readings
travel; the corpus never reconciles them.

**Three mechanisms by which intraday landmarks lower cognitive load,** where cognitive load means the
amount of information held in working memory:

1. **Slicing the day.** Breaking the day into manageable segments reduces strain on working memory
   and boosts efficiency.
2. **Simplifying decisions.** Knowing exactly when to start and stop entirely removes the "what's
   next" decision, which drastically reduces procrastination.
3. **Outsourcing time management.** Time management shifts from an active mental juggling act to a
   passive unconscious process. You act like a driver following a GPS, trusting the system to tell
   you when to turn, so all your attention stays on the task in front of you.

**Forgiveness rule.** Miss one alarm and you simply pivot on the next one to get the 24-hour cycle
back on track.

## The Fastest Method (Domino Habit plus landmarks plus hinges)

**Type:** [PRO] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q34

**What it is.** q34's named three-step protocol that uses time itself as the mechanism to auto-correct
behaviour. It is the assembly instruction for the two sections above.

**The complete sequence:**

1. **Identify your Domino Habit.** Look back at your most fulfilling, productive periods and identify
   the one habit that acted as a constant foundation.
2. **Reverse-engineer your temporal landmarks.** Map every essential milestone in the prior 24 hours
   you must hit to guarantee the Domino Habit happens.
3. **Set your Hinges.** Phone alarms for a maximum of **6** of those landmarks.

**Mechanism.** Hinges decrease cognitive load because they outsource time management to the alarm,
which lets you focus fully on the present moment.

## Laziness as Life Maintenance Load

**Type:** [PRN] / [MEC] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q36, q60

**The rejection.** Laziness is not an explanation. What masquerades as laziness or a lack of
motivation is the invisible, draining toll of Life Maintenance Load and Systemic Friction.

**Life Maintenance Load (LML).** The mundane everyday tasks required to keep your life running:
grocery shopping, paying bills, cleaning, running errands. These are "exotelic" activities, performed
purely as a means to an end to avoid negative consequences.

**What it does to performance.** It constantly fragments your day, steals time from meaningful work,
and keeps the brain's default mode network buzzing with trivial, self-referential chatter that
actively blocks flow.

**The replacement diagnosis.** Run the Life Maintenance Audit (below) instead of trying to fix your
motivation.

**Standing claim.** A low Life Maintenance Load is a premium, compounding asset that buys back the
calendar space required for peak performance and deep flow.

## Systemic Friction

**Type:** [MEC] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q36

**What it is.** The constant micro "I have to deal with this" moments that interrupt smooth linear
effort and hijack it toward something utterly meaningless.

**Mechanism.** Every bit of friction acts like a slow leak in a car tire: tiny zaps of irritation
that literally spike cortisol, causing physical stress that leaves you flat, exhausted and unable to
start hard work.

**The full micro-irritation list, all seven as given:**

1. Digging a phone charger out of your backpack and having to untangle it.
2. Hunching over to plug a power cable into an awkwardly placed outlet.
3. Being interrupted by a prompt to enter a two-factor authentication code.
4. Getting put on hold with your bank for **45 minutes**.
5. Realising you forgot your lunch and having to walk all the way back to get it.
6. Pinging emails back and forth trying to schedule a call across different time zones.
7. Doing dishes, general cleaning, and waiting in lines.

**Note on the opposite prescription.** The corpus also instructs you to *manufacture* friction
deliberately against phone habits, by hiding the device or putting it in another room. q60 flags this
as one of its own six self-contradictions. Both readings stand: friction is the enemy inside the
workspace and a tool against a bad habit.

## The Life Maintenance Audit

**Type:** [PRO] / [NUM] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q36 (as the laziness correction), q25 (as the "simple math" method, with the equation and the repeat cadence)

**What it is.** The instrument that replaces "I am lazy" with a list of removable tasks. q25 presents
the identical procedure under a second name, the "simple math" method, framed as forcing your brain
out of a rut by calculating how many waking hours go to life maintenance and swapping them for
high-value activities.

**When to use it.** When the work you care about keeps losing to errands, and when you have concluded
the problem is your motivation.

**The fixed equation, from q25.** **24 hours** in a day, minus about **8 hours** of sleep, leaves
exactly **16 hours** of waking life. That is the quantity the audit is dividing up.

**The complete protocol:**

1. **List and rank.** Write down every single daily, weekly and monthly life maintenance task
   consuming your time and energy. Rank them by how much time they require and by their general
   necessity.
2. **Score autotelic value, 1 to 10.** Rate each activity on how intrinsically enjoyable it is to do
   for its own sake.
3. **Apply the cutoff: 7 out of 10.** Any activity scoring **less than 7** is immediately flagged as
   a target for elimination.
4. **Set the work order.** Start at the top of the list, with the tasks that consume the most time
   and yield the lowest enjoyment, and apply "cash and creativity" to handle them.
5. **Eliminate first.** The first goal is always to rip the task out of your life entirely rather
   than falling for "dumb optimization." Worked example: instead of trying to make your weekly
   grocery run **20%** faster, eliminate the chore entirely by setting up auto-delivery.
6. **Automate and outsource second.** If you cannot eliminate a task completely, automate it,
   delegate it, or hack it down to a manageable size. Throw money at the problem if you have the
   resources: pre-made meal delivery, a weekly cleaner, a robo-advisor instead of managing complex
   personal investments.
7. **Repeat every 6 months**, to catch new maintenance tasks that have crept back into the schedule
   (q25).

**Exotelic and autotelic, the terms the scoring uses.** Exotelic activities are performed merely to
meet a requirement or avoid a negative consequence: meal prep, cleaning, paying bills. Autotelic
activities are done purely for their own enjoyment and intrinsic purpose. A high volume of exotelic
tasks constantly fragments the day, forces the brain to switch gears repeatedly, and keeps the
default mode network buzzing with self-consciousness, which inhibits your ability to lose yourself in
your work.

**Population boundary.** [I] Every remedy the audit offers costs money: delivery services, a cleaner,
a robo-advisor, "throwing money at the problem." The file gives no version of the audit for someone
without discretionary cash or with non-delegable domestic obligations, and it treats all sub-7
activities as removable, which cannot hold for caregiving. This reading is Pass 1's, not the
corpus's.

## Scaling Blockers

**Type:** [PRN] / [WRN] · **Provenance:** [D]; the flow-channel frame is [M:Csikszentmihalyi] · **Sources:** q37, q60

**What it is.** A seemingly insurmountable challenge that suddenly halts a business's growth, feeling
so overwhelming to the founder that they view it as nearly impossible to overcome.

**Worked example.** A previously smooth sales process breaking down as the company matures, turning
what used to be energizing work into an exhausting grind.

**Mechanism.** Hitting a scaling blocker spikes the difficulty level and pushes you out of the flow
channel, the sweet spot between boredom and anxiety, into a state of high anxiety. q37 states this
flat with no attribution; the flow channel is Csikszentmihalyi's, per Pass 2 §1.1.

**The failure it produces.** Because founders lose access to the blissful state of flow that got them
into business building in the first place, they quit the current project and disperse their focus,
often by starting an entirely new company, in a misguided attempt to find an easier path back into
flow.

**Related construct.** q60 names the **Flow Plateau**: a flatline in growth where you master a skill,
become bored, and get tricked into wanting to start a shiny new business. Same exit, different
entrance.

## The Scaling Blocker Protocol

**Type:** [PRO] / [NUM] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q37

**What it is.** The five-step protocol for breaking through the one constraint that has halted
growth, without starting something new.

**When to use it.** When growth has stopped, one problem is obviously the cause, and you can feel the
pull toward a fresh project.

**The complete sequence, all five steps:**

1. **Pinpoint the blocker.** Define the exact constraint choking your business in a single sentence.
2. **Eliminate dispersion.** Pause everything else you are working on except basic maintenance
   activities and go all in on this one challenge.
3. **The 3-Hour Rule.** Dedicate the first **3 hours** of every day exclusively to solving the
   scaling blocker, before checking email, taking meetings or touching your phone.
4. **Close the skill gap.** Use part of those 3 hours to actively acquire the new skills needed to
   solve the problem, which lowers the challenge level back down into the flow channel.
5. **Persist through the plateau.** Accept that breaking through can take **3 months, 6 months, or
   even 18 months** of singular focus.

**Population boundary.** [I] The protocol assumes total schedule control: pause everything but basic
maintenance, hold the first three hours of every day before any email or meeting, and sustain that
for 3 to 18 months. Nothing in the file addresses an operator who cannot pause the rest. Pass 1's
reading, not the corpus's.

**Scheduling conflict, unresolved by the corpus.** Three protocols compete for the same block of the
day with no integration rule: the 3-Hour Rule wants the first three hours, Boring Breaks want a 45-on
/ 5-off cycle, and a certainty window wants an unbroken 10 to 30 minutes. The corpus never says how
they nest.

## The Explore/Exploit Drive

**Type:** [MEC] · **Provenance:** [D]; the dopamine-firing science is [M:Schultz], credited in q59 against the Output Dip · **Sources:** q37, q60

**What it is.** The trade-off the brain's dopaminergic system manages between sampling new options
and sticking with a known one.

- **Explore.** Sampling new, uncertain options in search of a better reward, like finding a new berry
  bush. Because the outcome is novel and unpredictable, the brain releases a massive surge of
  dopamine.
- **Exploit.** Sticking with a known, predictable reward.

**The number.** Once a reward becomes predictable, dopaminergic neurons reduce their firing by **50% to 70%**. [A] No researcher, study or sample is named for this figure anywhere in 44,047 words. Pass
2 §1.2 lists it among the seven residual unsourced claims.

**Why the brain fights you the moment the business works.** Success brings predictability. In the
exploitation phase operations run smoothly, revenue is forecast and financial rewards climb, but the
biochemical dopamine reward flatlines because the uncertainty is gone. Unhappy with the drop, the
brain starts scanning for anything novel to generate a new biochemical high, tempting you to launch
new products or start new companies exactly when you should be patiently compounding.

**The three archetypes, from q60:**

| Archetype | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| The Explorer | Flutters between projects, starting but never finishing |
| The Exploiter | Hyper-focuses but can become blind to new opportunities |
| The Captain | Master of switching modes: exploits a primary business to capture exponential compounding while satisfying the biological need to explore through high-flow hobbies, environmental novelty or strategic learning |

## Redirecting the Novelty Craving Outside Work

**Type:** [PRO] / [NUM] · **Provenance:** [D] · **Sources:** q37

**What it is.** The three routes for feeding the explore drive somewhere other than your business, so
the dopamine drop of a predictable phase does not make you burn the thing you built.

**When to use it.** During an exploitation phase, when operations are smooth and you feel the pull
toward a new venture.

**The three routes, with their numbers:**

1. **High-flow pursuits.** Take up a challenging, high-risk hobby such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu,
   skiing or rock climbing, and do it **3 times per week for at least 6 months**.
2. **Environmental novelty.** Change your surroundings by moving, booking **month-long** stays in
   unfamiliar places, or tackling a concrete physical project such as a workshop or a vehicle
   restoration.
3. **Strategic learning.** Read **2 to 3 books per week** to generate dopamine through the
   acquisition of new information.

**Warning.** Without an outside source of dopamine during an exploitation phase you will sabotage the
business by chasing shiny objects.

**Population boundary.** [I] The three routes assume the same freedom the persistence rule does:
discretionary cash, schedule control, and no caregiving load that a month elsewhere would break.

## Why People Quit

**Type:** [WRN] / [PRN] · **Provenance:** [D]; the burnout triad is [M:Maslach] and [M:Freudenberger], credited only in q59 · **Sources:** q37, q60

**The four causes the corpus gives, in the order it gives them:**

1. **The scaling blocker.** The challenge spikes, you leave the flow channel for anxiety, you lose
   the state that made the work worth doing, and you disperse into a new project to find an easier
   way back to flow.
2. **The exploitation dip.** The business starts working, predictability collapses the dopamine
   reward by 50 to 70%, and the brain goes shopping for novelty.
3. **Burnout.** A distinct medical condition characterised by exhaustion, cynicism and low
   self-efficacy, completely collapsing motivation and altering neural circuitry. q37 names no
   source. This is Maslach's three-factor model; q59 credits Maslach for the inventory and the six
   validated triggers, and Freudenberger for coining the term in the 1970s while studying free
   clinic volunteers. [A] Only 2 of Maslach's 6 triggers are ever enumerated in the corpus.
4. **Pursuing the wrong kind of hard.** When hard work feels like a grueling chore rather than an
   engaging challenge, you are either burned out or working against your strengths.

**The contradiction the corpus states about itself.** [A] q60 flags that Doris both instructs you to
build a Goal Stack spanning decades and warns that "thinking long term actually dramatically
increases the likelihood that you will quit," because of ambiguity intolerance. His own position is
6-to-18-month Certainty Windows, and he says he "hasn't been certain past 6 months for the last 20
years." Both instructions stand in the corpus. There is no upload date on either, so this cannot be
read as a change of mind rather than an inconsistency.

## The Right Kind of Hard

**Type:** [PRN] / [MEC] · **Provenance:** [D]; transient hypofrontality is [M:Dietrich], credited in q59 · **Sources:** q37, q32

**The claim.** Hard work should feel easy when it triggers "effortless exertion," driven by transient
hypofrontality, the temporary deactivation of the prefrontal cortex that lets you lose your sense of
self and time.

**How to get it.** Align tasks with your natural strengths and fuel them with intrinsic motivation:
curiosity, mastery, autotelicity, purpose, autonomy. Extrinsic rewards such as money or status do not
produce it.

**The payoff claimed.** Intrinsically motivated work gives a steady drip of dopamine and endorphins,
making complex problem-solving feel as effortless and addictive as playing a video game or scrolling
TikTok.

**The diagnostic pair.** If the work feels like a grind, the two candidate causes are burnout and the
wrong kind of hard. The corpus gives no test to tell them apart.

**The unresolved question this file cannot answer.** Pass 2 §3.4 item 8 records that procrastination
has two mutually exclusive treatments in this corpus and no selection test: force engagement through
the approach-avoidance conflict (q30, fixes 1 through 6), or abandon the task because the resistance
signals ambivalence, values misalignment or a burnout path (q30, fix 7). Pass 2 calls this the
highest-value unresolved item in the corpus. A lens asked "should I push or stop?" must say the
corpus does not decide.
