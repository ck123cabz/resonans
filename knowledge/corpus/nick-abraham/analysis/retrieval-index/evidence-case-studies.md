# Evidence & Case Studies: Nick Abraham

## Campaign Case Studies

### Competitor-Shutdown Campaign (Bench Bankruptcy)
**Source:** M131 [V] 200-2024-12-30
**Type:** Signal-based, event-triggered

When accounting platform Bench went bankrupt, Leadbird executed an immediate competitor-shutdown campaign: (1) scraped Bench's LinkedIn followers, (2) filtered out employees and finance companies, (3) cold emailed offering free migration — "Saw you follow Bench. We do what they did, but better. Here's why..."

This demonstrates the signal-based campaign archetype: a real-world event creates a window of relevance that makes cold email feel timely rather than intrusive.


### PE Off-Market Deal Sourcing
**Source:** E37 [V] 695-2024-08-09
**Result:** 142 meeting-ready leads

Generated 142 meeting-ready leads for a Private Equity client using cold email targeting off-market investment opportunities. Key finding: Apollo's revenue filter cannot be trusted for PE use cases — headcount was used as a proxy for revenue range instead. Lead qualification was done by phone (SDR calls to check last 12 months revenue and EBITDA) rather than attempting to qualify via email.


### Summer 2024 Deliverability Recovery
**Source:** E38 [V] 701-2024-08-05
**Result:** Reply rates from 0.5-0.8% to 3-5% in 6 weeks

During the Summer 2024 "deliverability apocalypse" that hit the entire industry, Leadbird took reply rates from 0.5-0.8% back to 3-5% in 6 weeks while sending approximately 1.5M cold emails. Recovery actions: dropped to 5 or fewer daily cold emails per inbox, reduced warm-up volume, implemented ESP matching (Google sends to Google, Microsoft to Microsoft), and hardened infrastructure rotation.


### 212 Leads in 60 Days
**Source:** E23 [V] 212-2025-08-08
**Result:** 212 leads, 1 lead per 140 contacts

Three success factors: (1) purely conversational CTAs ("Interested?" / "Open to hearing more?") instead of direct meeting asks; (2) SDR team followed up on every positive reply with a phone call within the hour; (3) client had a strong, cold-traffic-ready offer with clear risk reversal.


### Catch-All-Only Campaign
**Source:** M58 [V] 294-2025-05-27
**Result:** 7 additional qualified leads in one month

Running a separate campaign with only verified catch-all addresses (same winning copy, angle, and targeting as the primary SMTP-valid campaign) generated 7 additional qualified leads that would have been left on the table. Catch-all campaigns consistently produce 30-80% higher reply rates because these addresses tend to be at smaller companies with less inbox competition.


### Holiday Sending Validation
**Source:** E42 [V] 193-2024-12-31 and E31 [V] 524-2024-11-27
**Result:** 7+ leads/day including Christmas; Thanksgiving campaigns outperformed

On 150,000 emails sent over Thanksgiving Thursday and Friday, results met or exceeded typical weekday performance. Through December 23-31, cold emails still generated 7+ leads every day including Christmas. The holiday timing pattern creates a double advantage: less inbox competition (most senders pause) and more relaxed prospect mindset.


### Content-to-Demo Pipeline
**Source:** E49 [V] 326-2023-06-29
**Result:** 100 inbound demos in one month from content

Generated 100 inbound demos in May from content alone. The system: Nick records 1-2 hour Loom per week, content writer transforms it into 5 LinkedIn posts + 21 tweets, video editors create short clips. Total vendor spend approximately $5K/month at roughly $50 CPA. Small B2B audiences convert fine — the constraint is starting and staying consistent.


### Direct Mail ABM — Pizza Campaign
**Source:** E50 [V] 191-2025-01-11
**Result:** $15K spent → 75 clients signed → $1M attributed revenue

Sent 1,000 pizzas to target accounts at $15/pizza. 75 of those accounts signed as clients, generating $1M in directly attributable revenue. For small, high-value account lists, physical gifts outperform all digital channels on a per-contact basis.


### SaaS Free-Trial Cold Email
**Source:** M87 [V] 608-2024-10-08 and M89 [V] 616-2024-10-03
**Result:** $0 to $26K cash collected in 6 months

A developer created a unique SaaS account for each prospect and sent login credentials as a dynamic merge field in the cold email. Prospect logs in to try the product immediately — no demo required. The SaaS launch playbook: (1) lander with bold claim, VSL, social proof; (2) cold email campaign to ICP offering free trial; (3) build out after validating demand. Went from $0 to $26K cash collected in 6 months.


### Scrubby Validation — Fake-Automation MVP
**Source:** E25 [V] 269-2025-06-19
**Result:** $60K revenue before automating

Nick validated Scrubby (catch-all email verification SaaS) by building only the frontend and doing all verification manually as an agency service. Generated $60K in revenue before investing in automation. This is a case study in offer validation methodology — prove demand before building infrastructure.


### 14-Year-Old PPL Operator
**Source:** E54 [V] 129-2025-10-11

A 14-year-old running pay-per-lead for an untapped old-industry niche, discovered by asking ChatGPT for 100 personas to target and picking one. Cited as evidence that the methodology is accessible and that niche selection (not sophistication) is the primary variable.

## Failure Case Studies

### Over-Hiring Disaster (2023)
**Source:** O75 [V] 426-2025-01-28, O92 [V] 384-2025-03-04
**Cost:** Service quality collapse, significant churn

Over-hired and over-invested in systems in first half of 2023. New hires were undertrained, fulfillment quality degraded, clients churned. Recovery: simplify everything, lean out the team, refocus on fundamentals. Lesson permanently integrated: slow/steady growth beats growth-at-all-costs.


### Paid Ads Without Tracking
**Source:** E55 [V] 122-2025-12-30
**Cost:** $80,000

Spent $80K on paid ads relying on gut feeling without proper tracking or attribution systems. "I thought I could rely on my gut feeling. I was wrong. Data > gut feel." Lesson: know your numbers before spending on paid channels.


### Hiring Without Documentation
**Source:** O76 [V] 213-2025-08-07
**Cost:** $50,000+

Hired well but without documenting role expectations, output standards, or SOPs. New hires had no clear definition of "good" versus "not good enough." Fix: Loom + SOP for every task, clear output definitions, daily 1:1s for first 30 days.


### SaaS Distraction Trap
**Source:** O57 [V] 057-2026-01-13, O78 [V] 227-2025-07-25
**Cost:** Opportunity cost of agency growth

Built Scrubby, Quicklines, and Emy as SaaS byproducts of agency operations. Each required its own product roadmap, customer support, and sales process — becoming separate businesses that fragmented focus. The opportunity cost in forgone agency growth likely exceeded the SaaS revenue.


### Private SMTP Network
**Source:** IN18 [S→V] q39, IN34 [V] 169-2025-09-16
**Cost:** 1.5 years of building, $20K+/month eliminated

Spent 1.5 years building a private SMTP network. It worked until enterprise filters (Mimecast, Proofpoint) caught it. Eliminated $20K+/month in SMTP backup infrastructure after realizing the dual-failure scenario (both primary and SMTP failing simultaneously) never materialized in practice.


### AI Inbox Management at Scale
**Source:** O82 [V] 280-2025-06-09, O88 [V] 344-2025-04-08
**Cost:** 1,000 misrouted emails/day

Fully AI-powered inbox management achieved 90% accuracy, but the 10% failure rate across 10,000 replies/day meant 1,000 misrouted emails daily — categorically unacceptable across 100+ clients. The fix: AI auto-categorizes all replies, a human inbox manager reviews 100% of AI's work, correcting the 10% and learning from edge cases.


### No-Show Rate Crisis
**Source:** O66 [V] 122-2025-11-04, O68 [V] 130-2025-10-22
**Cost:** 30% no-show rate in 60 days

Cold caller no-show rate jumped to 30% because reps were compensated on bookings rather than quality. Root cause: reps did zero qualification to hit booking numbers. Fix: qualify on timeline, budget, decision-makers, and current solutions before booking; manager call reviews; compensation tied to show-up rate.


## Offer Transformation Examples

### HubSpot Demand-Capture → Demand-Gen Rewrite
**Source:** M32 [V] 047-2026-01-22
- **Before (demand-capture):** "We help HubSpot users market better"
- **After (demand-gen):** "[Name] — most HubSpot portals are missing X, Y, Z"
- **Principle:** Lead with the problem the prospect doesn't know they have, not the service category they might be searching for.

### Digital Transformation → Specific Outcome
**Source:** M50 [V] 215-2025-08-05
- **Before:** "Digital transformation consulting"
- **After:** "[Name] — if I could build processes/automations that save your team 8 hours/week, would you be interested in speaking?"
- **Principle:** Replace category language with quantified outcome.

### Bookkeeping Demand-Gen Reframe
**Source:** K33 [S] q37
- **Before:** Cold emailing all CEOs about bookkeeping
- **After:** Filter for first-time CEOs of companies founded <2 years with <20 employees and no finance team. Write copy specific to that moment: "Most first-time founders realize around month 8 that..."
- **Principle:** Targeting precision replaces personalization effort.

### Pay-When-We-Deliver Simplification
**Source:** K75 [V] 161-2025-06-30
- **$10K/mo agency:** "Our AI-powered Multi-Channel Engagement Matrix nurtures premium prospects through our proprietary 5-phase conversion architecture."
- **$100K/mo agency:** "You pay us when we get you leads."
- **Principle:** Complexity in positioning signals inexperience. Simplicity signals confidence.
