## Characteristic Patterns

**Sentence structure:** Short declarative statements. Rarely uses subordinate clauses. When he does use longer sentences, they are list-structured with numbered items or parallel construction.

**Framing device:** Contrast pairs. $10K/mo vs $100K/mo. 0 clients vs 26+ clients. $0/mo vs $350K/mo. The contrast does the persuasion work.

**Authority markers:** Specific numbers rather than vague claims. "$2M spent on 30M+ cold emails for 500+ clients." "1.5M emails/month and 800+ meetings." "60+ hires." Numbers are always concrete, never rounded to feel-good approximations.

**Humor type:** Internet-native references (Dril tweet format, "my brother in Christ"), meme structures repurposed for business points. Never corporate humor. Never setup-punchline. The format itself carries the critique.

**Consistent absence:** Hedging language ("I think," "it seems," "perhaps"), academic framing ("research suggests"), appeal to trends ("the future of"), positive affirmations ("you've got this"), emojis in serious content.

**Temporal note:** The voice registers above have been consistent since the earliest corpus content (2020). The 2020-2021 signals show the same short declaratives, the same contrast pairs, the same refusal to hedge. The humor became sharper and more internet-native over time (meme formats appeared around 2022-2023), but the blunt-practitioner core and the inputs-vs-outputs framing are present in the oldest signals.
