# Voice: Hooks & Openers -- Christian Bonnier Retrieval Index

The Copywriter Agent uses this file to calibrate opening lines, subject lines, and first-impression copy. Christian's openers share a few consistent traits: they are short, confident, and position the sender as already knowing something the prospect needs.

---

## Subject Line Patterns

### "Quick Question" (Durable but Cyclical)

Still works as of 2026, per LinkedIn Signal 37. Christian flagged it as burned in 2022 (X Signal 2: "Prospects are getting flooded with 'quick question' emails"), but market rotation brought it back. Use with awareness that saturation is cyclical.

### The "Intro" Fake-Warm Format

> "[Prospect Name] / [Your Name] intro"

Fakes a warm referral. NLM Signal 24. Deploy sparingly. Works because it mimics internal forwarding subject lines.

### Direct Guarantee Line

> "15 qualified calls per month guaranteed"

NLM Signal 24. Only for Level 1 offers where the guarantee is genuinely defensible.

### Curiosity Hook (Industry-Specific)

> "Looking to raise Capital"

NLM Signal 50 (web design teardown for startups). The subject has nothing to do with web design. It catches the prospect's attention by referencing their actual goal.

### Company Partnership Format

> "[Company] x [Your Company]"

LinkedIn Signal 37. B2B focused. Implies a collaboration rather than a pitch.

### Asset Availability Question

> "Do you have available [specific asset]?"

LinkedIn Signal 37. Service businesses. Frames the email as a business inquiry, not a sales pitch.

### Acquisition Hook

> "Acquisition target for [company name]"

LinkedIn Signal 37. Gets immediate attention because it implies someone wants to buy them. High-risk, high-reward.

### Blank Subject Line (Follow-Ups Only)

NLM Signal 6. All follow-ups thread into the existing conversation with no new subject line. This is not optional for follow-ups in Smartlead.

## Opening Line Patterns

### The Mass-Personalized Opener

Call out a trait shared by everyone on the list. Not a compliment. A contextual bridge into the pitch.

**Pattern:** "[Role/Industry/Location] + observation that connects to offer"

> "Hey [Name], I noticed you're in charge of [Company Name]'s marketing efforts."
> -- ListKit 47-word script, NLM Signal 37

> "I was looking at FFLs in Florida and found your company's website."
> -- Trojan Horse script, NLM Signal 38

> "I was checking out [Company]'s website/ads..."
> -- Trojan Horse template, NLM Signal 3

The opening line does two jobs: proves the email is relevant to them, and transitions directly into the pitch. No orphaned compliments.

### The "Dr. Last Name" Formality Variant

For professional verticals (physicians, lawyers), use formal address. Daavi campaign A/B tested "Dr. [Last Name]" against first names and formal won (NLM Signal 45). Adjust salutation formality to match the prospect's professional culture.

### The Bold Claim Opener (LinkedIn/Content Voice)

When Christian opens LinkedIn posts or content, he leads with a definitive statement or a reaction.

> "After sending 15 million cold emails..."
> -- LinkedIn voice pattern

> "This cold email is funny and borderline cringey."
> -- LinkedIn teardown opener

> "The businesses that want 'pay per closed deal' agreements are always the ones that can't close a door."
> -- LinkedIn Signal 19

These are not for cold email copy. They are for content, ads, and LinkedIn posts where the goal is attention, not reply.

### The Bewildered Incredulity Opener (Content Voice)

> "This guy opts in to our ad funnel. He starts receiving emails from us. Then he gets mad about receiving emails. Why did you opt in to begin with?"
> -- LinkedIn Signal 25

Christian frequently opens by expressing disbelief at how people behave, then shifts to patient explanation. The rhetorical question is the hook.

## The "I'll Skip the Fluff" Meta-Opener

> "Hey [Name], I'll skip the fluff and get right to it."
> -- Contextual Video Strategy script, NLM Signal 40

This is a direct, no-preamble opener that works when the email is slightly longer than the 50-word ceiling. It buys goodwill by acknowledging the prospect's time.

## Anti-Patterns for Openers

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Source |
|-------------|-------------|--------|
| "Hope this email finds you well" | Marks you as a salesperson instantly | NLM Signal 60 |
| "My name is [X] and I work at [Y]" | Wastes the 3-second window on zero-value info | NLM Signal 20 |
| "Big fan of your content" (when you don't follow them) | Fake compliment, ignored instantly | NLM Signal 26 |
| Self-introduction before value | NLM Signal 20: prospects decide in 3 seconds. If the opener is about you, you lose. | NLM Signal 20 |
| Company name as subject line with no context | Lazy. No curiosity trigger. | NLM Signal 49 |
| Self-disqualifying opener ("I know bookkeeping isn't the coolest topic") | Immediately bores the reader. If you don't believe in your offer, why should they? | NLM Signal 49 |

## Agent Instructions

When generating an opener:
1. Check the offer level (1/2/3). Level 1 can be direct. Level 2/3 needs a bridge.
2. Use mass-personalized openers for email campaigns (role/industry/location callout).
3. Use Sniper personalization only for DMs or Dream 100 (reference specific content, product, or podcast).
4. The opener must connect directly to the pitch. No orphaned compliments.
5. Keep the subject line under 5 words. Curiosity or internal-camouflage, never marketing-style.
