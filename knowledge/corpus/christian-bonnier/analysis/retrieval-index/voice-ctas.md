# Voice: CTA Phrasing Patterns -- Christian Bonnier Retrieval Index

The Copywriter Agent uses this file to generate closing lines for cold emails, follow-ups, video pitches, and ads. Christian's CTA philosophy: the first email sells the reply, not the meeting. Every CTA must tell the prospect exactly what happens next.

---

## Governing Principles

**Sell the Response, Not the Meeting.** For 99% of B2B offers, asking for a 30-minute call in the first email is fatal. The only goal of Email 1 is to get a "yes" to establish trust. (NLM Signal 23, LinkedIn Signal 42, confirmed `[D]`)

**No Calendar Links.** Propose two specific times first. Calendar link as backup only. Removing Calendly links and replacing with specific times produced an immediate jump in booking rate and higher show-up rate. (NLM Signal 25, X Signal 1, LinkedIn Signals 22, 32, confirmed `[D]`)

**Specificity Over Vagueness.** Stop using "Let me know if you're interested" or "Look forward to connecting." Every closing line must name a specific deliverable or commitment. (LinkedIn Signal 11)

---

## The Signature Cadence: "Mind if I [verb] it here?"

This is Christian's most durable CTA construction. It recurs across every source and every time period. (NLM Signal 36, confirmed across entire corpus)

**Examples from actual scripts:**

> "Mind if I share a quick video explaining further?"
> -- Trojan Horse script, NLM Signal 38

> "Mind if I share it with you here?"
> -- Free Deliverable script, NLM Signal 39

> "Mind if I share the leads here?"
> -- ListKit 47-word script, NLM Signal 37

> "Mind if I send over a portfolio?"
> -- Portfolio CTA archetype, NLM Signal 25

> "Mind if I send over a quick video showing what this could look like for you?"
> -- LinkedIn Signal 11

> "Mind if I share a video going over a cold email strategy I drew up for your business?"
> -- LinkedIn Signal 14

**Why it works:** Permission-request framing. Conversational. Low-pressure. Positions the sender as respectful of the prospect's space. The prospect only has to say "yes" or "sure." Binary response trigger.

## CTA Archetypes (Four Types)

### 1. Contextual Loom Video Pitch

The video CTA must give specific context about what's in the video. Not a generic explainer.

**Wrong:** "Interested in watching a video explaining how our outreach works?"
**Right:** "Mind if I share a video going over a cold email strategy I drew up for your business?"
**Right:** "Would you be open to checking out a video going over the exact script that generated $5K in revenue?"

The video must contain insights the prospect can't get from a competitor's pitch. (LinkedIn Signal 14)

After 15 million cold emails, this remains Christian's highest-converting CTA. (LinkedIn Signal 14)

### 2. Free Value / Lead Magnet

Offer a tangible deliverable that solves one specific problem.

> "I put together 50 sample leads based on the case studies on your website. Mind if I share the leads here?"
> -- ListKit script, NLM Signal 37

> "[Name], I revamped your welcome flow to optimize it for more revenue. Mind if I share it here so you can see what we do differently?"
> -- Free Deliverable script, NLM Signal 39

> "Mind if I send over a sample cold email script I wrote up for you based on your website?"
> -- Lead gen teardown, NLM Signal 53

**Lead magnet constraints (LinkedIn Signal 42):**
- Must solve one specific problem immediately
- Deliverable within 7-14 business days
- Priced at zero dollars
- Consumable independently without needing a call to explain
- Goal: achieve a quick win so booking the sales call becomes easier

### 3. Portfolio / Deliverable (Creative Services)

> "Mind if I send over a portfolio?"
> -- NLM Signal 25

> "I put together a Figma file of a redesigned landing page for you. Mind if I send it over?"
> -- Web design teardown "Home Run" CTA, NLM Signal 50

For visual/creative services, drop the Loom video and send the work itself. (NLM Signal 30)

### 4. Local "Coffee" Pitch (Brick-and-Mortar)

> "Connecting for a quick coffee at [Local Cafe]?"
> -- NLM Signal 25

For local businesses where in-person is natural. The named cafe adds specificity.

## The "Two Things" Template

A unique structural variant that front-loads two value propositions in a numbered format.

> "Two things quick... One, we can bring you 10 extra sales calls per month guaranteed... Two, I recorded a video going over an email marketing strategy I came up with for you."
> -- NLM Signal 52

This combines a guarantee CTA with a video CTA. Use when both proof and personalization are available.

---

## Booking Stage CTAs (Post-Reply)

Once a prospect replies positively, the CTA shifts from "sell the reply" to "book the call."

**Pattern:** Two specific times + backup calendar link

> "How does tomorrow or Friday at [time] work for a quick call to discuss pricing and next steps?"
> -- LinkedIn Signal 26

> "How does next week work for a quick demo?"
> -- ListKit subsequence, NLM Signal 47

**Rules:**
- NEVER just send a Calendly link (X Signal 1, LinkedIn Signal 22: "BOOK THE CALL MANUALLY !!!!!!!")
- Propose specific days and times first
- Calendar link only as a last-resort backup
- Book manually when the prospect verbally agrees (LinkedIn Signal 22)

## Follow-Up CTAs

### Detective Emoji (Email 4)

A single detective emoji. Nothing else. Step 4 in the 5-step Billboard sequence. (NLM Signal 5)

### Breakup Email (Email 5, Day 33+)

> "Would now be a better time to discuss [Benefit]?"
> -- NLM Signal 5

### Low-Friction Micro-CTA (Final Follow-Up)

> "I assume there's less than zero interest... reply 'send' and I'll shoot it over."
> -- LinkedIn Signal 29

Drops the commitment level to a single word reply.

## CTA Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| "I would love to show you..." | Needy, self-centered, breaks 2:1 Rule | Remove "I would love" entirely. Lead with prospect benefit. |
| "Let me know if you're interested" | Vague. No specific next step. | Name the deliverable: "Mind if I send over [specific thing]?" |
| "Hope to hear from you soon" | Passive. No action requested. | Propose a specific time or deliverable. |
| Calendar link in first email | Triggers spam. Presumes interest that hasn't been established. | Sell the reply first. Calendar link only post-reply as backup. |
| Sending a link unprompted | Triggers spam filters. | Ask permission first, send after "yes." |
| Pitching a 30-min call in Email 1 | Shows cards too early. Triggers sales defenses. | Offer free value or video instead. |
| "Let's jump on a quick call" | Asks for too much commitment from a cold prospect. | Lower the ask: video, lead magnet, portfolio. |

## CTA-to-Persona Matching

| Prospect Type | Recommended CTA | Source |
|--------------|----------------|--------|
| Visual/creative services buyer | Portfolio or Figma file | NLM Signal 30 |
| Startup founder | Frame around their actual goal ("raise Series A") | NLM Signal 50 |
| Older demographic (lawyers, doctors) | Ask for cell phone number, not Calendly | NLM Signal 30 |
| Influencers | State payment rate upfront ($500), offer to write the post | NLM Signal 30 |
| Brick-and-mortar | Local coffee meeting | NLM Signal 25 |
| Any prospect who replied positively | Two specific times + backup calendar link | NLM Signal 25, X Signal 1 |

## Agent Instructions

When generating a CTA:
1. Check the email position (Email 1 vs. follow-up vs. post-reply). Email 1 sells the reply. Post-reply sells the call.
2. Default to the "Mind if I [verb] it here?" cadence for Email 1.
3. Match CTA archetype to service type and prospect persona.
4. Never include a calendar link in Email 1. Ever.
5. For follow-up CTAs, rotate through: same CTA rephrased (Email 2), Loom video offer (Email 3), detective emoji (Email 4), breakup (Email 5).
6. Apply the 2:1 Rule to every CTA: count "I/me/we" vs. "you/your." Prospect references must outnumber self-references 2:1.
