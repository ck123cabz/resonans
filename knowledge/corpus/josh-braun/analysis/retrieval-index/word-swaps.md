# Word Swaps — Josh Braun Retrieval Index

Complete phrase substitution reference. Every "instead of X, say Y" pair from the corpus, plus ZOR trigger phrases and anti-pattern phrase lists. The Copywriter Agent and GTM Agent use this to filter generated output and coach cold call scripting.

## 1. Master Word Swap Table

These substitutions all move in the same direction: seller-centric to prospect-centric, high-pressure to low-pressure, formal to conversational.

**Sources:** P2.B1.S12, P2.B5.S27, P2.B2.S3, P2.B4.S31, P2.B4.S32, P2.B6.S3, P3.B1.S8, P3.B2.S2, P3.B4.S11, P3.B4.S13, P3.B6.S1

| # | Instead of (triggers ZOR) | Say this (lowers ZOR) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Can I be honest?" | "Mind if I'm direct?" | "Honest" implies you weren't before |
| 2 | "Does that make sense?" | "How does that sound to you?" / "What's your take?" | "Make sense" is condescending — tests comprehension |
| 3 | "Are you the decision maker?" | "Would you mind walking me through the decision-making process?" / "In addition to yourself, who else gets involved?" / "How did you make that decision last time?" | Makes them feel unimportant |
| 4 | "What's your budget?" | "Is there a ballpark range you're trying to stay within?" | Direct budget question triggers defensiveness |
| 5 | "I just wanted to check in" | "Worth revisiting or better to put this on the back burner for now?" | "Checking in" = withdrawal, no value |
| 6 | "Is price holding you back?" | "Feels like there's a ceiling to what you want to pay." | Direct price question puts them on the spot |
| 7 | "I haven't heard back from you" | "Have you deferred the project?" | "Haven't heard back" = blame. "Deferred" = neutral, triggers Zeigarnik |
| 8 | "Just following up" | Send a value-add deposit (article, calculator, relevant tool) | Following up says "I have nothing valuable but I need something" |
| 9 | "I'd love to..." | Drop entirely, or rephrase as prospect-centric | Centers the seller's desire = commission breath. Treat "I'd love to" like a porcupine. |
| 10 | "Just out of curiosity..." | Cut the preamble entirely | Fake-casual when you clearly have a vested interest |
| 11 | "I noticed / I saw" | "Looks like / It seems like / Your job ad suggests..." | First-person openers are overused and seller-centric |
| 12 | "Why did you...?" | "What made you choose to...?" | "Why" carries implicit judgment from childhood ("Why didn't you clean your room?") |
| 13 | "Can" / "Will" | "Might" / "Potentially" / "Possibly" | Autonomy support — softens the assumption |
| 14 | Formal tone | Casual tone (abbreviations, shorter sentences) | Formal reads corporate, casual reads human |
| 15 | "I understand how you feel. Many felt the same way. But what they found was..." | "It seems like you have some concerns." | Feel-felt-found is explicitly manipulative per Braun |
| 16 | "What keeps you up at night?" | "How are you currently handling [specific problem]?" | Lazy, overused, generic |
| 17 | "Do you have your calendar handy?" | "Would it make sense to..." / "Where would you like to go from here?" | Transparently transactional |
| 18 | "That's a great question!" | Just answer the question | Stalling, condescending |
| 19 | "We will increase revenue / save you time / reduce cost" | Describe the specific outcome in the prospect's context | Generic, unfalsifiable claims |
| 20 | "Trust me" | Don't say it. Build trust through actions and transparency. | Saying "trust me" signals you can't be trusted |
| 21 | "Sound fair?" / "Does that seem fair?" | "How does that sound?" / "What's your take on that?" | Manipulative upfront-contract language |
| 22 | "To be honest" / "Honestly" / "Frankly" | Just say the thing | Plants doubt about prior honesty |

## 2. CTA Swaps: Zone of Resistance vs. Zone of Acceptance

**Source:** P2.B4.S4
**Principle:** Gauge interest rather than ask for time.

### ZOR CTAs (avoid these)

- "Are you available for a brief call at {time}?"
- "Can we sync up?"
- "Do you have your calendar handy?"
- "Let's schedule a call on Thursday."
- "Can I get 30 seconds?"
- "Can I have 27 seconds of your time?"

### ZOA CTAs (use these)

- "Think this might help?"
- "Worth exploring?"
- "Open to taking a peek?"
- "Feel free to say no if you don't see a fit, but... might this help?"
- "Either way, rock the run."
- "Would it be a crime against humanity for you to see how they're doing it?"
- "Is this something you've explored?"
- "Worth a chat?"
- "Open to learning more?"
- "Worth considering?"
- "Open to another option?"
- "Would it be offensive to ask if you'd ever consider another option?"

### Unconventional CTAs

- "Send me your best objection :-)"
- "You're probably too busy to respond, but even a one-line reply would be enough"
- "Okay, thanks for reading. Send me your profanity-filled response."
- "If not, send me your best objection"

## 3. Cold Call Opening Swaps

**Sources:** P2.B6.S2, P2.B3.S18, P2.B5.S20

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Hi, this is Matt with Caldwell Realty. Is this Josh?" (attendance-checking triggers guard) | "Hi Josh, this is Mandy. I'm calling about the Inside Selling podcast, are you still the one handling sponsorships?" (first name only, no company, specific + relevant) |
| "Hi Kim, thanks for taking my call. Can I get 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling before you hang up on me?" (groveling, one-down) | "Hey Kim, it's Mike, we haven't met. I'm calling about the SDR team. Are you still the one leading that group?" (direct, confident, equal status) |
| "How are you today?" on a cold call (false familiarity) | "We've never spoken. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment." (honest, permission-based) |

## 4. Objection Response Swaps (Attached vs. Detached)

**Source:** P2.B4.S27
**Pattern:** Every attached response centers the seller. Every detached response labels the prospect's experience.

| Objection | Attached (don't) | Detached (do) |
|---|---|---|
| "Your price is too high" | "I understand how you feel. Many people felt the same way. But what they found was..." | "Seems like you're comparing this to something else." |
| "Send me an email" | "The best way is a 15-min call." | "Sure. To make sure I don't send irrelevant info, can I ask a couple questions?" |
| "We don't have budget" | Force a calendar slot anyway | "Sounds like the budget is reserved for existing vendors." |
| "Take me off your list" | "What would it take to change your mind?" | "Okay." |
| "I'm not interested" | Push for reasons | "That's okay. Before we hang up, is it timing or not relevant?" |
| "Call me back in Q2" | "What's going to change?" | "Sounds like you've got a lot on your plate right now." Then pause. |
| "I need to think about it" | "How much time do you need?" | "Seems like you have some concerns." / "Sounds like you're not sure it's the right fit." |
| "How are you different?" | List your differentiators | "What does 'different' look like for you, given what you're trying to achieve but haven't yet?" |

## 5. Question Upgrade Swaps ("Way 1 vs. Way 2")

**Source:** P3.B2.S1, P2.B4.S31

| Closed / Leading ("Way 1") | Open / Respectful ("Way 2") |
|---|---|
| "Is breathing air a priority for you?" | "How are you currently handling [specific problem]?" |
| "If I could 10X your revenue, would you be interested?" | "Not sure about you, but I often hear [specific problem]. How are you dealing with that?" |
| "Do you have any questions?" | "What questions do you have?" |
| "What are your pain points?" | "How are you currently dealing with [specific area]?" |
| "Do you want to move forward?" | "Where would you like to go from here?" |
| "Why did you build in-house?" | "What made you choose to build in-house?" |

## 6. ZOR Trigger Phrases and Replacements

**Source:** P1.B2.S17
**Context:** "Zone of Resistance" phrases — specific words and questions that make prospects enter a defensive state.

| ZOR Trigger | Why it triggers resistance | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "Who's the decision-maker?" | Makes the prospect feel unimportant | "Aside from you, who else needs to weigh in on this?" |
| "I haven't heard back from you" | Feels like blame | "Have you decided to defer the project?" |
| "What do I have to do to get your business?" | Desperation | Remove from vocabulary |
| "If I could do X, would you buy?" | Leading the witness | Ask a neutral question about the problem |
| "Can I have 27 seconds?" | Begging | State why you're calling with relevance |
| "That's exactly why I'm calling" | Dismissive, universal-plug-in response | Pause. Listen. Label what they said. |
| "We help companies like yours..." | Generic, self-serving opener | "Looks like you [specific observation]..." |
| "I know you're busy, but..." | Acknowledgment that what follows isn't worth their time | Drop the preamble, lead with relevance |

## 7. Softener Phrases (ZOR-Lowering Vocabulary)

**Sources:** P2.B4.S21, P2.B6.S6, P3.B3.S13
**Function:** Lower the Zone of Resistance by communicating "there are no assumptions here."

### Before Poke-the-Bear Questions
- "I'm not sure if you're grappling with this, but..."
- "I have no idea if you're bumping into this, but..."
- "Not sure about you, but..."
- "I'm not sure if this is for you, but..."

### Hedging Words (autonomy support)
- "Potentially"
- "Perhaps"
- "Maybe"
- "Might"
- "Possibly"

### Permission Phrases
- "If I'm not overstepping..."
- "Before we hang up..."
- "Would you be open to..."
- "Mind if I ask..."
- "Do you mind me asking..."

### Easy-Out Phrases
- "Either way..."
- "If you say no, I'll understand"
- "Feel free to say no if you don't see a fit"
- "Feel free to ignore"
- "Or not really"
- "Now you can throw this out (I get it)"

## 8. Equal Status vs. Low Status Language

**Source:** P2.B1.S7, P1.B4.S11
**Principle:** "Confidence is contagious. Equal stature creates honest discussion."

| Low Status (needy, deferential) | Equal Status (direct, peer-level) |
|---|---|
| "Thank you for picking up my call, I really appreciate it." | "I'm calling about the Inside Selling podcast. Do you still produce it?" |
| "Yes, of course. No problem. I'll send you a proposal right away." | "It sounds like if the proposal has what you want for the price we discussed, you're ready to move forward." |
| "I'll ask my boss if we can lower the price." | "It sounds like you're ready to move forward if the price is X." |
| "I understand that price is a significant consideration. Many people felt the same way. But what they found was..." | "It sounds like there's a ceiling to what you want to pay." |
| "I'd love to schedule a time to show you how we can help." | "Seems like this might help [outcome]. Is that something you'd potentially like to learn more about?" |
| "I'm sorry to bother you, I know you're really busy." | "[Name], we haven't met. I'm calling about [specific topic]." |
| "Would it be okay if I sent you some information?" | "I'll send you something specific. What would be most useful — X or Y?" |
| "Thank you so much for your time today. I really appreciate it." | "Good talking with you. I'll follow up with [specific next step] by [date]." |

## 9. Cold Email Copy Editing Swaps

**Source:** P2.B2.S5
**Principle:** "Write with an eraser."

| Flabby version | Clean version |
|---|---|
| "As per our conversation" | "Per our conversation" |
| "ACME is able to" | "ACME can" |
| "In order to" | "To" |
| "I wanted to reach out because..." | [Delete entirely — lead with the point] |
| "60X faster" | "In 5 minutes instead of 5 hours" |
| "We're the best / cutting edge / bleeding edge" | [Delete — self-serving claims] |
| "After sending my last email, I got really excited and wanted to share some ideas I had on how Starbucks could leverage Twilio" | "Here's how Starbucks might leverage Twilio." |
| "I know you are busy" / "I hope you're doing well" | [Delete — filler] |
| "A number of retail companies" | "Several retailers" |
| Round numbers ("saved 60% of time") | Specific numbers ("5 minutes instead of 5 hours") |
| "I trust this message finds you in good spirits" | [Delete — triggers immediate deletion] |

## 10. 26 Emotion Words for Cold Email Problem Statements

**Source:** P1.B4.S3
**Provenance:** [D:vis]
**Usage:** Drop into the {emotion} slot in the "What You Can Do" framework: "Do you know how {emotion} it is when {problem}?"

maddening, defeating, disheartening, nerve-wracking, mind-numbing, monotonous, unreliable, mistake-ridden, discouraging, tedious, arduous, draining, exhausting, aggravating, annoying, irritating, frustrating, upsetting, infuriating, disappointing, exasperating, displeasing, flustering, overwhelming, baffling, agitating

## 11. "Not [Objection]" Formula

**Source:** P1.B3.S15
**Principle:** Proactively bringing up objections defuses them.

- Brooklyn Best lemonade can: "Not too sweet"
- Cold email preemption: "No rebuilding your current process"
- "No long-term contracts or new tech"
- "No subscription fee"
- "No huge budgets or black hat techniques"
- "No new tech"
- "No prep needed"

**Formula:** Identify the primary objection your buyers have. Address it with "Not [objection]" or "No [objection]" before they raise it.
