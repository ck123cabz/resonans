## 1. Initial Outreach — Problem-Illumination Emails

### 1A. C2FO: Outstanding Invoices (B2B Fintech)

**Source:** P1.B1.S2
**Provenance:** [D]
**Framework demonstrated:** Cold Email Systems Check (all 10 elements)

```
Subject: Walmart invoices

James -

C2FO gives companies like yours access to early payments on
outstanding Walmart invoices.

Your latest AP aging report shows $412,212 in outstanding invoices.

Companies like X and Y (founded by [names]) use C2FO to accelerate
cash flow without taking on debt.

Think this might help?

Awaiting your fair but stern reply.

P.S. We tried your BBQ Bulgogi Sauce — incredible.
```

**Braun's annotations:**
- Two-word subject line ("Walmart invoices")
- Specific dollar amount from prospect data ($412,212)
- Named social proof (X and Y founders)
- Non-assumptive CTA ("Think this might help?")
- Humor deflection ("Awaiting your fair but stern reply")
- PS personalization referencing prospect's product (BBQ sauce)

**Why it works:** Every element from the 10-point systems check appears in a single email. The PS personalization ("We tried your BBQ Bulgogi Sauce") shows research depth beyond the business problem. It's personal, not just personalized.

5. GREEN DARKER — Preemptive objection handling ("No long-term contracts or tech")
6. PINK — Low-pressure CTA ("Open to learning more?")

**Why it works:** The observation is hyper-specific. The poke-the-bear question surfaces a metric gap the prospect might not have quantified. The objection handling is ultra-short — two concerns addressed in six words.

### 1F. Fleet Telematics: Tax Overpayment (B2B Logistics)

**Source:** P1.B3.S7
**Provenance:** [D] + [D:vis]
**Framework demonstrated:** 6-element anatomy (most detailed version)

```
Hey James -

Noticed you manage a fleet of 300+ vehicles and use
telematics for routing.

How are you using that data to make sure you're not
overpaying on fuel taxes?

Most fleets miss tax recovery opportunities because
general telematics data doesn't break things down at
the tax-jurisdiction level.

2,000+ fleets have recovered over $10M using our
tax-granularity layer.

You probably have a system in place that seems to be
working, but even well-run ops can have hidden
overpayments.

No rush, but is this worth exploring?
```

**Braun's annotations:**
1. RELEVANCE — specific observation about prospect (fleet size, telematics use), answers "why me?"
2. POKE THE BEAR — "How are you using that data to make sure you're not overpaying?" invites, doesn't push, opens a loop
3. KNOWLEDGE GAP — introduces a blind spot (tax granularity) that's "crispy" enough to be relatable
4. SOCIAL PROOF — specific and not overhyped (2,000+ fleets, $10M)
5. PREEMPTIVE OBJECTION — validates existing setup, gently introduces doubt ("even well-run ops can have hidden overpayments")
6. LOW-PRESSURE ASK — "No rush" lowers defenses, respects time

**Why it works:** The preemptive objection element is distinctive. Instead of waiting for "we already have something," the email validates the existing setup and introduces doubt gently. Braun's comment: "You can steal the template, but it's the underlying psychology that will help you apply it."

### 1J. Lawn Care: Neighbor Social Proof (B2C Local Services)

**Source:** P1.B3.S2
**Provenance:** [D:vis]
**Framework demonstrated:** 5-part psychology

```
Hey Pete -

Most homeowners in Boca Rio are paying around $140 every
two weeks for lawn care or $3,600 a year.

I run a network of independent lawn pros who batch jobs by
neighborhood. That cuts travel time and overhead, so we can
offer the same service for less.

Your neighbors, the Cantors, are paying $100 every two weeks
or $2,600 a year.

You probably have a long-standing relationship with your current
provider. I know trust beats price, especially if you've
never had issues.

Would it be offensive to ask if you'd ever consider another option?
```

**Braun's annotations (mapped to 5-part psychology):**
1. BLUE — "Why me?" — segment-level relevance ("homeowners in Boca Rio")
2. GREEN — "What's meaningfully different?" — mechanism explanation (batching by neighborhood)
3. YELLOW — "You're not alone" — neighbor as social proof ("the Cantors")
4. ORANGE — "I get your hesitation" — acknowledges incumbent relationship, doesn't knock it
5. PINK — "No pressure" — "Would it be offensive to ask" tiptoes like a neighbor over a backyard fence

## 3. Follow-Up and Re-engagement Emails

### 3A. Ghost Recovery Email (Cornerstone Template)

**Source:** P2.B1.S3
**Provenance:** [D]

```
Hi James -

It seems like you're putting [product/service] on hold,
which is not a problem.

Perhaps somewhere along the way, I dropped the ball.

Here's my ask, and I know it's a big ask because you don't
owe me a response.

Would you be open to sharing some feedback so we can better
serve you should a need arise in the future?

Either way, thanks for considering [company].
```

**Psychology:** Takes pressure off. Owns the situation ("maybe I dropped the ball"). "You don't owe me a response" makes it safe to reply. Reposted multiple times across corpus — Braun considers this a cornerstone play.

### 3B. One-Sentence "Deferred" Follow-Up

**Source:** P2.B3.S23, P2.B5.S8
**Provenance:** [D]

```

```

**Mechanism:** Leverages the Zeigarnik Effect — people remember unfinished tasks better than completed ones. Keep to 8-10 words. The word "deferred" implies an incomplete action, creating an open loop. "That's why the response rate is 100%."

## 8. ChatGPT Prompt Templates for Email Generation

### 8A. 4-T Cold Email Generator

**Source:** P2.B4.S22
**Provenance:** [D]

```
Generate a concise, value-driven cold email using:
(1) Trigger -- specific insight about prospect's role/team/company
(2) Think -- illuminate a potential problem
(3) Third-Party Credibility -- success metric or social proof
(4) Address Primary Objection -- remove friction
(5) Talk? -- simple CTA

Guidelines:
- Subject line under 3 words
- Email under 75 words
- Avoid fluff/jargon
```

### 8B. Cold Email From Customer Language

**Source:** P2.B3.S26
**Provenance:** [D]

Two-step prompt chain:
1. "What are the events or circumstances that cause [persona] to start looking for solutions like [product]?"
2. "Explain how [persona] are getting [trigger #1] done without [product]. Be specific."

Then use the specific current-state language in the cold email opening.
