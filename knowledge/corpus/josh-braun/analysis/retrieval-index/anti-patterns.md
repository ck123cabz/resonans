## 1. Phrases That Trigger Resistance

2. "If I could do X, would you buy?"
3. "Are you the decision maker?"
4. "Does that make sense?"
5. "Can I have 27 seconds?"
6. "We will increase revenue / save you time / reduce cost"
7. "Just checking in"
8. "Touching base"
9. "Sound fair?"
10. "Honestly"
11. "Frankly"
12. "Trust me"
13. "I understand how you feel. Many felt the same way. But what they found was..."
14. "That's exactly why I'm calling"
15. "To be honest"
16. "I'd love to..."
17. "Just out of curiosity..."
18. "That's a great question!"
19. "I know you're busy, but..."
20. "How are you today?" (on a cold call)

Each phrase fails for one of the same reasons: it centers the seller, presumes a relationship that doesn't exist, or creates pressure.

### 1C. Six Phrases That Kill Conversations

**Source:** P2.B4.S32
**Provenance:** [D]

| Phrase | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "To be honest" | Plants doubt about prior honesty | Just say the thing |
| "This feature is amazing" | Seller-centric hype — prospects decide what's amazing | Describe the outcome for them |
| "Do you have your calendar handy?" | Transparently transactional | "Where would you like to go from here?" |
| "Does that make sense?" | Condescending — tests comprehension | "What's your take on that?" |
| "I'd love to" | Centers seller desire = commission breath | Rephrase as prospect-centric |
| "Just out of curiosity" | Fake-casual when you clearly have a vested interest | Cut the preamble entirely |

### 1D. "I'd Love To" — Named Anti-Pattern

**Source:** P2.B4.S31
**Provenance:** [D]

Examples:
- "I'd love to hop on a quick call"
- "I'd love to get your feedback"
- "I'd love to show you a quick demo"
- "I'd love to set up a call"

All center the seller's desire. Prospects smell commission breath.

**Replacements:**
- "I'd love to show you..." -> "Would it be useful for you to see how others in your role are approaching Y?"
- "I'd love to know where we lost alignment" -> "Seems like we lost alignment"
- "I'd love to schedule..." -> Drop entirely

"Treat 'I'd love to' like a porcupine."

## 3. Structural Anti-Patterns (Tactics and Approaches)

### 3A. Aggressive Cadences / "Siege" Cadence

**Source:** P1.B1.S16, P1.B2.S13, P1.B3.S5
**Provenance:** [D]

The anti-pattern: 8-14 touches across 7-28 days, including multiple actions on Day 1. Tools like Outreach and SalesLoft default to these structures.

"If your message isn't interesting on touch #1, it won't magically become interesting on touch #14."

High-touch cadences are a symptom of bad messaging, not a strategy. Braun's alternative: 3-4 emails over a few weeks. One problem per email. Stop when they don't engage.

Braun names "Spider-Man Cadence" as a specific variant of the volume-as-strategy anti-pattern.

---

### 3B. Connect-and-Pitch on LinkedIn

**Source:** P1.B2.S27
**Provenance:** [D]

Example of the anti-pattern:
```
"Josh, thanks for connecting. We are an interactive media
platform that bridges the gap between traditional hands-on
training and the modern hands-off approach of modern video
training. Do you have time Thursday for a 15-minute chat?"
```

The message immediately asks for time without having earned any social capital. This is a withdrawal without a deposit.

---

### 3C. Sandler Upfront Contracts on First Calls

**Source:** P2.B1.S23
**Provenance:** [D]

The anti-pattern:
```
"If you have no further interest after our conversation we
can part ways, but if you do have interest, let's have
another meeting. Does that sound fair?"
```

Braun's translation: "At the end of this call, will you tell me if you want to buy my stuff?" Feels like a date saying "if this goes well, we can discuss next steps."

**Fix:** Gauge fit naturally. "It seems like this might be worth exploring further. Would it make sense to show this to your team?"

---

### 3D. Decorative Personalization

**Source:** P1.B2.S19, P2.B2.S7
**Provenance:** [D]

Personalization that doesn't connect to a problem or opportunity:
- "Saw you're based in Boca Raton — seen the iguanas running Boca streets?"
- "Josh, I noticed you like wine. What if you could hit all the right notes when it comes to retaining customers?"
- Researching individual hobbies/posts/schools and tying them to a pitch = "cosplay"

"Saying something nice to cash it in later is called manipulation."

**The distinction:** Relevant personalization (specific observation about how the prospect operates) vs. decorative personalization (local trivia, weather, sports teams, hobbies).

### 3E. AI-Generated Cold Email (Soulless Template)

**Source:** P1.B3.S26
**Provenance:** [D]

The FlashIntel example: AI-generated email that "checked all the boxes" but felt hollow.

Specific failure modes:
- Generic flattery ("I saw your post about using ChatGPT")
- Vague value prop ("automating personalized outreach")
- Corporate language ("streamline your efforts")
- No distinct personality
- "It felt like it came from a program, not a person"

This is the specific failure mode for the Copywriter Agent: hitting all structural elements while missing voice and personality. Structure-correct but soul-absent.

### 3F. Misleading Subject Lines

**Source:** P2.B2.S8
**Provenance:** [D]

- Using "Re:" in subject lines for first-time cold emails (fakes a prior conversation)
- "Re: contract" when there is no contract
- Any subject line designed to trick the recipient into opening

These may boost open rates but destroy trust on arrival. "The subject line's job is to get the email opened, not to deceive."

### 3G. Leading Questions (Leading the Witness)

**Source:** P1.B4.S4, P1.B4.S5, P3.B2.S21
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

Specific examples struck through in Braun's carousels:
- "If I could 10X your revenue, would you be interested?"
- "If I told you something interesting, would you be interested?"
- "Is breathing air a priority for you?"

These steer prospects toward a desired answer. They signal the caller has something to sell before any problem has been surfaced.

**Fix:** Ask neutral questions that illuminate problems without leading. Be a scientist testing a hypothesis, not a lawyer building a case.

### 3H. Feel-Felt-Found

**Source:** P2.B1.S28, P3.B2.S19
**Provenance:** [D]

"I understand how you feel. Many people felt the same way. But what they found was..."

Braun calls this manipulative and unethical. A technique designed to "force decisions today." Explicitly flagged in his phrase-to-avoid lists. Appears in every anti-pattern compilation in the corpus.

### 3I. Shame-Based Follow-Ups

**Source:** P1.B1.S6
**Provenance:** [D]

Example: "I haven't heard back from you. Do you have any questions? I think you're wasting your potential."

"When you tell people they're wasting their potential, you're subconsciously saying 'shame on you.' It feels like being scolded by your mom: 'Why haven't you cleaned your room?'"

Shame triggers defensiveness and resentment, not engagement.

### 3J. Parroting (Disguised as Labeling)

**Source:** P3.B5.S3
**Provenance:** [D]

Prospect: "I want to think about it."
Parroting: "Sounds like you want to think about it." (just repeating their words)
Real labeling (emotional echoing): "You don't want to rush into a decision you might regret."

Parroting reflects the surface statement. Labeling names the feeling underneath.

## 7. Named Anti-Pattern Labels

Braun creates memorable names for specific failure modes. These function as shorthand for coaching.

| Name | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Commission Breath** | The buyer-side sensation of being sold to by someone who needs the deal. Manifests as tightening, pushing, steering. |
| **Burn the Popcorn** | Cold call where the conversation is going well but you overstay your welcome asking questions instead of asking for the meeting. |
| **Premature Pitching Syndrome (PPS)** | Pitching your solution before establishing a problem. "Your solution has no value without a problem, so it's madness to pitch at the jump." Cure: whisper "Is there a problem?" |
| **Disney-Character Sales Voice** | The over-bright, over-eager, over-scripted tone. "People don't reject your pitch first. They reject your energy." |
| **Triple Tap Method** | Call once, let it ring 3 times, hang up, call back, hang up, call back a third time. Then lie: "I was trying to leave a voicemail and it got disconnected." |
| **Spider-Man Cadence** | Multi-touch volume cadence masquerading as strategy. The underlying belief that persistence = effectiveness. |
| **Zone of Resistance (ZOR)** | The psychological state prospects enter when they detect sales pressure. Not a sales technique name — a buyer experience name. |
| **Sour Grapes** | Cognitive dissonance: when prospects say "We don't need another tool" it's often the mind rewriting the story to avoid change discomfort. |
| **Love-Bombing** | Rapid agreement ("That's so interesting" / "You're spot on" / "Great point") that feels hollow from someone with something to sell. |

## 8. Mindset Anti-Patterns

These are not tactical mistakes but intent-level failures that produce bad tactics downstream. "Your intent affects your behavior. Neurons that fire together wire together."

### "Wanting People to Buy" as Root Cause

When the intent is to book meetings with everyone, the behavior becomes pushy. Prospects feel the push and pull away. The fix is not better technique — it's different intent. Shift from "get the meeting" to "discover if there's a problem."

### "Selling Is Helping" Fallacy

The helping mindset assumes people need your help. It leads to presuming rather than discovering. "People are already getting the job done. Your solution has no value without a problem they recognize."

### Treating Prospects Differently Than Friends

If a friend says "I can't talk," you say "Okay, I'll call you back later." But sellers say "That's exactly why I called. Most people can't talk when I first call because they don't know what it's about. Could I take 28 seconds..." The 28-second ask is a named anti-pattern.

### Intent-Behavior-Tone Chain

"Intent drives behavior. Behavior drives tone. Tone drives trust."

Wrong intent (close the deal) -> wrong behavior (pushing) -> wrong tone (desperate) -> trust destroyed.

Right intent (discover a problem) -> right behavior (asking) -> right tone (curious) -> trust built.

## 9. Things That Are NOT Inbound Leads

**Source:** P2.B1.S8
**Provenance:** [D]

These are intent signals, not inbound demand. Treat as outbound:

- Downloaded an ebook
- Attended a trade show

- Has a lead score above X
- Liked/commented on social media
- Subscribed to newsletter
- Watched a video
- Registered for free event
- Opened a cold email 6 times
- Clicked on an ad

**An actual inbound lead:** Filled out a demo request form.

## 10. Anti-Pattern Diagnostic: "Your Price Is Too High" Decoded

**Source:** P2.B4.S40
**Provenance:** [D]

When a prospect says "your price is too high," it means one of these things:

1. **They don't have the money** — you should have disqualified early
2. **They think they can get the same thing for less** — you didn't illuminate what's meaningfully different
3. **They think the solution isn't worth the money** — you didn't illuminate the cost of inaction

The anti-pattern: defending the price with logic ("but you'll save $X"). The fix: ask knowledge-gap questions that expose what cheaper alternatives are omitting.

## 11. Anti-Pattern Application Rule

Every anti-pattern in this document fails for the same meta-reason: it prioritizes the seller's needs over the prospect's experience.

The universal test (Braun's golden rule): "Sell unto others as you would have them sell unto you." If you wouldn't want it done to you, don't do it to them.
