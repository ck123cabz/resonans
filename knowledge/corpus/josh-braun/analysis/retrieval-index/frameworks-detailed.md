# Frameworks (Detailed) — Josh Braun Retrieval Index

Full versions of named frameworks that are too detailed for Core Knowledge. Includes all variants, worked examples, and the reasoning behind each element. The agent pulls individual frameworks on demand.

## 1. Cold Email Anatomy — All Variants

The cold email anatomy is Braun's most-iterated artifact. It evolved from a flat checklist (2023) to a psychologically-sequenced framework (2025). All variants below are compatible — different lenses on the same structure.

### 1A. 10-Point Systems Check (Priority-Ranked, July 2023)

**Source:** P1.B1.S1
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

Ranked by importance (the ranking itself is the insight):

1. **Two-word subject line** — "Walmart invoices," "Ironman Cozumel," "SDR job ad"
2. **Focused on an expensive problem** — not a feature, not a benefit: a problem
3. **Targets a niche group of people** — "When you're for any position, you're for no position"
4. **Addresses primary objection** — defuse the main reason they'd say no
5. **Meaningfully different idea** — what can they do now that they couldn't before?
6. **Social proof** — specific, not overhyped
7. **"You" to "we" ratio of 4:1** — prospect-centric language
8. **Personalization in the PS** — research proof, not the opener
9. **Doesn't ask for time** — gauges interest instead
10. **Less than 60 words** — density over length

**Agent note:** Subject line and problem specificity outrank social proof and personalization. Many sellers invert this priority.

### 1B. 4-T Framework (Named Mnemonic, March 2024)

**Source:** P2.B1.S11, P2.B2.S1
**Provenance:** [D]

Four elements, each starting with T:

1. **Trigger** — Why you're emailing this specific person. A recent event, job posting, team change, or company milestone that connects to a problem.
2. **Think** — Question illuminating a potential problem. The poke-the-bear element. "How are you making sure...?" or "What happens when...?"
3. **Third-party credibility** — Specific result similar people got + address primary objection. "SDRs at ACME use X to achieve Y. No long-term contracts."
4. **Talk?** — Gauge interest with low-pressure CTA. "Worth exploring?" / "Open to learning more?"

**Constraints:** Subject line under 3 words. Email under 75 words.

**Worked example (TitanX):**
```
Subject: SDR job ad

Pete -

Looks like you have 9 SDRs cold calling into HR.
Usually, that means 2-3 conversations per 50 dials.

How are you boosting your call-to-connect rate?

SDRs use Phone Ready Leads to have 8-10 conversations
every 40 minutes. No long-term contracts or new tech.

Worth exploring in Q2?
```

### 1C. 5-Part Psychology Map (Buyer Questions, April 2025)

**Source:** P1.B3.S1

Each section answers a different question in the buyer's head:

| Color | Buyer Question | Email Element |
|---|---|---|
| BLUE | "Why are you emailing me?" | Event/circumstance tied to a problem |
| GREEN | "What's meaningfully different?" | What can I do now that I couldn't before? What's the cost of inaction? |

### 1D. 6-Element Anatomy (Most Detailed, September 2025)

**Source:** P1.B3.S7
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

1. **Relevance** — Opens with something specific about the prospect, answers "Why are you emailing me?"
2. **Poke-the-bear question** — Doesn't push. Invites. Opens a loop. "How are you...?"
3. **Knowledge gap** — Introduces a potential blind spot the prospect isn't thinking about. Must be "crispy" (specific enough to be relatable).
4. **Social proof** — Specific, credible, not overhyped. "2,000+ fleets, $10M result."
5. **Preemptive objection handling** — Validates their current setup, gently introduces doubt. "Even well-run ops can have hidden overpayments."
6. **Low-pressure ask** — "No rush" lowers defenses. Respects time and space.

### Variant Reconciliation

All variants are compatible. The 4-T is a compression of the 7-element version. The 5-part psychology version adds the buyer's internal monologue. The 4-P is a late-period simplification for verbal contexts. No contradictions exist between variants.

**Evolution:** Flat checklist (2023) -> psychological sequencing (mid-2024) -> stabilized at 5-7 elements with color coding (late 2024-2025) -> simpler verbal variant (late 2025).

## 2. Cold Call Report Card (Complete Checklist)

**Source:** P1.B2.S10, P1.B4.S6
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

See `cold-call-scripts.md` for the full checklist. This framework encodes Braun's entire cold call methodology into discrete, observable behaviors. Designed for self-assessment after each call.

**Key terms defined:**
- "Fireballs not flowers" = lead with problems (pain) not benefits (features). Fireballs are burning problems the prospect needs solved. Flowers are nice-to-have benefits.
- "Mirroring and labeling" = Chris Voss techniques. Mirror: repeat last 1-3 words with questioning inflection. Label: "Seems like..." / "Sounds like..." / "Feels like..."
- "Two-beat pause" = specific, trainable micro-behavior. Count "One Mississippi, Two Mississippi" before responding.

## 3. JTBD Interview Playbook

**Source:** P1.B4.S13, P2.B1.S24
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

### Warm-up (2-3 minutes)
"I'm not here to sell you anything. I just want to understand the story of how you made your decision."

Frame yourself as a journalist.

### Timeline Reconstruction (7 phases)

**(a) Purchase/Switch**
"When did you buy? What do you remember about that day?"

**(b) First Thought**
"When did you first think you might need something different?"

**(c) Passive Looking**
"How were you managing before? What were you tolerating?"

**(d) Trigger to Act**
"What changed that made this urgent? Was there a specific event?"

**(e) Considering Options**
"What else did you look at? What almost stopped you?"

**(f) Decision**
"What tipped the scales? Who else was involved?"

**(g) Using the Solution**
"What progress have you made? What's better? What isn't?"

### Alternative 9-Question Version (For Customers < 45 Days Post-Purchase)

1. Confirm purchase date
2. When did you start looking?
3. What triggered the search?
4. How did you look for a solution?
5. What solutions did you try / why not stick with existing?
6. What was the internal buying conversation like?
7. Did you imagine life with the product before buying?
8. What anxiety did you have about purchasing?
9. What's changed for the better?

### Output

Capture exact phrases in a **"Lingo Library"** document. Use customer language word-for-word in cold emails. "Good cold email copy isn't written, it's found."

Specific mining technique: scan customer testimonials for the word "before." What follows is the problem statement.

## 5. Equal Status vs. Low Status Communication

**Source:** P1.B4.S11, P2.B1.S7, P2.B6.S16
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

Eight side-by-side contrast pairs. "Confidence is contagious. Equal stature creates honest discussion."

| Situation | Low Status (needy, deferential) | Equal Status (direct, peer-level) |
|---|---|---|
| Call open | "Thank you for picking up my call, I really appreciate it." | "I'm calling about the Inside Selling podcast. Do you still produce it?" |
| Proposal request | "Yes, of course. No problem. I'll send you a proposal right away." | "It sounds like if the proposal has what you want for the price we discussed, you're ready to move forward." |
| Price pushback | "I'll ask my boss if we can lower the price." | "It sounds like you're ready to move forward if the price is X." |
| Fee justification | "I understand that price is a significant consideration. Many people felt the same way. But what they found was..." | "It sounds like there's a ceiling to what you want to pay." |
| Meeting request | "I'd love to schedule a time to show you how we can help." | "Seems like this might help [outcome]. Is that something you'd potentially like to learn more about?" |
| Time request | "I'm sorry to bother you, I know you're really busy." | "[Name], we haven't met. I'm calling about [specific topic]." |
| Price delivery | "It's 45k, but we can break that up into payments. You can also use a credit card so you can get points." | "It's 45k. How does that sound?" |
| After a sale | "Thank you so much for your time today. I really appreciate it." | "Good talking with you. I'll follow up with [specific next step] by [date]." |

## 6. "What You Can Do" Framework (Positioning)

**Source:** P1.B4.S2
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

Fill-in-the-blanks formula for answering "What do you do?"

```
"Do you know how {emotion} it is when {problem}?
With {product/service}, you do X (what the customer does),
and Y happens (result they want but don't have)."
```

**Alternative formula:**
```
"With A, you do B, and C happens without D."
```

**Worked examples:**
- **Uber:** "With Uber, you press a button, and in 15 minutes, a Mercedes takes you where you want to go without taking out your wallet."
- **Calendly:** "With Calendly, you paste a link into your email and schedule meetings without going back and forth."
- **Phone Ready Leads:** "Do you know how frustrating it is when your reps make 50 dials and only talk to 2 people? With Phone Ready Leads, your SDRs call people who actually pick up, and they have 12-15 conversations every 45 minutes without any new tech."

**Emotion words for the {emotion} slot:** See `word-swaps.md`, Section 10.

## 7. JTBD Four Forces Model (Buying Psychology)

**Source:** P1.B2.S26, P3.B2.S12, P3.B3.S4, P3.B4.S5
**Provenance:** [D]

Four forces at play in every switching decision:

```
FAVORING CHANGE:
  Push = current way might not cut it anymore
  Pull = new way looks attractive

RESISTING CHANGE:
  Inertia = "I'd rather dance with the devil I know"
  Anxiety = "Will people use it? Will it work? Will I lose my job?"

Change happens when:
  Push + Pull > Inertia + Anxiety
```

**Practical implication:** "There's no such thing as 'no decision.' The prospect decided to wait." Until push + pull exceed inertia + anxiety, nurture without pressure. "You can't make fruit grow faster because it's the end of Q4."

**Six-stage buying timeline:**
1. Happy (no problem perceived)
2. First thought (something might not work)
3. Passive looking (browsing, not searching)
4. Active looking (researching solutions)
5. Deciding (comparing options)
6. Buying (pulling trigger)

**Stage-matching rule:** Don't treat inbound (stages 4-6) like outbound (stages 1-3). Different stages require different approaches.

## 8. Detached Seller Framework (Attached vs. Detached Mind)

**Source:** P1.B1.S11, P1.B2.S12
**Provenance:** [D:vis]

| Attached Mind | Detached Mind |
|---|---|
| Aggressive | Empathetic |
| Boastful | Curious |
| Pushy | Open-minded |
| Assumptive | Patient |
| Persuasive | Calm |
| Selfish | Indifferent (to outcome, not to person) |

**The mantra (pre-call reset):**
"It's not my job to talk people into booking a meeting. My job is to illuminate a potential problem so prospects can determine if they'd like to continue the conversation. Some will. Some won't. My role is to be genuinely curious and listen without having expectations."

**Five-pesos metaphor:** Imagine you're in Mexico with five pesos. If those five pesos are all you have, you grip them tight and act desperate. If you have plenty, you're relaxed. The antidote to sales anxiety is abundance: more conversations, more pipeline. "Think conversations, not a conversation."

## 9. Deposits Before Withdrawals

**Source:** P1.B2.S6, P2.B1.S9
**Provenance:** [D]

**Withdrawal** = connecting and immediately pitching. Subtext: "Notice me."
**Deposit** = giving genuine validation, value, or insight without asking for anything. Subtext: "I noticed you."

**Examples of deposits:**
- Send articles that make people smarter about topics they care about
- Relevant calculators or analysis tools
- Personal callbacks to prior conversation details
- Intros to people in their network
- Content that connects to previous conversations

**Examples of withdrawals:**
- "Just checking in"
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- "Any thoughts?"
- "Is this still of interest?"

**Checking in vs. Checking back:** "Checking in" is a withdrawal. "Checking back" references something personal from the last conversation: "Was thinking about you. How'd the triathlon in Mexico go?"

## 10. "Successful Day" / Daily Activity Framework

**Source:** P2.B2.S35, P2.B3.S35
**Provenance:** [D]

Baseline routine of non-negotiable daily activities you control:

1. Prepare list of 35-40 people to call (the night before)
2. Call those 35
3. Confirm meetings
4. Follow up with no-shows
5. Send 20-25 cold emails
6. Listen to 5 recorded calls (or one top performer call)
7. Review 2 of your own cold calls
8. Send 5 videos to new LinkedIn connections
9. Practice responses to 2 objections
10. 15 minutes unplugged / 10-minute walk
11. Send one physical letter
12. End day with tomorrow's call list already built

**Scoring:** 8+ activities/day = won the day. 40+/week = won the week.

**Notably absent from the list:** Meetings set. Reply rates. Revenue. "A good day isn't about what you can't control."

## 11. Traffic Light Rule / Three-Strike Rule

**Source:** P2.B1.S18, P2.B6.S33, P3.B3.S9
**Provenance:** [D]

- **Green** = first cancellation. Things happen.
- **Yellow** = second cancellation. Proceed with caution.
- **Red** = third cancellation. Stop.

**Breakup email at Red:**
"I might be misreading, but it seems like the timing is off."

Then add to nurture and move on. "You don't create motivation; you align with it."

## 12. Velvet Rope / Bring Up the Uglies First

**Source:** P3.B2.S9, P3.B4.S6
**Provenance:** [D]

Vocalize your own disqualifiers before the prospect raises objections:

```
"To make sure I don't waste your time, here are four
reasons why I might not be a fit:

(1) No service agreements or NDAs
(2) Payment upfront
(3) Workshops are $25k
(4) Next availability is [date]

Wanted to see if you're okay with the price, terms,
and timeline."
```

If no reply, it's a gift. "Not a fit is a terrific outcome if determined early. It's a terrible outcome if determined late."

Claimed 90% close rate on positive responders.

## 16. Challenger Email Template

**Source:** P1.B4.S9
**Provenance:** [D]

Braun's own email that landed Verizon. Partially recovered:

**Key elements:**
- Assertive problem statement: "is losing sales" (not "might be losing sales")
- Permission-based close: "Beth, feel free to say no, but..."

**When to use:** Only when you have strong evidence of the problem. Default to non-assumptive framing ("might," "potentially") otherwise. This is the exception to the standard softener approach.

## 18. Trigger Events (7 Categories)

**Source:** P3.B2.S18, P3.B5.S16
**Provenance:** [D]

1. **New Technology** — adopted a new tool, migrated platforms
2. **Area of Interest** — published content on a relevant topic
3. **Open Positions / Hiring** — job ads signal growth or gaps
4. **Regulation / Legal** — new compliance requirements
5. **Uncommon Commonality** — shared experience, alma mater, conference
6. **Former Customer Who Switched Companies** — they already know you
7. **Events / Conferences** — attending, speaking, sponsoring

A trigger event creates an opening for change and makes outreach timely. It maps directly to the "Trigger" element of the 4-T framework.

## 19. Shu-Ha-Ri (Mastery Progression)

**Source:** P2.B6.S35
**Provenance:** [D]

From martial arts:

- **Shu (follow rules):** Stick to scripts. Learn the framework. Execute mechanically.
- **Ha (bend rules):** Tweak tone. Add humor. Make it your own. Deviate when it makes sense.
- **Ri (forget rules):** Just talk to people. Read the room. The framework is internalized.

Most salespeople get stuck in Shu. "Sales coaching should graduate people through the stages, not keep them in the first one."

## 20. Four Types of False "Yes"

**Source:** P3.B6.S15
**Provenance:** [D]

1. **"Yes to keep the peace"** — polite yes from rapport. They like you. Doesn't mean they'll buy.
2. **"Yes to make it stop"** — relief yes from a busy person. They want the call to end.
3. **"Yes under pressure"** — eager tone framed the yes as the only option.
4. **"Yes without tension"** — no real curiosity or problem was surfaced. The conversation never created enough tension to drive action.

**Fix:** Better tension (poke the bear), not better persuasion. If you can easily talk people out of switching, they're not struggling enough to switch.
