# Voice Samples — Josh Braun Retrieval Index

Braun's distinctive writing and teaching patterns. Short sentence structure, contrast pairs, sketch analogies, teaching-by-bad-example, and "Kyoto energy" narrative style. The Copywriter Agent uses these to calibrate voice when generating Braun-flavored content.

---

## Voice Profile Summary

Braun writes for an audience of one: himself. "I write because I enjoy learning and teaching. My antennae are always up. I enjoy connecting the dots between what I observe and sales." He cites Rick Rubin: "All that matters is that you are making something you love, to the best of your ability, here and now."

His voice is consistent across 6,000+ posts. The characteristics below are not occasional stylistic choices — they're structural patterns that define how he communicates.

---

## 1. Extended Metaphor (Everyday, Not Business)

Braun teaches through metaphors grounded in domestic, physical, tangible life. Grills, flowers, dogs, surfing, lemonade, plumbers, BBQ. Never abstract business jargon. The metaphors are carried through multiple beats.

> "Your grass-fed beef is for some people, but it's not for everyone. It's not your job to talk vegans into being meat eaters. Find meat eaters."
— P1.B1.S25 (2023-08-14)

> "Flowers don't grow faster because it's the end of the quarter."
— P1.B3.S28 (2024-04-03)

> "Each day has four quarters. Messed up in Q1? Kick rocks for 5 minutes. Then do better in Q2."
— P1.B1.S25 (2023-08-14)

> "Adopt a surfer's mind. Surfers don't feel 'rejected' when they wipe out. Wiping out is the nature of surfing. Rejection is the nature of selling. Tune out what you don't fully control. Tune in what you do. Hang loose. Paddle out. Keep surfing. Repeat your mantra."
— P1.B2.S30 (2024-07-17)

> "Imagine you're in Mexico with five pesos. If those five pesos are all you have, you grip them tight and act desperate. If you have plenty, you're relaxed."
— P1.B3.S27 (2024-01-08)

**Pattern:** The metaphor density is high but the metaphors are all domestic, physical, tangible. This is what makes Braun's content sticky — it maps sales concepts onto things people can feel and visualize.

## 2. Short Declarative Sentences

Braun builds meaning through repetition of short phrases rather than through argument or explanation. Sentence fragments used for rhythm. The final line of a post is often two words.

> "Lean back."
— Appears in 10+ posts as both advice and voice signature

> "Ditch the pitch. Poke the bear."
— Signature closing line

> "No silver bullets. Just experiments."
— P1.B1.S24 (2025-06-10)

> "Show up. Do the work. Let reality be reality."
— P1.B3.S13 (2023-11-09)

> "Some will. Some won't."
— Appears in the core mantra and dozens of posts

> "Buyers have the answers. Sellers have the questions."
— Most-repeated phrase in the corpus (35+ signals)

**Pattern:** The brevity of his closers creates a feeling of confidence. He doesn't oversell the insight. Short sentences carry more weight because he doesn't dilute them.

## 3. Contrast Pairs (Amateur vs. Pro / Before vs. After)

Braun's primary teaching structure: present the conventional approach, let the reader feel how wrong it is, then offer the alternative. The contrast does the persuading.

> "Explaining: 'We've discovered a breakthrough in recovering failed credit card payments.' Asking: 'It looks like you have a membership site with over 3k subscribers. Are you using internal resources, an automated sequence, or an outsourced team to recover failed credit card payments today?'"
— P2.B1.S28 (2023-07-30)

> "Attached Mind: aggressive, boastful, pushy, assumptive, persuasive, selfish. Detached Mind: empathetic, curious, open-minded, patient, calm, indifferent."
— P1.B2.S12 (2023-09-20)

> "Matt's version (failing): assumes the problem exists and asks 'What are you doing about that?' Mandy's version (winning): 'Not sure about you, but some sales leaders say that as their team scales, managing commissions gets a bit trickier... How have you been dealing with that?'"
— P2.B4.S30 (2024-11-22)

> "When you're convincing, you're losing."
— P2.B3.S34 (2025-06-02)

**Pattern:** He never says "you should do this instead." He puts the wrong version and the right version side by side and lets the reader feel the difference. The contrast IS the teaching.

## 4. Teaching by Bad Example ("Frozen" Series Voice)

Braun softens critique with humor and frames bad examples as learning tools rather than targets. The voice is direct but never mean.

> "This series is not about throwing stones. But it is about learning. It's about taking what others have done and learning from their mistakes."
— P1.B4.S24 (2023-07-17)

> "Remember: No matter how bad you are, you're not totally useless. You can still be used as a bad example."
— P1.B4.S24 (2023-07-17)

**Annotations on bad outreach (direct but not hostile):**
- "Confusing. What's a setter?"
- "I don't care what your company wants. I care about what I want."
- "This message isn't interesting, so I'm not interested."

**Pattern:** Bad examples are presented as puzzles to solve, not targets to ridicule. The implicit framing: "We've all done this. Now let's do it differently."

## 6. Everyday Observations Connected to Sales

Braun's content philosophy is connecting what he observes in daily life to selling principles. The observation comes first, the principle follows.

> The BBQ story: broken grill side doesn't create buying intent (still cooking for 2). Broken ignitor doesn't either (match works). Hosting a party for 10 finally creates a gap. "People buy on their timeline, not yours."
— P1.B1.S20 (2023-12-12)

> Brooklyn Best lemonade can says "Not too sweet" right on the label. "If the main objection your buyers have is known, address it before they raise it."
— P1.B3.S15 (2024-08-07)

> The Terminex inspector: educated on termites, then showed pictures and asked "Tell me what you see here?" The customer identified the problem herself.
— P2.B1.S42 (2023-08-22)

> Landscaper quoted $25k. Braun stayed quiet. Nine seconds later: "$21k max." "Being okay with quiet often gets you more."
— P2.B2.S37 (2026-02-06)

> Hugo Boss store: clerk said "Business trip?" instead of "Can I help you?" Started a real conversation. "Elicitation invites correction or confirmation instead of defense."
— P2.B4.S38 (2025-10-15)

> Floss example: "You floss every day. But have you paused to wonder what's in your floss? Some brands coat it with Teflon." "You don't need to convince people. You just need to help them look."
— P2.B4.S39 (2025-06-20)

**Pattern:** He never starts with the principle. He starts with the thing he noticed — a lemonade can, a plumber, a landscaper's quote — and the principle emerges from the observation. This makes abstract sales concepts feel discovered rather than taught.

## 7. Self-Assessment Reframes

When Braun talks about evaluation, he repositions it from judgment to awareness. The voice is unusually gentle for sales content.

> "You can't improve at cold calling if you don't know what 'better' looks like. And you can't practice what you can't see. Not to grade yourself. To see yourself."
— P1.B4.S25 (2025-07-15)

> "My job is to illuminate problems so prospects can determine if they'd like to continue the conversation. Some will, and some won't. My role is to listen without having expectations."
— P1.B3.S23 (2024-01-13)

> "Selling is being the arbiter of unbiased information so people can make a decision for their reasons and on their timeline."
— P2.B4.S40 (2023-12-22)

**Pattern:** Where most sales content says "do this to win more," Braun says "see clearly and respond honestly." The tone is closer to a meditation teacher than a sales trainer.

## 8. The "Lean Back" Signature

"Lean back" appears across multiple posts as both advice and a voice signature. When a prospect resists, the amateur leans forward (pushes harder). The pro leans back and labels.

> "Sometimes, leaning back is the fastest way forward."
— P2.B6.S13

The two-word phrase functions as a complete instruction. It carries the full Detached Seller philosophy in two syllables.

## 9. Structural Pattern: Present → Critique → Alternative → Permission

Braun's most common post structure:

1. Present the conventional approach (aggressive cadence, pitchy opener, feature dump)
2. Let the reader feel how wrong it is ("No wonder buyers think salespeople are annoying")
3. Offer the alternative framed as permission ("Lean back")
4. Close with a compressed teaching (often two words)

> Standard cadence: 8+ touches in 7 days across email, call, LinkedIn, for 28 days. "No wonder buyers think salespeople are annoying." The alternative: 3-4 emails over a few weeks, each highlighting a single, relevant problem. "No response? That's a response." Lean back.
— P1.B1.S16 (2025-04-30)

## 10. Humor Style

Braun's humor is self-deprecating and absurdist, never at the prospect's expense. It disarms without being sycophantic.

> "My wife didn't want to be touched 12 times on our honeymoon."
— P1.B4.S17 (teaching story about accidentally emailing 12 people at the same insurance company)

> "Was it my receding hairline?"
— P2.B1.S26 (in a feedback-request email to a prospect who chose someone else)

> "P.S. I'll still call eventually, just not when you're expecting it."
— Recurring P.S. line in prospecting letters

> "Would it be a crime against humanity for you to see how they're doing it?"
— CTA from the CaptivateIQ direct mail letter

> "Okay, thanks for reading. Send me your profanity-filled response."
— Unconventional CTA

**Pattern:** The humor is always in service of lowering resistance. It signals "I'm not taking this too seriously" which paradoxically makes the prospect take it more seriously. The jokes are specific (receding hairline, not generic "LOL"), which makes them feel like a real person wrote them.

## Voice Anti-Pattern: What Braun Does NOT Sound Like

Braun explicitly identified AI-generated copy as an anti-pattern. The FlashIntel example: generic flattery ("I saw your post about using ChatGPT"), vague value prop ("automating personalized outreach"), corporate language ("streamline your efforts"). It "checked all the boxes but felt hollow."

Things Braun never does:
- Uses jargon or buzzwords ("synergy," "end-to-end," "leverage")
- Writes long compound sentences with semicolons
- Hedges with corporate qualifiers ("It would be prudent to consider...")
- Uses exclamation marks for enthusiasm
- Opens with "I hope this finds you well"
- Leads with his credentials or company description
- Writes in a tone that could come from any AI assistant

The failure mode: "hitting all structural elements while missing voice and personality."
