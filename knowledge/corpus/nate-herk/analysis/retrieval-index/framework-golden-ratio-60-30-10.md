# Golden AI Ratio (60/30/10)

Nate Herk's split for how much of a client process should be plain automation, AI help and human hands: 60% traditional automation, 30% AI-assisted, 10% human approval. He calls it "the golden AI ratio" on 2025-10-15 and "my golden ratio" on 2025-11-17.

## The ratio as he states it
`[F] · [D] · uploaded 2025-10-15; 2025-11-17 · you-re-doing-ai-automation-wrong-here-s-how-to-fix-it.md; how-to-sign-your-first-ai-automation-client-without-starting-an-agency.md · q31, q37 · stable`

The 2025-10-15 video frames it as leverage: automate the boring 60%, have AI assist a person on the 30% that needs judgment, leave the last 10% manual.

> maybe you automate the first 60% of a process the boring and repetitive stuff then you use ai to assist a human with the next 30% things that need judgment or context and the last 10% you can leave that fully manual (you-re-doing-ai-automation-wrong-here-s-how-to-fix-it.md)

A month later, on 2025-11-17, he gives the short form and ties it to selling outcomes.

> my golden ratio which is 60% traditional automation 30% ai assisted and then that last 10% being human touch or human approval (how-to-sign-your-first-ai-automation-client-without-starting-an-agency.md)

In the same 2025-11-17 passage he says the point is giving people leverage, and that you "don't need to lead with ai".

## Boring is beautiful: workflows beat agents nine times out of ten
`[J] · [D] · uploaded 2026-03-12; 2026-05-01 · build-sell-with-claude-code-10-hour-course.md; build-sell-claude-code-operating-systems-2-hour-course.md · q31, q33 · stable`

The ratio rests on a belief he repeats in 9 files: deterministic workflows beat agents for most business jobs. "Boring is beautiful" is the phrase.

> what you might actually need is more of a boring is beautiful workflow automation because workflows deterministic workflows beat ai agents nine times out of 10 (build-sell-claude-code-operating-systems-2-hour-course.md)

> in automation predictable is beautiful boring is beautiful because you know exactly what's going to happen every single time the automation runs (build-sell-with-claude-code-10-hour-course.md)

The same idea is step 4 of his 2025 agent formula: "never force an agent into a process that doesn't actually need it" (see `framework-5-step-agent-formula.md`).

## What the human 10% is for
`[J] · [M] · no transcript · q34 · stable`

[M] Gemini's version only; no caption ties this list to the ratio.

q34 reserves the human share for three kinds of action: spending money, deleting records, and sending to many people at once. His discount-code story backs the rule. An agent on his own team emailed a discount code to "about 150,000 people" off a to-do list item nobody told it to act on (5000-hours-of-building-ai-in-just-17-minutes.md, 2026-08-04; one later video says "almost 200,000"). His fix there is tool permissions and draft mode, covered in `framework-other-named-methods.md` under "fix the access, not the prompt".
