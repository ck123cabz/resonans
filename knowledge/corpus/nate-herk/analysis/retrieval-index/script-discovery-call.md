# Discovery call script

The questions Nate asks on a sales call, in the order he runs them. The oldest source is his live call of 2025-09-07; the fullest is the pricing retrospective of 2026-08-01. The framework under the middle of the call is LRP (see framework-lrp.md).

## Opener: why did you book the call
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2025-09-07 · how-i-sold-a-6000-ai-workflow-to-a-business-live-sales-call.md · q44 · stable`

On his live $6,000 call he opened by asking what made the prospect book and what they want. He said the point was to lay the agenda and get the prospect talking at once. He also called his own delivery "a tough watch".

> what what prompted you to book a call and what you're what you're looking to do here so that's the way I wanted to open it up just lay the agenda (how-i-sold-a-6000-ai-workflow-to-a-business-live-sales-call.md)

## Ask, then go silent
`[J] · [D] · uploaded 2026-09-21; 2026-08-25 · build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md; the-3-step-system-behind-my-20k-month-ai-agency-retainers.md · q44 · stable`

He asked a prospect why she booked, then stopped talking. He held the pause twice because he did not believe her first answer.

> there's just a lot of power in silence so when I asked her the question of like why did you book this call like what is truly the pain that you're feeling here i stopped talking (build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md)

## Sizing pokes: how long, how many people, what goes wrong
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q20, q44 · stable`

These put a number on the value ceiling. He says they size the ceiling, not the build. "What happens when it goes wrong" is the one that surfaces hard-dollar error costs.

> how many people touch it and what happens when it goes wrong these are all sizing questions they measure the ceiling not the build (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Constraint question: supply-constrained business
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2026-09-21; 2026-03-12 · build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md; build-sell-with-claude-code-10-hour-course.md · q22, q44 · stable`

He sorts a business as supply-constrained (more customers than it can serve) or demand-constrained (not enough coming in). He pictures revenue as water in a pipe. For supply, he asks what breaks at 10x, then stays quiet. The 10-hour course (2026-03-12) asks the same with 300 new people.

> if a business is supply constraint if you had 10x the business tomorrow what would break first you ask them that and then you just be quiet (build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md)

> if tomorrow you've got 300 people that wanted your services what process would break first in that you know customer life cycle (build-sell-with-claude-code-10-hour-course.md)

## Constraint question: demand-constrained business
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2026-09-21 · build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md · q44 · stable`

Gemini merged this with the supply question. For a business short on customers, Nate asks how it would 10x what comes in.

> if they're demand constraint you basically just flip it you say "What would you do to 10x the business coming in tomorrow?" (build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md)

## Why this, why now, why me
`[F] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q22, q44 · stable`

Three buckets after the pivot. Why this checks the request reaches the outcome; he says the real fix is often "a really simple deterministic script and a Slack notification". Why now tests urgency: a closing window or a competitor. Why me is the one he calls uncomfortable, and he asks it out loud to pull hidden objections onto the call.

> why wouldn't you just do this internally couldn't you vibe code this couldn't you hand it to an intern and the reason you want to do this is because these questions or that question specifically surfaces objections (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

Who the three buckets come from is open. The same 2026-08-01 video credits Jonathan Stark for the case against hourly and the landscaper story, and says nothing on the buckets. Stark's published "why conversation" asks the same three whys.

## Reply to "my nephew could build this"
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q22, q44, q47 · stable`

Agree, then ask who watches it at 2 am, then stop. In his example it is "my nephew", not an intern.

> 'Yeah you know my nephew probably could build this.' You don't argue you go 'Yeah he probably could get a version of this up and running.' But what I'd want to know is who's going to watch it at 2 in the morning (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Reply to "just tell me a price"
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q22, q44 · stable`

No number before he knows the complexity. If a prospect keeps pushing for a quote to compare vendors, he calls it a red flag and says to leave the deal.

> in order for me to give you an accurate ballpark estimate of what this is going to cost I really need to understand more in depth the complexity (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

> if they keep pushing you for a price and all they want is to get a quote out of you so they can compare you across a few different other vendors then I would say that's probably a red flag (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)
