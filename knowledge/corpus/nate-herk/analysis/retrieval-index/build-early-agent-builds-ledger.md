# Early agent builds ledger ($1,200 to $12,000)

The priced builds Nate sold before and during his True Horizon agency days, told in videos uploaded 2025-08-17 to 2026-01-07. All six prices are spoken on camera: $1,200, $1,650, $2,600, $4,000, $6,000, $12,000. A $5,500 build has its own file (build-appointment-setting-agent.md), and so does the $1,650 outreach agent (build-outreach-and-lead-gen-agents.md). No $2,300 build exists in the captions.

## $1,200 LinkedIn content agent, 2 hours
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-08-17 · i-built-this-ai-agent-in-2-hours-and-got-paid-1200.md · q21, q29, q49 · stable`

His first paid build: an n8n workflow that wrote LinkedIn content in a client's brand and tone of voice, aimed at the audience that client's profile speaks to. He calls it very simple. He worked out the rate himself: $1,200 for 2 hours is $600 an hour. His point is that the client paid for the outcome, hours of weekly content work gone.

> it took me 2 hours which is 600 bucks an hour which is an insane rate but the client wasn't paying for the workflow or wasn't paying for me on an hourly basis he was paying for the outcome (i-built-this-ai-agent-in-2-hours-and-got-paid-1200.md)

## $2,600 client onboarding build, 2 hours
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-12-17 · i-built-an-ai-agent-in-2-hours-and-got-paid-2600.md · q29, q49, q51 · stable`

An onboarding system built in about 2 hours with little experience. His value math: 5 new clients a week x 30 minutes = 2.5 hours a week; at $50 an hour that is $125 a week, $500 a month, $6,000 a year. 6,000 / 2,600 = 2.3x in year one. The $1,300-an-hour rate in Gemini's q51 is Gemini's division; he never says it.

> someone actually paid me $2,600 for this one not $1,200 $2,600 (i-built-an-ai-agent-in-2-hours-and-got-paid-2600.md)

> five new clients a week and each one of those onboardings takes about 30 minutes that's two and a half hours per week (i-built-an-ai-agent-in-2-hours-and-got-paid-2600.md)

## $6,000 Slack personal assistant, priced on complexity
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-08-31 · how-i-sold-these-4-ai-agents-for-23-000-as-a-beginner.md · q29, q49 · stable`

A personal assistant for a business owner and his team, living in Slack: quick access to internal data, task management, routine admin. He admits admin work has no set cadence, so ROI was hard to pin down and they priced on build complexity instead of value.

> so we ended up charging $6,000 for this project and even though we tried to back that number with some roi calculations the truth is we priced more on complexity of the build (how-i-sold-these-4-ai-agents-for-23-000-as-a-beginner.md)

## $6,000 live sales call: hours tiers and 50/50 terms
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-09-07 · how-i-sold-a-6000-ai-workflow-to-a-business-live-sales-call.md · q22, q44, q49 · stable`

A recorded discovery call for a personal-assistant build. He opened by asking what prompted the booking. When the prospect asked about tiers, he quoted in hours: $4,500 for 20 hours or $8,000 for 40. He landed on $6,000, split $3,000 up front and $3,000 on completion, with 40 hours as his max estimate. Whether this is the same client as the $6,000 Slack assistant is not settled by the captions. The 50/50 split here is his early term; by 2026-08-01 he describes three payments 30 days apart. Gemini's 33/33/34 is in no caption.

> yeah so the first one is going to be 4 and a halfk for the 20 hours and then um 8k for 40 hours (how-i-sold-a-6000-ai-workflow-to-a-business-live-sales-call.md)

> maybe doing $3,000 upfront and then maybe a $3,000 upon completion which would put it a $6,000 total project (how-i-sold-a-6000-ai-workflow-to-a-business-live-sales-call.md)

## $12,000 AI concierge
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-08-31 · how-i-sold-these-4-ai-agents-for-23-000-as-a-beginner.md · q29, q49 · stable`

The fourth agent in the four-agents video: a concierge for a membership business covering onboarding, finding and starting events, guest passes and support. ## Scale $25,000 and growth $12,000 tiers
`[S] · [D] · uploaded 2025-08-31 · how-i-sold-these-4-ai-agents-for-23-000-as-a-beginner.md · q22, q49 · stable`

In the same video he describes packaging: lead with the highest anchor so clients self-select. Scale was $25,000; most clients took growth at $12,000, which then reads as the smart middle option. No $5,000 starter tier is spoken; Gemini's q22 adds one. These dollar tiers carry no percentages; his 10/25/50% rule comes later (2026-08-01).

> our scale package is 25,000 and it includes these functionalities but most clients actually go with the growth package at 12k (how-i-sold-these-4-ai-agents-for-23-000-as-a-beginner.md)

## What the ledger shows: price rose with value framing
`[I] · [M] · uploaded 2025-08-17 to 2026-01-07 · files above · q29 · stable`

Build time stayed near 2 to 3 hours for the first three builds while price went $1,200, $1,650, $2,600. He calls the $1,650 figure "pretty random". The $6,000 assistant was priced on complexity because its value was hard to measure. His later rules (10% to 20% of first-year value, 10x in year one) came after these builds, and he never calls the early prices a mistake. Gemini's lesson "keep systems 90% deterministic, 10% AI" has no caption; his stated ratio is 60/30/10 (framework-golden-ratio-60-30-10.md).
