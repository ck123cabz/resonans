# Appointment-setting agent ($5,500)

One $5,500 build, a system that took over inbound lead follow-up and meeting booking, is Nate's main worked pricing case. He tells it in two videos with different numbers: the PRICE video (2025-11-30) and the pricing retrospective (2026-08-01). Both tellings are verbatim. Whether they describe one client or two cannot be settled from the captions, so both stay open here.

## Appointment setter inputs: 20 leads a week, 1 hour each, $40 an hour
`[B] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01; 2025-11-30 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md; how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q20, q42, q51 · stable`

The inputs match across both videos. Leads came in from a website form, about 20 a week. Each took roughly an hour of an employee's time to reach out, qualify, nurture and book a call. The employees were valued at about $40 an hour. So the process cost $800 a week.

> this example was an appointment setting agent the business had employees manually setting these meetings and this was about 20 leads a week each one taking roughly an hour of a human's time (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Telling 1 (2026-08-01): $41,600 a year, priced at about 13%
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q42, q51 · stable`

He annualizes with 52 weeks: 800 x 52 = $41,600. He priced the build at "right around 13%": 5,500 / 41,600 = 13.2%. The same passage runs word for word inside build-sell-with-codex-5-hour-course.md (2026-09-21), so that course is one origin, not a second confirmation.

> 20 hours a week at $40 an hour is about $800 bucks a week and if you multiply that by 52 ... that's $41,600 annualized (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

> i priced the build right around 13% of what that annual number was which came out to 5,500 bucks (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Telling 1: 7.5x, and how he gets to 10x
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q42, q51 · stable`

41,600 / 5,500 = 7.56. He admits on camera that this misses his 10x golden rule. He covers the missing 2.5x with a forecast: the baseline of 20 leads a week would grow once the business had its time back. The 10x in this case is a projection, never a measured return.

> which comes out to about a 7.5 multiple on their initial investment now typically i like to say the golden rule is that you need to be able to show the client that their investment is going to 10x (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

> in this specific example where i accounted for that extra 2.5x that we were missing that would put us at 10 was the baseline so right now the baseline was about 20 leads a week (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Telling 1: $400 a month flat maintenance
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q42 · version-bound`

Maintenance was $400 a month, the same number across all his projects at that time. It keeps the agreed build working through API and model changes; new features are a separate conversation. 400 / 5,500 = 7.3% of the fee, so the "about 10%" q44 attaches to it is wrong. He warns that a $30,000 system may cost more than $400 a month to maintain.

> on top of the build we set up a standard maintenance plan so this was 400 bucks a month (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

## Telling 2 (2025-11-30): $38,400 a year, priced at 15%
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q42, q51 · stable`

Here he annualizes with 4-week months: 800 x 4 = $3,200 a month, x 12 = $38,400. He rounds to "about $38,000" in the same breath. He says 15%, but 15% of 38,400 is $5,760; $5,500 is 14.3%. The $260 slip is his, since both numbers come out of his mouth in one sentence. The 4-week month also understates the year: 800 x 52 = 41,600. This telling is the PRICE framework's worked case and is repeated in build-sell-with-claude-code-10-hour-course.md (2026-03-12).

> this meant that this one process was costing the business $800 per week which is $3,200 per month and when you zoom out and you annualize it it ends up being about $38,000 per year (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

> i priced the product at 15% of those annualized savings and that came out to $5,500 (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

38,400 / 5,500 = 6.98, so roughly 7x.

## Telling 2: $550 a month, about 10% of the fee
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q42, q51 · version-bound`

In the earlier telling the maintenance and optimization plan was $550 a month. 550 / 5,500 = 10%, so this one is internally correct. It covered bug fixes, holding the scope, adjustments as models updated, and monthly health reports. It was agreed at the Expand step, once the build moved into QA.

> we agreed on a simple maintenance and optimization plan at $550 per month which is about 10% of the original project's fee (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## Where the $5,500 sits in his pricing band
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2026-08-01 · 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q44, q51 · stable`

His rule is to start at 10% to 20% of first-year annualized value. Both 13% and 15% sit inside it. q42's "10% to 15%" band is wrong; the caption says 10% to 20%.

> what i like to do is pick somewhere between 10% and 20% of that number as my starting point (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)
