# PRICE framework

PRICE is Nate Herk's five-step process for pricing an AI build: Prepare, Research, Identify ROI, Communicate, Expand. He teaches it in one video on 2025-11-30, which the 10-hour course of 2026-03-12 repeats word for word, so those count as one origin. The 10x rule and the build anchor he uses inside it run through his later pricing video of 2026-08-01.

> that's why I came up with the price framework it's a five-step process that takes all of the guesswork out of pricing (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

> the P is for prepare we started off this video talking about the mindset so before you even think about numbers make sure you're grounding everything in value based pricing (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE step R: research, the discovery phase
`[F] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q50 · stable`

Map the manual workflow end to end: trigger, who does it, time taken, tools, cost to the business. The call itself runs on LRP (`framework-lrp.md`, `script-discovery-call.md`).

> the R is for research so this is your discovery phase you want to fully map out the manual workflow from start to finish how does it happen what triggers it who does it how long does it take (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE step I: identify the ROI, and the 10x golden rule
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q50, q51 · stable`

Turn research into monthly and annual savings. The client should see ten times the fee back in year one. He calls it his golden rule in 9 files from 2025-05-05.

> remember the 10 times investment kind of golden rule they should roughly see 10 times the return on whatever they pay you within the first year (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

> if the workflow is going to save them $12,000 10% of that would be $1,200 and that's your starting point for pricing (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE anchor: 10% in 2025, 10% to 20% in 2026
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30; 2026-08-01 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md; 18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md · q42, q44 · stable`

The PRICE video anchors at 10% of first-year savings. His pricing retrospective of 2026-08-01 widens it to a band. Gemini's q42 gives 10% to 15%; the caption says 20%.

> what I like to do is pick somewhere between 10% and 20% of that number as my starting point (18-months-of-pricing-ai-automations-in-21-mins.md)

Annual value is weekly hours × hourly cost × 52. The label "cost of inaction" that q42 and q44 use has zero caption hits; he says "annualized" and "ceiling".

## PRICE step C: communicate before the number
`[F] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q50 · stable`

Paint the business after go-live, say what is included, how QA works, and what you need from them. Proposal layout and the three tiers are in `script-proposal-and-tiers.md`.

> the C is for communicate now that you have that price you still have to present it in a way that makes sense you want to paint the picture of what the business will look like once the system is live (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE step E: expand the relationship
`[F] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q50 · stable`

> the E is for expand once the product is complete your job still is not over you want to look for opportunities to continue building on top of that relationship that could be maintenance optimization monitoring additional workflows V2 upgrades retainers or performance bonuses (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE worked case: $38,400 a year, 15%, $5,500 build, $550 a month
`[B] · [D][G] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q42, q51 · stable`

An inbound-lead appointment process: 20 leads a week, one hour each, staff at $40 an hour. That is $800 a week and $3,200 a month; he annualizes it to $38,400 (4-week months). He prices at 15% and arrives at $5,500, plus maintenance of $550 a month.

> I priced the product at 15% of those annualized savings and that came out to $5,500 (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

> $550 per month which is about 10% of the original project's fee (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

The slip is his: 15% of $38,400 is $5,760, so $5,500 is 14.3%. 38,400 / 5,500 is about 7x, short of the 10x rule. He says in the same passage they "did mess up" by not proving the system's value after launch.

## PRICE worked case, second telling: $41,600, 13%, $400 flat (open)

## PRICE: retainer margin, 50% to 70%, 50% as baseline
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q51 · stable`

> the general target margin for this type of work is 50 to 70% 50% is a good baseline to shoot for (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

> if it costs you $5,000 per month to deliver you might want to price the retainer at $10,000 per month that gives you a 50% (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

A $15,000 retainer on that $5,000 cost is Gemini's; no caption has it.

## PRICE: one $15,000 client over five $3,000 clients
`[J] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q24 · stable`

Same revenue, less chaos, room to over-deliver. Gemini's "$20k vs five $4k" is inflated.

> you'd much rather work with one $15,000 per month client than five $3,000 per month clients even though the total revenue is the same you'll have less chaos less stress (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

## PRICE: pushback means cut scope, not price
`[J] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q47 · stable`

> if they push back adjust the scope not the price reduce complexity remove a feature break it into phases but don't discount the value (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)

The 2026-08-01 budget script says the same with a $20,000 example and no dollar figure for the smaller project (`script-proposal-and-tiers.md`).

## PRICE: who owns the build
`[R] · [D] · uploaded 2025-11-30 · how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md · q47 · stable`

The client owns what he builds. His reusable parts stay his; the prompts, data and workflows are worth nothing outside that business.

> the real IP in the system is the prompts your data your workflows and how everything is combined in this ecosystem so that's very specific to your business and provides no value to anyone else (how-to-price-ai-workflows-without-losing-clients.md)
