## Cold outreach: permission first

**Rule:** never attach an unsolicited video or PDF. Ask permission, then send.

### Google listing gap
Subject: `quick question` or `[First Name] quick question`
> "Hey [Name], I was searching for a [business type] in [City] and noticed a gap on your Google listing that's probably costing you calls. I recorded a quick 2-minute video breakdown of what's going on. Want me to send it over?"

### Review gap
Subject: `quick question`
> "Hey [Name], I was searching for a roofer in Charlotte and eventually came across ABC Roofing. Great reviews by the way, but I had to scroll a while to find you. You've got 18 reviews and the guys at the top have 400+, and that gap is probably costing you calls. So I did a bit of research and recorded a quick 2-minute breakdown of what's going on. Want me to send it over?"

### Missed calls
Subject: `quick question`
> "Hey [Name], I called [Business Name] around 7 last night and it went to voicemail. Tried the chat on your website too, no answer there either. When that happens, that job usually goes to whoever picks up next. I recorded a quick 2-minute video showing a simple way to catch those calls without hiring anyone. Okay if I send it over?"

Expected: "Sure, send it over."

### The 60-second report
> "I pulled a quick marketing report on [Business Name] this morning, found a few things that are probably costing you customers right now. Want me to send it over? No charge, takes you 60 seconds to read."

Expected: roughly 1 in 5 reply.

### Stealth audit variant (nothing recorded until they say yes)
> "I was doing some research the other day and came across your website. I took the liberty of recording a quick video with proven strategies that could help you turn more search traffic into paying customers. Let me know if you're interested in seeing it and I'll send it right over."

## The four-message cold sequence

Spaced over 5 to 7 days. Every message carries something new. Never "just checking in."

**Day 1**, the permission ask above.

**Day 2, soft bump with new value**
> "Hey [Name], forgot to mention, one of the fastest things I've seen work for [business type] is an automated review request that goes out after every job. I talk about that in the breakdown. Want me to send it over?"

Video-audit variant:
> "Sorry, forgot to mention, the biggest thing I noticed was actually about how you're collecting reviews after each job, and it's about a 5-minute fix I covered in the video. Want me to send it over?"

**Day 4, acknowledge the awkwardness**
> "Hey [Name], I know cold messages are not exactly anyone's favorite part of the day. I just figured if there's a chance this is useful, it's worth one more note. Happy to send over what I found whenever works for you."

Alternative:
> "Hey [Name], I know salespeople are the worst, so I get it if you'd like me to stop. That said, I'd hate for you to miss out on what I found just because I didn't send one more message. Still happy to send it over if you want."

**Day 6, the breakup**
> "Hey [Name], before I close your file I just wanted to check one last time. If the timing's just been off, I'm still happy to share what I found. If not, no worries at all and I won't reach out again. Just hit reply and let me know and I can send it right over."

Shorter variant used after an audit:
> "Last one from me, promise! I know you're busy so totally get it if this slipped by. Just didn't want the video to get lost in the shuffle. Want me to send it over?"

He reports the breakup produces the highest response rate of the sequence. Same mechanism as the Freedom Close: removing the pressure is what produces the yes.

## Reactivation

### The nine-word email (Dean Jackson)
Subject: blank, or `[First Name]`, or `quick question`
> "Hey [First Name], are you still [X]?"

Variants he gives:
- "Hey Dave, are you still looking for a house in Austin?"
- "Hey Taylor, are you still planning a trip to London?"
- "Hey Robin, are you still looking to grow your agency?"
- "Hey Sarah, are you still looking to do that bathroom renovation?"
- "Are you still looking to install a sprinkler system?"

### The voucher bait (highest converting)
> "Hey [First Name], we've got some extra $20 gift cards here from a recent promotion, just wondering if you wanted one?"

Service-business variant:
> "Hey [First Name], I've got 10 free [teeth whitening / wiper blade replacement] vouchers here on my desk, would you be interested in one?"

On a positive reply:
> "Awesome! Here's the voucher link to claim it and book your spot: [link]"

No reply after 24 to 48 hours:
> "Hey [First Name], just following up on this, let me know if you want me to save one for you!"

Negative reply or STOP: unsubscribe immediately, sequence ends.

Dripped in batches across a 14-day window.

## The free-asset openers

### "I made you something"

> I noticed your Facebook page hasn't been updated in a while and I made you these two posts for [Business Name]. No catch, use them if you want. If you like them, I've got 12 more I can send over.

On reply:
> "Here are 12 more, that's 2 weeks worth if you post daily. If you'd rather I just schedule and post all of these for you so you don't have to think about it, I can do that for $500 a month and you can cancel anytime."

Expected: 20–30% reply. Target: operating businesses with dead social accounts.

### Free website, business has one

I found a few things that might be costing you leads, so I went ahead and put together a new version optimized for more calls and customers. Would it be okay to send you the link?"

### Free website, business has none
> "Hey [Name], I was trying to find your website when searching for [niche] in [city] but couldn't find one. I know this is a bit out of nowhere, but I went ahead and built you one. Would it be okay to send you the link to check it out?"

### Delivery
> "Hey [Name], I finished the site. Here's the preview link: [link]. Take a look through, test it out on your phone, and let me know what you think. If you want to keep it live, I charge $297 a month to host it and run the lead capture system. If not, no worries at all!"

## Warm network

### Direct warm contact (business owners you know)
> "Hey [Name], I'm starting a marketing service for local businesses. I've got an AI receptionist that answers calls, books appointments, and follows up with leads automatically 24/7. Would you be open to me showing you how it works? Takes about 5 minutes, totally free to look at."

Send to 10. Expect 2 or 3 positive.

### Bullseye audit opener
> "Hey Dave, long time no talk! Random one, I was messing around with a tool I use for work and ran a quick audit on [Business Name]. Noticed a few small things on your Google profile/website that are probably losing you guys customers. Want me to send the report over?"

### Referral ask to non-owners
> "Hey [Name], random question, I just started a marketing service where I set up AI receptionists for local businesses. It answers their calls, books appointments, follows up with leads, all that. You wouldn't happen to know just one person who runs a business and might be interested in seeing how it works, would you?"

### Intro template to hand them
> "Hey [Business Owner], meet my friend [Your Name]. They've been working on a simple AI follow-up system for local businesses and are doing a few quick walkthroughs. Thought it might be helpful, want me to connect you?"

### Research framing
> "Hey [Name], I'm doing a bit of informal research on local service businesses. Quick question: do you know anyone who runs a plumbing, dental, or salon type business? I'm noticing the same lead follow-up problems everywhere and testing a simple fix."

Then: "Would you feel comfortable introducing us? I'll keep it super low-key."

### Client referral ask (Phil Jones)
> "Hey, you wouldn't happen to know just one person, someone who just like you would benefit from [getting more reviews automatically / fixing missed calls], would you? Thanks!"

Timing: two or three weeks in, right after a visible win. Never during a problem or after a month of silence.

## Conversation control

### Green Light Method
**1. Open door, reciprocity**
> "Hey [Name], I was looking at your Google listing and put together a quick 2-minute video. Thought it might be helpful, no strings attached."

**2. If they go quiet, withdraw rather than badger**
> "Hey [Name], no worries if the timing's off. If you ever want a hand with that thing I spotted, happy to show you. Either way, hope business is going great."

**3. Diagnosis close**
> "What do you think is the biggest thing getting in the way of getting more customers?"

**4. Offer the stack against what they just named.**

### The 4-Message Framework (Reply Trap escape)
When they say "I'm interested," do not answer.

**Acknowledge**
> "Thanks for reaching out. Before I dive into details, I want to make sure this is actually the right fit for what you're dealing with. Mind if I ask you a couple questions?"

**Filter**
> "On a scale of 1 to 10, how painful is this problem for you right now?"

Under 7: "It sounds like this isn't a critical priority right now. Why don't we reconnect when it becomes more urgent?"

**Reveal**
> "The reason I ask is because I found this approach works best for people who [criteria, e.g. are already generating revenue but feel stuck with inconsistent marketing]…"

**Controlled advance**
> "Based on what you've told me, I think we could probably help with this. If you want, I can send over a link to grab 15 minutes this week so I can walk you through how this would work for your specific situation. Sound good?"

## Closing

### The single line
> "Would you like some help with that?"

or

> "Would you like me to set this up for you?"

Delivered right after the audit or the demo. Expected: "Yes, how do we get started?" or "How much does it cost?"

### The Freedom Close, four steps
**1. Value confirmation**
> "Before we get into next steps, I want to make sure this actually makes sense for you based on everything you've shared. Does this feel like it would solve the problem?"

**2. Invite obstacles**
> "Is there anything that would stop you from moving forward?"

**3. Freedom release**
> "Look, I think this could really work for you but there's no pressure. You're completely free to say no, take more time, or tell me this just isn't the right fit. I'd rather you make the right decision than feel pushed into something."

**4. The pause.** 5 to 7 seconds of complete silence. Do not speak, do not fill it, do not backpedal.

If they hesitate:
> "Honestly if you're not 100% sure I'd rather you take the time you need. I only want clients who are genuinely excited to move forward."

### Gatekeeper, four moves
**Open (0–60s)**
> "Thanks for reaching out. Before we dive in, I want to make sure we're a good fit. I'd hate for either of us to waste time if this isn't the right approach for where you're at. I've learned my method works really well for certain situations, but not all. Does that make sense?"

**Criteria reveal (2–5 min)**
> "The reason I ask is because I found this approach works best for people who are already generating revenue but feel stuck. For example, clients who know their business works but can't figure out why their marketing isn't consistent tend to see results fast. People who need a few weeks to think it over usually end up not moving forward, and I'd hate for that to happen."

**Mirror close (5–10 min)**
> "From everything you've just told me, I think this would work really well for you. The real question though is, does this feel like the right fit for what you're trying to accomplish?"

**Final filter**
> "Perfect. Just so I'm clear, you're ready to implement this week, right? Because like I mentioned, this only works if you're the type of person who takes action quickly."

**Pullback on hesitation**
> "No worries at all. If you need more time, you're completely free to take as long as you need. Like I said, I'm selective about who I work with, I'd rather you think about it and be absolutely sure than rush into something you're not ready for."

**Mirroring their own words**
Prospect: "I've been wanting to fix this for months."
You: "So you're past the research phase and ready to actually solve this."

### The disqualification pivot
> "You know what, I don't think we're a good fit here. You're looking for someone who's able to start immediately and for instant results, and I'm looking for clients who understand that good strategy does take a bit of time."

## Objection responses

Every response agrees first, then turns.

**Price**
> "I totally get that and I just want to point something out. If getting clients and increasing revenue is the exact thing you're struggling with, isn't that why we're having this conversation in the first place?"

> "One extra [roofing/plumbing] job is worth what, $5,000? This runs all month for a fraction of one job, you'd be paying me $297 to stop losing money."

> "If this catches just one job you would have missed, it pays for itself for the entire year. How many calls do you think you're missing per month?"

**Time**
> "Exactly. That's why you need systems that take less of it."

> "And you don't have to learn anything, set up anything, or remember anything. You run your business and I'll take care of the rest."

**Let me think about it**
> "No one ever is ready, but waiting rarely makes you more prepared. Action does."

Parking exit:
> "Totally understand. Quick question so I can close the loop on my end: is there anything specific you still need from me to decide or should I just park this for now? Either way is completely fine."

Free profile fix:
> "Of course. While you're thinking it over, want me to just fix the profile stuff, the photos, the hours, the missing details? Takes me about 20 minutes and there's no charge for it. Then you can see what it's like working with me before you decide anything."

Cost of delay:
> "Every day without a system is another lead handing money directly to your competitors. What does the next 6 months look like if nothing changes?"

**Spouse or partner**
> "Totally understand. And honestly, you should talk to your partner. This affects both of you and I'm not trying to rush you, I'm just trying to stop fear from making the decision for you. But let me ask you something before you go: You told me earlier this problem is costing you about $10,000 a month. If you spend the next 2 weeks thinking about it, that's another $5,000 gone. So if your partner asked you 'Is this worth it?', what would you tell them?"

Then: "What would happen if they said no?"
- "I'd do it anyway" means the partner is not the decision maker. Proceed.
- "I wouldn't move forward" means follow with "What parts do you think they wouldn't like?" to surface the real objection.

**Already have someone**
> "That's great. Most of the businesses I work with already had someone too. Would it be okay if I showed you one thing we do differently just so you have something to compare it to, no pressure either way?"

Local proof challenge:
> "Totally fair. Do me one favor though: open Google Maps and ask it for the best [appliance repair / plumber] company near you. If you come up, ignore me, no hard feelings. If he comes up, leave him alone, no hard feelings."

**Bad past experience**
> "And how much longer are you going to keep trying what's not working?"

> "That's actually really useful to know. A lot of the businesses I work with said the same thing before we started. Usually the issue is the system, not the idea. Would it be okay if I showed you exactly how we handle that just so you can see what's different?"

## The pre-call application (Combination Method)

Four questions on the booking form, before the calendar opens. Each has a matching call opener that reads their own words back.

**1. Challenge**, "What's the biggest challenge you're facing right now with [topic]?"
> On the call: "I saw in your application you said your biggest challenge is [their exact words]. Tell me more about that."

**2. Effort**, "What have you already tried to fix this?"
> On the call: "You mentioned you've already tried [their answer]. What do you think was missing from that approach?"

**3. Plan B**, "If we don't end up working together, what's your Plan B?"
> On the call: "I noticed you said your Plan B is [their answer]. What would it mean for you if you had to go that route?"

**4. Vision**, "Describe your business 6 months from now if we nail this."
> On the call: "You wrote that 6 months from now you want [their exact words]. Let's talk about what's standing between where you are and that."

Question 3 does the work. Most prospects discover on the form that they have no Plan B.

He reports close rates moving from ~20% to over 60% on the same offer at the same price, and the elimination of the cold "so what exactly do you do?" opening.

## Review requests

**Message 1, check-in, no ask, no link.** Immediately or one hour after job completion.
> "Hey Sarah, this is Mike from ABC Roofing just checking in to make sure everything looks good with the work we did today."

**Message 2, the request.** One to two hours later, or immediately if they replied.
> "Hi Sarah, Mike here from ABC Roofing. Just wanted to say thanks again for the opportunity to work with you! If you have 30 seconds, would you mind sharing your experience in a quick Google review? It helps other homeowners know they can trust us: [link]"

**Message 3, reminder.** 24 to 48 hours later, only if the link went unclicked.
> "Just a quick follow-up, if you haven't had a chance yet, we'd really appreciate a quick Google review. It helps others know they can trust us. [link]"

**Message 4, email fallback**, 1 to 2 days on if the text went unopened.

Sequence halts the instant the link is clicked or they reply STOP.

**Negative review response, four steps:**
1. Acknowledge: "Hi Dave, I'm sorry to hear about your experience. We know how frustrating it is when a job doesn't go as expected…"
2. Never argue publicly.
3. Move offline: "Please give the office a call and ask for Mike so we can look into what happened and make this right."
4. Text the business owner a heads-up.

I've already started reviewing your website and I'm seeing some quick wins that we could implement this week. I'll call you tomorrow at 2 p.m. unless I hear otherwise. Looking forward to connecting!"

*Transition*
> "Well, we work with [marketing agencies] to help them [grow their agency and get more clients]. Is [growing your agency] a priority to you right now?"

> "Great, seeing as you want to do this, I'd love to set up a quick meeting where I can walk you through things and show you exactly what's happening. Does [Tuesday at 10 AM] work for you?"

> "Oh interesting, don't usually hear that as most people do want [clients and revenue]. I'd still love to set up a quick time so I can figure out what's top of mind."
