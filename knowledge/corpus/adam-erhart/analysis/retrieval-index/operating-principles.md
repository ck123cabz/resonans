## The Four Cs

Commitment → Courage → Capability → Confidence. Credited to Dan Sullivan.

Most people sit waiting to feel ready. **Confidence is the reward for action, not the prerequisite.**

1. **Commitment**, decide to take on the goal before you know how.
2. **Courage**, act despite the fear. Courage is acting while scared, not the absence of fear.
3. **Capability**, reps build competence.
4. **Confidence**, evidence of small wins delivers the real thing.

Backed by Albert Bandura's self-efficacy research `[G]`, and a 1991 Molton, Brown and Lent review putting self-efficacy at roughly 14% of performance variance. Bandura ranked how belief gets built: encouragement helps a little, but **mastery experience**, doing the task successfully even once, beats everything else by a distance.

## Identity over goals

"You don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your minimum standards."

Be, Do, Have: raising income requires changing internal identity and standards first. He taught SMART goals for years and changed his mind, concluding that goals do not drive transformation and standards do.

## The 3-day time audit

Track every activity from waking to sleeping in **30-minute blocks for three consecutive working days**. Then sort every task into four boxes:

| | Enjoy | Dislike |
|---|---|---|
| **Good at** | Keep. Your zone. | Delegate to a VA, contractor or software. |
| **Not good at** | Train, or delegate over time. | **Get help or eliminate today.** |

The only exercise in the mindset lane with a defined output.

## The T-shaped marketer

Marketing spans dozens of disciplines. Trying to master all of them makes you average at everything.

- **The horizontal bar:** conversant across SEO, content, ads, email, PR, video, web and UI, enough to speak intelligently to clients and diagnose problems.
- **The vertical bar:** world-class in one or two specific areas, positioning, direct-response copy, or HighLevel automation.

## The Strategy of Preeminence

Adapted from Jay Abraham. Shift the frame from salesperson to trusted advisor.

Treat every client's business as if it were your own, a fiduciary stance. Stop asking "how do I get them to buy?" and start asking **"how can I serve them so well that buying is the next obvious step?"**

It removes sales anxiety. You step up to lead and advise rather than chase.

## Perfectionism

Perfectionism is fear and procrastination in a suit.

**"80% done and published beats 100% perfect every single time."** A flawed campaign live in the market gathers real data; a hidden masterpiece makes $0. Perfection also reads as intimidating and triggers skepticism.

The HighLevel version: "Perfect is not a strategy, installed is a strategy."

## Burnout

He ran a seven-figure course and coaching business on constant launches, ad tweaks and 16-hour days, and hit severe stress-related health problems.

Hard work is required in sprints. Long-term scaling requires **leverage, subtraction and systems**, not 24/7 hustle. Radical simplicity: one offer, one niche, one acquisition engine, done better.

## Critics

Filter for actionable feedback, discard the rest. Ask: is this constructive, or is it noise?

Negative online voices are a small loud minority. **High achievers do not criticise others**, criticism comes from people who are jealous, miserable or afraid of watching you succeed. Focus on the ten silent supporters behind every one hater.

## The nine contrarian positions

Each one against the conventional advice it rejects.

**1. Ugly ads beat polished ads.** Conventional: invest in design, videography, branding to look credible. His view: raw, unpolished, MS-Paint-style ads crush expensive agency ads on CTR and conversion, because the brain automatically filters out what looks like advertising. Raw creative breaks the scroll pattern and drops CPC.

**2. He shut a $1M/year course business for a lean agency.** Conventional: courses are the holy grail of passive income, agencies are stressful and unscalable. His view: the course model is a content treadmill with launch pressure. Local owners do not care about follower counts, they care whether you bring customers, and they pay month after month for real results.

**3. One-person beats headcount.** A traditional $1M agency keeps 10–20% net. A $200K one-person agency keeps 90%+, with no payroll liability and no management.

**4. No sales calls, pitch decks or custom proposals.** He reports closing over $5 million on 30-minute diagnostic conversations and Loom audits without sending a proposal. Custom proposals kill momentum and produce "let me think about it" then ghosting. Rackham's 35,000-call SPIN study `[G]` backs it: pitching triggers resistance, diagnostic questions let prospects close themselves.

**5. Content marketing is a bad starting strategy.** Conventional: build a personal brand, post daily, give value, attract organically. His view: content is slow and indirect, for mature agencies at $50K–$100K+/month. Beginners at $0–$20K should make direct offers to sales-qualified leads exclusively. He spent months on free blogs and posts making almost nothing, then shifted to direct offers and scaled. Kern Institute data: direct offer-driven marketing costs $50–$100 per sale against 10x that for brand-driven content.

**6. Adding friction increases conversions.** Conventional: remove all friction, shortest possible forms. His view: mandatory qualification applications and required pre-call homework raise close rates and client quality. Requiring a 7-minute overview video before booking made 40% cancel, the tire-kickers, while the remaining 60% arrived prepared, cutting calls from 45 minutes to 25 with higher close rates and retention.

**7. Answering questions loses deals.** Conventional: answer promptly and thoroughly to show expertise. His view: explaining deliverables or price upfront flips the power dynamic and makes you a needy vendor. Acknowledge, then ask a qualification question back.

**8. Don't-niche-down is the Baskin-Robbins trap.** Conventional: offer everything so you never turn away revenue. His view: a menu of 10+ services creates messy positioning, complicated pricing and fulfilment chaos. Iyengar's jam study `[G]`: 24 flavours produced a 3% purchase rate, six produced 30%. He cut his own site from 11 services to one and went from $0 in 60 days to 7 clients in 30.

**9. Cold outreach to strangers is the hardest path.** Average conversion 0.7%, one client per 142 messages. Lead with proof, a free video audit or a pre-built asset, or work warm water. Cold fails because you are asking for trust and time before delivering anything.

## What he has changed his mind about

The notebook exposes no dates, so this is almost the only place the corpus has a timeline.

1. **Niching down.** Resisted it for fear of leaving money on the table. Ran vets, realtors, fitness coaches and restaurants at once, burned out, and changed after mentors pointed out that one service to one audience makes both acquisition and fulfilment easier.
2. **SMART goals.** Taught them, then concluded they do not drive transformation. Standards and identity do.
3. **Visualisation and manifestation.** Went through an anti-*Secret* phase mocking vision boards, then changed after studying the reticular activating system, visualisation tunes the brain's filter to notice relevant opportunities.
4. **Pitch decks.** Believed professionalism meant 20 slides, case studies and proposal breakdowns. Ran out of time to build one, stated his price flat without defending it, got an instant yes. Concluded that over-explaining signals weakness and defending your price lowers authority.
5. **Multi-service websites.** Built the 11-service site in 2017, made $0 in 60 days, deleted all but review automation.

## The 2026 positions

Dated, and they must carry the date wherever reused.

- **The AI hype cycle.** AI sits at the peak of inflated expectations on the Gartner curve. Generic wrapper tools will collapse in the trough. Only tools integrated into core business infrastructure survive into the plateau.
- **The death of blue-link search.** Search is moving from ten blue links to single-source AI recommendations. In Ask Maps, Google picks four businesses out of 90+, making it winner-take-all.
- **The feed-first street market.** Social has shifted from a curated gallery to an algorithmic street market. Production value is secondary to pattern-interrupt hooks, rapid testing and automated comment-to-DM backends.
- **The Formula 1 analogy.** AI alone produces glorified Wikipedia summaries. Winning agencies pair AI speed and volume with human strategy, positioning and psychological oversight, the car and the driver.

## Odds and ends from the sweep

**The Rule of 100.** First thing every workday, 100 direct outreach connection attempts before any non-revenue task.

**Agency algebra.** Outreach scales linearly where paid ads do not, no ad fatigue, no non-linear curve. If 100 attempts yield 15 replies, 4 calls and 1 client, then 200 attempts yield 2 clients.

**The 2-minute audit conversion loop.** Ten cold video audits a week is about 30 minutes of work, returns ~2 replies, converts to 1 client every 2 to 3 weeks. At $400/mo that stacks to 5 clients ($2,000/mo) in three months and 25 clients ($10,000/mo) inside a year.

**The switching-friction experiment.** He tracked his own task-switching and found 43 tab or tool switches by 2pm, each costing ~5 minutes of mental recovery, nearly 2 hours of daily focus. He responded by consolidating nine AI subscriptions into one integrated AI browser.

**Zero-cost budgeting.** For every calendar commitment and every tool: "If I hadn't already started this project or bought this tool, would I start it today?" No means cut it now.

**The Perfect Tuesday exercise.** Write out your ideal average workday on a Tuesday, deliberately not a weekend, so you design a working life rather than a holiday.

**Journaling.** Morning pages, 15 minutes or 3 pages of unedited brain dump. Or the Winner's Bible approach, combining images, stories and goals to tune the reticular activating system.

**Amazon Accelerate insights.** Ads are an accelerator, not an initiator, running them on an unoptimised listing or a broken offer only burns cash faster. And from Neumeier's *Zag*: stop copying competitors' discounts, build something that does not need them to sell.
