# Email Sequences

Sequence structures, intervals, subject-line rules and every template at full wording. Source: `q37-email-nurture.md`. Provenance `[S]`.

Email is the owned channel. Social is rented.

---

## Sequence structures

### Welcome, 3 to 5 emails, triggered on opt-in, daily or across 5 to 7 days
1. **Minute 0.** Deliver the lead magnet and solve one small problem. No pitch.
2. **Day 1–2.** A relatable origin or client transformation story showing you understand the struggle.
3. **Day 2–3.** Shine a flashlight on the real underlying problem keeping them stuck. Build tension.
4. **Day 4–5.** Present the signature system as the natural next step, handle the core objections, invite action.

### Weekly nurture, 2 to 3 emails a week, indefinitely
One story (a client win, a failure, a lesson) plus one actionable tip or framework, ending on a soft CTA.

**He warns explicitly against monthly or fortnightly sending.** At a typical 25% open rate, monthly emails mean a subscriber may only see your name once every 120 days. They forget who you are, mark you spam, or unsubscribe.

He calls the frame "staying at the campfire": you are not always selling, you are staying visible so that when they are ready you are the obvious choice.

### Human follow-up, 4 emails over 7 to 14 days
For anyone who opts in from an ad or a lead capture.

| Email | Timing | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Instant | Acknowledge and set expectations |
| 2 | Day 2 | Teach a mini-win |
| 3 | Day 5 | Social proof or a case study story |
| 4 | Day 7–10 | Friendly ask, book a call or reply |

### Scale Tipper, 3 emails before a booked call, days 1, 2, 3
The Story Stack delivered before the conversation starts.
1. **Skeptic**, Maria in Dallas, burned before, $73k months.
2. **Fast Mover**, Tom in Denver, results in 30 days.
3. **Transformation / Validator**, Jennifer, who reviewed five case studies.

### Lighthouse, 4 messages over 5 to 7 days
For unresponsive cold leads. Audit or outreach → day 2 soft bump with new value → day 5 low-stakes check-in → day 7 "before I close your file."

### Reactivation (ACRC), quarterly, every 90 days
One core message plus one follow-up 24 to 48 hours later if no response.

## Subject line rules

1. **Lowercase and casual.** `quick question`, `i made you something`. It should read like a colleague, not an automated promotion.
2. **The from-name is king.** The sender's name is the single biggest factor in open rate. People open because they trust the sender, not because the subject was clever.
3. **Negative and danger-oriented hooks beat positive ones.** The brain reacts faster to risk than to a smile. "Never do this." "The worst way to…"
4. **Open loops.** An incomplete thought forces the open to close the mental tab. "The biggest mistake you're making is…"

### Examples
**Casual:** `quick question` · `I made you something` · `Got it. Here's what happens next.` · `checking in`
**Personalised:** `Hey [First Name], question` · `quick request for [First Name]`
**Re-engagement:** `Still want to hear from me?`
**By motivation angle**, with the open rates he reports:
- Achievement, "The 3 strategies top 1% marketers use to dominate their niche" (38%)
- Security, "Build a marketing system that works even when algorithms change" (42%)
- Growth, "Master the psychology behind every successful marketing campaign" (35%)

## Body structure by email type

**Welcome:** lead magnet link at the top → an empathy statement naming their core struggle → brief brand background → one clear next step.

**Nurture:** conversational, "like texting a smart friend" → a personal story, lesson or busted myth → an actionable framework → a soft CTA.

**Sales follow-up:** contextual opener ("based on what you told me…") → a mini-win or specific proof → a single casual invitation to book or reply.

**Cold outreach:** one or two sentences referencing their specific business, no personal backstory → a clear value claim ("we help X do Y without Z") → a curiosity statement and a soft CTA.

## Segmentation

**Vertical, not horizontal.** Tailor the copy to dental practices or roofers specifically rather than broad messaging across industries.

**By psychological motivation.** Split the list into 3 to 5 buyer types by what they value, Achievement, Security, Belonging, Growth, and send matched subject lines and copy.

**By buyer readiness**, the 3 / 10 / 87 pyramid:
- **3% ready now (0–30 days)**, direct offers, instant booking CTAs.
- **10% soon (30–90 days)**, short follow-up sequences and case study proof.
- **87% later (90+ days)**, weekly long-term nurture. This is why the weekly cadence matters; almost nine in ten of the list lives here.

**List hygiene.** Clean every 3 to 6 months, or every few weeks on a large fast-growing list. Send a re-engagement email, then unsubscribe the inactive. Removing unengaged subscribers improves domain deliverability and overall return.

## The templates

### 1. "I made you something"
**Subject:** `I made you something`
> "Hey [First Name], I noticed your Facebook page hasn't been updated in a while and I made you these two posts for [Business Name]. No catch, use them if you want. If you like them, I've got 12 more I can send over.
> [Your Name]"

### 2. The nine-word email (Dean Jackson)
**Subject:** `quick question`
> "Hey [First Name], are you still looking to [goal/result]?"

### 3. Voucher reactivation
**Subject:** `quick question`
> "Hey [First Name], it's been a while, just checking in… I've got 10 free [teeth whitening / wiper blade replacement / $20 gift card] vouchers here on my desk, want me to save one for you?"

### 4. The 4-part human follow-up

**Email 1, acknowledge and guide**
**Subject:** `Got it. Here's what happens next.`
> "Hey [First Name], just a simple message to say I saw you check this out. Here's what to expect next… [details/link]. No pitch, just wanted to make sure you got it."

**Email 2, teach something useful**
> "Hey [First Name], here's a quick 3-minute tip on how to fix [specific small problem]. Thought this might help you out today…"

**Email 3, social proof**
> "Hey [First Name], let me tell you about [Client Name] who was struggling with [problem]. Here's how we helped them fix it in 30 days…"

**Email 4, the friendly ask**
> "Hey [First Name], if you're interested, I'd love to invite you to book a quick strategy call or reply with any questions. Super casual and pressure-free."

### 5. Close your file
**Subject:** `quick question`
> "Hey [First Name], before I close your file, I just wanted to check one last time. If you're not interested, no worries at all, I won't reach out again. But if the timing's just been off, I'm still happy to send over what I found. Let me know!"

### 6. Forgot to mention (the soft bump)
**Subject:** `Re: quick question`
> "Hey [First Name], forgot to mention… One of the fastest things I've seen work for [business type] is an automated review request that goes out after every job. I cover that in the breakdown, happy to send it over if you want to take a look."

### 7. Quarterly reactivation
**Subject:** `checking in`
> "Hey [First Name], still around? If you ever want to revisit this, no pressure. Here's a quick win you can use in the meantime: [link]."

### 8. Cold audit permission
**Subject:** `quick request for [First Name]`
> "Hey [First Name], I was looking up [niche] companies in [City] and came across your website. We help [niche] clients get [desired outcome] per month without [thing they hate]. I put together a quick video audit highlighting a few gaps costing you calls, let me know if you're interested and I can send more info."

### 9. List cleaning
**Subject:** `Still want to hear from me?`
> "Hey [First Name], I'm cleaning up my email list this week. If you still want to hear from me and get my best strategies, click this link: [link]. If not, no hard feelings at all, I'll unsubscribe you so I stop cluttering your inbox!"

### 10. The generation prompt
> "Write me a high-converting email in the style of Adam Erhart using proven marketing psychology. Keep it conversational, story-driven, and designed to educate, build trust, and lead naturally into my offer. Before writing this email, ask me two to three specific questions to understand my business, my offer, and my target market."
