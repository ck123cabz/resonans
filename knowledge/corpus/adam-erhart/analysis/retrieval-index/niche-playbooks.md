# Niche Playbooks

Per-niche economics, channel and what differs. Source: `q29`, `q06`, `q44`. Provenance `[S]`.

The core delivery never changes, reviews first, then AI receptionist or missed-call text-back, then the revenue website. What changes is the average job value, the channel, and the reason they will listen.

Two questions decide every niche: **is a single job worth enough to fund a retainer**, and **is anyone else already pitching them**.

---

## Runway versus destination

The rule that governs which niche you pick when.

**Runway niches** are the boring, ugly, uncrowded ones. Garage doors, tree services, junk removal, gutter cleaning, appliance repair, pest control, funeral homes. You go here first to reach 3 to 10 clients and real case studies fast, because nobody else is pitching them.

**Destination niches** are the proven, profitable, crowded ones. Dentists, chiropractors, realtors, general lawyers. He teaches with them because their unit economics make the frameworks legible, and they are wrong as a starting block.

**Enter a crowded niche only with one of these four:**
1. Prior domain background, you were a dental student, a realtor, a healthcare worker, and you speak the language from inside.
2. Post-runway cash flow and testimonials from an easier niche.
3. A drilled-down sub-niche or single high-ticket procedure. Orthodontics, implant dentistry, teeth whitening, estate law. Never "dentists" or "lawyers".
4. Warm-water proximity, local relationships and referrals, not cold email across the country.

**Who should stay out:** complete beginners with no case studies, no testimonials and no industry background. And generalists with no angle running template outreach.

**What has to be different to win in one:** an ultra-specific painkiller outcome ("10 new patient procedure appointments in 14 days without spending more on ads"), a hard performance guarantee because they have been burned before, a named signature system so they cannot price-compare you against generalists, and proof led with an audit rather than a pitch.

When prospects get pitched weekly their market sophistication is high and template outreach is useless. The moves that still work: send the 2-minute X-ray instead of the brochure, control the watering hole so they come to you, and go selective, "We are reviewing 2 to 3 dental practices in [City] this quarter to see if there's a fit."

## The trades

### Plumbers
- **Job value:** $300–$600 a service call, ~$500 average, $500–$1,000 customer value
- **Channel:** Google Maps and Google Ads
- **Offer:** reviews $97–$297 front door, then AI receptionist / missed-call text-back $397, then revenue website $497
- **What's different:** extreme urgency. Someone standing in two inches of water at 9pm. They live on their phones and cannot answer under a sink. With 62% of calls unanswered and 78% hiring whoever responds first, instant text-back or voice AI captures jobs competitors lose outright.

### Roofers
- **Job value:** $5,000–$8,300, up to $15,000+ on a full replacement
- **Channel:** Maps and Search for emergency leaks; Facebook lead ads for free inspection offers
- **Offer:** review engine $297, Ghost Town profile audits, free roof inspection vouchers, Facebook lead gen
- **What's different:** one extra job a year covers the annual fee ten times over. But roofers are heavily targeted and their inbox is a graveyard of cold pitches. Lead with the 2-minute visual audit showing their review gap, never a pitch deck.

### HVAC
- **Job value:** $300–$2,000 a repair, $8,000+ on a system replacement
- **Channel:** Maps and Google Search
- **Offer:** 24/7 AI voice receptionist $397–$500, review system $297, free AC diagnostic checklists, maintenance agreements
- **What's different:** severe seasonality and temperature panic. AC dies in 105°F heat and customers will not leave voicemails, they dial down the Maps list until someone answers. Plus a diagnostic-to-replacement upside: a $200 call becoming an $8,000 unit.

### Electricians
- **Job value:** $300–$500 a call
- **Channel:** Google Maps
- **Offer:** Ghost Town listing repair $297, review automation, missed-call text-back
- **What's different:** characteristically abandoned listings. No photo updates in years, dozens of unanswered reviews. Pointing out that nearby competitors sit at 300+ active reviews while they have 15 is a one-step sale with no formal call needed.

### Garage door repair
- **Job value:** $400–$1,500
- **Channel:** Google Maps, "garage door repair near me"
- **Offer:** $397 AI conversation layer, or $297 review automation
- **What's different:** a prime boring goldmine. Sudden high-urgency friction, the door stuck halfway at 7pm with a car trapped inside, and virtually zero agency competition compared to plumbers or roofers.

### Tree services
- **Job value:** $1,000–$3,000+ a removal
- **Channel:** Maps and organic local
- **Offer:** review automation $197–$297, profile optimisation
- **What's different:** unsexy, low competition, solo owners or small crews. Buyers weigh safety and recent review proof over a polished website.

### Junk removal
- **Job value:** $200–$600 a haul
- **Channel:** Google Maps
- **Offer:** review automation $197–$297, Map Pack ranking
- **What's different:** on the dirty-niches list. Agencies rarely pitch junk haulers, so a list of 50 local operators returns fast.

### Gutter cleaning and guards
- **Job value:** $2,000 on a guard installation
- **Channel:** Maps and direct email audits
- **Offer:** $297/mo starter package, review and profile system
- **What's different:** unlike roofers who get pitched daily, gutter installers are virtually ignored.

### Appliance repair
- **Job value:** $200 average, $800+ on a compressor or refrigerator overhaul
- **Channel:** Google Maps and AI search
- **Offer:** AI search / Ask Maps profile optimisation $500, review automation
- **What's different:** the AI search angle bites hardest here. Ask Maps picks one to three favourites on review velocity, so the blind-spot pitch lands.

### Auto repair and detailing
- **Job value:** $300–$500 a ticket
- **Channel:** SMS database reactivation and Google Maps
- **Offer:** past-customer reactivation with a free wiper blade or car wash, AI voice receptionist
- **What's different:** they sit on thousands of dormant past-customer numbers that can be monetised in minutes.

## The professional practices

### Chiropractors
- **Value:** $60–$80 a visit, $1,000–$2,000+ lifetime, more when family members join
- **Channel:** Facebook and Instagram ads for discovery, SMS database reactivation
- **Offer:** Facebook lead ads at a $1,500–$2,500/mo retainer with a low-friction entry (a $21 consultation), risk-reversal patient guarantees, automated reactivation
- **What's different:** a crowded red ocean. Generic lead-gen pitches fail. The offer must name an exact outcome, "20 new spinal decompression patients in 30 days without cold calling", and carry a guarantee.

### Dentists
- **Value:** $4,000–$10,000 lifetime per patient
- **Channel:** Maps and reviews for general practice, Facebook and Instagram for cosmetic procedures, SMS for reactivation
- **Offer:** database reactivation ("free teeth whitening session for returning patients who book this week"), review engine $197–$297, Facebook ads for implants, Invisalign and whitening
- **What's different:** crowded and pitch-fatigued, and extremely rating-sensitive, he cites 97% of consumers checking reviews before booking. But one patient at $4K–$10K means 1 or 2 new patients a year is a 10x return on a review service.

### Doggy daycare and pet services
- **Value:** $500–$1,000+ lifetime per pet owner
- **Channel:** local SEO, booking automation, social
- **Offer:** automated booking and reminders, profile SEO, social posting
- **What's different:** highly emotional, passionate owners, strong word of mouth, recurring boarding demand.

### Pest control, alarm systems, funeral homes
- **Value:** high recurring contract or transaction value
- **Channel:** Maps, review automation, AI receptionist
- **What's different:** the dirty and ugly list. Beginners avoid them for status reasons, leaving near-zero competition for high-margin retainers.

## Named weak niches

**Oversaturated red oceans**, dentists, chiropractors, realtors, cannabis, general lawyers. Beginners following generic agency courses flood them, and the owners are bombarded daily and deeply skeptical. *See runway versus destination above: these are destinations, not exclusions.*

**Low ticket, low lifetime value**, bakeries, ice cream and gelato shops, cafes. Selling $1–$5 items leaves no margin to fund a retainer.

**"Pretty" vanity niches**, photography, videography, esports, travel and tourism, fashion, e-commerce, info-product and course sellers. They attract amateur marketers chasing status rather than profit, and fulfilment is subjective and hard to scale.

**Dying tugboats**, newspapers, physical bookstores, office supply. Declining demand makes lead generation close to impossible.

**Veterinarians, specifically.** Most already run six-month waitlists and have more clients than they can handle. Selling them lead generation is the clearest case of the offer-first mistake.
