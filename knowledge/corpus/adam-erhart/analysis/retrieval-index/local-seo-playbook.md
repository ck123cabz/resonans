# Local SEO Playbook

Map Pack ranking, the Anchor Method, NAP consistency, the review engine and the Google Trifecta, as a runnable checklist. Source: `q25`, `q26`, `q27`, `q28`, `q33`. Provenance `[S]`, `[G]` where a study is named.

Prospecting and fulfilment run off one list. The five signals that identify a Ghost Town prospect are the five things this playbook fixes once they are a client.

---

## Ranking factors

1. **100% profile completeness.** Name, primary and secondary categories, description, address, phone, hours, services, high-resolution photos.
2. **Citation volume with exact NAP consistency.** Directory listings carrying the business name, address and phone, identical in syntax and formatting everywhere.
3. **Review quantity, rating, recency and keywords.** Fresh reviews outweigh a large block of old ones. The specific words customers write teach AI engines a capability.
4. **Signals of life.** Replying to reviews, weekly photos and posts, active messaging.

Recency and review keywords are the two factors most local operators ignore, and they are the two that now decide the AI recommendation.

---

## The optimisation checklist, in order

### Step 1, baseline
- Claim and verify the listing if it is unmanaged.
- Fill every field: hours (including holidays), primary and secondary categories, business description carrying service keywords, exact address, phone, official website URL, itemised services and products, booking link.
- Upload 5 or 6 professional real job and team photos. Never stock.

One to two hours once. Then a 5–10 minute monthly check for hours, holidays and new services.

### Step 2, citations
- Audit for missing or mismatched NAP across the major directories.
- Deploy identical citations via Yext (integrated in HighLevel) or FatJoe.

### Step 3, review engine
- Automate the post-job request, triggered on job completion, invoice sent, or CRM tag.
- Send every customer the same direct Google link. **No gating.**
- Run a reactivation campaign across the past-customer database, dripped 2 to 3 every 20 minutes during business hours so volume looks natural.
- Reply to the backlog: AI auto-responders on a 10–15 minute delay for 4 and 5 stars, anything under 4 by hand within 24 hours.
- Flag and dispute fake, spam or policy-violating reviews.

### Step 4, maintenance
- Fresh photos and a short post weekly.
- Keep the review requests running indefinitely. Recency is a ranking factor, so the work never stops.

## The Anchor Method

Three signals, for both Map Pack and AI search.

### Anchor 1, Words
AI reads the text inside reviews. Twelve customers writing "fixed my leak the same day" teaches it that capability and gets the business recommended for that query. Reviews older than two years read as possibly-closed.

**Do:** the 3-message compliant flow (full text in `scripts-library.md`). Check-in with no link and no ask; the request with the direct link one to two hours later; a gentle reminder at 24–48 hours only if the link went unclicked. Plus a database reactivation campaign to kickstart volume.

**How often:** continuously, after every completed job.
**How much:** 10 to 20+ fresh reviews a month.
**Measure:** total count, rating trend, recency and velocity, and request conversion, texts sent against opened against reviews generated.

### Anchor 2, Signals of Life
AI checks whether the business is operating right now. Ancient photos and unanswered reviews read as a ghost town.

**Do:** real unpolished phone photos of completed jobs, equipment, trucks and team. AI-drafted replies to 4 and 5 star reviews. Weekly profile posts.

**How often:** photos every week or two; replies on a 10–15 minute delay so they do not read as robotic; negative feedback handled within 24 hours; posts weekly.
**How much:** 2 to 6 new photos a month, 100% response rate.
**Measure:** owner response rate, photo upload frequency, post recency.

### Anchor 3, Profile Completeness
"Complete beats better." A GatherUp study found Google's AI recommended a 3.3-star car wash over 4.8-star competitors because its listing carried clearance height and hours `[G]`. Over half of local service profiles are missing critical fields.

**Measure:** the audit score. Baseline runs 30–50%, target 78–100%.

## NAP consistency

Name, Address and Phone must be 100% character-for-character identical everywhere. "Suite 1" on Google against "ST 1" or "Building 1" elsewhere splits the ranking authority.

**Directories that matter:** Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Maps, MapQuest, Yelp, Facebook, YellowPages, Chamber of Commerce, and industry-specific registries.

**Audit:** HighLevel's prospecting tool, Yext, D7 Lead Finder or SpyFu. They flag "no match" for a missing listing and "mismatch" for inconsistent formatting.

**Fix:** establish one canonical string, e.g. `1943 Belgrade Ave, Suite 1, Charleston, SC 29407, (843) 555-0199`, push it through Yext or FatJoe, overwrite mismatches and duplicates, build the missing ones.

This is data hygiene dressed as SEO, which is why it sells as a recurring service.

## The review engine in detail

**Triggers:** job marked complete, invoice sent, CRM tag added, or the owner texting the customer's details in.

**Timing:**
| Message | When |
|---|---|
| 1, check-in, no ask, no link | Immediately or +1 hour |
| 2, the request with the link | +1 to 2 hours, or immediately on a reply |
| 3, reminder | +24 to 48 hours, only if unclicked |
| 4, email fallback | +1 to 2 days if the text went unopened |

Three to four touches. The sequence halts the instant the link is clicked or they reply STOP.

**No review gating, ever.** Every customer gets the identical request and the identical direct Google link regardless of sentiment. Filtering happy customers to Google while routing unhappy ones to a private form breaks Google's terms and FTC rules, and risks the reviews being wiped or the profile suspended.

Message 1 is what makes this work. It carries no ask, so a complaint surfaces before the public request goes out, and the business can fix it. Customers whose problem is resolved fast often leave the most enthusiastic reviews. It is a quality gate that happens to be compliant, rather than a gate on who gets asked.

**Negative reviews** get a human: acknowledge, never argue publicly, move it offline, text the owner a heads-up. Roughly 4 minutes of work per review.

**Reported results.** Reactivation over a past list converts at 8–15%. 500 contacts yields 40 to 75 reviews in the first weeks. A landscaping client on 16 reviews over 11 years got 9 in six days. Ongoing volume moves from 1–2 a month to 10–20+.

**External support:** Harvard Business School, a one-star rating increase moves revenue 5–9% `[G]`. GatherUp, 45% of consumers weigh review recency over the star rating `[G]`.

**Economics:** $197–$297–$397/mo against 15 to 30 minutes of monthly maintenance.

## Ghost Town listings

A profile for a real, operating business that looks abandoned.

Search Maps for the category, scroll past the top 3, and look for:
1. Under 30–50 reviews while competitors sit at 100–300+.
2. Last review 3, 6, 9 months or years ago.
3. Zero owner replies.
4. Unclaimed or incomplete profile, no hours, no description, no service list, ancient photos, no website link.
5. Invisible to AI search, which skips stagnant profiles for ones with recent activity, photos and replies.

**Two of these confirms it.** A decision procedure, not a judgement call, so it survives being handed to someone else or to software.

Target pages 2 and 3, or smaller suburban towns. Page 1 has no gap to sell against; page 4 has no business to save.

## AI search (AEO / GEO)

Ask Maps answers conversational questions, "who can fix a burst pipe today?", and returns one to four businesses. In a Seattle plumber test with 90+ companies it named four, leaving 86 invisible.

| | Traditional SEO | AI search |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank a page for a keyword | Become a source the model cites |
| Visibility | Tiered, positions 1–10 | Binary. Recommended, or invisible. |
| Authority | Favours big domains | Democratised. Structured clarity wins. |
| Behaviour | Skim, ten tabs | Long sessions, high pre-established trust |

### Content optimisation, the Proof Path
1. Phrase for full conversational questions, not stuffed keywords.
2. Put the direct answer at the top under the heading. Do not bury it under a narrative intro.
3. Clean H2/H3, short paragraphs, bullets, summary boxes.
4. Schema markup labelling page type, Service, How-To, FAQ.
5. A credibility ladder: studies, proprietary client data, case studies, author bios with real experience.
6. Breadcrumbs of authority across podcasts, YouTube, LinkedIn, Reddit and industry blogs.

### The sales wedge
An owner whose phone went quiet does not know why. Pitching web design or social management is hard because they think that is handled. Showing them AI naming three competitors and not them creates urgency immediately. Priced at $297–$500+/mo to maintain the three Anchors and review velocity.

### His 2026 predictions
Google cites its own Business Profiles over 8x more than external pages. The AI market sits at the peak of inflated expectations and wrapper tools will collapse, leaving infrastructure-integrated tools. These are positions, not observations, and carry that date.

## The Google Trifecta

Own the paid ad, the Map Pack pin and the organic listing on one results page.

**Why the three reinforce each other:** you own the click wherever it lands; repeated appearance on one page runs the mere exposure effect; and the same work, reviews, citations, NAP consistency, feeds Map Pack and organic simultaneously. Ads capture immediate demand while the other two compound.

**Build order for a new local business:**
1. **Map Pack.** Highest ROI, no media budget, fastest calls.
2. **Organic and a dedicated landing page.** Never send search traffic to the homepage.
3. **Google Ads.** Once the page and offer convert reliably.

Ads go last on purpose. It is the 5 Ms applied to one channel: Machine before Media.

**Budget:** local SEO runs on $50–$297/mo of software at near-100% margin. Ads need a real media budget, starting at $10–$20/day or a Target CPA.
