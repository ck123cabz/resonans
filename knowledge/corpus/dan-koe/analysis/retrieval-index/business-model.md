# Business Model

Layer 2 retrieval index for the Dan Koe corpus, source lanes q01–q06. One item per `##`
heading, each section self-contained. Context is repeated rather than cross-referenced, so a
single grep returns the whole usable thing.

**Provenance key.** `[D]` his own construction with no source acknowledged · `[D+M:name]` his
name over someone else's model, both halves true and neither one droppable · `[M:name]` a
straight relay under the other person's name · `[G]` backed by his own reported outcome ·
`[I]` inferred, not stated in the corpus · `[A]` a gap the corpus does not fill. Knowledge
types: `[FRM]` framework · `[TAC]` tactic · `[TPL]` verbatim template or line · `[NUM]` number
· `[PRN]` principle · `[WRN]` warning or rejection · `[BIO]` biography · `[CIT]` cited authority.

**Standing confound on every revenue and audience figure in this file.** Every number he gives
is his own, produced in a business whose product is teaching this method. His largest single
launch was a writing product sold to people who want to write, and q66 records that he built it
because he knew the writing niche was profitable. **No figure in this corpus comes from a
student.** The figures evidence demand for the instruction at least as much as efficacy of the
thing instructed.

**Standing attribution: Naval Ravikant sits under the whole model, not one framework of it.**
Digital leverage, specific knowledge, permissionless work and zero marginal cost of replication
are four separate Naval concepts and together they are the structure the One-Person Business
Model rests on. Pass 2 marks all four `[D+M:Naval]`. He names Naval five times in q06 and does
not name him in q01 or q12, where the same concepts carry the argument.

**Competing values are printed with both figures and both files. Nothing here is computed.**

## The One-Person Business Model

**What it is** `[FRM]` A philosophy and framework for turning yourself, your interests and your
expertise into a digital business run by one person, with no employees, no investors and no
manual fulfillment at the top end.

**Provenance: he disclaims coining it, in his own words.** q68 records him saying **"I didn't
come up with the concept of one person business but I took it and I gave it an entirely new
meaning"**, the new meaning being the merge with productizing self-actualization. There is no
named originator in the corpus to compound the marker against, so the model carries `[D]` with
the disclaimer attached, and the disclaimer travels with it. q67 additionally lists Naval
Ravikant and Alan Watts as woven into it. The corpus profile originally called this a pure `[D]`
coinage; Pass 2 corrected that against his own statement.

**When to use it** As the frame for anyone building an audience-first solo business. It assumes a
person who will write publicly every day for years.

**The Four Components, complete** `[FRM][D]`

| Component | His label | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | The Goal | Your online character and the overarching vision for your future. You attract followers by showing them the desirable goals you are working toward or have already achieved. |
| Content | The Map | Social media as a public note-taking system. You write about the roadblocks you face, the ideas you read, and the lessons you learn. |
| Product | The System | A digital product, consulting offer or cohort built on the exact step-by-step system you used to overcome your own problems. |
| Marketing / Promotions | The Why | You persuade people to buy by explaining the benefits and how your system will positively transform their lives. |

**The same four slots appear under three different label sets across the corpus** (q01, q04, q12).
Pass 2 reconciles them as one framework with three vocabularies rather than three frameworks:

| Slot | q01 | q04 (Value Equation) | q12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brand = The Goal | Brand = your Purpose | Brand = your Character |
| 2 | Content = The Map | Content = your Priorities | Content = your Mindset and Skillset |
| 3 | Product = The System | Product = your Path | Product = your Process |
| 4 | Promotions = The Why | — | Promotions = your "Why" |

**Cost and margin** `[NUM][D]` The business launches for **$0** using free no-code tools and
social media, and scales through digital products at **95%+ profit margins** rather than through
manpower. The 95%+ figure appears in q01, q07 and q09. q65 gives a different margin statement for
his own business, "taking home about 90%", and the corpus never reconciles the two.

**What he rejects, 1: the startup** `[WRN][D]` Traditional startups require significant startup
capital, investor funding and a large team. They are highly risky and often trap founders in a
stressful race for a unicorn valuation.

**What he rejects, 2: freelancing and agencies as an endpoint** `[WRN][D]` Freelancing is a common
starting point and fundamentally flawed for long-term freedom, because you still trade time for
money. Manual fulfillment builds you a second 9-to-5 where you trade one boss for **5 to 10
bosses**, your clients, with income capped by hours worked.

**The two constraints the model is designed around**

- **The 4-hour workday** `[FRM][D+M:Ferriss]`. q68 records that he explicitly credits Tim
  Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Workweek*: he saw the title in college, misread it as "the 4-hour
  workday", and kept the misreading as his framework. What the rename adds is a daily rather than
  weekly constraint. The corpus states the workday as **4 hours** in q01 and as **2 to 4 hours**
  in q03, q06 and q12, and never reconciles the two.
- **Koe's Law** `[FRM][D+M:Parkinson]`, stated as **"creative work evolves to earn more in the
  time allotted for its completion"**, specifically under a strict 4-hour workday. q68 records
  that he explicitly credits Parkinson's Law ("work expands to fill the time allotted") and calls
  his version an evolution and a spin-off. What the rename adds is inverting the law from a
  warning about padding into a forcing function for creative earnings.

**Sources:** q01 (authoritative for the four components and the constraints), q68 (the
disclaimer and both compound credits), q67, q03, q06, q12, q65.

## You Are The Niche

**What it is** `[PRN][D]` The corpus's single most repeated claim, appearing in q01, q02, q04,
q07, q08 and q12. Your most profitable and unique niche is your own identity, worldview and
experience. Instead of a business as a separate entity built to serve a demographic, you turn
yourself, your story, your daily learning and your problem-solving into the business.

**When to use it** At the point where standard advice would tell someone to pick a market. It
replaces market selection with self-selection.

**The formula** `[TAC][D]` Solve your own problems, document the process online, and sell the
solution to people who are one or two steps behind you. You attract followers with a similar
personality and worldview because they resonate with your story.

**Self-monetization** `[PRN][D]` Instead of inventing a customer avatar, **you make yourself the
customer avatar**.

**The four reasons he rejects niching down** `[WRN][D]`

1. It forces you into **"work you don't care about for people you don't care about to live a life
   you don't care about"** (verbatim, q02).
2. It limits personal evolution. Interests change; locking into a hyper-specific niche, his
   example being SEO for dentists, means the business cannot evolve as you do.
3. It creates **"glorified search engines"**. Posting about one specific skill leaves you without
   depth or personality, easily replaceable by AI or by competitors.
4. Most businesses fail because founders try to solve problems they have never personally
   experienced.

**The three-audience writing rule, verbatim structure** `[TPL][D]` Write **to your past self** to
give advice on overcoming struggles, **to your present self** to reinforce good habits, and **to
your future self** for encouragement.

**What everyone is said to want** `[PRN][D]` Health, wealth, relationships and happiness
(self-actualization). What makes a person unique is the combination of interests used to get
there.

**He changed his position on this, and the corpus records both sides.** Past: he bought the
"niche down" advice, searched the top 100 niches, and targeted dentists and gyms. Now: "niche
down" is "terrible advice for smart people" and "the most profitable niche is you". The tactical
concession he keeps is that you still niche the product and the funnel. Against this, q66 records
that his cheapest foundational course, The One-Person Business Launchpad, teaches beginners how to
find a niche.

**Sources:** q02 (authoritative), q01, q04, q07, q08, q12, q15, q66, q69.

## The Topic Tree

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The method for generating an endless content reservoir out of your own
consumption history, used once "you are the niche" has replaced market selection.

**When to use it** At the start, before writing anything, and again whenever the content well
runs dry.

**The four steps, complete**

1. **Audit your consumption.** YouTube watch history, favourite books, podcasts, creators you
   follow. **"The things you can't shut up about are your starting points."**
2. **Pick 2 to 3 broad interests.** His example set: health, business, psychology.
3. **Break each into sub-topics.** Health breaks into nutrition, bodybuilding, sleep.
4. **Under each sub-topic, list** the specific problems you have faced, the principles you have
   learned, and step-by-step how-tos for overcoming them.

**Numbers** `[NUM][D]` 2 to 3 broad interests. q15 gives the same count as "two or three broad
interests" with the example set fitness, online business and spirituality. q12's Domain of Mastery
framework gives a stricter version of the same instruction: **three core interests, one that makes
money, one that excites you, and one development or philosophy-based**, each broken down into
principles, topics, mentors, connections and real-world problems.

**Sources:** q02 (authoritative), q12, q15.

## Brand Coherence — one goal, many interests

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` The two rules that stop a personal brand built on multiple interests
from reading as random.

**Rule 1: frame everything through a singular overarching goal.** Your brand is the ultimate
transformation or vision you are leading people toward.

**The worked example, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` If your overarching brand goal is "Financial Freedom"
and you want to talk about basket weaving, you explain how the mental clarity and creativity
gained from basket weaving helps you focus better on your business and so reach Financial Freedom.
His line: **"Your interests are just the unique vehicles used to arrive at a shared destination."**

**Rule 2: niche down your products, not your brand.** The personal brand is a city within social
media. You cast a wide net with top-of-funnel content by talking broadly about your life,
philosophy and multiple interests, then direct people to specific buildings. Productivity advice,
they buy your niche productivity planner. Writing advice, they buy your niche writing course.

**The city metaphor is used twice for two different conclusions.** q02 uses it for this rule
(broad brand, narrow products). q09 uses it for the product ladder (social media is the public
square; free guides, courses and high-ticket services are the individual buildings people enter
based on their problems and financial readiness).

**Sources:** q02 (authoritative), q09, q15.

## Zero to $1M — the Three-Stage Progression (q01)

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` The revenue road as q01 states it, in three stages, each with its
offer type, its revenue band and its full time allocation.

**When to use it** For sequencing a solo business. q03 gives the same road cut into four phases
with follower thresholds attached; both are recorded, and the corpus does not say which
supersedes.

**Stage 1 — Client Work** `[TAC][D]` Sell a high-ticket service (freelancing, coaching or
consulting) directly in your DMs while beginning to write content to build an audience.

- Price: **$1,000 to $10,000 per client** `[NUM][D]`
- Volume: **2 to 3 clients a month** to hit **$100,000 a year**, which replaces a typical job
- Time allocation, complete: **1 hour a day prospecting · 3 to 5 hours a week on sales calls ·
  2 to 4 hours a week on client fulfillment · 1 hour a day writing content** `[NUM][D]`

**Stage 2 — Audience and Hybrid Model** `[TAC][D]` Because you can only take on so many 1-on-1
clients inside a 4-hour workday, you use the growing audience to pivot the offer into a hybrid
model: a group coaching program, a cohort, or a guided tutorial.

- Revenue: **$300,000 to $500,000 a year** at a slightly lower price point with more customers
  at once `[NUM][D]`
- Fulfillment time drops to **1 to 2 hours a week** `[NUM][D]`

**Stage 3 — Productization and Scale** `[TAC][D]` Rely entirely on the distribution network and
convert the proven coaching system into a standalone digital product requiring zero manual
fulfillment. Run cohort launches **four times a year** for revenue spikes.

- Revenue: **$1 million+ a year** `[NUM][D]`
- Time allocation, complete: still about 4 hours a day, broken into **1 to 2 hours a week
  fulfilling a cohort · 2 hours a day writing content for growth · 1 to 2 hours a day building
  new projects** `[NUM][D]`

**Sources:** q01 (authoritative for stage time allocations), q03.

## Zero to Business — the Four-Phase Path (q03)

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` The same road as the three-stage progression, cut into four phases,
with follower thresholds and a timeframe attached to each. q03 is the only file that gives the
follower numbers.

**When to use it** When someone wants to know what to build at their current audience size.

**Phase 1 — Minimum Viable Offer and Client Work**

- **What to build first** `[TAC][D]` A **Micro Offer**: a consulting, coaching or tutoring service
  structured as a pack of **4 weekly one-on-one calls**. Alternatively a single-skill freelance
  service such as web design or email marketing.
- **Infrastructure** `[TAC][D]` No website and no landing page. A social media profile acting as a
  digital storefront, plus a simple intake questionnaire (a Google Form).
- **Acquisition** `[NUM][D]` Write **2 to 3 short posts a day** and manually send **30 DMs a week**
  to people who engage with your content or share your interests.
- **Price** `[NUM][D]` Start at **$500 to $1,000** for the 4-call pack, raising to **$2,500 or
  $5,000** as you get results.
- **Deadline** `[NUM][D]` A strict **60-day timeframe to make your first $1,000**.
- **Grounded milestone** `[NUM][G]` At **500 followers** he had a **$3,000 month**. Confound above.
- **Benchmark** `[NUM][D]` By **10,000 followers** you should be generating around **$10,000 a
  month ($100,000 a year)**, because you only need 2 to 3 clients a month. Stated prescriptively
  ("you should be"), so it is a benchmark he sets rather than a reported result.

**Phase 2 — Hybrid Model and Deepening Authority**

- **What to build second** `[TAC][D]` A hybrid model: take the step-by-step curriculum perfected in
  1-on-1 calls and turn it into a group coaching setting, a guided tutorial or a cohort-based
  program. Also start a **weekly newsletter** to build deep trust and authority.
- **Acquisition** `[TAC][D]` Wean off manual DMs. Daily writing and the newsletter attract leads to
  higher-ticket group offers.
- **Range** `[NUM][D]` **10,000 to 50,000 followers**, earning potential **$300,000 to $500,000 a
  year**, at the same working hours.
- **The failure mode** `[WRN][D]` Creators who never build a hybrid model get trapped in another
  9-to-5, hovering **between 10,000 and 30,000 followers**, constantly stressed about losing their
  1-on-1 clients.
- **Grounded milestone** `[NUM][G]` At **50,000 followers** he had a **$50,000 month**.

**Phase 3 — Productization and Scale**

- **What to build third** `[TAC][D]` A standalone digital product built from the frameworks and
  client results, plus the cohort program run **four times a year** for revenue spikes.
- **Promotion** `[TAC][D]` Automate it. Product links at the bottom of weekly newsletters and in
  social threads. Begin networking with creators at **100,000 to 1,000,000+ followers** to trade
  value and cross-pollinate audiences.
- **Range** `[NUM][D]` **80,000 to 100,000+ followers**; a digital product can push income past
  **$1,000,000 a year**.
- **Grounded milestones** `[NUM][G]` At **100,000 followers** he hit **$100,000 a month**. At
  **200,000 followers** a Black Friday launch generated a **$200,000 month**.

**Phase 4 — Escape Velocity**

- `[NUM][D]` A large audience plus a cash-flowing digital product plus **2 to 4 hours a day** on
  maintenance and content equals distribution achieved. Then build whatever you want: fund and
  launch software startups, write physical books, build physical e-commerce lines, or step back
  into early retirement.

**Timeframe to $1 million** `[NUM][D]` **3 to 5 years** of consistent writing, iteration and
audience building.

**His own audience size, and the conflict.** q03 states **2.8 million followers** at the time of
the source. q65 states **3.8 million to 4 million followers** across all platforms. Both are
printed; the corpus carries no dates, so neither can be called the later figure.

**Sources:** q03 (authoritative for follower thresholds), q01, q13, q65.

## The Micro Offer (Minimum Viable Offer)

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The first thing a beginner sells: a 1-on-1 coaching, consulting or
tutoring service structured as a pack of **4 weekly calls**, priced **$500 to $1,000** for the
package, scaling later to **$2,500, $5,000 or $10,000 per client**.

**When to use it** At zero audience, before any product exists, and again as the live-validation
route for any product you intend to build later.

**How it is sold** `[TAC][D]` Directly in DMs, to people who engage with your content or share
your interests, at **30 DMs a week**. No website, no landing page, no funnel. A social profile as
the storefront and a Google Form as intake.

**What it is for beyond the money** `[TAC][D]` You walk clients through your curriculum live, note
where they get stuck, give them worksheets, and help them get results. This collects testimonials
and refines the system before it becomes a standalone product. You are paid to learn exactly what
customers need.

**Provenance note** `[I]` The name riffs on lean-startup's minimum viable product. The corpus never
says so, so this is an inference and not a stated source.

**Sources:** q03, q07, q08, q09, q13, q65.

## The Value Equation (Purpose, Path, Priority) — Koe's, not Alex Hormozi's

**Whose it is.** Two well-known frameworks in the same creator-business market share this name and
share almost no content. **This section is Dan Koe's Value Equation: Purpose, Path, Priority.**
Alex Hormozi's Value Equation is a different composition (Dream Outcome, Perceived Likelihood of
Achievement, Time Delay, Effort and Sacrifice) and it is not what Koe means. Neither q67 nor q68
mentions Hormozi anywhere in the corpus, so the corpus does not say whether Koe arrived at the name
independently or simply does not address the collision. Never present "the Value Equation" from this
corpus without saying whose.

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` His universal framework for understanding human behaviour, crafting
persuasive marketing and building products: the psychological anatomy of why people change
behaviour and why they buy. q68 does not list it among the coinages with acknowledged sources, so
`[D]` stands. q67 does record that **Eugene Schwartz's** five levels of customer awareness
(unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware) and **Anthony De Mello's**
*Awareness* are integrated into it.

**When to use it** To decide what to sell, and to structure any piece of persuasive writing.

**The three parts, complete** `[FRM][D]`

| Part | His gloss | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The Goal / Transformation | The desirable end result someone is trying to achieve. A compelling reason to move toward a better future, the light at the end of the tunnel that reveals itself once a person becomes aware of a painful problem. |
| Priority | The Burning Problem | The burning problem or limitation a person faces, seen through what they currently deem important. In his words, "a problem to one person might not register as a problem to another depending on their identity and level of awareness." To capture attention you must speak to a problem that is currently a priority for them. |
| Path | The System / Solution | The step-by-step process or framework bridging Priority to Purpose. Someone aware of a problem who also wants a goal experiences overwhelm; the Path supplies the clarity needed to act. |

**The verbatim statement** `[TPL][D]` **"people buying a product is human behavior... you need a
problem, you need a goal, and you need to bounce between the two. Your product is the path that
helps them get from one to another."**

**The mapping onto the business** `[FRM][D]` Your **Brand is your Purpose** (the overarching
vision). Your **Content is your Priorities** (the roadblocks, pain points and burning problems in
the way). Your **Product is your Path** (the clear system that solves them).

**The three-step method for deciding what to sell** `[TAC][D]`

1. **Identify a Priority.** What burning problem in an Eternal Market have you personally faced and
   overcome? Because you lived through it, you understand the psychology and identity of the
   sufferer, who is your past self.
2. **Define the Purpose.** What meaningful goal or outcome did you achieve? This becomes the
   marketing promise.
3. **Package the Path.** What exact steps bridged the gap? What did you need to learn, practise and
   implement daily? Refine them, put a compelling unique-mechanism name on them, and sell that
   system.

**The three worked examples, verbatim** `[TPL][D]`

- "If your Priority was a lack of focus and your Purpose was deep work, your Path is the workflow
  planner you created to stay on track. **Sell the planner.**"
- "If your Priority was being out of shape and your Purpose was getting fit, your Path is the
  specific workout and diet system you used. **Sell the fitness program.**"
- "If your Priority was feeling spiritually disconnected and your Purpose was peace of mind, your
  Path is the meditation routine you built. **Sell the meditation program.**"

**Purpose-identification prompts, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` "did you unlock extreme focus? Did you
build a side business? Did you get a six-pack without doing cardio?"

**The warning attached** `[WRN][D]` Do not look outward for a starving market to exploit. Look
inward and reverse-engineer your own life experiences.

**Sources:** q04 (authoritative), q67, q68.

## The Trust Matrix

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` Previously called the social matrix. Three interconnected pillars,
**Growth, Authenticity and Authority**, that have to be balanced to build a brand people cannot
help but trust. Premise as transcribed: **"trust money is a measure of trust"** (the corpus's
transcription is garbled; the intended line is almost certainly "money is a measure of trust").

**Provenance** `[D]` confirmed. q68 records that he claims the original model as his own without
citing an external creator, and acknowledges spin-offs of it made by others since.

**When to use it** To diagnose which of the three a brand is short on, and to set the content mix
for the current stage.

**Pillar 1 — Growth (attracting people)** `[FRM][D]` Doing what works to capture attention and
generate an influx of traffic. Fuelled primarily by short-form content.

- **The idea-to-execution muscle** `[TAC][D]` Pay attention to ideas sitting at the intersection of
  *performance* (potential to do well) and *excitement* (genuine interest in understanding or
  articulating it).
- **Validated Content as training wheels** `[TAC][D]` Find ideas already proven to stop the scroll,
  take them, and make them your own by experimenting until growth is consistent.
- Verbatim `[TPL][D]` Growth becomes a natural byproduct **"when you practice writing in a way that
  stops the scroll and nearly demands readers to absorb the idea."**

**Pillar 2 — Authenticity (expressing core beliefs)** `[FRM][D]` Expressing your core beliefs and
values to attract the right people. Growth gets people to look at your ideas; authenticity gets
them to stay. Fuelled primarily by long-form content and big-idea synthesis.

- Verbatim `[TPL][D]` **"people don't follow ideas they follow people who share ideas."**
- **Time Under Attention** `[TAC][D+M:Time-Under-Tension]` Create long-form content (articles,
  newsletters, books, videos). The more time people spend with your deep philosophy, the more they
  engage with even your simplest posts, because they read those posts through your established
  worldview. q68 records that he explicitly acknowledges the term as a spin-off of the bodybuilding
  concept "time under tension" while claiming the labelling: "I finally labeled this concept I
  created this concept I'm so I'm proud of this." The source is a training concept rather than a
  person. What the rename adds is transposing a physiological dose-response idea onto attention as
  a brand-authority metric.
- **Alignment of values** `[TAC][D]` Show people who you are, where you came from and what you stand
  for.
- **Authentic polarization, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` **"if you're liked by everybody you're liked by
  nobody you need to give people reasons to heavily disagree and thus heavily agree with you."**

**Pillar 3 — Authority (displaying expertise)** `[FRM][D]` Demonstrating expertise, competence and
the ability to get results. Authenticity makes people like you; authority is what convinces them to
buy. Rests on the claim that persuasive education changes behaviour.

- **Three authority-building actions** `[TAC][D]` Generate product or service results for yourself
  and for clients · teach through a new lens, reframing an already-taught topic through your own
  story and core beliefs · identify pain points and desired outcomes, because authority is built
  when you can articulate someone's problem better than they can.

**On the overlap, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` **"of course these all overlap each other right don't don't
get narrow viewed into one of those that I just listed they all overlap it's just priority."**

**The stage weighting** `[NUM][D]`

| Stage | Weighting |
|---|---|
| 1 · beginner | Roughly **80%** of content entirely on the Growth axis (short-form), because without traffic the other two pillars do not matter |
| 2 · intermediate | Once you have an audience and a service, shift priority toward Authority and Authenticity with long-form content that has depth |
| 3 · advanced | Double down on your best ideas until they become a **"mental monopoly"**, where your ideas live rent-free in your audience's heads, producing authenticity and authority at scale |

**The pivot rule, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` **"if you aren't growing pivot if you aren't making sales
pivot if you aren't gaining loyal fans pivot iterate as you get more real world feedback."**

**Sources:** q05 (authoritative), q68, q14, q15.

## Leverage — labor, capital, code and media

**What it is** `[CIT][M:Naval]` The three forms of leverage, credited to **Naval Ravikant** by name
in q06: **labor, capital, and products with no marginal cost of replication (code and media)**. q06
is the cleanest example of correct citation in the corpus.

**When to use it** To decide where a solo operator's effort goes, and to argue against hiring.

**The three forms, with his verdict on each**

| Form | Naval's category | Koe's verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Labor (people) | Hiring employees, the oldest form | `[D+M:Naval]` Outdated for the solo creator: management overhead, complexity, and highly susceptible to replacement by AI |
| Capital (money) | Using money to make more money through investment | `[M:Naval]` Requires you to already possess wealth or something worth investing in |
| Code and media | Products with zero marginal cost of replication | `[CIT][M:Jack Butcher]` The highest and most powerful form, embodying **Jack Butcher's** "build once, sell twice", credited to Butcher by name |

**The application** `[TAC][D+M:Naval]` Solo creators should ignore labor and capital entirely at the
start and focus on media (content) and digital products (courses, ebooks, templates).

**His own ordering on top of Naval's** `[PRN][D]` **Prioritize media over code.** Writing content
builds an audience and therefore distribution; building software without marketing skills leaves you
with no users.

**Numbers** `[NUM][D]` A digital product carries no manufacturing or shipping cost and can be sold to
**one million people as easily as to one**. Digital leverage lets creators earn millions while
working **two to four hours a day**.

**Sources:** q06 (authoritative), q01, q12, q67.

## Specific Knowledge

**What it is** `[CIT][M:Naval]` Credited to Naval Ravikant by name in q06: **knowledge you cannot be
trained for**. If society or a school curriculum can train you to do a job, they can train someone
else to replace you.

**When to use it** As the answer to "what do I actually have that is defensible", and as his stated
defence against AI.

**How to acquire it** `[TAC][D+M:Naval]` Treat your life as a science project. Encounter your own
problems, research multi-disciplinary interests, test solutions through self-experimentation, and
synthesize the findings into your own systems. The self-experimentation protocol is his; the concept
is Naval's.

**The AI argument** `[PRN][D+M:Naval]` Specific knowledge is the defence against AI, because AI is
trained on generalized historical data and does not have your lived experience, your combination of
interests or your taste. Building a business out of nuanced specific knowledge creates a **"personal
monopoly"** that neither a human nor a machine can easily copy. The AI-defence extension is his; the
concept is Naval's.

**Adjacent** `[FRM][D]` The same argument appears in q15 as becoming a **"specialized generalist"** or
**"modern renaissance man"** by combining two or three broad interests into an intersection that
specialists cannot replicate. No source is given there, and Pass 2 notes the specialized-generalist
idea is widely circulated inside this corpus's own citation family.

**Sources:** q06 (authoritative), q15, q67.

## Permissionless Leverage and the Illusion of Market Saturation

**What it is** `[CIT][M:Naval]` He uses Naval Ravikant's exact phrase, **"permissionless leverage"**,
for code and media.

**The argument** `[PRN][D]` Historically individuals needed permission from gatekeepers: publishers to
print a book, television networks to broadcast an idea, employers to assign a job, venture capitalists
to fund a product. The internet destroyed that. Today you do not need permission to identify a
problem, build a solution and distribute it, reaching a global audience with an internet connection
and zero startup capital.

**The one downside he names** `[WRN][D]` The barrier to entry is so low that everyone looks like they
are doing the same thing, which creates **the illusion of market saturation**. His answer is to avoid
acting like a generic templated influencer and to lean on specific knowledge.

**Provenance** Pass 2 upgrades permissionless work to `[D+M:Naval]` alongside digital leverage,
specific knowledge and zero marginal cost. He credits Naval by name here in q06 and does not in q01
or q12, where the same concepts do the same work.

**Sources:** q06 (authoritative), q01, q12, q67.

## The Revenue Confound, stated once in full

**Why every figure in this file carries it.** This corpus has the largest grounded layer of the
three in its family (182 grounded signals, 13% of the total) and the most confounded. Every
revenue, launch and audience number is his own, reported by him, produced inside a business whose
product is the teaching of this method.

**The specific case that makes it concrete.** His largest single reported launch was The 2-Hour
Writer, a writing product, sold to people who want to write, and q66 records that he built it
because he knew how profitable the writing niche was. That sits directly against "you are the
niche" and "follow the problem you cannot pull away from".

**The rule for any use of these numbers.** The revenue is real, and it is evidence that a large
audience will pay for this instruction. It is not evidence that a reader following the instruction
reaches those numbers. **No figure in this corpus comes from a student**, and the corpus contains
no failed adopter.

**What the corpus never reports** `[A]` No cost side. No ad spend beyond a single $100 example, no
payroll, no refund rate, no churn rate. "Taking home about 90%" (q65) is the only margin figure for
his own business, against the "95%+ profit margins" he teaches as the model's property (q01, q07,
q09).

**Sources:** q65, q66, Pass 2 §2.

## The Meaning Economy

**What it is** `[FRM][D+M:Schmachtenberger]` The phase he says replaces the "Attention Economy".
Meaning is the scarcest commodity in modern civilization, so the future belongs to "Value Creators"
providing depth, purpose and education rather than cheap entertainment.

**Sources:** q67, q70.

## The Swap Test

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` A test for whether AI can replace you. If a creator and their creation
could be swapped for another generic piece of content without losing value, AI can replace it. If
the value is tied to the creator's own perspective, story and energy, it cannot.

**Sources:** q70.
