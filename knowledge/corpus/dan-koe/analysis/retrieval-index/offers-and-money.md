## The Micro Education Business

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` Turning your life experiences, solved problems and interests into a
decentralized school. You act as a value creator who documents the journey, distils the solutions
into a replicable step-by-step system, and sells it to people one or two steps behind you.

**When to use it** As the container for every product below. It is the business form the whole
ladder sits inside.

**Create your own customers** `[PRN][D]` Instead of finding a traditional starving market, free
social media content educates people about problems they did not realize they had and outcomes they
desire, moving them gradually to a level of awareness where they are ready to buy. Provenance note:
this is Eugene Schwartz's customer-awareness machinery, which q67 records as a source, and it is
unattributed at this point in the corpus.

**Margin** `[NUM][D]` Because it deals in information rather than physical goods, the model runs
profit margins **upwards of 95%** with low overhead. The same 95%+ figure appears in q01 and q09.
q65 gives a different figure for his own business, "taking home about 90%", and the corpus never
reconciles the two.

**The three product classes, complete** `[FRM][D]`

1. **Free Lead Magnets** — checklists, beginner guides, templates.
2. **Minimum Viable Offers (MVO)** — 1-on-1 coaching, consulting, digital tutoring.
3. **Digital Products** — e-books, asynchronous or evergreen courses, cohort-based programs, paid
   communities.

Products are designed to spark positive behaviour change.

**The warning on communities, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` **"monthly recurring revenue equals monthly
recurring work."** Because you must constantly engage and retain subscribers, a community is harder
to sell and can trap you in a high-maintenance second 9-to-5. The line appears verbatim in q07 and
q09.

**Sources:** q07 (authoritative), q01, q09, q65, q67.

## The Product Ladder — all six tiers with every price

**What it is** `[FRM][D+M:Wilber]` The product ecosystem, designed as an **"actualization
hierarchy"**: cast a wide net with broad free content and funnel the audience into increasingly
specific and valuable products. q68 records that the Actualization Hierarchy explicitly credits
**Ken Wilber's** holons and natural hierarchies as its structural foundation, framed as the opposite
of a dominator hierarchy. What the rename adds is applying a developmental-metaphysics structure to a
sales funnel. Pass 2 flags a second possibility: "actualization hierarchy" may be a straight relay
rather than a coinage, since Wilber pairs actualization against dominator hierarchy himself.

**When to use it** To place any offer at a price, and to plan what to build next.

**The city metaphor** `[PRN][D]` The personal brand is a city. Social media is the public square.
Free guides, courses and high-ticket services are the individual buildings people enter based on
their specific problems and financial readiness.

**The six tiers, complete, with the products he names at each**

| Tier | Name | Price | What sits there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free Lead Magnets | **$0** | Checklists, Notion templates, beginner video tutorials, and his own named assets the **Power Planner** and the **7 Days to Genius Ideas** challenge |
| 2 | Low-Ticket Products | **$27 – $50** | E-books or physical books, including his book **The Art of Focus at around $28** |
| 3 | Paid Communities | **$5 – $27 per month** | His **Modern Mastery** community: **$5 for the first month, then rebilling at $27 a month** |
| 4 | Evergreen Courses | **$150** | Module-based digital systems paired with actionable tools. $150 matches his flagship **2-Hour Writer** and **Solopreneur Sprints** |
| 5 | Cohort-Based Courses | **$399+** | A **4-to-8 week** program with live group calls and a community, opening only a few times a year |
| 6 | High-Ticket / Minimum Viable Offer | **$500 – $10,000** | 1-on-1 coaching, direct freelancing, digital tutoring. Beginners start at **4 weekly calls for $500 to $1,000**, scaling to **$2,500, $5,000 or even $10,000 per client** |

**Tier 1, what it does** `[NUM][D]` The top-of-funnel net. Establishes initial authority on a single
topic and bridges social followers onto an email list, beyond algorithm risk.

**Tier 2, what it does** `[PRN][D]` Entry-level impulse buys solving one highly specific problem, his
recurring worked example being **"how to focus 12 hours a day"**. They act as **"trust tripwires"**.
Because **"buyers buy again"**, getting someone to spend a small amount builds deep trust and
significantly increases the likelihood of a premium course purchase later.

**Tier 3, the caution** `[WRN][D]` A heavy operational lift: "monthly recurring revenue equals monthly
recurring work."

**Tier 4, what it does** `[NUM][D]` The core scalable asset. It expands the $27 e-book into a complete
system that sells passively 24/7 at **95%+ profit margins**, decoupling income from time.

**Tier 5, what it does** `[NUM][D]` Opening enrollment only a few times a year generates scarcity and
high perceived value, producing revenue spikes.

**Tier 6, what it does** `[TAC][D]` The validation tool. Beginners use it for immediate cash flow while
they still lack the audience needed to sell hundreds of $150 courses, and they are paid to test the
curriculum live.

**The ladder is a description of his own catalogue generalized into a rule.** Two of the price anchors
are self-referential: the $150 evergreen tier is defined by his own 2-Hour Writer and Solopreneur
Sprints prices, and the $27–$50 low-ticket tier by The Art of Focus at around $28.

**Sources:** q09 (authoritative, the only file with the community tier, the low-ticket band and the
named products at each price), q07, q08, q01, q03, q65, q66, q68.

## Reverse the Ladder — the order you actually build in

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The build order runs backwards from the price ladder's display order. You do
not build the $150 course first.

**When to use it** At the point where someone with no audience is deciding what to make.

**The three steps, complete**

1. **Start at the top.** Sell the **$1,000 high-ticket service** in your DMs to validate the ideas.
2. **Productize once proven.** After the system gets results and the audience has grown, convert that
   exact service into the **$150 evergreen course**, which drastically cuts the manual labour to
   fulfill it.
3. **Then capture the mass market.** Add **free lead magnets** and **low-ticket e-books** last, which
   creates an automated funnel that educates new followers until they are ready for the higher tiers.

**Sources:** q09 (authoritative), q08, q03.

## The Beginner Price Progression

**What it is** `[NUM][D]` The exact four-step sequence he gives a beginner, distinct from the six-tier
ladder above and stated with prices: **Lead Magnet (Free)** → **E-book ($27)** → **Evergreen Course
($150)** → **Cohort-Based Course ($399+)**.

**When to use it** For someone who wants the shortest possible answer to "what do I sell, in what
order, at what price".

**Per step**

- **Lead magnet** `[TAC][D]` A free download, a basic checklist or a video tutorial, to capture emails,
  build the newsletter and establish authority in one specific topic.
- **E-book at $27** `[TPL][D]` The recurring worked topic, verbatim: **"how to focus 12 hours a day."**
  Quick to make, hard to charge a premium for, good as an entry-level purchase.
- **Evergreen course at $150** `[TAC][D]` Made more valuable by pairing it with actionable tools. His
  named example is a Notion **"Focus Hub"** template, so people can implement.
- **Cohort at $399+** `[NUM][D]` A highly structured **4-to-8 week** curriculum with live group calls
  and a community setting, opening for enrollment about **four times a year**, using scarcity and
  perceived value to create revenue spikes.

**Sources:** q07 (authoritative), q08, q09.

## Digital Product Design — what to define before building anything

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` Four definitions that have to exist before a single module gets built.

**When to use it** Before writing any product content, and before any landing page copy.

**The four, complete**

1. **The Big Problem.** Where are people now, and what happens if they do not change?
2. **The Desired Outcome.** Point A to Point B.
3. **A Believable Timeframe.**
4. **Your Personal System.** Every exact step from A to B.

**Believable timeframes he names** `[NUM][D]` **14 days, 30 days, 6 weeks**. Putting a specific
timeframe on the transformation supercharges the marketing and compresses what you include in the
product.

**The naming move** `[TAC][D]` Test the steps on yourself or on a target persona, refine them, then
**"slap a unique name on the process."** His own worked example of the move is **"The 2-Hour Content
Ecosystem."** Carry the caution with it: Pass 2 records that the 2-Hour Content Ecosystem is a brand
name and not a measurement, since his own itemised budget for it sums to 45 to 90 minutes.

**The ordering rule** `[PRN][D]` **You must create the marketing before you create the product.** Most
people waste months building something in secret that nobody wants to buy.

**Where the product comes from** `[TAC][D]` Identify an area where you have the most experience, a
burning problem you overcame, or a topic you have spent hours studying. You already have a highly
profitable product trapped in your head.

**On the first version** `[PRN][D]` **"Your first product is going to suck, and that is exactly how it
is supposed to be."** Get the first iteration onto the public market so you can gather real-world data,
accept criticism, and make the second version better.

**Sources:** q08 (authoritative), q07, q09, q25.

## Market Research — "sell what's already selling"

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` His stated method for finding a product that will sell, and, elsewhere in the
corpus, his stated best business advice.

**When to use it** After picking the problem, before defining the four product definitions.

**The method, complete**

1. Search your topic on **Udemy, Skillshare, YouTube and Reddit**.
2. Deconstruct the products and courses **you have personally bought, used and benefited from**.
3. Find the intersection of what currently works and what you can do better based on your own
   experience.

**The wider version of the same advice** `[WRN]` Pass 2 records his stated best business advice as
selling what is already selling by buying competitors' courses and reverse-engineering their funnels.

**The contradiction to carry with it.** This sits against the originality and authenticity demanded
everywhere else in the corpus, including "you are the niche". Pass 2 lists it as one of five
philosophy-versus-tactic conflicts, all running the same direction: the philosophy is the
top-of-funnel claim, the tactic is the bottom-of-funnel practice, and the reconciliation is only ever
offered to people already inside. Keep both halves.

**Sources:** q08 (authoritative), Pass 2 §3.4.

## Validation Route 1 — Pre-orders

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` Selling the product before it exists, so the first dollar arrives before the
build.

**When to use it** When you already have some audience. The rule attached is that **you should make
your first dollar before you even start building the product**.

**The steps, complete**

1. Build a simple landing page (he names **Stan Store** as the tool) detailing the problem, the
   outcome and the features.
2. Set a release date **3 to 4 weeks out**.
3. Promote it in your content and newsletters, and open pre-orders.
4. Once you make the first sale the deadline is real, and you build the product before launch day.

**Why it works, in his framing** `[TAC][D+M:Prigogine]` A public deadline plus that first pre-order
sale provides **"tactical stress"**, the pressure a real-world deadline creates. q68 grounds Tactical
Stress in physicist **Ilya Prigogine's** dissipative structures, so at concept level the marker is
compound; the launch application here is his.

**Build times** `[NUM][D]` The landing-page MVP should take **1 day to 2 weeks**. Your first digital
product should take **no longer than a week to build**. On the pre-order route you dedicate intense
focus over the **2 to 3 weeks** before the promised launch date.

**Sources:** q08 (authoritative), q10, q68.

## Validation Route 2 — the Minimum Viable Offer as live validation

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` Selling the system as a 1-on-1 tutoring or consulting service first, for
beginners with no audience, before any product exists.

**When to use it** At zero audience, where pre-orders have nobody to sell to.

**The steps, complete**

1. Sell a pack of **4 weekly 1-on-1 calls** at **$500 to $1,000**, in DMs.
2. Walk your first few clients through the curriculum live over the series of calls.
3. Give them worksheets, and watch where they struggle.
4. Collect testimonials and results.
5. Package the proven lessons into the standalone product.

**The payoff** `[TAC][D]` You get paid to learn exactly what customers need, and the product that comes
out the other side has already been tested on people.

**Later pricing** `[NUM][D]` Raise to **$2,500 or $5,000** as you get results, and eventually to
**$10,000 per client**.

**Sources:** q08 (authoritative for the live-validation mechanics), q07, q09, q03.

## Launch Sequencing — the full runway

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The promotional sequence for any launch, from announcement to launch day.

**When to use it** For every product and every cohort. He also gives a cadence for how often to run it.

**The primary rule** `[PRN][D]` **Sell the product before it even exists.** He strongly advises against
building in secret for months and then launching to crickets.

**The sequence, complete**

| Timing | What happens |
|---|---|
| **3 to 4 weeks out** | Announce the release date, open pre-orders, and start plugging the launch at the bottom of your weekly newsletters and in your social media bios |
| **Through the runway** | Align daily social posts (threads, tweets, carousels) with the product topic and sprinkle in soft calls to action |
| **1 to 2 weeks out** | Drastically increase how often and how hard you promote, and offer an **early-bird discount** specifically for people who pre-order before the official launch |
| **Launch day** | The product ships to the pre-order list |

**The soft CTA, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` **"If you struggle with [pain point], the product goes live on
[date]."**

**Launch cadence** `[NUM][D]` Build and launch a new product or service **every single quarter for 2 to
3 years**, to force continuous evolution and revenue spikes. Cohort programs specifically open **four
times a year**.

**The offer bundle** `[TAC][D]` Pair the core education with implementation tools: Notion templates,
worksheets and daily trackers. That pairing is often what makes the product worth paying for.

**His most elaborate bundle, complete contents** `[BIO][G]` The Art of Focus **"Keepsake Edition" box**:
the physical hardcover book · immediate access to the digital and Kindle version · a physical
productivity notebook called the **Fosi Planner** · a private invitation to an annual **"Future of
Work"** live event with guest speakers, which q66 records he values at **$400 alone** · a personal
signature in the book. The edition was capped at **2,000 copies**.

**Sources:** q10 (authoritative), q08, q11, q66.

## Scarcity Mechanics He Actually Uses

**What it is** `[TAC][G]` Three real-constraint mechanics, used in place of the fabricated urgency he
rejects.

**When to use it** In the last two weeks of a launch runway, and on any cohort.

**The three, complete, with the specific instances he names**

1. **Hard inventory caps.** A strict limit of exactly **2,000 copies** of the Art of Focus Keepsake box
   before pulling it from the market and launching a standard paperback. Once sold out it was cut off
   permanently.
2. **Scheduled price increases, promoted as launch events.** He heavily promoted that the price of
   **Digital Economics** was **doubling on May 15th**, and that the price of **Mental Monetization** was
   **increasing on June 3rd**.
3. **Strict enrollment windows** on cohort courses, named as **Solopreneur Sprints** and **Cortex
   University**, with a firm closing date to push fence-sitters to decide.

**What he rejects in the same breath** `[WRN][D]` Fake countdown timers and fabricated scarcity, which
he calls sleazy.

**The contradiction, kept rather than smoothed.** q11 has him rejecting fabricated scarcity in one
paragraph and running hard caps, announced price doublings and closing windows in the next. His own
resolution is that his caps are real: the 2,000 copies actually stopped. The distinction he draws is
truthfulness, and not absence of pressure. Pass 2 lists this as one of five philosophy-versus-tactic
conflicts.

**Sources:** q10 and q11 (near-verbatim in both; treat as one block sourced from both), q66.

## The $0.50 to $1.00 Per Follower Per Month Rule

**What it is** `[NUM][D]` His stated rule of thumb: with the right product ecosystem and distribution
you should average roughly **$0.50 to $1.00 per follower per month** during your first few years.

**When to use it** As a rough revenue expectation at a given audience size, with the artifact below
attached every time.

**The five worked examples he gives** `[NUM][G]`

| Followers | Monthly revenue as stated | Dollars per follower |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | $3,000 | $6.00 |
| 10,000 | $10,000 | $1.00 |
| 50,000 | $50,000 | $1.00 |
| 100,000 | $100,000 | $1.00 |
| 200,000 | $200,000 | $1.00 |

**The artifact, and it must travel with the rule.** His stated range tops out at $1.00, and every
worked example above 500 followers sits at exactly $1.00, the ceiling of his own range. The 500-follower
example sits far above the ceiling. Nothing in the corpus illustrates the $0.50 half of the range. Pass 2
records this as a survivorship artifact and Layer 3 carries it as one. The clean 1-to-1 ratio of
followers to monthly dollars reads as a rounded heuristic presented as observed history, and q65 flags
it as the most quotable and least verifiable number in the corpus.

**The confound** These are his own figures, from a business that sells the teaching of this method. No
figure here comes from a student.

**Perishability** `[A]` Nothing in the corpus is dated, and this is one of four perishable
platform-and-market claims in these lanes (the others: 30 DMs a week, 2 to 3 posts a day, and "market
sophistication has risen"). There is no way to say what audience conditions the rule was measured under.

**Sources:** q10, q13, q16, q65.

## The $1 Million a Year Math

**What it is** `[NUM][D]` The arithmetic he gives for reaching $1 million a year from a single evergreen
product, stated as a chain.

**The chain, complete, as stated**

- $1,000,000 a year is **$2,777 a day**
- A **$150** evergreen digital product therefore needs **18 sales a day**
- At a **2.5%** landing-page conversion rate, that needs **720 visitors to the page per day**

**The timeframe** `[NUM][D]` 720 clicks a day is achievable over **3 to 5 years** by writing daily,
building the email list and networking for shares.

**The matching conversion target elsewhere** `[NUM][D]` q16 gives **2.5% or higher** as the baseline
landing-page conversion target for the newsletter funnel, which is the same figure used here.

**Sources:** q13 (authoritative), q16.

## Sales and Persuasion Principles

**What it is** `[PRN][D]` His stance on selling: persuasion as applied psychology for mutual value
exchange, structuring communication to help someone overcome a problem, rather than as manipulation.

**When to use it** On any sales conversation, and as the argument for why the content does the selling.

**The core mechanic** `[NUM][D]` **90% of selling should be done before a conversation even starts.** By
addressing common limiting beliefs and roadblocks in daily posts, a prospect has essentially already
been through **"four sales calls with you"** by the time they reach out.

**His own reported sales behaviour** `[BIO][G]` He admits he is **"fairly bad at sales"** in the
traditional sense, because he does not study aggressive objection handling. If a prospect says they need
to talk to their wife or wait a week, he does not push back or try to save the sale. He says **"yeah no
problem"** and lets them go.

**Beginners without testimonials** `[TAC][D]` Rely on your own personal results, statistics and industry
anecdotes to prove your points.

**On testimonials** `[PRN][D]` **Testimonials from other creators are "appreciating assets."** A review
from a peer who later grows to millions of followers becomes exponentially more valuable over time.

**Sources:** q11 (authoritative).

## Risk Reversal and the Conditional Guarantee

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` **Risk reversal is a "micronutrient of value"**, designed to reduce perceived
friction and uncertainty so buyers feel safe pulling the trigger.

**When to use it** On the offer page, at the point of purchase.

**The conditional guarantee, complete** `[TAC][D]` Refund their money, **but only if** they can prove they
executed every step of your program and still failed. This creates a win-win that puts pressure on you to
deliver a great product and on them to actually do the work.

**The instance he reports** `[NUM][G]` At the **Modern Mastery** launch he offered an **"outrageous fifty
thousand dollar guarantee" ($50,000)** because he was that confident in the systems. His own figure, with
the standing confound attached.

**Provenance note** Risk reversal, social proof, the pattern interrupt, PAS and PASTOR are all
`[M:copywriting-tradition]` per Pass 2. They are presented in this corpus as his own or as neutral fact.
Eugene Schwartz is named in q67's citation map for the awareness ladder, and not in the files where the
material is taught.

**Sources:** q11 (authoritative), q20, q22, q67, Pass 2 §1.4.

## Social Proof and the Humble Flex

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` Social proof bridges what he calls the information gap. The primary rule is the
**"humble flex"**: instead of bragging like a stereotypical internet marketer, share massive milestones as
casual, relatable learning moments, which builds authority without looking cocky.

**When to use it** Whenever a result is worth reporting publicly.

**The milestone he uses as the worked example** `[NUM][G]` Making **$468,000 in a month**. His own figure,
with the standing confound attached.

**Sources:** q11 (authoritative).

## What He Rejects in Marketing

**What it is** `[WRN][D]` The named list of tactics he refuses, and the reason he gives for refusing them.

**The list, complete** Complex funnels · fake countdown timers · exaggerated income promises · rented
Lamborghinis · fabricated scarcity · aggressive objection handling.

**The reason he gives is strategic rather than moral** `[PRN][D]` **Market sophistication has risen.**
Consumers are exhausted by ridiculous claims and immediately recognize old-school tactics as scams.
Because the barrier to entry for information products is so low, the market is flooded with average
creators making massive promises, so surviving means doubling down on trust, simplicity and individuality
rather than gimmicks.

**The contrast example, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` Instead of writing a hook that promises **"how to make a
million dollars in 3 days,"** you provide a believable goal, state what you are actually doing, and offer
a clear, step-by-step path to help others achieve it.

**The contradiction to carry.** The same file records the three scarcity mechanics he does run: the
2,000-copy cap, the announced price doublings on Digital Economics and Mental Monetization, and the
closing enrollment windows. His defence is that his caps are real.

**Sources:** q11 (authoritative), q10.

creators wanting to monetize digital work.

**Micro-offer launches, the numbers** `[NUM][D]` Charge a low price for **one to two calls**, and launch

Minimum Viable Offer at $500 to $1,000.

## Digital Economics (the course)

**What it is** `[BIO][G]` His more expensive, comprehensive masterclass, available as a cohort or as a
self-paced course, teaching the complete architecture of the one-person business: branding, content,
marketing, product creation and automated promotions. q66 calls it a Master's-tier course and records that
buying it includes The 2-Hour Writer. Audience: serious creators who want to fully productize themselves.

**Price** `[A]` **No price anywhere in the corpus.** q66 records only "expensive, price frequently
increases", and q10 records that he promoted its price **doubling on May 15th**. The base number is absent.

## Productizing Your Mind

**What it is** `[PRN][D]` The shift from monetizing your *time* (hours for an hourly wage or a freelance
fee) to monetizing your *intelligence and experience*: extracting the aggregated knowledge, specific skills
and systems you developed to solve your own problems, and packaging them into a digital asset.

**The economics underneath it** `[PRN][D+M:Naval]` Digital products made of code and media have **zero
marginal cost of replication**. You build the system once and it sells infinitely. The no-marginal-cost
formulation is Naval Ravikant's, credited by name in q06 and uncredited here in q12.
