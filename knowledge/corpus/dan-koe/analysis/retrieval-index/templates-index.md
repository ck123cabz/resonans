# Templates Index — Dan Koe

Every verbatim artifact Pass 1 marked `[TPL]`, reproduced word for word: hooks, example lines,
scripts, AI prompts, structural specs and before/after pairs.

In the `rian-doris` corpus the thing most easily lost was the exact numbers. In this one it is the
exact lines. His voice is the product being taught, so a writing framework retrieved without its
examples cannot be applied. A paraphrased hook is useless.

## How to read an entry

Each entry is numbered `T1`–`T125`. The verbatim text sits in a blockquote or code fence and is
never edited, never cleaned, never rounded. Below it: what it illustrates, the q-file it came from,
and its Pass 1 provenance marker.

Nine entries carry two Pass 1 signals because the same line appears in two q-files. Both files are
named on the entry. 125 entries × the 9 doubles = the full 134. The ledger at the bottom maps it.

Provenance markers used here:

| Marker | Meaning in this file |
|---|---|
| `[D]` | His own line, no acknowledged source underneath |
| `[G]` | The line states one of his own reported outcomes |
| `[D+M:name]` | His phrasing over someone else's model |
| `[M:name]` | Someone else's line or term, relayed |

## Warning before you quote from this file

This corpus teaches persuasive patterns the repo's own writing filter bans: em dashes as the
default separator, three-item lists for rhythm, absolutes and exaggeration as conviction, and the
"not X, Y" exclusion hook. Every line below is quotation. Quote him as him. Do not let the cadence
into our own prose.

## Structural templates — the newsletter

Target length 1,000 to 1,500 words, one per week. The section order below is q27's, given in prose
rather than as a quoted spec, and reproduced here as structure rather than as verbatim text.

```
1a  The Big Idea      one sentence, opens the piece
1b  The Problem       a few sentences, a relatable pain point
1c  The Amplification a vivid picture of the impact, carried by a personal story
      → 1a + 1b + 1c together are the first two paragraphs, roughly five lines
2   The Context       why overcoming it is crucial, plus the benefits of solving it
3a  The Setup         a headline stating what the solution is
3b  The Steps         Step 1, Step 2, Step 3
3c  The Execution     under each step's sub-headline, 1-2 paragraphs of what, why, how
4   The Promotion     at the very bottom: product, service, or free lead magnet
```

Source: q27 (authoritative) · merges q25, q29 · Provenance: `[D]` on the structure,
`[D+M:copywriting-tradition]` on the bPAS skeleton underneath

The alternate newsletter framework named alongside bPAS is Pain and Process (T55, T56).
The Cross-Domain Synthesis newsletter structure runs problem → a concept from a different
discipline → your own step-by-step process, and its worked examples are at T67–T69.

## Structural templates — the thread

Maximum 10 to 15 tweets, which is also what makes a thread convert one-to-one into a 10-to-15 slide
carousel.

```
1  The Hook                     scroll-stopping opener; if it fails the rest goes unread
2  The Lead (Problem)           introduce and agitate
3  The Body (Process)           step-by-step, separated by numbers or sub-headlines
4  The Context / Objections     answer the limiting beliefs about the steps just given
5  The Conclusion & CTA         summarise, then a clear directive

then, as the ending:
   The Punchline   the last content tweet, tying the thought into a memorable realisation
   The Recap       a TL;DR of the steps, so people can comment without scrolling back
   The Promotion   the final reply: newsletter, lead magnet, or paid product
```

Source: q28 · Provenance: `[D]`

**The six hook building blocks**, pieced together in the most potent combination: 1 Big Problem ·
2 Big Benefit · 3 Big Idea · 4 Transformation Process · 5 Time Frame · 6 Negative Personal
Experience. Balanced against three constraints: **relevance** (what is in it for the reader),
**awareness** (language simple or complex enough for the audience), **effort** (how fast and easily
they get the result). q18 lists only five blocks; q22 and q28 give the full six. Source: q22, q28.

## AI prompts — the reusable form underneath them

Pass 1's judgment, and worth more than any single prompt: every meta-prompt he writes has the same
skeleton, and the skeleton is the transferable part.

```
Phase 1 · Context gathering
          interview the user before doing anything
          explicit questions-per-turn limit:
            one question at a time            (first-principles, brand coach)
            two to three questions at a time  (copywriting, to prevent overwhelm)
Phase 2 · Execution
          produce the artifact from the gathered context
Phase 3 · Coaching  (optional; present in the brand-coach prompt only)
          run the plan with the user over time
```

Plus a standing rule stated across the AI material: the model is a director's instrument, not a
writer. He rejects using AI to write his content on the grounds that it strips the work of his
perspective and voice, and he rejects treating a model like a "slot machine" by typing "write a
viral tweet" and accepting what comes back.

The **Meta-Prompt workflow** that produces these, in five steps: (1) choose a task; (2) find an
expert video, article or book on it; (3) paste the source in and ask for a detailed step-by-step
guide; (4) feed the guide into a prompt-writing prompt and ask for a structured prompt that gathers
your context first and executes second; (5) save, test, refine.

Prompt-embedded figures, for the numbers index: IQ of **180** · "multiple **billion dollar**
companies" · a **30**-day programme in **three** phases · a **500 to 10,000**-word landing page ·
**two to three** questions at a time · **one** question at a time · **10-20** titles · **7** YouTube
prompts · **50** user interviews · **three** product bets · **10** headline variations · **five**
founder archetypes.
