## The Blue-Light Glasses, and Where "Audience Before Product" Comes From

**What happened** `[BIO][G]` He built an e-commerce brand selling blue-light glasses aimed at web
developers, on the reasoning that they stare at screens all day. He **called his dad and borrowed
$3,000**, ordered product, set up the store, studied influencer marketing and ads, and **blew the
entire $3,000.** It sat inside roughly **$8,000 of total debt** across his college ventures.

**The lesson he draws** `[PRN][G]` The extreme value of building an organic audience. With an
audience you do not have to "shovel money into a furnace" testing advertisements; you can validate
what people want for free, through content, before building a product.

**Why this one section matters more than the other five** **Audience-before-product is the core of
the One-Person Business Model, and it rests on this single $3,000 loss and on nothing else in the
corpus.** It is also the strongest grounding anywhere in the corpus: one specific, costly,
self-reported failure. It would be disconfirmed only by finding the loss did not happen.

**One thing the corpus never connects** `[WRN][A]` He sold blue-light glasses as a failed business,
and elsewhere warns that blue light "destroys your mitochondrial health and definitely contributes
to chronic disease", with no evidence, while telling viewers he does not know the science well and
referring them to Twitter biohackers. The commercial history and the health claim sit in the same
corpus and are never linked.

**Sources** q64, q63, q70c.

## Kortex, Eden, and the $3 Million Mistake

**What it was** `[BIO][G]` Kortex, originally built as a second-brain note-taking app. Spelled
**Cortex** in one source file; one product, two spellings.

**What he calls it** `[BIO][G]` **"A $3 million mistake."**

**Why, in his own post-mortem** `[G]` Massive technical debt from trying to build their own

lost their advantage.

**What they did about it** `[G]` During a trip to **Japan** the team rebuilt the software from
scratch **in three weeks** and rebranded it **Eden**: an intelligent drive and canvas workspace
that automatically downloads and transcribes pasted media (YouTube videos, Instagram Reels), makes
**every frame searchable**, and uses AI agents to synthesize ideas for content creation. For
creators, writers, marketing teams and digital artisans.

**The rule he extracts** `[WRN][G]` Do not build your own auth and sync. Ship the mobile app for a
note-taking tool. Speed is the advantage and technical debt is how you lose it.

**Where it shows in the revenue** `[NUM][G]` Year 5 dropped to around **$2 million** because
revenue was split between his personal brand and the software company.

**The current position** `[PRN][G]` He releases videos calling "niche down" **"terrible advice for

**The tactical concession inside it** `[PRN][D]` Despite preaching nicheless, he admits you **do**

top-of-funnel rhetoric.

"you are the niche, follow the problem you cannot pull away from".

## The Contradictions, as One Pattern

`[G]` Five of them, and they all run the same direction: the philosophy is the top-of-funnel claim,
the tactic is the bottom-of-funnel practice, and the reconciliation is only ever offered to people
already inside the product.

1. **The niche.** "Niche down is terrible advice" against a beginner course teaching how to find a
   niche, and a flagship product built because the niche was profitable. Reconciliation: broad at
   the top of the funnel, specific at the bottom.
2. **The 4-Hour Workday.** He mocks hustle bros who treat 16-hour days as a status symbol and says
   rest is the most productive form of work. He also admits he frequently works **12 to 16 hours a
   day**. Reconciliation: four hours belongs to the Consistency phase, twelve-hour days to the
   Intensity phase.
3. **Authenticity.** Originality is demanded while the growth method is copying viral structures,
   and his stated best business advice is to **sell what is already selling** by buying
   competitors' courses and reverse-engineering their funnels, landing pages and guarantees.
   Defence: "templates and frameworks don't make you inauthentic", because they act as constraints
   for creativity.
4. **Paid ads.** "The worst thing you can do", and he boosted his own posts at the start "because
   my content was good".
5. **The anti-guru.** He attacks schools and gurus for assigning pre-packaged paths, and sells
   16-week curriculums, Notion templates and daily processes.

**A sixth, on scarcity** `[G]` He rejects fabricated urgency while running hard caps, announced
price doublings and closing windows. His defence is that his caps are real.

**Sources** q69, q66, q65.

## What He Had at 22

`[WRN][A]` The riskiest advice in his body of work assumes conditions most readers do not have, and
those conditions map one-to-one onto his own at the time.

**His conditions** `[G]`

- Childless, 22, no dependents.
- Sharing a beaten-down frat house with seven other guys at **$250 to $400 a month** rent.
- **Parental credit available**: he borrowed $3,000 from his dad for the glasses venture and lost
  all of it, and still had somewhere to land.
- Roughly **$8,000 in credit card debt** and **$20,000 in student loans**, carried while
  experimenting.
- A demonstrated capacity to absorb a **college coding curriculum in three weeks**.
- A **$50,000 to $55,000** salaried job whose actual work fit into two or three hours a day, paying
  for the experiments.

**The advice to gate against them** Signing a lease at twice what you can afford. Disappearing for
six months. Working 12 to 16 hours a day. Dropping life savings on a coaching program. Mastering
four disparate disciplines in 6 to 12 months by self-educating on YouTube. Treating anxiety and
depression as symptoms of pursuing society's goals.

**What the corpus does not contain** `[A]` A failed adopter. Six documented failures, all his own
and all before the success, and no counterfactual anywhere. No cost side either: no ad spend beyond
the single $100 example, no team payroll, no refund or churn rate. "Taking home about 90%" is the
only margin figure.
