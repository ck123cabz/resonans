each section self-contained. Context is repeated rather than cross-referenced, so a single grep
returns the whole usable thing. Every platform has its own heading, so a grep for "LinkedIn" or

over someone else's model, both halves true and neither one droppable · `[M:name]` a straight relay
under the other person's name · `[G]` backed by his own reported outcome · `[I]` inferred, not
stated in the corpus · `[A]` a gap the corpus does not fill. Knowledge types: `[FRM]` framework ·
`[TAC]` tactic · `[TPL]` verbatim template or line · `[NUM]` number · `[PRN]` principle · `[WRN]`

## Audience Growth by Stage

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The three-stage growth road, with the tactic set that belongs to each
audience size.

**When to use it** To pick the growth move that matches the follower count someone actually has.

**Stage 1 — 0 followers** `[PRN][D]` Nobody knows who you are and the algorithm will not save you.
The whole stage runs on manual distribution.

- **Training wheels** `[TAC][D]` Do not try to be entirely original. Study top-performing tweets with
  a tool like **Tweet Hunter**, deconstruct their structure, and plug your own ideas into those proven
  formats.
- **Time budget** `[NUM][D]` **30 minutes a day writing and 30 minutes a day getting eyes on the
  content.**
- **Replies** `[TAC][D]` Leave highly valuable replies under the posts of large accounts in your
  niche, so their followers click through to your profile.
- **DMs** `[NUM][D]` Send **30 DMs a week** to people who engage with similar content.
- **The offer to pitch in those DMs** `[TAC][D]` A Minimum Viable Offer, a **4-call coaching pack**.
- **Grounded milestone** `[NUM][G]` He hit a **$3,000 month at just 500 followers**, on manual
  outreach and writing alone. His own figure, with the standing confound attached.

**Stage 2 — 1,000 to 10,000 followers, "beginner hell"** `[WRN][D]` The trap where you have a
baseline audience and not enough leverage to break out.

- **Build a Tribe** `[TAC][D]` Form a mastermind group chat with **5 to 10 creators of similar
  account size**, share strategies, and routinely repost or share each other's best content to
  trigger the algorithm and cross-pollinate audiences.
- **Pay for shares instead of running ads** `[TAC][D]` DM larger accounts, **20,000+ followers**, and
  offer to pay them to retweet or share your best posts. This buys manual traffic and builds the
  relationship at the same time.
- **Benchmark** `[NUM][D]` At **10,000 followers you should be making around $10,000 a month**,
  selling high-ticket freelance or consulting services. Stated prescriptively ("you should be"), so it
  is a benchmark he sets rather than a reported result.

**Stage 3 — 50,000 to 100,000+ followers, "High Leverage"** `[TAC][D]` Detach income from time by
selling digital products.

- **Diversify platforms** `[TAC][D]` Take the **top 100 tweets** that generated the most followers,
  screenshot them or turn them into carousels, and post them to Instagram and LinkedIn. Growth is
  exponentially faster because the ideas are already validated.
- **Trade social capital** `[TAC][D]` Offer a massive creator a share to your 100k Twitter audience in
  exchange for a share to their 100k Instagram audience.
- **Build a Mental Monopoly** `[FRM][D]` At scale, shift to long-form big-idea synthesis, stop relying
  on cheap hooks, and create your own philosophies and coined concepts, so you become irreplaceable.
- **Grounded milestones** `[NUM][G]` At **50,000 followers, a $50,000 month**. At **100,000 followers,
  a $100,000 month**. At **200,000 followers, a Black Friday launch generated $200,000 in a single
  month**. All his own figures, with the standing confound attached.

**The contradiction to carry.** Paid follower growth is called "the worst thing you can do" elsewhere
in the corpus, while the Stage 2 tactic here is paying larger accounts for shares, and q65 records him
boosting posts at the start "because my content was good". Pass 2 lists this among the five
philosophy-versus-tactic conflicts.

**Sources:** q13 (authoritative), q03, q65, Pass 2 §3.4.

## Non-Needy Networking — the 7-step DM process

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` A seven-step process for reaching a creator you want something from. The
premise is that you must make friends and not blindly pitch people.

**When to use it** Any time you want a share, an introduction or a collaboration from someone bigger
than you.

**The seven steps, complete** `[TAC][D]`

1. **Find a target.** Pick someone whose work you genuinely admire or want to collaborate with.
2. **Send an inspired compliment.** Do not say "Hey, how are you". Send a specific piece of their
   content and explain exactly why it impacted you.
3. **Show genuine interest.** Ask what they are currently building or working on.
4. **Lead with value.** Send an article, a software tool, or a connection from your network that helps
   them hit their current goal.
5. **Get on a call (optional).** Ask to jump on Zoom just to talk shop and deepen the friendship.
6. **Follow up.** Weeks later, send another resource related to their goals.
7. **The Ask.** Only after the relationship exists, send a high-effort post you wrote and ask casually.

**The Ask, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` *"Hey, I just wrote this. If you could share it, that would be
awesome, but no worries if not."*

**The named failure mode** `[WRN][D]` Do not open a DM with "Hey, how are you". An unspecific opener is
what step 2 exists to replace.

**Sources:** q13 (authoritative).

## The $1 Million a Year Math

**What it is** `[NUM][D]` The arithmetic he gives for reaching $1 million a year from one evergreen
product, stated as a chain.

**The chain, complete, as stated**

- $1,000,000 a year is **$2,777 a day**
- A **$150** evergreen digital product therefore needs **18 sales a day**
- At a **2.5%** landing-page conversion rate, that needs **720 visitors to the page per day**

**The timeframe** `[NUM][D]` 720 clicks a day is achievable over **3 to 5 years** by writing daily,
building the email list and networking for shares.

**The same conversion figure elsewhere** `[NUM][D]` q16 gives **2.5% or higher** as the baseline
landing-page conversion target for the newsletter funnel.

**Sources:** q13 (authoritative), q16.

## Revenue Per Follower — the $0.50 to $1.00 Rule

**What it is** `[NUM][D]` His stated rule of thumb: with the right product ecosystem and distribution
you should average roughly **$0.50 to $1.00 per follower per month** during your first few years. q16
states the same figure as the macro-metric across the whole newsletter funnel.

**When to use it** As a rough revenue expectation at a given audience size, with the artifact below
attached every time.

**The five worked examples he gives** `[NUM][G]`

| Followers | Monthly revenue as stated | Dollars per follower |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | $3,000 | $6.00 |
| 10,000 | $10,000 | $1.00 |
| 50,000 | $50,000 | $1.00 |
| 100,000 | $100,000 | $1.00 |
| 200,000 | $200,000 | $1.00 |

**The artifact, and it travels with the rule.** His stated range tops out at $1.00, and every worked
example above 500 followers sits at exactly $1.00, the ceiling of his own range. The 500-follower
example sits far above that ceiling. Nothing in the corpus illustrates the $0.50 half of the range.
Pass 2 records this as a survivorship artifact and Layer 3 carries it as one. q65 flags the clean
1-to-1 ratio of followers to monthly dollars as the most quotable and least verifiable number in the
corpus: presented as observed history, reading as a rounded heuristic.

**The confound** These are his own figures, from a business that sells the teaching of this method. No
figure here comes from a student.

**Perishability** `[A]` Undated, like every other figure in these lanes.

**Sources:** q13, q16, q10, q65.

## Mental Real Estate

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` The human mind treated as a finite space, an "intellectual structure" or
"house", that can be occupied by ideas, beliefs and worldviews. His claim: **"power belongs to those
who occupy the most mental real estate in the collective psyche"**, and the modern Gold Rush is
occupying digital and mental space rather than physical real estate.

**Provenance flag** `[D+M:positioning-tradition]` candidate. This is the marketing tradition's
mindshare or share-of-mind idea, Ries and Trout positioning, under a new name. The corpus attributes
nothing, and neither q67 nor q68 covers it.

**When to use it** As the argument for why attention is worth pursuing at all, upstream of any tactic.

**His verbatim self-description** `[TPL][D]` His brand is a *"city within social media"*, where every
newsletter, product and lead magnet is digital real estate occupying a specific niche in his audience's
minds.

**The supporting claims** `[PRN][D]`

- *"attention is the currency of the third millennium"*, presented as his own assertion with no source.
  The phrase belongs to the attention-economy literature (Goldhaber, Davenport and Beck), and neither
  q67 nor q68 records it.
- In an age of infinite information, human attention is the scarcest resource. A product, idea or belief
  that receives no attention functionally does not exist.
- Your ability to redirect people's focus toward your ideas is what grants modern leverage,
  profitability and power.
- You cannot expand someone's mind or change their life without first getting them to look at you.

**The moral argument he attaches** `[PRN][D]` "society is a pyramid scheme of attention": mega-corporations,
mainstream media and algorithmic feeds have power only because the masses feed them attention from the
bottom up. The people currently holding the most attention are pushing cheap dopamine, processed digital
food and rage-bait. `[WRN][D]` If intelligent, conscious people refuse to learn marketing, writing and
persuasion because they think it is sleazy, they are choosing to let unethical people win. He frames
mastering attention as a moral imperative and a "spiritual war".

**Sources:** q14 (authoritative), q09, q15.

## Capture, Hold, Convert — the three stages of attention

**What it is** `[FRM][D]` The three things you do with attention, each with its own content format and
mechanics. It is the operating model under Mental Real Estate.

**When to use it** To decide which format a given piece of content should be, and why.

**Capture** `[TAC][D]` Primarily through short-form content: tweets, reels, shorts.

- Mechanics: novel perspectives, **specific numbers as pattern interrupts**, and heavy leverage of
  problems.
- `[PRN][M:copywriting-tradition]` Stating a relatable, burning problem opens a **"curiosity gap"** that
  hacks the brain's survival mechanism and forces the reader to stop scrolling for the solution. The
  curiosity gap is the tradition's own term (information-gap theory), used unchanged and uncredited.
- `[PRN][D]` Short-form holds attention for only a few seconds, which is virtually useless for building
  deep trust.

**Hold** `[TAC][D]` Through long-form content: newsletters, books, podcasts, long videos.

- Mechanics: once the curiosity loop is open, you hold attention by guiding the reader through a story,
  because stories mimic the natural highs and lows of human emotion.
- `[PRN][D]` If your long-form ideas spark genuine behaviour change, your ideas will **"live in their
  head rent-free"**. Someone who builds a new habit because of your book gives you their attention all
  day long with no extra work from you.

**Convert** `[TAC][M:copywriting-tradition]` By driving the audience to a product, service or landing
page. The mechanics are the awareness ladder: make them aware they have a problem, amplify how it
negatively impacts their life, introduce your step-by-step system as the solution, then pitch your
product as the fastest and most convenient way to implement that solution. Eugene Schwartz is named for
the awareness ladder in q67's citation map, and not in the files where it is taught.

**What he says you do not need** `[WRN][D]` You do not need to be a sleazy salesman. You need to
understand psychology.

**Sources:** q14 (authoritative), q22, q29, q67.

## Time Under Attention

**What it is** `[FRM][D+M:Time-Under-Tension]` His metric for brand authority: the volume of deep
attention held, rather than the count of impressions. The more time people spend with your deep
philosophy, the more they engage with even your simplest posts, because they read those posts through
your established worldview.

**Provenance, in his own words.** q68 records that he explicitly acknowledges the term as a spin-off of
the bodybuilding and weight-training concept "time under tension", while claiming the labelling: "I
finally labeled this concept I created this concept I'm so I'm proud of this." The source is a training
concept and not a person. What the rename adds is transposing a physiological dose-response idea onto
attention as a brand-authority metric. q14 states the term with no source at all, which is the file-class
pattern Pass 2 identifies: he credits generously when the subject is provenance and does not repeat it
when the subject is a tactic.

**When to use it** To justify long-form work against short-form metrics, and to argue why a newsletter
beats a viral tweet for a brand.

**How to increase it** `[TAC]` Create long-form content: articles, newsletters, books, videos. Short-form
is for initial growth; deep authority and trust come from newsletters, podcasts and YouTube videos.

**The math he gives** `[NUM][D]` A newsletter reader spending **20 minutes a week** equals **52 hours of
deep attention per year**, against 10-second tweets.

**The opposite pole** `[WRN][D]` Short-form video platforms are "shallow" and "entropic" because they hold
only **10 to 30 seconds** of attention, which he calls virtually useless for deep brand loyalty or trust.

**Sources:** q05, q14, q16, q68.

## Branding and Positioning

**What it is** `[PRN][D]` A personal brand as a **vessel for your life's work** and a "city within social
media", rather than a collection of logos, bios or brand colours. It is a public resume, portfolio and
digital journal where you document your intellectual and personal development in public.

**When to use it** When someone is about to build a brand around a topic instead of around themselves.

**The positioning claim** `[FRM][D]` "You Are The Niche", an explicit rejection of niching down. Niching
down turns you into a replaceable, one-dimensional **"glorified search engine"**. Your identity, story and
worldview are what he calls your **"ultimate competitive moat"**, and because nobody else has lived your
exact experiences, your story makes competition irrelevant.

**The method** `[TAC][D]` Treat yourself as the customer avatar: solve your own problems, document the
journey, sell the solutions to your past self.

**Synthesis over specialization** `[FRM][D]` Become a **"specialized generalist"** or **"modern renaissance
man"** by combining **two or three broad interests**. His example set here: fitness, online business and
spirituality. The unique web of interests is what lets you make cross-domain connections. Provenance note:
the specialized-generalist idea is widely circulated inside this corpus's own citation family and no source
is given here.

**On information versus perspective, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` *"Your audience doesn't want information they
want your perspective on information"*. In an age of AI and infinite content, information is commoditized:
ten people can share the same concept and only one will articulate it in a way that changes minds.

**On originality** `[PRN][D]` Originality is not the invention of new concepts. It is exploring existing,
proven ideas through your own lens, goals and experiences.

**Authentic Polarization** `[TAC][D]` Express your core beliefs with absolute confidence and conviction, and
purposefully give people a reason to heavily disagree, and so heavily agree, with you. Verbatim: *"if you're
liked by everybody you're liked by nobody"*. Polarizing statements filter out the masses and build a
tight-knit community of loyal fans.

**What he warns against** `[WRN][D]` Do not be a "Level 1" creator or "trendjacker" who chases algorithms and
posts shallow "thought McNuggets" for cheap dopamine hits. Commodity creators post isolated viral content;
distinct creators build a "body of coherent work" with a meaningful mission and a through-line.

**Sources:** q15 (authoritative), q02, q05, q14.

## Newsletter Strategy

**What it is** `[TAC][D]` The newsletter as the only audience you own, and the primary vehicle for selling
products. Social algorithms change and platforms can ban you; an email list is what you actually own.

**When to use it** Immediately, at zero subscribers, because "nobody is watching", which he calls the
perfect place to get over the fear of publishing and to practise developing depth, original thought and
authority.

**Topic selection** `[TAC][D]` Pick a topic you cannot shut up about.

**Length** `[NUM][D]` **1,000 to 1,500 words.**

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` **One long-form newsletter per week**, strictly. He publishes newsletters and their
accompanying YouTube videos on the weekends, Saturdays or Sundays. An occasional second paid letter goes to
premium subscribers.

**Structure option 1 — bPAS** `[FRM][D+M:copywriting-tradition]` Start with a one-sentence **B**ig Idea,
illustrate a relatable **P**roblem, **A**mplify how that problem impacts the reader's life, then provide a
step-by-step **S**olution broken apart by sub-headlines. PAS is the direct-response tradition's own
structure; the "b" prefix and the newsletter application are his. The framework's fuller treatment sits in
q20, outside this lane.

**Structure option 2 — Cross-Domain Synthesis** `[FRM][D]` Start with the problem, teach a concept from a
completely different discipline (his example: using physics to explain deep work), then give your own
step-by-step process. Fuller treatment in q24, outside this lane.

**Structure as q17 states it** `[FRM][D]` Introduce a relatable problem, amplify it, provide a unique
step-by-step solution, end with a promotion for your product.

**How the list gets built** `[TAC][D]` You do not grow a newsletter by hoping people find it. You grow it by
leveraging short-form social to drive traffic. The funnel: short-form (tweets, LinkedIn posts, Instagram
reels) captures broad attention → medium-form (threads, carousels) displays competence → those readers get
filtered into the long-form newsletter, which builds super fans and deep trust. List-building runs entirely
on the 2-Hour Content Ecosystem, whose fuller treatment sits in q25, outside this lane.

**The plugs** `[TAC][D]` Send everyone to the newsletter: link in social media bios, at the bottom of Twitter
threads, in YouTube descriptions, and cross-post it daily.

**The repurposing chain** `[TAC][D]` Upload the weekly newsletter as a blog post for SEO and lifespan · read
it directly to camera as a YouTube video and a podcast · extract ideas from it to fuel daily short-form
posts.

**Lead magnets** `[TAC][D]` They accelerate email capture by establishing authority on a single topic and
giving people a compelling reason to hand over an email address. Build simple, beginner-level ones:
checklists, Notion templates, ebooks, or video tutorials. `[NUM][G]` He has built **8 to 10 different lead
magnets** over the years to test what the audience wants. `[BIO][G]` Named ones: the **Power Planner**, the
**Internet Profit Starter Pack**, and a 7-day idea generation course called **7 Days to Genius Ideas**.
Offering lead magnets builds a "niche audience of downloaders" inside the broader personal brand.

**Headlines** `[PRN][D]` An email subject line or newsletter headline is the same object as a social media
hook, and it has to stop the reader in their tracks.

- **The formula** `[FRM][D]` A condensed version of the most potent parts of the essay, combining a **big
  problem, a desired outcome, a believable timeframe, and a personal system**.
- **Worked example, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` *"Study for 12 hours a day without Adderall with a 14-day
  program"*.
- **Specific-numbers fragment, verbatim** `[TPL][D]` *"$1,175 iPhone"*.
- **Method** `[TAC][D]` Apply the 10 Commandments of Engagement to headlines, using specific numbers,
  pattern interrupts, and the negativity bias, which means spinning a positive outcome into a negative
  warning because humans are wired to notice threats. The 10 Commandments themselves sit in q21 and q22,
  outside this lane.

**Metrics** `[PRN][D]` He does not obsess over traditional email metrics like open rates. He tracks the
business and engagement metrics around the newsletter instead: paid products promoted at the bottom of the
emails, and clicks tracked to the landing pages. `[NUM][D]` Baseline landing-page conversion target: **2.5%
or higher**. `[NUM][D]` Macro-metric across the whole funnel: **$0.50 to $1.00 per follower per month**.

**Time Under Attention math for the newsletter** `[NUM][D]` A reader spending **20 minutes a week** equals
**52 hours of deep attention per year**, against 10-second tweets.

**Sources:** q16 (authoritative), q17, q13, q25, q20, q21, q24.

## Platform Rules — X (Twitter)

**Its role** `[PRN][D]` The ultimate testing ground and the foundation of the entire content ecosystem. As a
text-based "platform for ideas" it needs no video editing or design skill. It is the easiest platform to
grow on, because the retweet button provides low-friction sharing, and success relies entirely on your
ability to articulate ideas.

**How to use it** `[TAC][D]` "Spray and pray" ideas, see what gets engagement, then cross-post the validated
high performers to every other platform. Use **Tweet Hunter** or **Twemax** to study the top-performing
tweets of your favourite accounts and reverse-engineer their structures. (Pass 2 records "Twemx" and
"Twemax" as one tool spelled two ways in the corpus.)

**Formats** `[NUM][D]` Short text posts **under 300 characters**, lists, and long-form threads.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` **1 to 3 short tweets per day, and 1 long-form thread per week.**

**Reported outcome** `[NUM][G]` He grew to **300,000 followers** on X. Platform monetization pays him about
**$300 a month**, which he calls negligible next to product revenue. His own figures, with the standing
confound attached.

**Where to start** `[TAC][D]` The foundation for a beginner is X or LinkedIn paired with an email newsletter.
Do not start on a visual platform like YouTube or Instagram if you lack design or video-editing skills.

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q13, q65.

## Platform Rules — Substack and Newsletters

**Its role** `[PRN][D]` Your owned audience and the most important asset you have, because platforms can ban
you or change algorithms at any time. Newsletters hold deep attention, build immense trust, and are the
primary vehicle for selling products.

**How to use it** `[TAC][D]` Start writing even at zero subscribers, to practise developing depth. Once
written, the newsletter is the script for YouTube videos and solo podcasts, and it is uploaded as a blog
post for SEO. Plug it aggressively and daily across every social platform.

**Format** `[NUM][D]` A **1,000 to 1,500-word** long-form essay.

**Structure** `[FRM][D]` Introduce a relatable problem, amplify it, provide a unique step-by-step solution,
end with a promotion for your product.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` **1 per week**, with an occasional second paid letter for premium subscribers.

**Reported outcome** `[NUM][G]` **120,000 newsletter subscribers.** His paid Substack tier is **$10 a month**.
His own figures, with the standing confound attached.

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q16, q65, q66.

## Platform Rules — Instagram

**Its role** `[PRN][D]` A top-of-funnel growth platform. You do not need to be a lifestyle vlogger or post
shirtless pictures to grow on it.

**How to use it** `[TAC][D]` Repurpose validated writing from X rather than creating natively. Accelerate
growth by DMing large accounts or your tribe and having them share your carousels to their Instagram
Stories.

**Formats, verbatim detail** `[TPL][D]` Screenshots of tweets pasted into a simple **4x5 image template**
(using Figma or Canva) · swipeable carousels, which are X threads repurposed · Reels.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` Sourced dynamically from your daily tweets and weekly threads. **No fixed number is
given.**

**Reported outcome** `[NUM][G]` He grew to **over 1.4 million followers** primarily by turning his best
tweets into images or animations. q65 gives the fuller progression: to **240,000** through writing-based
image posts, then to **1.2 million in just 1 to 2 months** using animated reels, and currently around
**1.5 million to 1.6 million**. Instagram Reels monetization pays about **$300 a month**. q66 records that
his animation agency **Keyframe** sells the exact style of animated video that took his Instagram from
**250,000 to over 1 million followers**. His own figures, with the standing confound attached.

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q13, q65, q66.

## Platform Rules — LinkedIn

**Its role** `[PRN][D]` No longer just a resume site. Highly lucrative for creators because it is full of
professionals who actually have money to spend. It also lacks the trolls found on X or Instagram, because
users are afraid of losing their jobs.

**How to use it** `[TAC][D]` Cross-post validated X content. LinkedIn paired with a newsletter is one of the
two acceptable starting foundations for a beginner, the other being X.

**Formats** `[NUM][D]` Text posts, carousels, and "micro-articles".

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` **1 to 3 short posts a day and 1 to 2 long posts a week**, mirrored from the core
writing schedule.

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q13.

## Platform Rules — YouTube

**Its role** `[PRN][D]` A long-form platform for deep authority, and much slower and harder to grow on for
beginners than X or LinkedIn.

**Warnings** `[WRN][D]` Do not start on YouTube unless you have the skills. Build the audience on short-form
platforms first and transfer them over. Do not rely on YouTube AdSense to make a living; use the platform to
drive traffic to your own high-margin digital products.

**How to generate ideas** `[TAC][D]` Filter your favourite channels by "most popular" and use their titles as
the hooks for your own videos.

**Format** `[TAC][D]` Talking-head videos read directly from the weekly newsletter script.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` **1 video per week**, published on the weekend alongside the newsletter.

**Reported outcome** `[NUM][G]` Growth was slow until a viral podcast clip netted **200,000 subscribers in a
few months**; later past **275,000**; currently around **500,000**. AdSense pays about **$10,000 a month**,
which he uses as his argument for owning the offer rather than renting the platform's payout. His own

## Platform Rules — Podcasts

**Its role** `[WRN][D]` Notoriously hard to grow, because their native search engines are "absolute trash".
Do not start one to get famous.

**The two reasons to have one** `[TAC][D]`

1. A repository for the audio versions of your YouTube videos, for people who prefer listening.
2. A high-level networking tool. Asking a larger creator to guest is the ultimate "trojan horse" for getting
   them on a Zoom call, building a friendship, and leveraging their network.

**Formats** `[TAC][D]` Solo episodes, which are the ripped audio from the weekly YouTube video and
newsletter, or guest interviews.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` Typically **1 solo episode a week**, aligned with the YouTube and newsletter release.

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q16.

## Platform Rules — TikTok, Shorts and Reels

**Its role** `[WRN][D]` Short-form video platforms are inherently "shallow" and "entropic", because they hold
only **10 to 30 seconds** of attention, which he calls virtually useless for deep brand loyalty or trust.

**Why use them anyway** `[TAC][D]` They are a necessary top-of-funnel tool for capturing mass traffic. Use
them strictly to capture attention and immediately funnel viewers to long-form (YouTube, newsletter), where
trust and selling happen.

**Format** `[TAC][D]` Speak to camera or use animations. The scripts are your highest-performing tweets read
aloud.

**Cadence** `[NUM][D]` Scaled dynamically to how many of your daily short posts you want to record. **No fixed
number is given.**

**Sources:** q17 (authoritative), q14.
