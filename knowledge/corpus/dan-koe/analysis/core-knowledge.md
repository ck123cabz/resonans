# Dan Koe — Core Knowledge

*Layer 1 of the corpus at `knowledge/corpus/dan-koe/`. Built 2026-07-28 from 1,418 Pass 1 signals across 70 NotebookLM extraction files covering 161 sources, ~54,650 words. Loadable whole. Every verbatim hook, prompt, price and step-list lives in `retrieval-index/`; what is missing lives in `gap-register.md`.*

## How to read this

Koe is a synthesizer. He takes an existing model, renames it, packages it for a creator audience, and sells the package. The packaging is a real contribution and this document treats it as one. What it never does is present the package as the discovery.

Markers:

- `[D]` — his own, no acknowledged source underneath. 989 signals, about 70%, concentrated almost entirely in the tactical material.
- `[D+M:name]` — his name over someone else's architecture. 87 signals. The signature case, and both halves travel together.
- `[M:name]` — a straight relay under the source's own name. 105 signals.
- `[G]` — backed by his own reported outcome. 182 signals, 13%, the largest grounded layer in this corpus family and the most confounded.
- `[I]` — our inference. He does not say it.
- `[A]` — a known absence, carried in the gap register.

**Two standing rules.**

First, every revenue figure in this corpus is his own, produced by a business whose product is teaching the method that produced them. His single largest reported launch was a writing product sold to people who want to write, and q66 records that he built it because he knew the writing niche was profitable. **No number in this corpus comes from a student.** The figures are evidence that a large audience will pay for this instruction, not evidence that a reader following it reaches those numbers. Attach that line wherever a figure is used.

Second, this corpus teaches persuasive writing techniques that this repo's own filter bans. Quote him as quotation. Never write in his voice. The specific patterns are listed in §14.

## 1 · The two-layer structure

**He states the design himself: big-picture philosophy at the top to capture people looking for meaning, then numbered protocols underneath so they execute.** `[PRN][D]` Pass 1 confirms it by count. The tactical lanes run about 90% `[D]` with almost nothing borrowed underneath. The philosophy lanes run 28% compound markers. **His original work is the tactics. His borrowed work is the meaning.**

That one fact explains most of what follows, including why the same material reads as rigorous in one video and as unattributed in another.

**The corpus audits itself, and the audit is incomplete.** q67 is a citation map naming 39 thinkers, what each is claimed to have said, and which Koe concept rests on it. q68 is a coinage audit covering 15 of his named terms, stating for each whether a source sits underneath. Both are real and both undercount. q67 misses roughly a quarter of the corpus's attributed sources (Ferriss, Kleon, Mizner, Parkinson, the Zettelkasten tradition, Billy Wilson, Matt Ogus, the Prussian education model, and the biohacker "Grimhood"). q68 covers only the terms it names and misses the largest compound cases entirely. **Working rule applied throughout: q68 governs the 15 terms it names, q67 governs everything else, and silence in either is not evidence that no source exists.**

**The credit tally, from q68's own numbers: 9 explicitly credited, 1 explicitly disclaimed, 3 partially credited, 2 fully uncredited.** He credits considerably more than he hides. The accurate frame is renaming with attribution, not concealment. The 4-Hour Workday shows how far that honesty goes: he records that he saw *The 4-Hour Workweek* in college, misread the title as "workday", and kept the misreading.

**The qualification is a file-class pattern rather than a per-source one.** Files whose subject is a thinker's model credit generously and by full name. Files whose subject is a tactic strip the same material of attribution. Csikszentmihalyi is never named across the twelve philosophy files despite carrying the Focus Matrix, the Intensity phase of Cycles of Progress, and Gamify Your Life. Five of those twelve name nobody at all. Kotler is never named in the same lane despite one file relaying his five intrinsic drivers verbatim. So both hold at once: when he addresses provenance directly he is honest, and in tactical delivery he does not repeat it. A reader meeting the material through one tactical video receives Wilber's, Cook-Greuter's and Csikszentmihalyi's work with no attribution at all.

**He gives the structure as an instruction, not only as an accident.** In q50 he sorts creators into trendjackers who master hooks and post shallow content, "brilliant nobodies" with real insight and too much pride to learn marketing, and value creators who translate between the two. The Level 3 move is to use Level 1's attention tactics as the delivery vehicle for Level 2's depth `[D]`, and the tactics he prescribes there are the ones he calls entropic and meaningless one paragraph earlier.

## 2 · The model beneath the frameworks

One argument runs through every lane:

**You are the niche. Solve your own problems, document the solving in public, and sell the solution to people one or two steps behind you. Attention is the asset, writing is the instrument that captures it, a digital product is how it converts, and leverage is what makes the whole thing work without employees.** `[PRN][D]` in every part; `[I]` as a single sentence, since he never states it as one.

Four functional families sit under it, and knowing which family a framework belongs to is usually enough to know when to reach for it.

- **Capture attention.** The writing frameworks (APAG, PPP, the 10 Commandments, hook construction), Time Under Attention, the per-platform rules, Mental Real Estate.
- **Convert attention.** The product ladder, the Value Equation, launches, the Trust Matrix, the sales principles.
- **Protect the capacity to make it.** The 4-Hour Workday, Fill/Empty/Use, the Priority Ladder, Monk and War Mode, the daily routine, the health rules.
- **Grow the person doing it.** Human 3.0, the ego-development ladder, the Mastery Method, Project-Based Learning, the skill stack.

**One four-slot framework appears under three vocabularies and should not be read as three frameworks.** `[I]` Brand / Content / Product / Promotions is given as Goal / Map / System / Why in q01, Purpose / Priorities / Path in q04, and Character / Mindset-and-Skillset / Process / Why in q12. No reconciliation is offered anywhere.

## 3 · The business model

**"You are the niche."** `[PRN][D]` Your most profitable niche is your own identity, worldview and experience. Instead of inventing a customer avatar you become the customer avatar, and you attract followers who share your personality because they resonate with your story.

He rejects conventional niching on four counts `[WRN][D]`: it forces you into "work you don't care about for people you don't care about to live a life you don't care about"; it caps personal evolution, since interests change and a hyper-specific niche cannot follow; it turns creators into "glorified search engines" with no depth, easily replaced by AI; and most businesses fail because founders try to solve problems they never had.

**The tactical concession matters more than the slogan.** `[PRN][D]` q69 records that despite preaching nicheless, he admits you do have to niche the product and the funnel. Top-of-funnel content stays broad; the bottom of the funnel targets a specific person with a specific problem. Carry the concession as the operative rule and mark the slogan as top-of-funnel rhetoric. He has also changed position here: he once bought the standard advice, searched the top 100 niches, and targeted dentists and gyms, and now calls "niche down" terrible advice for smart people. `[G]`

**Naval Ravikant sits underneath the whole business model, not one framework of it.** `[D+M:Naval]` Four separate Naval concepts hold it up: the three forms of leverage (labor, capital, and products with zero marginal cost of replication, meaning code and media), specific knowledge, permissionless leverage, and zero-marginal-cost replication. q06 credits him by name five times, which is the cleanest citation in the corpus. q01 states the model without naming him and q12 restates zero marginal cost bare. Jack Butcher is credited by name for "build once, sell twice" `[M:Jack-Butcher]`.

What Koe adds on top `[D]`: prioritise media over code, since writing builds distribution and software without marketing leaves you with no users; acquire specific knowledge by treating your life as a science project; and treat specific knowledge as the defence against AI, since a model trained on generalised historical data has neither your lived experience nor your taste.

**The One-Person Business Model is explicitly disclaimed by him.** q68 quotes him directly: "I didn't come up with the concept of one person business but I took it and I gave it an entirely new meaning," by merging it with productizing self-actualization. There is no named originator to compound against, so the marker stays `[D]` with the disclaimer attached, and it must never be presented as his coinage.

**The four components** `[FRM][D]`: Brand is the goal, your character and the vision you are working toward. Content is the map, social media as public note-taking about roadblocks and lessons. Product is the system you used to solve your own problem. Marketing is the why, the transformation you are promising.

**The staged progression** `[NUM][D]`. Stage 1 is client work: a high-ticket service sold in DMs at $1,000 to $10,000, where 2 to 3 clients a month is $100,000 a year. Stage 2 is the hybrid model, a group program or cohort, $300,000 to $500,000 a year with fulfilment down to 1 to 2 hours a week. Stage 3 is productization: a standalone course with no manual fulfilment plus cohort launches four times a year, past $1,000,000. He puts the realistic timeframe at 3 to 5 years, with follower thresholds of 500, 10,000, 50,000, 80,000 to 100,000 and 200,000 alongside.

**Koe's Law** `[FRM][D+M:Parkinson]`: "creative work evolves to earn more in the time allotted for its completion." He credits Parkinson's Law out loud and calls his version an evolution and a spin-off. What the rename adds is inverting a warning about padded work into a forcing function for earnings: cap the day at four hours and the business has to get more valuable inside the cap rather than longer outside it.

**The Micro Education Business** `[FRM][D]` is the same model named for its output: a decentralised school built from your own solved problems, at margins he states as 95%+. His own reported take-home over five years is about 90%, the lower number and his own.

**What he warns against** `[WRN][D]`: freelancing as a permanent state, since you trade one boss for 5 to 10 clients and stay capped by hours; creators who never build the hybrid model and stall between 10,000 and 30,000 followers; and startups, which need capital, investors and a team.

## 4 · Offers and money

**The Value Equation: Purpose, Path, Priority.** `[FRM][D]` Purpose is the transformation someone wants. Priority is the burning problem they currently face, which depends entirely on what they already deem important. Path is the step-by-step system bridging the two. His summary: "people buying a product is human behavior... you need a problem, you need a goal, and you need to bounce between the two. Your product is the path that helps them get from one to another." q68 does not list it among the audited coinages, so `[D]` stands, but q67 records that Eugene Schwartz's five levels of awareness and Anthony De Mello's *Awareness* are integrated into it. **Two flags.** The name collides exactly with Alex Hormozi's differently composed Value Equation (dream outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, effort and sacrifice), and Hormozi appears nowhere in the corpus. Never present "the Value Equation" without saying whose.

**The Eternal Markets** (health, wealth, relationships, happiness) `[PRN][D]` appear in neither audit file and are standard direct-response taxonomy. Treat as an uncredited inheritance rather than a coinage.

**The product ladder, with his own prices as the anchors** `[NUM][G]`: free lead magnets · low-ticket books at $27 to $50 · a paid community at $5 then $27/month · evergreen courses at $150 · cohort courses at $399+ · high-ticket services from $500 to $10,000. The ladder is a description of his own catalogue generalised into a rule, and **his four most expensive products carry no price anywhere in the corpus** (Digital Economics, Mental Monetization, Cortex University, Keyframe). `[A]`

**Reverse the ladder to build it.** `[TAC][D]` Start at the top by selling the $1,000 service in DMs to validate, productize the proven system into the $150 course, then add free lead magnets and low-ticket books to feed it. Low-ticket items are "trust tripwires" because "buyers buy again."

**Launches** `[TAC][D]`: sell the product before it exists. Landing page, pre-orders, release 3 to 4 weeks out, promotion from the bottom of the newsletter, escalation at 1 to 2 weeks out with an early-bird discount, and a new product every quarter for 2 to 3 years. The first pre-order supplies the deadline that forces the build.

**On scarcity he holds both positions.** `[WRN][D]` He rejects the sleazy internet marketing of the past decade by name: complex funnels, fake countdown timers, exaggerated income promises, rented Lamborghinis. The rejection is strategic rather than moral, because market sophistication has risen and consumers recognise the old tactics. `[TAC][G]` He then runs a hard cap of exactly 2,000 copies of the Art of Focus box, promotes that Digital Economics doubles on May 15th and Mental Monetization rises on June 3rd, and closes cohort enrolment on a firm date. His defence is that his caps are real: the 2,000 copies actually stopped. The discriminator he draws is truthfulness, not absence of pressure.

**Sales principles** `[PRN][D]`: 90% of selling happens before any conversation, because a prospect who reads your daily posts has effectively been through "four sales calls with you." Risk reversal is executed as a conditional guarantee, refunding only if they prove they did every step. Social proof is delivered as a "humble flex", a milestone shared as a relatable learning moment. Peer testimonials are "appreciating assets" because the peer keeps growing. He says outright that he is "fairly bad at sales" and does not handle objections; if someone wants to think about it he says "yeah no problem" and lets them go. `[BIO][G]`

**The revenue history, as reported and never computed.** `[NUM][G]` Year 1 around $10,000. Year 2 around $100,000. Year 3 reaches $150,000, then $800,000 is also given for the same year. Year 4 surges to $3.1M and then around $4M. Year 5 around $2M, split between the personal brand and the software company. He states a five-year total of "a little over $5 million" with about 90% taken home. **The years sum to roughly $6.9M against that stated $5M, and Year 3 carries two figures.** Report the conflict; never produce a total. Month-level peaks climb from a first $10,000 month to a peak upward of $700,000. Audience: 2.8M in one place and 3.8M to 4M in another, 120,000 newsletter subscribers. Platform ad revenue is negligible next to products, at roughly $300/month each from Reels and X against about $10,000/month from YouTube.

**The per-follower rule and its artifact.** `[NUM][D]` His stated rule of thumb is $0.50 to $1.00 per follower per month. Every worked example he gives sits at exactly $1.00: 10k to $10k, 50k to $50k, 100k to $100k, 200k to $200k. Only the first rung breaks it ($3,000 at 500 followers). Every example lands at the ceiling of his own range, which is a survivorship artifact rather than a measured average.

**The one arithmetically checkable claim in the corpus** `[NUM][D]`: $1M a year is $2,777 a day, a $150 product needs 18 sales a day, and at 2.5% landing-page conversion that is 720 visitors a day. The arithmetic closes, and its only soft input is the 2.5% rate, which he states as his baseline elsewhere.

## 5 · Audience and writing

**Platform and cadence** `[TAC][D]`. Start on X or LinkedIn paired with an email newsletter; the newsletter is the only owned audience, because platforms change algorithms and ban accounts. The cadence is 1 to 3 short posts a day (q25 says 2 to 3), 1 to 2 long posts a week, 1 newsletter a week of 1,000 to 1,500 words. Full per-platform specs sit in Layer 2.

**Growth by stage** `[TAC][D]`. At zero followers: "training wheels", meaning deconstruct top-performing posts and plug your own ideas into their structure, plus manual outreach. In "beginner hell" between 1,000 and 10,000: a tribe of 5 to 10 creators of similar size who share each other's work, plus paid shares from larger accounts. Above 50,000: take your top 100 follower-generating posts to other platforms, since the ideas are already validated, and trade audience shares. Non-Needy Networking is a seven-step DM sequence ending in a low-pressure ask, on the premise that you make friends rather than pitch.

**APAG** `[FRM][D+M:copywriting-tradition]`: Attention (the hook), Perspective (paint the enemy or the faulty view), Advantage (paint the hero and the new vision), Gamify (hand over a hierarchy of goals or a step list). He presents it as his own writing framework, and q68 records that he explicitly credits PAS, AIDA and PASTOR for the psychological foundations it relies on. He supplies verbatim example lines for all four stages and names ten surfaces it applies to.

**PPP** `[FRM][D]`: Pull, Perspective, Punchline, for short-form, with two worked examples broken down word by word. q68 states no source exists. **This and the 2-Hour Content Ecosystem are the only two items in the corpus that survive the coinage audit as pure `[D]`.**

**The rest of the writing frameworks are the direct-response tradition's own.** PAS (which he renders Problem, Amplify, Solution rather than Agitate) and PASTOR arrive with no attribution `[M:copywriting-tradition]`; one word swapped is not enough packaging to earn a compound marker. bPAS (Big Idea prefixed to PAS, triggered by an anomaly post) and PAO do carry his packaging `[D+M:copywriting-tradition]`. **PAO's acronym does not match its own expansion**: the corpus gives "Problem, Amplify, Solution, Offer", which spells PASO. Carry the framework with the defect flagged and never teach the label as correct.

**The 10 Commandments of Engagement** `[FRM][D]`, in his order: specific numbers · pattern interrupts · negativity bias · target call out · problem call out · potential benefit · social proof · confidence and conviction · punch, rhythm and readability · warnings. The numbered set is his packaging; several individual entries are other people's vocabulary used unchanged. Pattern interrupt is the persuasion tradition's `[M:copywriting-tradition]`, negativity bias is established psychology `[M:cognitive-psychology]`, social proof is Cialdini's `[M:Cialdini]`. Nine of the ten carry a verbatim example; Potential Benefit has none. `[A]`

**Hook construction** `[FRM][D]`: balance relevance, awareness and effort, then assemble the most potent combination of six building blocks (big problem, big benefit, big idea, transformation process, time frame, negative personal experience). **Macronutrients and micronutrients of value** `[FRM][D]` sit under it: Problem, Goal and Process as the three macronutrients, then Concept, Proof, Risk Reversal, Pain Points, Benefits and Awareness as the six micronutrients. Risk reversal is the tradition's own term and Awareness is Schwartz's ladder; the nutrition metaphor and the part-lists are his.

**The five levels of awareness** `[M:copywriting-tradition]`: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware, with a prescription for each. This is Eugene Schwartz's ladder from *Breakthrough Advertising*, stage names unchanged, appearing with no attribution where it is taught. q67 credits Schwartz for it under the Value Equation, and q33 shows him uploading the book to an AI to build a copywriting prompt. **Carry the asymmetry**: in q24 he credits De Mello's *Awareness* by name for inspiring him to write about levels of awareness, while the ladder itself goes uncredited. Example hooks exist for three of the five rungs. `[A]`

**Sentence-level rules** `[TAC][D]`: a 5th-grade reading level without watering down the idea; "clear not clever" (a long-standing copywriting maxim, uncredited); start short; heavy line breaks; bullets ordered shortest to longest; em dashes and parentheses instead of commas; no passive voice; no hedging words; absolutes and exaggeration for energy; ignore grammar rules and run-ons; expect a bad first draft and edit after publishing using engagement as the signal. **These are the rules our own filter bans, stated as craft.** Extract them as his instruction and never apply them to our prose.

**Time Under Attention** `[FRM][D+M:Time-Under-Tension]`. q68 records that he explicitly acknowledges it as a spin-off of bodybuilding's "time under tension" while claiming the labelling: "I finally labeled this concept I created this concept I'm so I'm proud of this." The arithmetic that carries it: 365 short posts a year at 30 seconds each is 3 hours of attention per reader, against one long-form piece a week at 20 minutes, which is 52 hours. He calls the failure mode the James Clear Effect `[D]`: post a 30-second tweet about habits and a reader who has read *Atomic Habits* will unconsciously attribute your idea to Clear.

**Branding** `[PRN][D]`: a personal brand is a "city within social media" and a vessel for your life's work, not logos and colours. Combine two or three broad interests to become a "specialized generalist" (circulating vocabulary, not his). Practise authentic polarization, because "if you're liked by everybody you're liked by nobody." At scale, build a "mental monopoly" by coining your own concepts.

**The Trust Matrix** `[FRM][D]` (formerly the Social Matrix): Growth attracts, fuelled by short-form; Authenticity keeps people, fuelled by long-form; Authority converts them to buyers. Beginners put roughly 80% of output on growth. q68 records no external source and notes that spin-offs have appeared since. It is unclaimed rather than proven original, and nothing in the corpus tests it against, for instance, know-like-trust.

**Cross-Domain Synthesis** `[FRM][D]`: state a problem, teach a pattern from an unrelated discipline to explain why it exists, then give your own process. His worked pairings are physics with productivity, spirituality with marketing, metaphysics with focus, neuroscience with philosophy.

## 6 · Content and ideas

**The 2-Hour Content Ecosystem** `[FRM][D]`. Write one anchor piece, a newsletter, then push it outward: email the list, publish it as a blog post for SEO, read it to camera as a YouTube video and a podcast, condense it into a thread and carousels, extract individual lines as short posts, cross-post those everywhere, and link back to the owned platform underneath every one.

**The name is a brand, not a measurement.** His own itemised budget sums to 45 to 90 minutes: 20 to 45 minutes on a section of the weekly newsletter, 10 to 30 minutes generating short ideas, about 15 minutes cross-posting. The stated daily work block is also 45 to 90 minutes. Two hours is a ceiling he never spends. A second two-hour figure sits nearby and measures something else entirely: "batch-write 23 posts in just two hours a week." Never repeat either number without its unit, and never repeat the product name as a quantity.

**Newsletter structure** `[TAC][D]`: big idea, problem, amplification, then context and benefits, then a headline stating the solution, then numbered steps, then the promotion at the very bottom, because the bottom is the most authoritative place to sell. **Thread structure** runs hook, lead, body, objection handling, conclusion and call to action, with the promotion again last.

**Idea generation** `[TAC][D]`. The input filter is the Dopamine Compass: capture only what produces the reaction "I wish I wrote that." The store is the Idea Museum, his name for a swipe file. When ideas dry up, change the inputs: 3 to 5 sources that consistently make you envious, old little-known books, curated blogs. **CorNotes** `[FRM][D+M:Zettelkasten]` then expand a captured idea into reusable "mental Legos" by attaching Problem, Goal, Example, Benefits, Process and a Concept name, picking 3 to 4 per note; q68 credits the Smart Notes and Zettelkasten methodologies for Value Notes, which later became Cor Notes. **The component count conflicts inside the corpus**: q31 and q34 list 6, q32 lists 7 by adding Topic-and-Summary and Objections. Report both.

**Intelligent Imitation** `[PRN][D+M:Kleon]`. Choose 5 to 10 creators you admire, find their anomalies (posts that performed 2x or 3x their normal engagement), deconstruct why they worked, and use the structure as training wheels for your own ideas. Never copy the words, only the architecture. **He says two contradictory things about this term and both are in the corpus**: "intelligent imitation is just something I fucking made up," and elsewhere an explicit credit to Austin Kleon's *Steal Like an Artist* and to Wilson Mizner's aphorism about taking from many writers. The Mizner line is quoted in q43 as Koe's own observation, inside an answer about not passing off other people's work.

**The second brain** `[PRN][D]` is a "search engine for your memories" built for output, not a hoard. He rejects Notion and Obsidian as places where "ideas go to get lost", rejects clipping whole articles, and rejects tag webs built for their own sake. The term is Tiago Forte's and is used bare `[I]`; the memory-search-engine framing, CorNotes and the output-driven workflow are his. The implementation he names is his own paid product, and the transferable claims underneath its features are capture at the point of use, reference while drafting, atomic reuse, and ambient resurfacing.

**AI: director, not doer.** `[PRN][D]` Never outsource thinking, creativity or the writing of your core ideas, and never treat a model like a slot machine. The five human traits he says AI lacks `[FRM][D]`: perspective, energy signature, sensemaking, trajectory, evolving taste. **The Swap Test** `[FRM][D]`: if the creator and the creation can be swapped for a generic equivalent without losing value, AI replaces it.

**The reusable form in his prompt library is worth more than any single prompt.** `[TPL][D]` Every meta-prompt he writes runs phase 1 context-gathering by interview, phase 2 execution, and an optional phase 3 coaching, with an explicit questions-per-turn limit. **Two prompts in q33 are not his** and are marked in the corpus as sourced from someone else's post `[M:Signal]`; they must not enter a Koe prompt library unattributed.

**He warns against AI-written content and sells AI software in the same breath.** The line he draws is director versus doer, and it holds on its face. It is the sharpest place in the operational lanes where the sell sits adjacent to the teaching.

## 7 · Productivity and lifestyle

**The 4-Hour Workday** `[FRM][D+M:Ferriss]` is presented as a philosophy and an identity rather than a hack. He credits Ferriss explicitly and tells the misreading story. The mechanism he claims: because humans survive on a conceptual level, identifying deeply as someone who only works four hours makes the brain treat overspill as a threat, which forces you to systemise, automate or delegate rather than work longer.

**The count holds at four hours because some work is not counted as work.** `[I]` By his own reported clock, desk time sums to roughly 3.5 to 4 hours. The 30 to 60 minute "creativity block" walk is spent consuming lectures and audiobooks and capturing ideas for future writing sessions. The afternoon reading block "hunts for new ideas." Messages and community replies are handled over breakfast, between the two morning blocks. Reclassifying input work as leisure is what keeps the number at four. This is the corpus's headline promise and a lens has to say so plainly.

**And the number only covers half his own model.** `[G]` q69 records him admitting he frequently works 12 to 16 hours a day. His reconciliation is phase-based: four hours belongs to the Consistency phase of maintaining a business, and 12-hour days belong to the Intensity phase of building something new. The reconciliation is real, and it appears in one file. Retrieved without it, the four-hour claim is misleading.

**The mechanics** `[TAC][D]`: work first thing, waking between 5:00 am and 7:00 am, before anything can reach you. Break the four hours into 60 to 90 minute blocks (stated elsewhere as 45 to 90, and as 1 to 2 hours for the Use phase), on the 2 to 3 highest-priority lever-moving tasks (q44 says 3 to 5), with 10 to 30 minutes of active rest between them and a dead stop at the end. He relays the Zeigarnik effect under its own name `[M:Zeigarnik]` to argue that stopping mid-task is a good outcome, and claims a 3 to 5 hour daily ceiling on deep focus, citing "scientific studies" with no name attached.

**The Priority Ladder** `[FRM][D]`: four rungs decreasing in challenge and increasing in distraction potential. Priority 1 is the Anchor Project, first thing, hardest, newest. Priority 2 is lever-moving work. Priority 3 is momentum work. Priority 4 is maintenance and conversation, deliberately last, because early exposure to messages invites psychic entropy. When a Priority 1 task becomes systemised you bump it down and open the slot. He claims one protected hour a day can change your life in six months, and no case is attached to that claim, not even his own. `[A]`

**Fill, Empty, Use** `[FRM][D+M:Pang]` rests on mental metabolism, which q67 backs with Alex Soojung-Kim Pang's *Rest*. Use your mind in the morning for creation; fill it in the afternoon with reading, walking and conversation; empty it at night with journaling and planning. The label order is not the execution order.

**Monk Mode and War Mode** `[FRM][D]`. Monk mode is withdrawal: eliminate distractions, then run four daily habits (one project, one book, one meditation, one workout), for 30 days as a dopamine detox or up to 6 months as a reset. War mode is the inverse: say yes to everything, expose yourself to chaos, expand into a new identity, for 2 to 3 months with burnout as the stated exit condition. He oscillates between them `[G]`, and neither is the steady state: the daily routine is the baseline both depart from `[I]`.

**Tactical Stress** `[FRM][D+M:Prigogine]`: deliberately force yourself into a do-or-die situation. q68 records that he named the process and based its mechanics on Ilya Prigogine's dissipative structures, where order emerges from extreme chaos. His examples are signing a lease you cannot afford, pre-selling an unbuilt product, dropping $5,000 on a program, moving across the country, and occasionally partying so the regret slingshots you back into work. He gates it himself `[WRN][D]`: not for beginners, and aim one or two levels above where you are rather than a hundred. The same construct appears elsewhere under a second name, "Glitches", and the two should be unified.

**The reported day** `[G]` runs 5:00 am wake and cardio in silence, a 6:30 am deep work block, breakfast plus maintenance at 8:00, a second block at 9:00, the creativity walk at 10:00, admin at 11:30, the gym at 12:30 as the hard transition out of work mode, lunch and leisure reading, an early dinner, "normie nights", journaling before bed, asleep by 9:00 pm.

**Health** `[PRN][D]` `[G]`: walking and resistance training are the two non-negotiables. 10,000 to 15,000 steps a day, up to 25,000 in walkable cities (q36 gives a flat 15,000). A 220-pound frame on 4,000 to 4,500 calories and 220 grams of protein. He recommends Stan Efferding's Vertical Diet by name for a reset `[M:Stan-Efferding]`. ## 8 · Learning

**Project-Based Learning** `[FRM][D]`: build the project first and search for information only when a specific roadblock demands it. Choose the project, brain-dump what you know, deconstruct 3 to 5 sources of inspiration, build with what you have, hit a wall, seek exactly what the wall requires, implement, repeat. The premise is that learning comes from struggle rather than memorisation, and that a project narrows the frame so the brain absorbs only what is relevant. The failure mode is "tutorial hell" `[WRN][D]`: hoarding theory with no output channel.

**Just-in-time versus just-in-case learning** is the same idea under the personal-knowledge-management tradition's own labels, presented with no source `[M:unattributed]`. **The premortem** (visualise the project failing before you start, list the causes, prevent them) is Gary Klein's, also uncredited `[M:unattributed]`. Neither is covered by q67 or q68.

**The Mastery Method** `[FRM][D]`, seven steps: expand your mind with an unrealistically large vision used as a filter rather than a target; build a hierarchy of goals; turn goals into projects; acquire techniques rather than whole skills; reinvent yourself by starving the old identity of its information; treat mistakes as course-correction feedback; treat your life as a science project. The Hierarchy of Goals itself is `[D+M:Maltz]`; q67 states his need for a goal hierarchy rests entirely on Maxwell Maltz's cybernetics, and his definition of intelligence as the ability to course-correct comes from the same place.

**Techniques, not skills** `[TAC][D]`: identify the exact technique the current problem needs, apply it, and accumulate techniques until the broader skill forms. He relays the Feynman Technique and the Protégé Effect under their own names `[M:Feynman]` `[M:protégé-effect]` for the teach-what-you-learn step.

**The Future-Proof Skill Stack** `[FRM][D]`, in build order. Phase 1, the evergreen skills: marketing and sales (your message), writing and thinking (your medium), entrepreneurship (your vessel). Phase 2, technical know-how (the how), with a stated 10 to 20 year turnover horizon. Phase 3, personal interests (the what), which is what makes you unreplaceable. q67 records that he expands Devon Eriksen's list of modern liberal arts into this. The phase order is asserted and never argued. `[A]`

**Reading and inputs** `[TAC][D]`: a 30 to 60 minute daily learning block; three books at a time across genres for pattern recognition; quit early, since the first two or three chapters usually carry the idea; read slowly, even if a book takes a year; stop the moment a sentence hits hard, work out why it resonated, capture it; take notes as if teaching a beginner rather than copying quotes; use AI as a study partner rather than a summariser. He warns explicitly against bullet-point book summaries, because "the fluff is where the context, nuance, and magic of new discoveries happen." His line for input dependency: "you can only cook with what's in the fridge."

**Knowing versus Understanding** `[FRM][D+M:Cook-Greuter]`: knowing is horizontal development, the accumulation of facts; understanding is vertical development, the sophistication of the cognitive operating system. The horizontal/vertical distinction is standard adult-development vocabulary; the relabelling and the apps-versus-operating-system metaphor are his. His summary line: "Knowledge is constructed upward, while understanding is discovered inward."

**Three neuroscience claims carry weight in this material and none carries a source.** The default mode network as the idea engine, the reticular activating system as a goal-driven filter, and dopamine as a survival signal for pattern recognition. Per the profile's rule these stay `[D]` with an evidence flag rather than becoming attributions.

## 9 · Philosophy and psychology

**Human 3.0** `[FRM][D+M:Wilber]` is the map: four domains, three macro-levels, and a phase-based progression, credited in text as adapted from Ken Wilber's AQAL model. The domains are Mind (personal interior), Body (personal exterior), Spirit (collective interior) and Vocation (collective exterior). The renaming adds a version-number handle and one-word life-area labels in place of AQAL's abstract quadrant names, which is what makes it usable by a lay audience. **Cross-Quadrant Unlocks** `[D+M:Wilber]` is his diagnostic move on top: a deficit in one domain blocks progress in another, so find the blocking quadrant. You do not level up your whole life at once.

**The Levels of Mind and the NPC-to-Synthesizer progression** `[FRM][D+M:Cook-Greuter]`. Human 1.0 is the Conformist, the NPC running a pre-written societal script. Human 2.0 is the Individualist, the main character who rejects the norm and then believes their own path is the only right one. Human 3.0 is the Synthesist, the programmer who holds multiple perspectives and designs games others can play. The underlying ladder is Susanne Cook-Greuter's nine stages of ego development, which he relays in full and credits by name in exactly one file (q51); across q47, q48 and q49 the same material appears with no credit at all. The renaming compresses nine academic stages into three and adds a game metaphor that carries the whole ladder.

**The Spiral Dynamics colours in this corpus are wrong, and the error must not propagate.** He names red, amber, orange, green and teal, and credits Don Beck and Christopher Cowan. **Spiral Dynamics runs beige, purple, red, blue, orange, green, yellow, turquoise. Amber and teal are Wilber's integral altitude colours.** He has blended two developmental systems and attributed the result to one of them, and his list is five long against their eight. State the real sequence; never repeat his five as Spiral Dynamics. `[M:Beck-Cowan]` is otherwise one of the cleanest credited relays in the corpus, and Wilber is credited by name in the same file for AQAL, holons and the pre-trans fallacy.

**The Actualization Hierarchy** `[FRM][D+M:Wilber]`, credited in text to Wilber's holons and natural hierarchies, and contrasted with a dominator hierarchy where a few at the top extract from the many. **Flag:** both paired terms are Wilber's own, so this may be closer to a straight relay than a coinage, and it needs a primary-source check. What the packaging clearly adds is the **Brand Actualization ladder** `[TAC][D+M:Wilber]`: a short post is a whole and part of a newsletter, the newsletter part of a lead magnet, the lead magnet part of a service, the service part of a product, the product part of a brand. That is a publishing sequence the source never had. Maslow is credited by name in the same file `[M:Maslow]`, including self-transcendence rather than only the popular five tiers. **The creator developmental ladder** `[FRM][D]` (Trendjacker, Brilliant Nobody, Value Creator) is the second place where the packaging produces a new object rather than a relabelling. Cite those two when arguing that his renaming is a contribution.

**The Focus Matrix** `[FRM][D+M:Csikszentmihalyi]`: a 2x2 plotting unconscious against conscious focus, and narrow against open. Unconscious narrow is stress and hyper-fixation; unconscious open is overwhelm; conscious narrow is deep work; conscious open is creative wandering. The prescription is to notice the drift to the unconscious side, shift right, then choose narrow or open by what the situation needs. q68 has him presenting the visual matrix as his own while explicitly crediting the underlying quadrants to Csikszentmihalyi's flow psychology and to the task-positive and default mode networks. **The honest reading is that the 2x2 layout is his and everything inside it is borrowed, and that is the corpus's synthesis move stated most cleanly.** The file that teaches it credits nobody.

**Conceptual Survival** `[PRN][D+M:Naval]` `[D+M:Hermeticism]`: human survival instincts have transcended the physical realm, so we protect and reproduce the information in our consciousness the way animals protect their genes. He introduces it as "my law of conceptual survival" while acknowledging Hermetic Mentalism and Naval's "purpose is your axis of suffering" as influences. It explains why people fight over politics, diets and sports teams, and why echo chambers form. Two things underneath it are uncredited anywhere: memes as replicating units of culture is Dawkins's, and the cybernetic homing mechanism doing the filtering is Maltz's `[D+M:Maltz]`.

**Enjoyment versus pleasure and psychic entropy** `[PRN][D+M:Csikszentmihalyi]`: enjoyment comes from investing attention and is process-focused; pleasure comes from spending it and is outcome-focused. Psychic entropy is the mind's drift toward disorder when unstructured, cured by investing attention in challenging activities with clear feedback and self-generated goals. He credits Csikszentmihalyi explicitly in q60 and uses psychic entropy as bare vocabulary in q38.

**Cycles of Progress** `[FRM][D+M:Hermeticism]` `[D+M:Csikszentmihalyi]`: Perplexity, Curiosity, Intensity, Consistency, each with a duration and a named failure mode. Perplexity fails by refusing boredom and numbing with cheap dopamine, Curiosity by forcing intensity too early, Intensity by burning out, Consistency by stagnating. Intensity runs 3 to 6 months and is the one phase where he endorses 12-hour days, because they happen without forced discipline. The failure modes and durations are his; the cyclical frame is Hermetic and the Intensity phase is Csikszentmihalyi's flow.

**Identity and motivation** `[PRN][D]`: all lasting behaviour change is identity change. The tactics are an anti-vision written until the current trajectory disgusts you, an environment purge that starves the old identity of its information, an Alter Ego built by merging 2 to 3 aspirational mentors `[D+M:Herman]` (modelled on Todd Herman's *The Alter Ego Effect*), and an identity-evidence list where each small action is a "vote" for the person you are becoming `[D+M:Clear]`. **That last one is James Clear's formulation from *Atomic Habits*, and q68's coinage audit misses it entirely.** He also holds that discipline is discovered rather than forced, since a gamer needs no discipline to play for twelve hours, and that most people need clarity rather than motivation. **Gamify Your Life** `[D+M:Csikszentmihalyi]` sets rules, a goal hierarchy and quantifiable daily tasks so challenge matches skill. **The five intrinsic drivers** (curiosity, passion, purpose, autonomy, mastery) are relayed verbatim from Steven Kotler with no credit where they are taught `[M:Kotler]`; his rendering of mastery adds a monetization gloss Kotler does not carry.

**Consciousness and the self** `[PRN][D]`: consciousness is the fabric of reality, the self is a "mental body" of ideas and conditioning, and the ego is a tool rather than an enemy. He rejects the modern spiritual instruction to kill the ego and says instead to develop it until you can pick it up and put it down. His Hermetic material is credited in text throughout `[M:Hermeticism]`: Mentalism, polarity, "as above, so below", and *The Kybalion* by name. Watts is credited by name for the law of reversed effort and the bodhisattva posture `[M:Watts]`. Wilber is credited by name for holons and the pre-trans fallacy `[M:Wilber]`, and the pre-trans example (the bible thumper, the mystic, the militant atheist) is his packaging of Wilber's argument.

**He argues with his sources, and a lens that only reports borrowing will miss it.** `[CIT]` **James Clear is the one thinker he names in order to disagree**: against "winners and losers have the same goals", Koe argues systems cannot exist without goals, and the disagreement generates a framework of his own. **Mark Manson is the second-order case**: he cites Manson's blog post on the rise and fall of Ken Wilber, his own largest single source, to argue that even highly evolved people keep an ego. Alongside those, one further piece of explicit epistemics: he quotes "you can't solve a problem from the same level of consciousness that created it", attributes it to Einstein, and **says himself he is not sure Einstein said it**. That is the one place in 54,650 words where he flags his own misattribution risk.

**Three population claims carry philosophical weight and none has a method behind it** `[NUM][D]`, and they disagree with each other: 80% to 90% of the population sits at Human 1.0 or 2.0; 80% to 95% of social media users operate at conventional stages; two to four hours of focused work depletes 95% of mental energy. Flag all three as asserted rather than measured.

## 13 · Contradictions

**Five philosophy-versus-tactic conflicts, all running the same direction.** The philosophy is the top-of-funnel claim, the tactic is the bottom-of-funnel practice, and the reconciliation is only ever offered to people already inside. Read them as the two-layer structure observed in the wild rather than as hypocrisy, and keep both halves.

1. **Grind culture.** Consistent 12 to 16 hour days are "a sign of major dysfunction". War Mode is 2 to 3 months of giving "absolutely everything you have" with burnout as the exit condition, entered by signing a lease you cannot afford. His reconciliation is phase-based, four hours to Consistency and twelve to Intensity, and it appears in one file. The discriminator he offers is that War Mode is bounded and chosen; he does not address that the hustler's fourteen-hour day is also self-reported as chosen.
2. **Authenticity.** Originality is demanded while the growth method is copying viral structures, and his stated best business advice is to sell what is already selling by buying competitors' courses and reverse-engineering their funnels and guarantees. His defence: templates act as constraints for creativity rather than replacements for it.
3. **Paid ads.** Running ads for followers is "the worst thing you can do", and he admits boosting posts at the start: "I tried it at the beginning and it works because my content was good." The $100-to-$2,500 ad result belongs to the same era.
4. **Anti-guru.** He attacks schools and gurus for handing people pre-packaged paths, and sells 16-week curriculums, Notion templates and daily processes.
5. **Scarcity.** He rejects fabricated urgency and runs hard caps, announced price doublings and closing windows. His defence is that his caps are real.

**Three internal contradictions with no reconciliation offered anywhere:**

- He threw out his Whoop because a recovery score overrode how he actually felt, while tracking steps, calories, protein, block lengths and priority rungs on the work. The stated reason the tracker was bad applies unchanged to a step target. Anti-optimisation on the body, maximal on the mind, never examined.
- He warns against attaching identity to a diet, and says the 4-Hour Workday works precisely because you deeply identify as someone who works four hours. Same mechanism, opposite verdict, same lane.
- His Priority Ladder puts maintenance and conversation at the very end, because early messages invite psychic entropy. His reported routine answers messages at breakfast, between the two morning deep-work blocks. Priority 3 is invisible in the reported day, so the lived ladder has three rungs.

Two smaller ones worth carrying: shiny object syndrome is a standing failure mode elsewhere and a phase requirement inside Curiosity; and he built The 2-Hour Writer because he knew the writing niche was profitable, which sits against "you are the niche" and "follow the problem you cannot pull away from."

## 14 · Cautions for the lens

**Nothing in this corpus is dated.** "Year 1" through "Year 5" are never mapped to calendar years, the biography does not reconcile (an arrest around 2016 plus five years of college against quitting in 2019), and the only dating signal in 54,650 words is that he names Claude by model tier and mentions Claude Code, placing some sources in 2025 or later. **The platform tactics are the most perishable material here and none of it is timestamped**: the under-300-character post, the retweet button as the growth mechanism, the named growth tools, LinkedIn as troll-free, Instagram growth via tweet screenshots.

**Two product names are not measurements.** The 2-Hour Content Ecosystem's own itemised budget is 45 to 90 minutes. The 4-Hour Workday holds at four hours only because the creativity walk, the afternoon reading and the breakfast messages are not counted as work, and it covers only the Consistency phase of his own model. Retrieve it from three places or not at all: q68 for the origin, q67 for the borrowed substrate, q69 for the phase constraint.

**Never compute the revenue total.** The years do not sum to the stated figure and Year 3 has two values. Report both and say so. More generally, say "he reports", never "it works": every benefit figure is his own, from a business that sells the method.

**Six failed businesses, not seven.** q64 enumerates the fitness channel, the Photoshop Instagram, the Facebook Ads agency, rave dropshipping, the blue-light glasses, and the leather wallets. A seventh appears only by counting creator-era info products, which belong to a different period.

**Names and errors to carry correctly.** Susanne Cook-Greuter, not "Suzanne", because the `xandros` lens holds adjacent developmental material and will link on the right spelling. Alex Soojung-Kim Pang, not "Alex Sujong Kim Pang". Kortex, not Cortex, for the software. The garbled hook figure is $45,275. "Manis" is almost certainly Manus. **Incomplete lists. Do not fabricate the missing items.** The eight core human desires the Three Tensions condense are never enumerated.
