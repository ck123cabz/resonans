## Core Four: the four ways to make an offer known

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-36.1 · **Files:** q36, q37, q44, q65

The only four primary advertising activities one person can do to make an offer known:
1. **Warm outreach.** One-to-one, to people who know you or gave permission: phone contacts,
   email lists, existing social followers.
2. **Posting free content.** One-to-many, public media for an audience that follows or finds
   you: social posts, podcasts, short and long-form video, articles.
3. **Cold outreach.** One-to-one, to strangers who have not given permission: cold DMs, cold
   emails, cold calls, scraped lists.
4. **Paid ads.** One-to-many, paid placements (Meta, YouTube, Google) that buy reach to
   strangers.

The 2x2: audience (warm = know you, trust you or gave permission; cold = strangers with low
trust) by path (one-to-one private; one-to-many public). Warm one-to-one is warm outreach; warm
one-to-many is content; cold one-to-one is cold outreach; cold one-to-many is paid ads.

q65 names the sources: $100M Leads parts 3 and 7, plus two 2026 videos.

## Core Four: trade-offs and which to start with

**Type:** MEC · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-36.2, S-36.4 · **Files:** q36, q37, q44

**Trade-offs.** Warm outreach and content cost $0 in ad spend but take time, labour and
consistency. Cold outreach needs large volume to beat low response rates. Paid ads trade capital
for immediate reach: faster, with higher financial risk if the offer is not validated (the only
place the set ties ad risk to offer validation). One-to-one is lower leverage per attempt but
direct and reliable; one-to-many gives high leverage and scale.

**Match the channel to your resources.** More time than money (bootstrapping, under $1M): start
with warm outreach for the first 5 customers, then add content. More money than time: go
straight to paid ads, or build an automated or staffed cold outreach engine.

## 1-1-1 rule: one offer, one avatar, one channel

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** businesses under $1M · **Signals:** S-36.3, S-51.1, S-62.10, S-42.2 · **Files:** q36, q37, q42, q51, q62, q66

One product or offer, one target avatar, one lead channel, until the business reaches
$1,000,000. Running all four channels at the start dilutes focus and delays mastery.

- q51: $1,000,000 in revenue.

## Channel roadmap for a new business, seven steps

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** new business up to $100M+ · **Signals:** S-37.5 · **Files:** q37, q36

1. **Warm outreach (start here).** Reliable, free, needs no advertising skill. Pull contacts from
   phone, email and social, personalise greetings, reach out 1-on-1, and offer the service or lead
   magnet free in exchange for feedback, reviews and referrals, to get the first 5 customers.
2. **Post free content** about results, case studies and lessons from the first clients.
3. **Cold audiences.** Cold outreach if more time than money (scrape lists, personalised cold
   email, DMs, calls with high-value offers); paid ads if more money than time.
4. **Customer referrals.** Delighted customers make a loop where 1 brings 2, 2 bring 4.
5. **Hire employees and use agencies** once your own capacity is maxed.
6. **Build an affiliate army** of non-competing businesses that hold an audience of your
   prospects.
7. **Hire executive department heads** to run each channel: a self-sustaining $100M+ lead
   machine.

q36 gives the first three as "progressive stacking": master warm outreach to test the pitch and
get cash flow; post content to turn wins into goodwill; once the first channel is maxed, add cold
outreach or paid ads.

## More, Better, New: the scaling order

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-37.3 · **Files:** q37, q42, q65

In strict order:
1. **More.** Raise input volume on what already works, to max capacity (Rule of 100). Most owners
   underestimate how much more volume their channel can take.
2. **Better.** Find the biggest conversion constraint and test variations (headlines, hooks,
   offers).
3. **New.** Only after More and Better are maxed, when their return drops below what a new effort
   would produce. New expands in this order: new placements on the current platform (Stories,
   Reels), then new platforms for the same content (YouTube Shorts to Instagram), then a new Core
   Four method (organic content to paid ads).

q65 cites $100M Leads part 7 and two 2026 videos.

## Rule of 100

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-42.1 · **Files:** q36, q37, q40, q41, q42, q43, q44, q45, q65, q66

100 primary advertising actions every day for 100 days in a row, before judging results or
switching. Per channel:
- **Warm outreach:** 100 personalised reach outs per day (texts, emails, DMs or calls).
- **Content:** 100 minutes per day creating, and at least 1 piece per day (q43: at least one
  primary piece per day per platform).
- **Cold outreach:** 100 cold reach outs per day (calls, emails, DMs, or flyers; flyers only in
  q42).
- **Paid ads:** 100 minutes per day creating, testing and managing ads.

Commitment removes guesswork, builds volume and "guarantees" engaged leads. Consistent across ten
files. q65 cites $100M Leads parts 7 and 11.

## 70/20/10 allocation

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general; ad version sized for a creative team · **Signals:** S-42.3, S-62.22 · **Files:** q42, q44, q62, q65, q66

- **Marketing effort:** 70% copying and doubling down on what works in the core channel; 20%
  adjacent variations; 10% new experiments or channels.
- **Ad creative:** 70% copies of proven hooks and creatives; 20% adjacent variations (same hook,

  creative fatigue.
- **Resources** (q66): 70% core business, 20% adjacent moves, 10% high-risk moonshots.

  the market does.

## Warm outreach: the 10 steps (two versions)

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** solo founder, first 5 to 10 customers · **Signals:** S-40.2, S-52.1 · **Files:** q40, q52

**q40 version:**
1. Gather the list across email, social and phone.
2. Pick the platform holding the most contacts.
3. Personalise: 30 seconds on each profile for a personal detail (job change, recent post, new
   baby).
4. Reach out to 100 contacts per day, following up up to 3 times (1x per day for 3 days or until
   they reply).
5. Warm them up with ACA: active listening, compliments, domain-related questions.
6. Make an indirect offer: ask if they know anyone who fits and needs help.
7. Make it free and low friction: the service completely free for three commitments.
8. Cycle: finish one platform, move to the next; when all are done, restart at the top.
9. Transition to paid: free case studies, then 80% off, 60% off, 40% off, full price.
10. Keep the list warm: check in, share value, keep goodwill.

**q52 version (first 5 to 10 customers without ads):**
1. Build a 3-part contact list: email, social contacts you can DM, phone and private contacts.
2. Start on the platform with the most contacts (Instagram DMs, text or email).
3. Personalise: 30 seconds on the profile, reference something specific.
4. Reach 100 contacts every day; 3,000 contacts in about 30 days.
5. Warm replies with ACA.
6. Ask for referrals; do not pitch the person.
7. Offer a monetarily free case study with 3 commitments, capped at 5.
8. Keep looping 100 a day until all 5 spots fill.
9. Move to paid pricing in batches of 5.
10. Keep the list warm: check in, post success stories and testimonials, until contacts come to
    you.

The step-up as matched to his audio is 80, 60, 40 "and so forth", with a 20% step (Pass 2 §2);
q40's list stops at 40%.

## ACA: Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** DMs, chat or phone · **Signals:** S-40.4, S-52.3 · **Files:** q40, q41, q45, q52, q65

- **Acknowledge:** restate what they said in your own words to show you listened.
- **Compliment:** tie it to a positive trait (q41: hard-working, proactive).
- **Ask:** a low-friction question that moves toward your domain and the problem your offer
  solves.

## Warm outreach: the referral-framed offer ("does anyone you hate come to mind")

**Type:** SCR · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** solo founder, first 5 customers · **Signals:** S-40.5, S-52.4, S-45.8 · **Files:** q40, q45, q46, q52

**q40:** "By the way, do you know anybody who is [describe struggle], looking to [dream outcome]
in [time delay] without [effort and sacrifice]? I'm taking on 5 case studies for free because I
just want to get some testimonials for my product/service. I help them [dream outcome] without
[effort/sacrifice] and guarantee the results. I just helped [Client Name] achieve [dream outcome]
even though she had [same struggle]. Does anyone you like come to mind?" Fallback: "Haha, well
does anyone you hate come to mind?"

**q45:** "By the way, do you know anybody who is [describe struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in
[time delay]? I'm taking on 5 case studies for free [or at an X% discount] because I want to get
testimonials for my service. I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] without [effort and
sacrifice]. Does anyone you like come to mind?" Fallback: "Haha, does anyone you hate come to
mind?"

**q46:** "By the way, do you know anybody who is [describe struggle], looking to [dream outcome]
in [time delay]?" Fallback: "Haha, well do you know anyone you hate?"

**q52:** "Do you know anyone who is [struggling with X] and looking to achieve [dream outcome] in
[timeframe]? I'm taking on 5 case studies for free to get testimonials for my service." One-liner:
"I help [type of customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort and sacrifice],
and I guarantee [XYZ] to decrease risk." If no: "Haha, does anyone you hate come to mind?"

Only q40 has "and guarantee the results" and the "[Client Name]" proof line; a first-time seller
has no client to name. Only q45 has "[or at an X% discount]".

**Why it works** (q45, q40): the "do you know anybody who" framing carries no pressure; the
prospect is asked only to name someone, and interested people raise their own hands.

The matched free-case-study terms script ("since I'm only taking on five people… Does that sound
fair?") is `[D]`, Leads part 3; it is printed in full in free-work-trials-and-first-customers.

## Warm outreach: volume and pipeline numbers

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** solo founder, US market, $400 offer · **Signals:** S-40.7, S-40.8, S-40.9, S-52.6, S-69.2 · **Files:** q40, q52, q69

- **Volume:** 100 reach outs per day; 500 per week (5 days); each non-responsive lead contacted up
  to 3 times before pausing.
- **Pipeline per 100** (q40): 100 reach outs, 20 replies (20%), 4 free clients (1 in 5
  respondents), 1 paying customer through upsells, renewals or referrals. Benchmark: 1 paying
  customer per 100 warm reach outs.
- **Against q52 and q69:** 3,000 messages yield about 5 free case studies (0.17%). q40's 4 per 100
  (4%) is 24 times higher. One is probably not his figure.
- **To $100,000 a year** (q40): 1% purchase conversion on a $400 offer; 500 reach outs a week = 5
  paying customers a week; 5 x $400 = $2,000 a week; $2,000 x 52 = $104,000 a year, no ad spend,
  more than double the median US household income. It assumes a list that never runs out; a
  1,000-contact list lasts 2 weeks at 500 a week.

## Cold outreach: three problems and their fixes

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-41.1, S-41.2 · **Files:** q41

1. **You lack their contact information:** build lists through software scraping (filter by job
   title, revenue, industry, pull verified contacts), list brokers (paid custom databases), or
   elbow grease and interest groups (scrape or message members of niche communities, forums,
   social groups).
2. **They don't know you and ignore you:** personalise with 1 to 3 specific details (recent news,
   posts, mutual interests) so a cold message feels warm.
3. **They don't trust you and aren't interested:** **Big Fast Value.** Strangers give little
   attention, so a mediocre pitch or a hard sales call is rejected at once. Lead with a
   high-value lead magnet, free audit or case study that solves a narrow problem so well that
   they feel stupid saying no.

## Cold outreach: calls, DMs and email

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-41.3, S-41.4 · **Files:** q41, q43

- **Cold calling:** call targeted lists by hand or with multi-line dialers; open with a brief
  personalised callout, handle gatekeepers and hang-ups directly, pitch a low-friction lead
  magnet or a brief discovery call. No word-for-word cold-call script is given.
- **Cold DMs:** LinkedIn, Instagram, Twitter/X, text, personalised voice notes or short video
  memos. Voice and video stand out because they signal effort.
- **Cold email:** short targeted text emails: a 1 to 3 sentence personalised opener, the Big Fast
  Value offer, then a simple low-friction question.
- **Reading level:** running cold email through a reading-level app to get below a 3rd-grade
  level raises response by 50% (no source or sample given). Content: write at or below a 3rd to
  5th-grade level, "clear, not clever".

## Cold outreach: benchmarks per 100

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** B2B outbound with a strong offer · **Signals:** S-41.9, S-41.10 · **Files:** q41, q66

- **Cold calling:** 100 dials, ~20% pickup (20 conversations), ~25% take the lead magnet, ~4
  engaged leads per 100 dials (~1 engaged lead per hour). 20 x 25% = 5, not 4.
- **Cold email:** 100 personalised emails, ~30% open, ~10% reply, ~3 engaged leads per 100 (a ~3%
  benchmark).
- **Cold DMs with voice or video memos:** up to 20% reply (~20 engaged leads per 100). This counts
  replies as engaged leads, which the email line does not.
- **q66:** 1% to 3% cold engagement from attempts into engaged leads; 20% response or pickup for
  cold calls and messages. Lower than q41's ~4% and ~20%.
- **To customers:** about 1 in 10 engaged leads becomes a paying customer (q41). q66: 10% of free
  lead magnet users buy the core offer. Same 10% on two bases. Warm compares at 4 free clients to
  1 paying (25%).

## Follow-up: multi-touch, multi-channel, and the cadence

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-41.7, S-41.8, S-45.4 · **Files:** q40, q41, q45, q66

- **Multi-channel:** contact each lead several times across channels (email first, then a call and
  a DM; phone, text, voicemail, email, DM). Use an unanswered attempt as the reason for the next:
  "I'm calling to follow up on my email..." Expect 2 to 3 conversations before closing
  higher-ticket sales.
- **Initial cadence, two readings.** q45 ("3x3"): up to 3 times per day for 3 days in a row, or
  until they respond (up to 9 touches). q40 and q66: once per day for 3 days or until they respond
  (3 touches). Unchecked against the audio.
- **Direct mail:** follow up once per week (q45).
- **List re-contact:** after working a list, wait 3 to 6 months, then reach out again; timing and
  circumstances change (q41, q45, q66).

## Engaging leads: speed to lead and the "maniac" caller

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** inbound lead flow · **Signals:** S-45.1, S-45.2, S-45.3, S-50.10, S-50.11 · **Files:** q45, q47, q50, q66

- Contacting a lead within 60 seconds of opting in gives a 391% increase in sales (4.91x, "nearly
  5x"). q47: a 4x to 5x increase in conversions. Likely a third-party statistic; no source named.
- q50: raising close rate from 50% to 80% is a 1.6x lift; cutting response time under a minute can
  give 5x throughput. Speed often moves revenue more than the closing script.
- **The maniac:** hire a rep whose only job is to stop whatever they are doing and call incoming
  leads at once, 24/7 (q47: within 60 seconds). q47 calls it one of the highest-return hires in
  lead generation. Cited as other owners' case studies, and it needs lead volume.
- **Onboarding speed:** satisfaction and retention are mostly set in the first 24 to 48 hours
  after purchase (q45); q66 gives a 48-hour window. Set expectations and deliver quick wins.
