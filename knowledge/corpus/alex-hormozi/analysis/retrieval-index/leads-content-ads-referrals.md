## Content: topics, framing and the pillar

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-43.1 to S-43.4, S-43.6, S-43.11 · **Files:** q43

- **One central pillar first:** the core topic that unites your audience (your "Who"). Personal
  traits or hobbies go around the edges.
- **5 topic categories:** (1) far past: earlier life lessons tied to your offer; (2) recent past:
  recent experiences, meetings, client case studies and testimonials; (3) present: real-time
  ideas posted publicly to test what resonates; (4) trending: your commentary on news or trends;
  (5) manufactured: a challenge engineered for content ("I lived on $100 for a month").
- **Document, don't manufacture.** Documenting daily work, client results and lessons is more
  sustainable than complex concepts. "Do epic stuff first, then talk about what you did."
- **"How I", not "How To":** talk about what you did, not what strangers should do; it keeps you
  safe from critics and from sounding preachy.
- **Repetition:** people need reminding more than teaching. You tire of your content long before
  the audience has seen it.
- Content is not the compounding asset; the audience it builds is.

## Content: posting volume

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** solo up to a media team · **Signals:** S-43.5, S-63.7 · **Files:** q43, q63, q66

- Rule of 100: at least 100 minutes per day, or at least one primary piece per day per platform.
- Platform maxima at scale: up to 10 short-form posts per day per platform; long-form up to 5
  days a week.
- Batch production: sit down twice a month to record 30 short clips and long-form breakdowns,
  producing hundreds of assets with a small team. Whether 30 is per session or per month is
  unclear.
- q66: 450 pieces per week is the benchmark for a global multi-platform brand. This is
  Acquisition.com's own media-team output (q63), not a rule for a small business.

## Content: hooks, headlines and callouts

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-43.7 to S-43.10 · **Files:** q43, q65

- **Hook:** the first 1 to 5 seconds or the headline. Formula: Proof + Promise + Plan, sometimes
  with Picture + Pain. q65 cites two 2026 videos.
- **Headline elements** (use at least two): Recency, Relevancy, Celebrity/Authority, Proximity,
  Conflict, Unusual, Ongoing.
- **Callouts.** Verbal: Labels ("Clark County moms", "gym owners"), Yes Questions, If-Then
  Statements, Ridiculous Results. Non-verbal: Contrast (bright clothing, movement, loud noises)
  and Likeness (familiar people and settings).
- **Content unit: Hook, Retain, Reward.** Retain through Lists, Steps or Stories with unresolved
  questions; reward with clear value. q65 cites $100M Leads part 4.
- **Short-form result he reports** (q64): cutting 3 seconds of preamble so the hook hit at once
  took a video from 40,000 to 780,000 views, "a 19x lift" (19.5x).

## Content: give-to-ask ratio and the path to a customer

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-43.12, S-43.13, S-43.14, S-48.12, S-63.8 · **Files:** q43, q48, q55, q56, q63, q65, q66

**Give-to-ask, 3.5:1.** Over-deliver value without asking. Mature platforms run 3.5:1; growing
personal brands should skew further toward giving. q65: "3.5:1 minimum or over-giving". Basis
(q63): 3.5 minutes of TV content per 1 minute of ads; 3 organic posts per 1 ad on Facebook. q48
and q56 render it as 70% value, 30% pitch, which is about 2.33:1; 3.5:1 is about 78% to 22%; the
Facebook basis is 3:1. One of the three figures is wrong. Video named in q48: *How to Grow Your
Business SO Fast in 2026*. The book discussed value-giving without a ratio.

**Micro-promises to macro-promises:** keeping small promises in free content builds trust that
you will keep large ones in paid products.

**Content-to-customer pathway** (q43):
1. Direct viewers to a lead magnet.
2. Calls to action: Integrated Asks (brief CTAs inside long-form) or Intermittent Asks
   (promotional posts after a string of value posts).
3. Nurture: long-form content works as an indoctrination sequence; up to 78% of clients consume
   long-form before booking a call or buying (no stated base).
4. The lead magnet solves one narrow problem and reveals the broader one only the core offer
   solves.

## Paid ads: when to start and what to sell on them

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general; ad-funded · **Signals:** S-44.1, S-44.5, S-44.6, S-44.11 · **Files:** q44

- Paid ads come last in the Core Four sequence: skills from outreach and content transfer, and
  profit from earlier channels funds spend. They suit more money than time and rent attention.
- Commoditised offers (standard monthly retainers, generic services) fail on paid traffic because
  cold audiences compare them with cheaper competitors. Paid ads need a differentiated offer.
- Front-end cash liquidators (a 6-week challenge, or a setup fee with product upsells) recover ad
  cost at once and let you outspend competitors.
- Cost per lead rises as channels mature; scaling depends on growing LTV, not hoping for cheaper
  leads.

## Paid ads: testing and scaling budgets

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** ad-funded business · **Signals:** S-44.3, S-44.4, S-44.7, S-66.1 · **Files:** q44, q66

- **Testing budget:** let a new ad spend up to 2x the gross cash a new customer generates in their
  first 30 days before judging it. Example: $100 cash profit in 30 days, so up to $200. q44 states
  the base as "gross cash collected", then gives the example as "cash profit".
- **Kill rule:** zero leads after spending 1x the 30-day cash (e.g. $100), shut it off.
- **Scaling budget:** Target Monthly Spend = (Desired Customers x CAC) x 1.20, the 20% padding for
  scaling inefficiency.
- **30-day payback ("client-financed acquisition"):** if a customer pays more than the cost to
  acquire and fulfil them in the first 30 days, ad spend can be recycled without limit using
  30-day credit card windows. q66: collect 100%+ of CAC in gross profit within 30 days.
- The top 0.1% of digital advertisers split-test 11 times more creatives than average (q66, no
  source for the cohort).

## Paid ads: his reported returns

**Type:** CLM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** his ad accounts; one gym; an agency · **Signals:** S-44.8, S-44.9, S-44.10, S-44.12 · **Files:** q44, q66

- Lifetime return on ad spend across all portfolio companies: 36:1 average.
- Agency offer case: from a $1,000 retainer to a $3,997 setup fee with performance pricing, front

- Cost per lead: q44 was asked and gave none (`[A]`). The only per-lead cost in the set is q39's
  $75 ad + $25 fulfilment = $100 per engaged lead call.

## Customer referrals: goodwill, incentives and scripts

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general; B2C at checkout · **Signals:** S-46.1 to S-46.8 · **Files:** q46, q65, q66

- **Goodwill:** Value - Price = Goodwill, which drives referral volume.
- **Incentives.** One-sided: pay your average CAC to the referrer or the friend. Two-sided: split
  the CAC (half to the referrer as cash or credit, half to the friend as a discount). Referral
  events: 1 to 4 week events (Bring-a-Friend, Spouse Challenge) with points, credits or prizes.
  Non-cash: VIP perks, status, extra support, free products ("Sell 3, get it free").
- **"Not IF, but WHO" at checkout:** "People who do our program with someone else tend to get 3
  times the results. Who else could do this program with you?" The "3 times" is inside the
  script, not a study.
- **Hinge method:** a 3-way text or group chat with the referring client and the prospect (q65
  cites $100M Leads part 3). Script using it: "I can't do anything less than [Price], but if you
  make a 3-way text introduction to 3 friends right now, I'd be happy to cut that initiation
  fee."
- **Numbers:** asking at the point of sale adds 25% to 30% more sign-ups. If every customer brings
  2 referrals, 4:1 LTGP:CAC becomes 12:1. Growth equation: % monthly referrals - % monthly churn =
  % monthly compounding growth (q66: the golden rule of organic compounding).
- **Third-party cases he cites:** Dropbox 39x'd in 15 months; PayPal reached 100M users with $10
  give / $10 get.

## Affiliates: qualifying, payout tiers and offers

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** affiliate programs; local service via partners · **Signals:** S-46.10 to S-46.14, S-46.17 · **Files:** q46

- **Who:** independent businesses with warm audiences of your ideal customers. Offer them a fast,
  simple way to make money or add value for their customers.
- **Skin in the game:** they buy product in bulk or pay for certification or onboarding; charge
  10% to 20% of what an average affiliate makes in Year 1.
- **3-tier payout on maximum allowable CAC:** Tier 1, 25% of CAC for signing up; Tier 2, 50% for
  activation (training done or campaign launched); Tier 3, 100% for sustained performance (e.g. 5
  active clients). Pay in product credit at retail value, which costs only hard cost.
- **"Peel off a piece" offer:** "Sell 3 of my [services/products] to your clients for whatever you
  want over $300. You keep 100% of the money—I just fulfill the service." You earn on the upsell.
- **Minimum order line:** "So, do you want anything extra or do you just want the minimum order?"
- **3 integration strategies:** (1) the affiliate gives your lead magnet free with their product;
  (2) the affiliate sells your lead magnet (e.g. a workshop or an audit) and keeps 100% of the
  front-end cash, while you upsell during fulfilment; (3) the affiliate sells your core offer for
  an ongoing or lifetime commission.
- **Unit economics (worked example):** spend $4,000 to acquire an affiliate who generates $120,000
  in sales ($90,000 gross profit); after payouts, $54,000 net gross profit, 13.5:1 LTGP:CAC
  (implies $36,000 in payouts).

## Employees for lead generation

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** business with payroll · **Signals:** S-47.1 to S-47.4, S-47.8 to S-47.11 · **Files:** q47, q65

- Employees scale lead generation past the founder's own time.
- **Fewer, better:** one A-player marketer can produce 10x to 100x a B or C player. Keep marketing
  teams tight, skilled and well paid.
- **3Ds of training:** Document (step-by-step checklist), Demonstrate (do it in front of them),
  Duplicate (they do it in front of you with the document until they reproduce the result). q65
  cites $100M Leads part 9 and two videos.
- **Staff ROI:** total payroll / total engaged leads = cost per lead; then track CAC against LTGP.
- **Master the core first:** acquiring customers and delivering are the two core functions; learn
  to acquire customers yourself before delegating.
- **Mindset swaps:** "If you want it done right, do it yourself" becomes "If you want it done
  right, get someone to spend all their time doing it". "No one can replace me" becomes "Everyone
  is replaceable, especially me".
- A business that runs only on the founder's labour is a risky job; replacing yourself makes it an
  asset with higher valuation multiples.
- **Time audit:** track a full week; group tasks taking 20% to 40% of the schedule into one
  full-time role and hire for it.

## Agencies: the trap and hire-to-learn

**Type:** WRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** a client hiring an agency · **Signals:** S-47.5, S-47.6, S-47.7 · **Files:** q47

- **The agency trap:** agencies put senior reps on onboarding, then hand the account to junior
  reps, bringing falling performance and permanent dependence.
- **Hire to learn and replace:** a purpose (learn a platform or method) and a deadline (typically 3
  to 6 months). Tell the agency up front you are paying to learn their decision-making. Record
  every call, document their methods, train an internal employee side by side, and when the
  internal team beats them, move the agency to a lower-cost consulting role.
- **Choosing one:** peer referrals from founders with real results, prominent case studies, simple
  metric tracking, premium pricing (top agencies are expensive).

Written from the buyer's side.

## Sweep: frameworks q65 names that sit outside the offer lanes

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** mixed · **Signals:** S-65.1 to S-65.8, S-65.10 to S-65.14 · **Files:** q65

- **4 product criteria for high margins:** Unique, Expensive, Sticky (recurring), Air (low or no
  marginal fulfilment cost). Video: "If I Wanted To Become a Millionaire in 2026, This Is What I'd
  Do [FULL BLUEPRINT]".
- **What, Who, When (ad copy):** What = value elements and hidden costs; Who = status effects on
  the prospect and peers; When = past, present and future. $100M Leads part 6.
- **Five levels of awareness** (Eugene Schwartz, which he teaches): Unaware, Problem Aware,
  Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware.
- **3Ps of niche selection:** a Pain you overcame, a Passion, a Profession.
- **Three pillars of a business:** Acquisition, Delivery, Operations (finance, HR, legal).
- **Management diamond** (fixing underperformance): Knowledge (did not know), Skill (did not know
  how), Timeline (did not know when), Motivation (did not know why), External blockers.
- **Four Rs of customer success, in order:** Retain, Review, Refer, Resell. Video: "The Man That
  Makes Millionaires".
- **Maker versus manager** (Paul Graham's terms): uninterrupted creation time apart from
  fragmented meeting time.
- **TEAM for partners:** Time, Expertise, Assets or Money. Video: "How to Start a Business From
  Nothing (Thank Me Later)".
- **4 elements of equity:** Control, Risk, Profits, Sale or Enterprise Value.
- **3 buckets for reinvesting profit:** more customers; higher LTV or gross profit per customer;
  less risk.
- **Entrepreneur life cycle** (Kelley and Conner): Uninformed Optimism, Informed Pessimism, Valley
  of Despair, Informed Optimism, Goal Achievement.
- **3 ways to grow:** more customers, higher average purchase value, more purchases per customer.
  q65 cites $100M Offers part 2.

## Sweep: numbers q66 gives as rules

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** mixed · **Signals:** S-66.2 to S-66.12 · **Files:** q66

- **80%+ gross margin** target for service and digital products.
- **20%+ net profit margin** baseline; at a 20% net margin, a 20% price rise doubles net profit
  (if volume and costs hold).
- **50% discount threshold:** a discount must be 50% or more to change demand; 10% rarely does.
- **SMB pricing:** $400/month or less for SMB "nuisance" software or services, 30 to 40 month
  stick; $1,500 to $3,000/month is the "dead zone", highest churn, 4 to 6 month stick. US
  figures. The dead zone was not found in the transcribed audio (Pass 2 §2), so book or video
  origin is `[A]`.
- **14-day cash collected:** the cash a client generates in their first 14 days, the key
  onboarding KPI.
- **1 in 3 (33%)** close rate target on qualified calls.
- **25% to 30%** baseline for landing-page opt-ins or booked-call show-up (one range for two
  measures; also the referral-lift range).
- **6-week or 90-day cycles** as the target length for front-end transformation offers.
- **$1,000 management authority:** managers may spend up to $1,000 to fix a customer or
  operational issue without asking.
- **Price's Law** (Derek de Solla Price): the square root of the headcount produces 50% of output.
- **Time horizons:** 3 to 5 years to scale location models; 10 years to build $100M+ enterprise
  value (the split between the two is Pass 1's reading).
