## Scarcity and urgency: definitions and why they work

**Type:** MEC · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-22.1, S-22.2 · **Files:** q22, q23, q24

- Scarcity is a function of quantity. Urgency is a function of time.
- Scarcity unlocks pricing power because people are driven far more by fear of loss than by desire for gain (FOMO).
- Decreasing supply while demand stays constant or grows shifts the supply-demand curve, allowing more sales at higher prices. (q22's "higher sales volume at dramatically higher price points" is Gemini's phrasing; less supply cannot raise unit volume.)
- Deadlines drive decisions.

Naval Ravikant, quoted: "Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want." To keep desire ravenous in the customer base, keep supply and the satisfaction of desire below the demand you generate.

## Scarcity: seven ways to limit supply

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general; service examples sized as small agencies · **Signals:** S-22.4 · **Files:** q22, q24

q24's seven:
1. **Total business cap:** a maximum number of active clients at one time, with a waitlist, squeezing out lower-margin accounts for higher-paying ones.
2. **Growth rate cap:** new clients onboarded per week.
3. **Cohort cap:** clients per class, group or launch.
4. **Limited supply of bonuses:** bonus items only for the first X buyers.
5. **Never available again:** unique products, colours or flavours never produced again.
6. **Ultra-exclusive 1-on-1 access** via Zoom, phone, DMs or voice notes, capped at a tiny number at premium prices.
7. **"Once you're out, never return" policy:** a client who cancels or leaves is permanently barred from rejoining, so clients think hard before quitting.

q22 groups the same into five offer types: physical products and limited drops; service capacity caps (total, growth rate, cohort); bonus scarcity; extreme access and "never return"; lead-magnet scarcity.

The seven are Gemini's breakdown of the three types plus two extras. Item 7 is a churn lever, not a sales lever; whether he presents it as scarcity or retention is unchecked.

## Scarcity: honest capacity number and the four rules

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [D] for the 81% line, [M*] for the rules · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-22.11, S-24.3 · **Files:** q22, q24, q25, q26

Four rules to make the limit real:
1. **Honest scarcity:** base limits on real operational constraints. Define the number of clients you or your team can comfortably onboard (q22: "in a week or month"; q25, q26: per week), for example 5, 10 or 25, and advertise that capacity. His line, matched to Offers part 5: "81% of capacity in your total business" [D]. q24's wording [M*]: "We are at 81% capacity."
2. **Always sell out:** better to offer a smaller quantity and sell out completely than to over-offer and miss the limit.
3. **Publicly announce when sold out** to the whole market: social proof, validates value, triggers fear of missing out for the next release.
4. **Resist satisfying all demand:** fulfilling 100% of demand destroys pent-up desire; keeping prospects slightly unsatisfied makes them act faster and pay more at the next promotion.

Rules 2 and 4 assume a demand surplus.

## Urgency script: rolling cohorts

**Type:** SCR · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** service or program business · **Signals:** S-23.2, S-23.3, S-11.3 · **Files:** q11, q23, q24

Clients start in groups on a fixed cadence (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly or quarterly) rather than continuously. Set a firm kickoff date:

> "Our next group starts Monday."
> "If you sign up today, you start with our Monday group; otherwise, you must wait for the next kickoff date."
> q11, marked "e.g.": "Next cohort starts Monday."

Miss the cutoff and wait for the following cohort. Cohorts also let you build a structured, choreographed onboarding process.

**Someone wants to buy after the cutoff:**
- Option A (fast-action bonus): offer a speedy personalised 1-on-1 onboarding session as a bonus to catch them up with the current group.
- Option B (preferred): hold the deadline firm, enrol them in the next cohort, give them extra time to review prep materials, and offer an extended payment plan since the start date is further out.

## Urgency: pricing deadline, the pipeline clean-out

**Type:** SCR · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** any business with a lead pipeline · **Signals:** S-23.5, S-16.4 · **Files:** q16, q17, q23, q24

When raising prices, notify every active lead or fence-sitter in the pipeline that prices go up on a specific date (q16: for example end of the week or month). Anyone who signs before the cutoff locks in the old rate. The prospect can still buy later but loses the special price.

It shows a position of strength and triggers an influx of upfront cash from procrastinating leads. It needs a real planned price increase to stay honest.

## Fake scarcity and fake urgency: the warning

**Type:** WRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-24.1, S-24.2 · **Files:** q23, q24, q25, q26, q35

Fake forms named: countdown timers that reset on refresh, fabricated stock numbers, false expiration dates, artificial capacity limits, deadlines continuously reset without a change of campaign.

What it costs:
1. Destroys credibility and authority: prospects realise the limits are fabricated and you "just look like every other wannabe marketer".
2. Destroys long-term profits, lower net revenue over time: "you'll lose money. So definitely use it the right way". q26 adds that destroyed trust kills repeat business, referrals and brand equity (possibly Gemini's elaboration).
3. Pent-up desire vanishes and future promotions fail once prospects catch on.

Manufactured scarcity cannot replicate the authentic "zero-fucks-given vibe" that occurs when an owner has a superior product and truly does not need or beg for a prospect's money.
