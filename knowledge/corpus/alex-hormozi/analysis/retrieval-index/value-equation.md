## Value equation: the formula and its four drivers

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [D] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-01.1, S-01.2, S-17.4 · **Files:** q01, q04, q05, q07, q17

Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice).

- The two numerator drivers are increased: Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood of Achievement.
- The two denominator drivers are decreased toward zero: Time Delay and Effort and Sacrifice.
- The equation sets how much you can charge and how easily prospects say yes.
- As a pricing rule (q17): raise price by raising the top (dream outcome, track record, proof) and driving the bottom (time delay, effort and sacrifice) toward zero.

Why division and not addition: if Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice reaches zero, value becomes infinite. His hypotheticals: click buy on a weight-loss product and instantly see a six-pack; sign a marketing contract and qualified prospects ring your phone immediately.

## Value equation: lazy top side, winning bottom side

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general (Apple, Amazon, Netflix named) · **Signals:** S-01.4, S-35.7 · **Files:** q01, q03, q35

Beginner marketers lazily make bigger claims on the top of the equation. q03 phrases it as "making larger or exaggerated claims" being a lazy marketer tactic. World-class businesses (Apple, Amazon, Netflix) win by removing time delay and effort on the bottom.

Warning form (q35): focusing only on the top (bigger dream-outcome promises, more social proof) while ignoring time delay and effort and sacrifice. Bigger promises are easy and lazy and become uncredible in an over-hyped market. The most valuable businesses drive effort, sacrifice and time delay toward zero.

## Value equation: psychological solutions over logical ones

**Type:** MEC · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general (public infrastructure examples) · **Signals:** S-01.5 · **Files:** q01, q05

Logical solutions have usually been tried already, so psychological solutions often give the highest perceived value. q05 frames these as the lever "when physical time cannot be cut".

| Case | Logical solution | Psychological solution |
|---|---|---|
| London Underground | spend billions to make trains marginally faster | a dotted map showing train arrival times; cost "a few million" and raised rider satisfaction more |
| Elevator | a faster elevator | floor-to-ceiling mirrors so people look at themselves |
| Train | a faster train | hire models to host the trip so passengers wish it took longer |
| Product pricing | make it cheaper | make fewer units and raise the price to increase desirability |

q05 says the map removed the anxiety and boredom of waiting. The London figures are imprecise in both files ("a few million", "billions").

## Dream outcome: how to choose it

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-03.1, S-06.10 · **Files:** q01, q02, q03, q07, q11

Dream Outcome (numerator, increase): the feelings and experiences the prospect has envisioned; the gap between current reality and their dreams. How to choose it:

1. Channel existing, unchanging desire. Do not create desire.
2. Sell the destination, not the vehicle or "plane flight". Nobody wants a gym membership or boot camp; they want to lose weight and feel good.
3. Anchor it in one of three core human markets: Health, Wealth, Relationships. Every enduring market hooks into one of these three.
4. Aim at universal desires: to be perceived as beautiful, respected, powerful, loved, and to increase status.

q02 and q03 describe these as desires "wars are fought over and marriages are lost over".

## Dream outcome: status and the viewpoint of others

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-03.2, S-03.3, S-03.4, S-03.5, S-03.6 · **Files:** q01, q02, q03

**Making the dream outcome bigger** (q03):
1. Depict their exact envisioned destination back to them so they feel understood, then show how the vehicle gets them there.
2. Tie it to a perceived increase in status, not to bigger claims.
3. Pair it with lower time delay and effort: the big outcome sets the ceiling and lower friction makes perceived value explode. (Point 3 may be NotebookLM joining two sections.)

**Status** is the perceived increase or decrease in relative standing compared with others, socially or professionally. The dream outcomes that most directly raise a prospect's status are the ones they value most.

**Copy rule:** frame benefits from the viewpoint of others. Connect the dots on how other people will perceive the prospect's achievement.

**Golf club script** (full in q02 and q03; q01 gives only the first two sentences):
> "If you buy this golf club, your drive will increase by 40 yards. Your golf buddies' jaws will drop when they see your ball soar 40 yards past theirs. They will ask you what's changed. Only you will know."

**Status examples:** men generally value offers that make them money over offers that make them look handsome, because money drives status more for men. Colette Brunson (Russell Brunson's wife) prefers a minivan over a Lamborghini: a Lamborghini would lower her status among her mom friends; a minivan shows she is a good mom. Vehicles for the desire for beauty (q01): makeup, anti-aging serums, supplements, shapewear, plastic surgery, fitness.

## Perceived likelihood: definition and the surgeon test

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-04.1, S-04.2 · **Files:** q01, q04

Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (numerator, increase): how likely the prospect believes they are to get the result if they buy. People pay for certainty. Raising conviction raises perceived value and price even when the fulfillment work is identical.

Plastic surgeon: compare paying to be a surgeon's 10,000th patient against their 1st. A sane person pays far more for the 10,000th and might demand to be paid to be the 1st, though both surgeries take the same time (the experienced surgeon is likely faster). The track record is what raises value.

## Perceived likelihood: tools to raise it

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-04.3, S-04.4, S-04.9, S-04.12 · **Files:** q01, q02, q04, q05, q07

Two lists exist:

- **q04, five tools:** (1) guarantees and risk reversal; (2) specific mechanisms and fulfillment vehicles (DIY to DWY to DFY); (3) hard proof and verified track record; (4) authority, implied demand, third-party edification; (5) testimonials and peer case studies. This grouping mirrors the question's own list; treat the count as unconfirmed.
- **q01, six items:** proof, track record, case studies, guarantees, messaging, third-party edification.

**Risk:** the single biggest obstacle in sales. A guarantee raises certainty by moving the burden of failure from buyer to seller.

**Vehicle shift:** moving from DIY to DWY or DFY raises perceived likelihood because prospects believe an expert doing the work makes the outcome more certain. DWY tools named: 5-minute meal prep calculators, turn-key ad scripts, restaurant eating-out guides, so clients feel they cannot fail. The same move also cuts effort and sacrifice.

**Authority and implied demand:** Dr. Oz, Dr. Phil, Oprah; people assume demand and expertise, which lets them charge very high rates. Specialized consultants charge $50,000 for a single day because they hold the key to a rare problem worth millions.

## Time delay: long-term outcome and short-term wins

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general, with gym and weight-loss cases · **Signals:** S-05.1, S-05.2, S-05.3 · **Files:** q01, q05, q07

Time Delay (denominator, decrease): the time between purchase and receiving the promised benefit. Two parts:

- **Long-term outcome:** what they buy ("fast results").
- **Short-term experience or milestones:** what keeps them long enough to reach it ("fast feelings of progress").

The two labels in quotes come from the question. Clients buy the long-term dream and stay because of short-term wins delivered as close to the purchase date as possible; early wins confirm they made the right decision.

**Gym Launch case:** adding $239,000/year to a gym takes time (q05: 11 months), so clients got ads live and closed their first $2,000 sale within 7 days to build immediate trust.

**Weight-loss case:** a more aggressive initial diet, or pairing the client at once with a "grocery buddy" or community member, gives physical and social wins before the 12 to 24 month body change finishes.

## Effort and sacrifice: fitness against liposuction

**Type:** NUM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** consumer fitness against surgery · **Signals:** S-05.5 · **Files:** q01, q05, q07

Effort and Sacrifice (denominator, decrease): the tangible and intangible costs along the way: time, physical and mental discomfort, hunger, embarrassment, risk, inconvenience.

**Fitness:** time delay 12 to 24 months for a bikini body. Effort: waking 1 to 2 hours earlier, losing 5 to 10 hours per week, giving up favorite foods, constant hunger, soreness, gym embarrassment, injury risk, literal nausea, meal prepping, expensive groceries, new clothing, fear of regaining weight. Sellers "arm wrestle" prospects for $100/month, 1/10th or 1/100th of the surgery price.

**Liposuction with tummy tuck:** done in an afternoon for $25,000 paid upfront. Fall asleep, wake up thin, sore for 2 to 4 weeks.

Three fitness prices appear across the corpus for three different products: $100/month (boot camp, here), $99/month (boot camp, dream-outcome section), $29/month (big-box gym). Keep them apart.

## Time delay and effort: techniques to cut them

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general, with his fitness business · **Signals:** S-05.6, S-05.7, S-05.8 · **Files:** q05, q07, q10

q05's four techniques:
1. Shift the vehicle DIY to DWY to DFY. DFY removes client effort and raises perceived likelihood.
2. Psychological levers when physical time cannot be cut (London map, elevator mirrors).
3. Build one-to-many, low-cost, high-value assets (calculators, pre-made Instacart carts, 5-minute guides, buddy systems) that remove client effort at near-zero ongoing cost.
4. Remove "one-thing" objections that stop a client starting or continuing.

q07 also gives four items, but its item 4 is "trim and stack" and it adds the delivery cube as its own item. The grouping is NotebookLM's.

**Eating Out Guide story:** he almost lost a gym sale to a business executive who ate out for lunch every day. Instead of making her cook, he built an Eating Out Guide for restaurant menus. It turned the lost sale into a client and became a permanent bonus for later sales.

**Macro sheet:** he spent 100 hours building an Excel macro sheet that generated 100 custom macro-based meal plans and Instacart grocery lists automatically. After that each personalized plan took 15 minutes to produce, and was sold at premium rates. q34 adds that the same assets were later reused by 4,000+ gyms and hundreds of thousands of fitness clients. Whether "100 custom" means the macro covers 100 variants is not said.
