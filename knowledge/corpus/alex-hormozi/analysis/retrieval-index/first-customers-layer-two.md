## Service front ends: salty pretzel, sealant, free bribe

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** local services, home services, gym · **Signals:** S-82.1, S-82.6, S-82.17, S-82.5 · **Files:** q82

- **Salty pretzel rule.** A free or low-cost front end gives a complete solution to a narrow
  problem; solving it triggers a new, immediate need that the higher-ticket core offer solves
  (salty pretzels at a bar make people thirsty for a paid drink). Likely layer one ($100M Leads);
  see `free-work-trials-and-first-customers.md` § Lead magnet: never solve the core problem.
- **Free wood sealant** (garage door, home restoration): apply the first coat free, show that full
  weather protection needs 3 coats, sell coats 2 and 3 as a paid bundle.
- **Free bribe (SCR):** "Our 6-week challenge is $1,000, or sign up as a member for $200/month and
  get the $1,000 challenge for free."
- **Internal plays:** specialised mini-programs for existing customers 4 times a year at about 98%
  gross margin, for fast low-cost revenue.

## Consulting and agency front ends

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** consulting, small agency, local SMB agency · **Signals:** S-82.7 to S-82.10, S-82.13 to S-82.15, S-83.13 · **Files:** q82, q83

Several of these cannot be tied to Money Models or Lost Chapters and may come from layer-one
videos (S-82.20).

- **Commodity retainer (WRN).** The agency retainer of $1,000 down plus $1,000/month forces price
  comparisons and causes high churn. Replace retainers with a one-time setup fee and
  pay-for-performance (cost per show or per lead).
- **Pay-per-show (SCR):** "$4,000 one-time setup, no monthly retainer. Cover ad spend, pay only when
  people show up; guaranteed 20 people in month 1 or next month free; includes daily sales coaching
  and complete sales playbooks for free."
- **Agency-to-consulting hybrid:** teach the client's team to run ads and outreach for $750/hour or
  a high upfront fee over 6 months, then move them to a low-cost consulting retainer.
- **Free agency window:** provide agency fulfilment free for a set window (for example 4 months, or
  an initial launch campaign), collecting only cash from backend sales. The "4 months" has no
  worked example in the answer; the only concrete case is Gym Launch's 21-day launch.
- **Free short-video trial (SCR):** "I will edit short-form videos from your existing long-form
  content and post them for you for free—you just say yes." Convert to a paying monthly client
  once value is proven. q83 uses it as the example of removing hidden costs.
- **Local SMB prices:** $300 to $500/month for Google Maps top-3 ranking or review management (low
  barrier, high stick rate); website-as-a-service at $450/month.
- **Consulting front ends:** $100M Scaling Roadmap ($0), a free constraint-assessment tool that
  outputs a personalised report and offers an optional 1-on-1 strategy call (Acquisition.com's own);
  a free real estate book ($0 plus shipping, about $16 to $17) upselling audiobooks, contract
  templates, deal-sourcing training and zero-down financing; a $1,000 stack of a book bundled with
  free 1-on-1 sales call reviews, funnel write-ups and a 2-hour offer consultation.
- **3 to 5 hours of free help (q83):** give 3 to 5 hours of 1-on-1 consulting free to reach a
  specific result, then pitch a paid long-term engagement at the end.

## Friction: when free brings the wrong leads

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general · **Signals:** S-82.2 · **Files:** q82, q83, q84

If a free offer brings too many unqualified leads or creates operational drag, add friction:
qualification questions, a deposit, a longer application, or a nominal fee. q84 adds that friction
cuts raw lead volume but raises total revenue and profit. This is the "add friction" step of
"create flow, monetize flow, then add friction" (`money-model.md` § Customer financed acquisition:
the order to pull the levers).

## First avatar: picking the first customer type

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** general; new business · **Signals:** S-84.2, S-84.3, S-84.9 · **Files:** q84

Three rules for the first customer type (Lost Chapters ch. 2, "Your First Avatar"):
1. **Start with what you know:** an industry or niche from past jobs, family, friends or experience,
   so you speak their language.
2. **Pick who you can help most:** the narrowest group you can deliver the most value to.
3. **Pick customers who can afford to pay.** You can charge far more because of who the customer is
   rather than who you are.

**Sales page designer example (q84).** Lifting a sales page's conversion from 5% to 7%:
- Company A, $100,000/month revenue: adds $40,000/year in value; at a 10% fee you earn
  $4,000/year.
- Company B, $10,000,000/month revenue: adds $4,000,000/month ($48M/year); at a 10% fee you earn
  $4,000,000/year.

The units are mixed. A 5% to 7% lift is a 40% revenue rise, so Company A gains $40,000 a month,
not a year, and 10% of Company B's $48M a year is $4.8M, not $4M. The point survives: the same work
earns about 100x the fee.

**For a business with no customers yet** `[A]`: not covered beyond these three rules. The four-step
method below starts by surveying existing customers.

## First avatar: the four-step survey method

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** business with existing customers · **Signals:** S-84.1, S-84.4, S-84.5, S-84.6, S-102.1 · **Files:** q84, q102

**Origin.** Inspired by the private equity firm Vista, which scores a target company's current
customers on who stays longest and spends most, cuts acquisition channels that bring low-value
customers, and doubles down on channels that bring top spenders. Applying 80/20 (replace the bottom
80% with top-20% spenders) scales profit without adding operational chaos. q102 names
Acquisition.com's version VAM, the "Value Acceleration Method", in the same chapter; it may be the
name for this process.

**Step 1, survey existing customers** by form, or live on a call or at an event, with a perk for
completing. Four categories:
- A. **Demographics:** age, gender, political affiliation, geographic location, digital location,
  marital status, business structure (solopreneur, partner in business).
- B. **Business stats, before and current:** revenue, profit, employees, churn, pricing, products,
  customer LTV, number of customers, niche, time in business.
- C. **Aspirations:** their main goal when buying; the specific problem they were solving.
- D. **Buying process:** the single best reason they bought; any trigger event; specific content
  or testimonials consumed and how many pieces; timeline from first hearing to buying; where they
  first saw you or whether they were referred.

**Step 2, find the biggest spenders.** Sort responses by the customers you like most, who spend
most and stay longest. Focus only on the top 20%.

**Step 3, see what they have in common.** Find the fewest common qualifiers, typically 3 to 5.

**Step 4, execute.**
- 4A. Speak to the new avatar: spell out customer requirements in ads, landing pages and content;
  stop selling to anyone who does not meet them; double down on channels where they are.
- 4B. Re-engineer the sales process to recreate, for every new lead, the buying journey your best
  customers took.

**What to build for the avatar.** Specificity over generics in offer, message and fulfilment
("Specificity is what gives copy its edge"); keep qualification steps; build a value-in-advance
buyer journey that feeds prospects educational assets in the media types your top buyers consumed.

## First avatar: the Gym Launch case

**Type:** CLM · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** Gym Launch · **Signals:** S-84.7, S-84.8, S-100.2 · **Files:** q84, Q100

**Top-20% avatar (BIO):** right-leaning or conservative, married, male, 25 to 45, US gym or business
owner. Signed lease, 1+ employees, $10,000+/month revenue, at least 30 existing members.
Aspirations: grow a $1M+ gym, work less, open more locations. Buying reasons: lack of leads, bad
local market, bad pricing, cannot keep good staff.

78% of top customers had consumed at least two pieces of long-form content before buying.

What they did: stated the minimum requirements in ads and landing pages; rejected unqualified
owners; made every lead consume two long-form pieces before booking a call; gave the sales team a
"greatest hits" content list to send.

**Results (his figures, no record):** customer lifetime gross profit rose from the industry average
of $6,000 to $8,000 to over $45,000. An unnamed competitor that served "anyone with a pulse and a
credit card" made 70x less profit on the same monthly sales volume, because of high churn, low
prices and high CAC. Q100 calls this the "expensive customer problem".

## Layer two on audits: free diagnostic, not a product

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*]; the paid audit [A] · **Scale:** service lead magnets · **Signals:** S-83.1 to S-83.7, S-105.1, S-83.14, S-105.3 · **Files:** q83, q105

Layer one holds the audit material: `free-work-trials-and-first-customers.md` § Audit or problem
reveal: the CAC math, and § Paid audit: what he says. Layer two repeats it and adds little.

What layer two confirms:
- Audits, assessments and diagnostics are "reveal a problem" lead magnets. They measure the gap
  (the "delta") between where the prospect is and could be, which creates deprivation and urgency.
  They work best on problems that worsen while waiting (slow websites, posture decay, active pests,
  back taxes, falling cash flow). The diagnostic solves only identification, which reveals a broader
  problem only the core paid offer fixes.
- Never solve the core problem with the audit.
- Only give free audits to qualified prospects: put application questions or required dropdowns
  (revenue minimum, budget, authority) in front of it.
- Be specific on the negative, vague on the positive: high specificity about the prospect's exact
  pain builds trust; you can be broader about the outcome.
- **The numbers, same as layer one:** 1 hour of labour at $25 hard cost; perceived value $250+,
  offered at $0; 1 in 4 recipients buys the core offer; CAC = $25 x 4 = $100. Profitable if the core
  offer's margin exceeds $100.
- **q105's structure:** (1) qualify first, budget and decision-maker; (2) deliver it (about 1 hour
  of labour) to show how bad the problem is or how much they are losing; (3) move straight to the
  core paid service.
- **The mechanic frame (SCR, q83):** "It would be unethical for me to answer what's wrong with your
  business/body without looking under the hood first."
- **Diagnostic examples (q83):** free website speed test (every second of load time loses 3% of
  conversions, unsourced) leading to website optimisation; free posture or alignment test leading
  to chiropractic or physical therapy; free termite or home inspection leading to extermination or
  repair; free financial or tax audit leading to bookkeeping; free strategy call (BANT triage)
  leading to a second sales call; $19 heavy metals test leading to a $2,100 10-week detox; free
  nutrition orientation plus a free digital guide after a prospect declines a gym membership,
  upselling supplement bundles.

**What layer two adds:** the one priced diagnostic, the $19 test in a two-step sale `[D]` (see
`attraction-offers.md` § Two-step sale: the $19 diagnostic), whose only job is to cut no-shows.

**Paid audit as its own product** `[A]`: not in the sources. No price for a stand-alone audit, no
margin for one, no rule for setting an audit price against the core offer. Second fresh query to
find nothing (q83, q105). Pass 2 L2.2 keeps it `[A]`.

## Layer two on free work and the free five

**Type:** PRN · **Prov:** [D] for "matters less"; rest [M*] · **Scale:** new offer, no track record · **Signals:** S-82.4, S-83.8 to S-83.12, S-107.1 to S-107.5 · **Files:** q82, q83, q107, q108

Layer one holds this with the matched audio: `free-work-trials-and-first-customers.md` § First five
free: the number and why, § First five free: the three terms of exchange, § Price step-up after the
free five: 80/60/40/20, § Price step-up: raise 20% every five sales, § Free to paid: the transition
script, § Hidden costs.

**How many free clients.** q107 calls 5 "The Exact Number" ("5 free case studies"); q82 names it the
"Rule of First 5"; q83 repeats it. Question-shaped: the "exact" wording is the model's. His Leads
part 3 audio `[D]`: "The exact number matters less than knowing why you benefit from it." Neither
layer-two audiobook restates it (Pass 2 L2.2, L2.3).

**The ladder, as q107 and q108 give it** (they agree on every step):

| Clients | Price |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 | 100% free, for reviews and testimonials |
| 6 to 10 | 80% off retail |
| 11 to 15 | 60% off |
| 16 to 20 | 40% off |
| 21 to 25 | 20% off |
| 26 on | full retail, then raise 20% for every 5 new sales until demand softens |

q103 names the last step "the 5-to-5-to-5 rule": a 20% rise after every 5 sales in a row, until
conversion rates start to drop.

**When to start charging (q107, q83):** once your free clients start referring other people, or
capacity fills. Referrals show the service can be delivered and is worth paying for.

**Why start free (q83).** He rejects "never work for free": that view is mostly held by people who
do not make much money; high earners use free work to refine the product, gather proof and get
testimonials. "The objective right now is to learn, not to earn. We must crawl before we run." Four
reasons: you do not know what you are doing yet and need room for error; fastest way to
testimonials; builds sales conviction without a track record; creates lead flow and word of mouth.

**Terms (q83):** free work is a transaction, not a gift. The client must (1) actually use it, (2)
give detailed feedback, (3) leave a killer review or video testimonial if it delivers.

**Hidden costs (q83):** Ben Graham told a young Warren Buffett his offer to work for free was
"overpriced" because training him took Graham's time. When prospects reject free work, it is the
hidden costs (time, effort, inconvenience, sacrifice). Remove them all.

**The pitch (SCR, q107):** "By the way, do you know anybody who is [describe their struggle] looking
to [dream outcome] in [timeframe]? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can
handle. I just want to get some testimonials for my product or service." Then: "Since I'm only
taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And
I'll give it to you all for free so long as you promise to: 1) actually use it, 2) give me feedback,
and 3) leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?" q108 frames it
under ACA (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask), uses "[time delay]" for "[timeframe]" and stops at "...to
get some testimonials...". The [D] version is in layer one.

**Free to paid (SCR, q107):** "I am accepting somebody who's paying full price in order to do this
slot. If you would like me to keep doing this for you, you would have to start paying to match them.
Are you comfortable with that?" If they decline: "No worries at all! Can I just get the killer
review I asked you for?" q83's version: "I have someone willing to pay full price for this slot. If
you'd like to keep working together, you'll need to match them. Are you comfortable with that?" It
relies on real demand for the slot.

**His own record (CLM, no outside record):** he trained workout clients free for a year, asking only
for charity donations or testimonials, and converted them when demand surged (q83). Gym Launch's
original model: $0 upfront turnaround; he flew to gyms, paid for ads, flights and hotels himself,
worked the front desk, closed and fulfilled, and kept 100% of the upfront cash from the 21-day
launch; 33 gyms over 18 months (q82, q83; matches layer one `[D]`). Free case-study video title:
"How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego" (q82).

## Free trial into continuity: the setup script

**Type:** SCR · **Prov:** [M*] · **Scale:** trial into continuity; fitness wording · **Signals:** S-107.6 · **Files:** q107

Set up paid continuity before a free trial starts. Ask: "If this program got you the result, will
you stay long-term?"

If they ask why or hesitate: "I don't want you to just try it; I want you to get results. You're not
going to hit your long-term goals during this trial alone, but you will establish the habits. We're
going to help you do that for free, but if you want to get your long-term results, you're going to
have to stay on after. I just want to make sure you're not looking for a quick fix."

cheap to recurring: activation points and the continuity pitch.

## Layer two on no-ad-budget models

**Type:** FRM · **Prov:** [M*]; the money model with no ad budget [A] · **Scale:** $0 ad budget; one person · **Signals:** S-108.1 to S-108.12, S-101.7 · **Files:** q103, q108

Most of q108 is $100M Leads material that layer one already holds (`leads-and-outreach.md`,
`leads-content-ads-referrals.md`). Layer two adds the referral-at-checkout figure and the
bargaining-chip rule.

**Replace money with time and labour** through two unpaid channels: outreach (direct, linear,
predictable: 100 inputs give a fixed output) and referrals or word of mouth (exponential: 1 customer
→ 2 → 4 → 8). Without ad spend you cannot buy reach; you earn it with manual volume, which is why the
rules are daily counts of actions, not budgets.

**Rule of 100.** 100 primary actions a day for 100 days in a row.
- Warm: 100 personalised messages, emails, calls or DMs a day to people you know or have permission
  to contact.
- Cold: 100 cold reach-outs a day (calls, cold emails, DMs, door knocks or flyers) to targeted lists.
- Never set a daily cold outreach goal below 100 attempts, for 100 days minimum.
- q103's other forms: 100 minutes of content a day, $100/day in ad spend, 100 warm messages a day.
  100 days is the least time to run a channel before judging or changing it.
- His flier story (CLM, q101): 300 fliers on cars gave 0 leads and 1 call complaining about a
  scratched car; a mentor told him he had tested with 150,000 fliers.

**Warm outreach funnel (q108).** 100 warm reach-outs → 20 replies (20%) → 4 take the free offer (20%)
→ 1 converts to paying on the back end. 500 reach-outs a week = 5 paying customers a week. At a $400
offer: $2,000/week = $104,000 a year, zero ad spend. 100 → 1 is 1% overall; 4 → 1 is the same 1 in 4
as the free audit.

**Cold benchmark:** expect 1% to 3% of cold contacts to become engaged leads (not customers).

**Referrals:**
- Ask at purchase, on the contract or during onboarding. Asking at checkout regularly adds 25% to 30%
  of sign-ups as referrals.
- Once someone is a customer, do not ask if they know someone. Ask who they know.
- Bargaining chip: if a prospect asks for a discount, cut the price only in exchange for 3 direct
  text introductions to qualified friends.
- Two-sided incentive: pay up to your maximum CAC as store credit, cash or gift cards, split between
  the person referring and the friend.
- Referral growth equation: monthly growth % = % referring − % churning (`money-model.md` § Churn
  and lifespan: the 5% target and net organic growth).

**High-ticket projection (CLM, not usable as given).** 100 reach-outs a day ("$700/week") brings 2
customers a week at $10,000/year (or $200/month over 4 years); 100 customers at $10,000 = $1M a year.
Pass 2 L2.4 row 24: a projection; $200 × 48 months = $9,600, "$700/week" is unexplained, and
$10,000 a year against $10,000 over 4 years cannot both hold.

**The money model with no ad budget** `[A]`: not covered. Nothing says how the four offer stages or
the 30-day GP ≥ CAC target change when acquisition cost is labour. The nearest formula is cost per
engaged lead, payroll ÷ engaged leads (`money-model.md` § CAC from payroll: cost per engaged lead).
