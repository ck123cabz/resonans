## 1. The model in five lines

1. A buyer weighs **value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice)**. Raise the top, push the bottom toward zero. `[D]` (S-01.1)
2. The ranking of levers is **market, then offer, then persuasion**. A starving crowd beats a good offer, and a good offer beats a good pitch. `[M*]` (S-07.3)
3. A **grand slam offer** is one that cannot be compared with anything else, so the buyer chooses it or nothing. Its five parts: a promotion, a value proposition, a premium price, a guarantee, and a money model that gets you paid to acquire the customer. `[M*]` (S-02.2)
4. Price follows value, and he argues for **high**: low price brings the worst clients and the worst results, high price brings attention and follow-through. `[M*]` (S-12.4 to S-12.6)
5. At the start, **create flow, monetize flow, then add friction**: do the work by hand, carry the risk, collect cash, and only then build systems. `[M*]` (S-33.6; "fast beats free" is `[D]`, S-05.4)

## 2. Building the offer

**The order he teaches.** `[M*]` (S-11.1)
1. Name the dream outcome as a destination, not a vehicle ("lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks", not "$99 a month boot camp").
2. List every problem the buyer meets before, during and after, in time order.
3. Turn each problem into a "How to…" solution.
4. Brainstorm delivery vehicles with no cost limit: one-to-one, small group, one-to-many; do it yourself, with you, for you; live or recorded.
5. Trim and stack: drop high-cost, low-value items; keep low-cost, high-value ones; name each item and put a dollar value on it.
6. Add the enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, naming.

**Two tests worth keeping.**
- *Ten times, one tenth.* If a client paid ten times the price, what would you add? If a client paid a tenth, how would you still get them the result? `[M*]` (S-09.2)
- *The sales-to-fulfilment seesaw.* What is easy to sell (you do everything) is hard to fulfil; what is easy to fulfil (a recording) is hard to sell. Aim for both. `[M*]` (S-09.3)

**The market.** Four tests: massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing. `[M*]` (S-06.1) A resume service for unemployed people failed on purchasing power alone. `[M*]` (S-06.3) Under $10M a year, stay in one tight niche; he says this holds for 99.6% of businesses. `[M*]` (S-06.8) Niching lifts price: a $19 time-management course sold for $1,997 once it named power-tool sales reps. `[M*]` (S-06.6)

**Source caution.** NotebookLM added structure the sources may not hold: the six-lever delivery cube, the five-tool list for likelihood, and a few "e.g." scripts. Treat counts as unconfirmed (Pass 2 §3).

## 3. Pricing

**His case for charging more.** Competing on price can only go to zero, and price can rise without a limit. Cheap brings entitled clients who follow instructions least, so results fall and margins go. Expensive brings people who act. "Those who pay the most pay the most attention." `[M*]` (S-12.1 to S-12.6) He cites a wine study without naming it: three wines with the prices visible, rated in price order. `[D]` (S-12.7)

**Setting the price.** Price the offer so it "stings a little"; aim for perceived value ten times the price; never copy competitors. `[M*]` (S-13.1 to S-13.3) Raise price only after raising value. The gym consulting offer sold at $16,000 for 16 weeks, "three times the highest price player and 32 times more than the lowest" ($500 a month); 35% went on to $42,000 a year. `[D]` (S-12.9) The 32× sets a one-time fee against a monthly one.

**Tiers.** *He does not teach a good-better-best menu for one core offer.* Two fresh questions (q68, q106) found none; the earlier three-tier pyramid came from the question's own wording. What he does teach, checked in Money Models and Lost Chapters: an **anchor upsell** (show a premium version "five to ten times the price first", then the main offer looks like a better deal) `[D]`; a **decoy menu** (The Economist: $59 digital, $125 print-only, $125 digital plus print) `[D]`; and **pick-your-price rungs** above a free base: free, then $99 adds a one-on-one call, $199 adds the supplement handbook, $499 adds a 10-lb guarantee (or credit) `[D]` (Lost Chapters ch. 19). The rungs are the nearest to tiers, and each rung adds access or risk reversal, not more of the same. He also says to open a market at the top and move down (S-68.6).

**The small-business dead zone.** Services to small businesses priced at $1,500 to $3,000 a month churn fast (they stay 4 to 6 months). Go cheap and automated ($300 to $400 a month or less, which stays 29 to 38 months) or go high-touch and high-ticket ($10,000 or performance-based). `[M*]` (S-51.15, S-68.7)

**Worst-month pricing.** Price a recurring fee for the client's worst month. Competitors at $800 to $1,200 a month kept clients 36 to 40 months; his $3,500 a month saw cancellations in bad months. `[M*]` (S-49.10) He cut a back-end price from $800 to $600 a week to lower churn, churn did not move, and he says it cost $5,000,000 a year in profit. `[M*]` (S-62.1, confirmed in a fresh query, S-71)

**Discounts.** Never discount the core offer to close. Add a bonus matched to the objection instead: "I completely understand… if we get you started today I'll throw in [bonus] for free. Sound fair enough?" `[M*]` (S-15.3) Raising prices: tell every open lead the date, so the pipeline buys before it. `[M*]` (S-16.4) Anchoring: to move $2,000 to $3,000, quote $4,000 as $2,000 now plus $2,000 in 30 days, or $3,000 paid today. `[M*]` (S-50.9)

**First-month discounts.** Layer one found none. Lost Chapters ch. 23 has them, in a form that differs from a half-price month: advertise a deep discount on month one and add a setup fee that collects the cash. Two worked cases: a recurring service at "95% off the first month, $1,900 off… first month for $100… still charged a $1,900 setup fee… all in all $2,000, then straight into recurring"; a 12-week program at $3,000 sold as "88% off first month… 120 bucks… then a $1,000 setup fee… $1,120 for the first month" and $1,000 each for the rest. `[D]` He adds that a higher up-front fee lowers churn: a tanning-salon owner charged $100 to join a $10 membership and saw next to none, while members who paid $19 down on a $19 month left sooner. `[D]` Also: a $1 first month rather than a free one, so a card is on file `[D]` (Money Models part 6), and 50% off for life ($400 a month to $200) for founding members `[D]` (Lost Chapters ch. 22).

**No source for:** a price-rise letter to existing clients, or a priced stand-alone audit (S-16.5). A diagnostic does appear as a $19 first step before a $2,100 program, to cut no-shows `[D]` (Lost Chapters ch. 6).

## 4. Guarantees

**Formula.** "If you do not achieve [X] in [Y time], we will [Z]." Without the remedy it has no teeth. `[M*]` (S-18.1) The four types are in the book. `[D]` (Offers part 6)

**Four types.** `[M*]` (S-18.4 to S-18.12)

| Type | What it is | When he says to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional | Money back, no questions | Low ticket, B2C, low fulfilment cost, high volume |
| Conditional | Remedy depends on what the client does; his favourite is "we keep working for free until you hit the goal" | High ticket, effort-heavy, B2B |
| Anti-guarantee | All sales final, with a stated reason | Custom work, copyable material |
| Implied | You are paid only on performance | Agencies and software with trackable results |

**The Gym Launch line:** "We will fill your gym in 30 days. If we don't, we keep working free until we do, provided you run the ad templates and answer lead calls within 5 minutes." `[M*]` (S-18.9) **His consumer boot-camp line** is verbatim in the book: "I am guaranteeing that you will get $500 worth of value… I'll write you a check the day you tell me we suck," with two refunds in 4,000 sales over three and a half years. `[D]` (S-19.3)

**Why it pays.** Risk is the first objection in any sale; a guarantee moves it to the seller. His math: sales up 30%, refunds from 5% to 10%, net sales 95 to 117. `[M*]` (S-18.2) It counts sales, not profit, and ignores the cost of serving refunded buyers.

**Do not use** an unconditional refund on high-labour service (you pay the work and the refund), on copyable material, or to cover a weak product. `[M*]` (S-21.1)

**Performance pricing risks:** tracking and collection, client dishonesty, a shrinking market, fulfilment burnout, and poor client sales staff. Do not use it where you cannot see the numbers yourself or control the close; bill on shows or bookings instead of sales. `[M*]` (S-20.8, S-20.9)

## 5. Scarcity, urgency, bonuses and names

**Scarcity is quantity; urgency is time.** `[M*]` (S-22.1)
- Cap the business (a fixed number of active clients, with a waiting list), cap the weekly intake, cap the cohort, cap a bonus, or make something never available again. `[M*]` (S-22.3, S-22.4)
- Honest scarcity uses a real capacity number: "81% of capacity in your total business." `[D]` (S-22.11) Sell out by offering less than you can fill.
- Rolling cohorts give urgency and an onboarding schedule at once: "our next group starts Monday." `[M*]` (S-23.2)
- For a service open all year (he names agencies), tie the deadline to the bonus, the promotion or the price, not to access: "our New Year promotion ends January 30th." `[M*]` (S-23.8)
- Fake counters and reset timers cost credibility and later launches. `[M*]` (S-24.1)

**Bonuses.** Split the deliverable into named parts so the stack outweighs the price. Each bonus should answer one objection ("I travel too much" gets a travel guide). Tools and checklists beat extra training. `[M*]` (S-26.3 to S-26.7) *Other people's services* give a bonus at no cost: a pain clinic offered free massages, chiropractor visits and a gym pass from neighbours, and earned referral fees from them. `[M*]` (S-26.4, S-27.3) The weight-loss stack of seven items lists a total of $4,351 against a $599 price; the seven values he reads add to $4,347, so the total is his own arithmetic slip. `[D]` (S-27.2)

**Naming (MAGIC).** Magnet (reason), Avatar (who), Goal (result), Interval (time), Container (the word that makes it a system: challenge, blueprint, intensive). Pick three to five; order for flow. `[M*]` (S-29.1) Local examples: "Free 6-Week Lean by Halloween Challenge", "Free 21-Day Mommy Makeover". On Meta and Google, a number plus a time reads as an implied guarantee and can be rejected. `[M*]` (S-31.1) He gives no rule that a name must carry both result and time (S-31.2, `[A]`).

## 6. Free work, trials, audits and first customers

This is the section Resonans needs. Markers per line.

**He starts a new service free, for the first five.** In his words: "When you launch a new product or service, make the first five free. The exact number matters less than knowing why you benefit from it." `[D]` (Leads part 3) The reasons he gives: you get reps, you probably are not good yet and free clients forgive, you learn what makes the thing hard to use, and free clients turn into customers, referrals and testimonials. `[D]`

**The script and the three terms.** "Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And I'll give it to you all for free so long as you promise to one, use it. Two, give me feedback. And three, leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?" `[D]` (S-40.6, S-69.3) Cold-start yield, his figures: about 100 reach-outs a day; 100 reach-outs give about 20 replies, 4 free clients and 1 paying one. `[M*]` (S-40.8; an earlier answer said 3,000 messages for five, so the rate is unsettled)

**The step-up.** Once free clients start referring friends, start charging: "80% off for the next five, then 60% off for the next five, then 40% off… and so forth," and "keep raising it by 20% every five until you find your sweet spot." `[D]` (S-69.5) The step is by client number, not by time. A fresh answer lists 80, 60, 40, 20, then full price. `[M*]`

**A variant with no price.** His first sale ever was free work in exchange for a donation to a charity of the client's choice, to keep them invested while he was afraid to ask for money. `[D]` (Leads part 3)

**The cost of free.** "Your free stuff is too expensive": the hidden cost to the client is time, effort and sacrifice. Find those costs and cut them. `[D]` (Leads part 3)

**What he did himself.** His early Gym Launch offer was a recorded case study: "we'll do the whole thing for free, and we only get paid off the sales you make." `[D]` (S-38.11, Leads part 2) In the flying-to-gyms years he paid his own travel, ran the ads and the front desk for 21 days, kept the first six weeks of new members' fees as his pay, and refunded the owner's $500 deposit. He did 33 gyms in 18 months, at a peak of 8 a month. `[D]` (S-67.1 to S-67.5, Offers part 2) That is a free pilot with a cash event on day one. Resonans's free week has no such event.

**Audits are lead magnets that "reveal a problem."** They work best when the problem gets worse while the owner waits, and only for qualified leads: budget, authority, a clear need, intent to act. His arithmetic: $25 of labour on a $250 value, one in four buys, so $100 to win a customer. `[M*]` (S-70.2, S-70.3, S-70.5) A website-speed audit is his example: 9 seconds against 3, about 3% of conversions lost per second. `[M*]` (S-70.6) No source charges for an audit. (S-70.1, `[A]`)

**The five-step formula for giving away value.** Look at what competitors charge for; work out your hard cost to deliver it; give that exact thing away; measure how many leads engage; convert them into back-end buyers. `[M*]` (q39)

**When free works and when it fails.** From the full re-run of q39: `[M*]`

| Free works when | Free fails when |
|---|---|
| A back end exists (upsell, continuity, high ticket) | There is no back end, so the leads are effort spent on non-buyers |
| It solves a narrow first problem that exposes the next | It gives away the whole core solution and the client no longer needs to buy |
| Filters, a form or an application screen the leads | It opens to unvetted leads and fills up with freebie seekers |
| The recipient's time and effort stay small | The hidden cost to the recipient outweighs the value |
| It sits in front of a premium core price | It is mixed up with lowering the core price |

**Free trials for gyms he opposes.** A $21, 20-day trial collects too little cash; a $500 deposit plus $200 to $300 of supplements collects $700 to $800 on day one, so he could outspend rivals on ads. `[M*]` (S-49.8) Trials he accepts carry friction: a card on file with a $50 penalty for no-shows, a restricted free trial upsold on the call to a $199 to $299 challenge (75% took it), or four months of free leads that leave a gap when they stop. `[M*]` (S-49.4, S-49.6, S-38.18) The last one conflicts with his rule that a lead magnet must never solve the core problem (S-38.6); both stand.

**Vetting.** Selling to someone who will not get a result is deceptive selling. His levers: target by income, add steps before booking, make the offer paid, ask a long-form question with a minimum spend ("Are you willing to spend at least $500?"), and require a short tactical video. `[M*]` (S-57.2, S-57.4, S-72.3) His ad copy turned away trainers and online coaches. `[M*]` (S-72.4)

## 6b. Getting leads ($100M Leads)

All `[M*]` unless marked; Leads parts 2, 3, 4 and 9 were checked against local transcripts for the passages quoted in §6.

- **Core Four.** Four ways one person can make an offer known: warm outreach (one to one, people who know you), posting free content (one to many, an audience), cold outreach (one to one, strangers), paid ads (one to many, strangers). (S-36.1)
- **Three steps:** make an offer (lead with a lead magnet for higher-ticket or cold audiences), pick one channel, then scale by More, then Better, then New, in that order. (S-37.1, S-37.3)
- **Start warm.** With more time than money, begin with warm outreach for the first five customers, then add content. With more money than time, go to paid ads or a cold engine. (S-36.4, S-37.5)
- **One thing at a time.** 1-1-1: one offer, one avatar, one channel until $1 million (the threshold wording varies by answer). Under $1 million, give the first four hours of each workday to lead generation. (S-36.3, S-44.2)
- **Rule of 100.** 100 outreach actions a day, or 100 minutes of content or ads, for 100 days before judging a channel. (S-42.1)
- **Warm outreach in steps.** List every contact from email, social and phone; start on the platform with the most; personalise in 30 seconds; reach out to 100 a day; warm replies with Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask; ask whether they know anyone who fits, instead of pitching; offer the service free on the three terms; when all platforms are done, restart at the top. (S-40.2, S-40.4; the free-on-three-terms line is `[D]`)
- **Cold outreach.** Three problems and three fixes: no contact details (build lists), they do not know you (personalise one to three details), they do not trust you (lead with Big Fast Value, a high-value lead magnet or free audit that solves a narrow problem). (S-41.1, S-41.2)
- **Lead magnets.** Three types: reveal a problem, a sample or trial, one step of a multi-step process. Four delivery forms: software, information, services, physical products. Never solve the core problem. (S-38.2, S-38.4, S-38.6) A clothing-store fitting room, the Costco sampler and the bar's salty pretzel are his three pictures of it. (S-38.3, S-38.5)
- **Follow-up.** Contact new leads fast (60 seconds is his stated figure for inbound), then up to three attempts. Cadence is unsettled between answers (once a day for three days, or three times a day for three days). (S-45.4, S-50.10)
- **Referrals.** Ask for a three-way introduction, not a name; his "hinge" method has you text a photo of the two parties. `[D]` (Leads part 3)
- **Employees and agencies** come after your own capacity is full; the roadmap ends at department heads and an affiliate army, which assumes payroll Resonans lacks. (S-37.5)
- **No cost per lead figure is given.** The only number near one is $25 of fulfilment plus $75 of ads, or $100 per engaged call. (S-38.9, S-44.12 `[A]`)

## 6c. The money model (Money Models and Lost Chapters)

All `[M*]` unless marked `[D]` (matched to the transcripts in Pass 2, layer two).

- **A money model is a sequence of offers.** Each offer solves a problem and exposes the next. Three stages: get cash (attraction offers), get more cash (upsell and downsell), keep cash (continuity). (S-73.1, S-73.2)
- **The first test is cash in 30 days.** Customer financed acquisition has levels by 30-day gross profit against the cost to acquire a customer (CAC): below it, equal to it, or at least twice it. Twice CAC in 30 days is his minimum standard for scaling a bootstrapped business. (S-74.1 to S-74.3) Lifetime gross profit to CAC should be at least 3:1, world class 5:1 to 10:1 and up. Payback in 30 days or less. (S-74.4, S-74.8)
- **Formulas.** CAC is total marketing and sales cost divided by new customers. Lifetime gross profit for a recurring business is monthly gross profit per client divided by monthly churn. (S-74.6)
- **Order of the levers.** Lower CAC with attraction offers to create flow, shorten payback with upsells and downsells, then raise lifetime value with continuity. (S-75.4)
- **Attraction offers.** Money Models names five: win your money back, giveaways, decoy, buy X get Y free, pay less now or pay more later. Lost Chapters adds free presentations, freemium, pick your price, premium promotions, free promotions, discount promotions, lifetime discounts, and free with an alternative revenue stream. (S-78.1)
- **Win your money back.** A deposit ($500 to $600 in his gym examples) returned as cash or credit when the client hits a goal or does set actions in 28 days to 6 weeks. His own prerequisite: a standard refund rate under 5%. (S-78.2 to S-78.4)
- **Upsells.** Four types: classic, menu, anchor, rollover. The anchor shows a premium option first, five to ten times the price. `[D]` A rollover credits past spend toward a bigger offer; the back end must be at least four times the credit. (S-74.13)
- **Downsells.** Three: a payment plan (same product, same price, some now and the rest later), a trial with a penalty, and a feature downsell (less quantity or fewer parts). `[D]`
- **Continuity.** Six types, including a discount on the recurring fee, a waived fee, and lifetime upgrades or discounts. To push buyers into recurring, price the one-time option well above the monthly: 1.33× for half of buyers, 2.33× for 80%, 2.66× for 90% (the worked examples in the answers do not fit these ratios). (S-74.14)
- **Free and premium are different games.** On a premium offer the lead costs five to ten times more, so the price must be five to ten times higher; "free makes more money" in his comparison. `[D]` (Lost Chapters ch. 5)
- **Not covered:** a price or scope for an agency retainer, anything between $400 and $1,500 a month, a two-person or non-US agency, or how the cash-in-30-days target changes when the cost of acquiring a customer is your own hours. (S-94.3, S-108.12, `[A]`)

## 8. Warnings and ceilings

- **Most numbers rest on ads.** Cash-on-day-one logic (S-49.2) assumes you buy traffic. Resonans's roadmap rules out paid ads before 15 Dec, so that logic does not apply yet.

- **NotebookLM shaped some answers to the question.** Pass 2 §3 lists them. Do not quote a "gasp test", a "magnetic middle" or a "fence the offer" rule as his.

## 9. How Resonans should shape a free audit-led trial and three tiers

*Status: written 16 Oct 2026, revised the same day after layer two (Money Models and Lost Chapters). For the offer draft due Fri 23 Oct. Hormozi's rules carry their marker and signal id. The application is mine and is labelled so. Nothing here is his advice for Dumaguete.*

### 9.1 The trial

**Keep the audit as the free part and cap the rest.**
- *His rules:* a lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem and never the core problem (S-38.1, S-38.6). Audits are "reveal a problem" magnets for qualified leads and work best when the problem grows with waiting (S-70.2, S-70.3). His arithmetic is $25 of labour on a $250 value, one buyer in four (S-70.5). Free fails when it gives away the whole core solution (q39 table).
- *Applied:* the audit is the reason for each free piece, as Christian decided on 16 Oct. Write it so it ends in a number the owner cares about (missed bookings a week, searches that go to a rival), and tie each free piece to a gap the audit found.
- *The tension with the current design.* Christian's plan lets the client keep the site, the brand kit, a week of content and the analysis if they do not continue. By his table that is close to the failure case "gives away the whole core solution". The core Resonans sells is the weekly flow of leads and bookings, so a mock site, a logo SVG and a brand kit are one-time assets; the week of content and the demand-generation analysis come nearer to the core. Hold back the part that is the ongoing flow (the follow-up, the booking work, the weekly count in GHL) and let the one-time assets and the audit be the gift. That keeps the first-step logic and the "warm contact" outcome he decided on.
- *Count the cost.* His free pilot was paid for by the gym's own customers on day one (S-67). Resonans has no such event in week one. Hours per trial client, valued at the audit's own scale, decide whether the free week is a $25-to-$100 cost or a loss; automate until it is the first.

**Free in money, not in terms.**
- *His rule:* the first five free, three terms: use it, give feedback, leave a review (S-40.6, `[D]`). A second way to hold the client in: free work for a donation to a charity of their choice (`[D]`, Leads part 3).
- *Applied:* a half-page terms of exchange before work starts: use the setup, talk to Resonans weekly, share the numbers, give a testimonial and permission to show the results. Christian's vetting rule (the owner talks directly; the business sells something people buy weekly) meets his purchasing-power and authority tests. ### 9.2 The three tiers

**State the fact first:** Hormozi does not teach a good-better-best menu for one core offer. Two fresh queries found none (q68, q106). He teaches one differentiated offer, an ascension from a front-end offer to a back end, a capped top level, and three tier-like devices: the anchor upsell at five to ten times the core, the decoy menu, and pick-your-price rungs above a free base (`[D]`, Lost Chapters ch. 19; Money Models part 5).

**What he gives that bears on tier design:**
1. **Avoid the middle.** Small-business services at $1,500 to $3,000 a month churn fast. Go cheap and automated ($400 a month or less) or high-touch and high-ticket. (S-51.15) These are US dollars and come from the videos; they were not found in the local book transcripts.
2. **Price for the client's worst month.** (S-49.10)
3. **Cap the top.** A premium level with direct access is limited by capacity, and a published capacity number is honest scarcity. (S-22.4, S-22.11)
4. **One promise per offer, named with MAGIC, with a guarantee that fits the labour:** a conditional service guarantee, not an unconditional refund, on work that costs hours. (S-21.1)

5. **Pick-your-price rungs are the nearest to Resonans's shape.** A free base, then rungs that each add access or a guarantee ($99 a one-on-one call, $199 the handbook, $499 a guarantee). The free audit is the base; each paid tier adds something the client feels, not more volume. `[D]`
6. **The anchor sits at five to ten times the core.** At ₱10,000 for the core, his anchor would be ₱50,000 to ₱100,000. A ₱20,000 top tier is twice the core, so it works as a capped-access tier, not as his anchor.

**How this maps onto ₱5,000, ₱10,000 and ₱20,000 (my application):**
- At roughly ₱55 to ₱60 to the dollar (an assumption to check), all three sit under his $400 floor for a cheap, automated service. A tier that needs a person's weekly hours loses money at ₱5,000 to ₱10,000.
- Build each tier around **how much is automated and how much access the client gets**, not around more deliverables:
  - *Entry (₱5,000):* the repeatable part. Audit refresh, the monthly content pack, the GHL dashboard with the weekly count. Near-zero marginal cost.
  - *Core (₱10,000):* the named offer. Entry plus lead follow-up and booking work, one named result and a conditional service guarantee (for example, "if you do not get X bookings in Y days we keep working at no charge until you do, provided you answer leads within a set time").
  - *Top (₱20,000):* access and speed, capped at a published number of clients. The hands-on work lives here.
- Say in the draft that the three tiers are Christian's decision and follow access, not Hormozi's menu.
