# Portfolio playbook: rubric, case studies, critiques, hiring

How Jason Murray says to build an art director portfolio, and what he said about 28 real ones. Source paths are relative to `knowledge/corpus/jason-murray/.raw/`. Video quotes keep the caption's missing punctuation. Markers: `[D]` his words, `[D:vis]` read off a frame, `[D:guest:<name>]` a guest's words, `[X:<name>]` a cited person, `[G]` a checked fact, `[M]` NotebookLM wording with no source found, `[A]` a gap.

Main sources: `video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md` (2026), `video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md` (2025), `video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md` (the live review stream), `video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md` (with Taylor Dahl), `video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md` and `-2026.md`.

## The rubric as a checklist

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "you're going to make one to two 360 campaigns i've got examples there of what those could look like you've got one really craft focused or design focused project" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)

**The portfolio**

- [ ] Four to six projects in total. [D] "in total ideally you have four to six strong projects no weak projects they can all be spec they can all be fake"
- [ ] One to two 360 campaigns.
- [ ] One craft or design-focused project.
- [ ] One to two passion projects.
- [ ] Optional: one experimental or non-traditional project.
- [ ] Optional: one social or content project.
- [ ] Optional: one stunt or cultural response, ideally done for real.
- [ ] No weak project. [D] "If you've got six projects and one of them is crap just get rid of the crap one."
- [ ] Each project proves a different thing about you.
- [ ] Half your effort shows how you think. [D] "Showcasing how you think should be 50% of this 50% of your effort and your energy"

**Each brand project**

- [ ] A globally recognised brand, and not one whose real ads you cannot beat.
- [ ] A title: the name of the idea.
- [ ] A key visual or lockup at the top.
- [ ] A short overview that carries the insight and the idea.
- [ ] One strong idea, not a dozen.
- [ ] Four or more touchpoints, with channels labelled.
- [ ] A line of your thinking between touchpoints.
- [ ] Two or three ingredients from the Art Direction Menu that carry across every piece.

**The site**

- [ ] Work is the first thing a visitor reaches.
- [ ] A plain line that says what you are.
- [ ] Thumbnails that are key visuals, with a tag saying what each project was.
- [ ] No tabs split by discipline.
- [ ] No text over photos or video.
- [ ] An about page with a photo.
- [ ] Some craft in the site itself: a logo, a colour, motion.

- **How firm each slot is:** it shifts by video. Passion project: "not optional you need to show at least one or two" (2025), "preferably you include a passion project" (2026). Craft project: "this one isn't optional" (2025), no such stress in 2026. He also says "It doesn't have to be exact."

### Why the rubric exists: employers won't hire what they can't see

- **Provenance:** [D] "The hard truth is that employers won't hire what they can't see" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** [D] "No one is going to hire you for something you can't prove you have experience in or you can't prove you have capabilities in." It does not mean ten years of experience: "they're looking for evidence".

### Concepts + Craft

- **Provenance:** [D] "Showcasing how you think should be 50% of this 50% of your effort and your energy" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`); slide text "CONCEPTS + CRAFT" is [D:vis]
- **Signal:** [D] "The other 50% of the role of art direction is the ideas." Craft alone means you should specialise as a designer or executor.
- **Note:** He never says "50/50". The slide reads "CONCEPTS + CRAFT". He says it of the role and of your effort, not per project.

### Slot: the 360 campaign

- **Provenance:** [D] "a 360 campaign just means you've got a big idea that stretches across multiple touch points and you show those touch points you mock it up" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** One to two. It proves you can think big. State the insight and the idea. He likes print and out of home here because they show the idea "in a simpler format than always thinking about video".

### Slot: the craft or design project

- **Provenance:** [D] "the purpose of this one is really to make sure they don't see any weakness in in your craft" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** One piece "you just obsessed over". Forms he names: animation, a typography system, a typeface project, a print-only campaign. A designer pivoting can use an existing design project.
- **Example:** Braden Clark's hand-drawn print ads (below).

### Slot: the passion project

- **Provenance:** [D] "passion projects aren't branded at all they show your craft your thinking things you care about maybe your diverse background" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** See "Passion projects" below.

### Slot: social or content project

- **Provenance:** [D] "this is where you need to show that you are native to the internet you understand how reals memes carousels trends all that works show that you're a master of the hook" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** Optional. Ideally every 360 campaign already shows social.

### Slot: experimental or non-traditional project

- **Provenance:** [D] "advertising doesn't have to be all about what's on a billboard anything that can make a splash" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** Optional. An app, or "something a little different". His example is Duo Wingwoman (below).

### Slot: a stunt or something done for real

- **Provenance:** [D] "do something ideally for real but do something that shows you can get a response from people" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** Optional, and "a piece that really helped me get a job". Doing it for real puts you "one step ahead of every other student". His own is "Venmo the Homeless" (below).

### How many projects

- **Provenance:** [D] "it's better to have four good projects than six okay projects or even having four great projects and then two really bad ones" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)
- **Signal:** A bad project makes the viewer think "what if they deliver at that quality for me?" He started with four pieces and graduated with six. With a longer career, seven to ten strong ones is fine.
- **Guest view:** [D:guest:Taylor Dahl] "I would say minimum like five to seven things but then you can kind of go as much as you want from there" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)

### How many touchpoints: his two numbers

- **Provenance:** [D] "four to 10 touch points three is okay but but ideally it's it's at least four uh different touch points" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`, 2025)
- **2026 wording:** [D] "You want at least four to six touch points and that might be you know three four to six different channels" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **What both share:** a floor of four. The reason: "you're showing your idea has the has the legs has the breadth to stretch".
- **Channels he lists:** social, out of home, TV, an in-real-life piece, a guerrilla stunt, an activation or pop-up, an app, print.

### One idea per project, one job per project

- **Provenance:** [D] "each piece has a single strong idea not a dozen different disperate ideas You got to show that you can unify multiple touch points to a single idea" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** Give each project a job: "This is the project I show that I can think really big This is the project that I show that I have these craft skills This is a project that shows this passion".

### Spec work is accepted

- **Provenance:** [D] "your portfolio can be completely fake and that's okay everyone understands that" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Signal:** No budget and no legal department, so "you can be as provocative as you want to be". Newsletter: [D] "you don't fill your portfolio with the small freelance graphics you designed for your local dentist. no, you make fake campaigns for the biggest brands in the world." (`newsletter/2026-08-14-how-to-prove-you-re-an-art-director-mad-010.md`)
- **For working art directors too:** [D] "maybe you're not making enough funny work and you want to make more funny work than make some funny spec ads um and no one no creative director is ever going to look down on that" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md`)

### Which brand to pick

- **Provenance:** [D] "don't make spec ads for Apple because you're never going to make fake ads that are better than the real ads" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md`)
- **Signal:** Pick "a globally recognized brand ideally" so you spend no space explaining it. He credits the Apple rule to other people's videos. Kraft Heinz "might be on the verge".
- **Note:** [M] Hydro Flask, Dollar Shave Club, Duolingo and Qdoba are brands in examples he shows. He never recommends them.

## Case-study writing

### The project checklist

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "Give your project a title as in the idea has a name the campaign concept has a name That's going to show that you can distill an idea down to a single line" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Six items:** 1. A globally recognised brand. 2. A title. 3. A key visual or lockup, "optional… but I highly recommend". 4. A short overview at the top. 5. Proof of an insight and an idea, labelled or not. 6. Four or more touchpoints, channels labelled, with your thinking between them.

### The order of a project page

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "key visual lock up at the top then you know this the overview then the different hero executions" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Order:** key visual or lockup, overview, hero executions, further touchpoints. Idea first, as the "connective thread".

### Key visual or lockup

- **Type:** [DEF]
- **Provenance:** [D] "a single image that represents the entire campaign" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** From the reviews: "the thing that tells the idea in a super clear way in in one image". It carries the idea's name and more than the brand's logo.
- **Example, Nike "Rip the Script":** [D] "the title rip the script and they've got the cracked screen the digital cracked screen down the middle where it's like breaking You're visually hinting at the entire idea". Campaign facts: `mentioned/campaigns/nike-rip-the-script.md`.

### The overview: say the why

- **Provenance:** [D] "Some people like to literally label this is my inside this is my idea but you don't have to do that I don't always recommend that Just talk about your project and and what the concept is Emphasize the why" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** For juniors, keep it "super simple": the human truth, then a plain "so we did this" sentence.
- **Note:** [M] a "2–3 sentence" count has no source.

### STAR: situation, task, actions, results

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the formula I'll share is called star it's an acronym it's situation s situation T task a actions and then R results" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** Situation and task can be bundled. Actions: what you did; for spec work "you can just fake all this". Results: views, shares, press; "results don't have to be metrics".
- **His caveat:** [D] "you don't have to follow star and this exact framework for everything I sure I don't at all"

### Results for spec work

- **Provenance:** [D] "I might not do results but also I just try to think about ways ways to get some sort of results so one one way is Awards" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Two routes:** enter student award shows and put the award on the project. Or make it for real.

### Label channels and write your thinking between touchpoints

- **Provenance:** [D] "between each of those touch points maybe you just label the different channels We've got social we've got this we've got that But I'd also love to hear a little bit of your thinking" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **What to write:** "how does this tie in to the overarching concept how does this help and build up to the big idea".

### Reviewers skim

- **Provenance:** [D] "you can see I'm not even like reading all the text I'm kind of like skimming through" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** Creative directors are "just skimming the thumbnail". On film: "I'm not going to always spend the time to watch an entire commercial". So the image has to carry the idea.
- **Note:** [M] he gives no number of seconds.

### Ask of every element: what am I communicating with this?

- **Provenance:** [D] "if there's something that you're doing on there that's like not communicating anything just cut it out" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)

### Show process, and state your role

- **Provenance:** [D] "He shows some of his process mood That's I love that he's showing his thinking where he's getting his inspiration" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Role line:** [D] "my role and this is super helpful anytime you can say this is what I did" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Note:** [M] the model line "My Role: Art Director — Concept, Visual Execution & Motion" exists in no first-order layer.

## Site building

### Work first, statement short

- **Provenance:** [D] "the number one thing is just making sure your work is the first thing people can access or is just super accessible" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** A freelancer's site leads with a statement and services. Someone after an agency job "usually don't even need some sort of statement at the top".

### Thumbnails: a key visual and a tag

- **Provenance:** [D] "just little things under your thumbnails or when you hover over your thumbnails those those that information can pop up that just kind of tells you what this was that they did" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Tag examples:** brand identity, campaign, content production, relaunch, campaigns and socials.

### Do not split the site by discipline

- **Provenance:** [D] "a lot of creative directors just when they see a lot of different disciplines they just don't know what you are and they don't know if you know what you are" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** Fold design and photography into the art direction work. If it is not good enough, cut it or park it on one page called "more". He learned it "the hard way" and says to take it with a grain of salt.

### Do not put text over photos or video

- **Provenance:** [D] "my preference is to just like separate them show some of the photos it could be in like a frame on the side so it doesn't have to be text overlaid on top of Photography" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** Learned at Amazon working with UX designers on accessible design.

### Describe yourself plainly

- **Provenance:** [D] "a lot of times we like to get very flowery with how we describe ourselves I do that too" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Signal:** He turns it on his own line, "an independent creative and art director I make bold conceptual brand campaigns": "what do you actually do".

### The site itself is a portfolio piece

- **Provenance:** [D] "The website itself should almost be a portfolio piece It should reflect your personality" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** Brand yourself: a logo, an icon, a colour. His own was an animated signature, there to prove After Effects.

### Site builder

- **Provenance:** [D] "I've swapped my portfolio around to a lot of different website builders but this is the one that I'm finally landing on and I'd recommend to start with" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** Framer in 2026 (sponsor, code Jason1). In 2025: "use Squarespace use Wix use whatever's easy".

### Motion reel, Behance, homework

- **Motion reel:** [D] "for most art director like junior art director roles they're not going to care to to watch a real" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`). Put motion in thumbnails and GIFs.
- **Behance:** [D] "if you're trying to get a job it's not going to be your your best bet is to just make a website" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`)
- **Homework portfolios:** [D] "those portfolios are really hard to hire because they're just not enough" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)

### A portfolio should be polarising

- **Provenance:** [X:Jason Bagley] relayed by Jason from Creative Mega Machine (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** Fill it "with just things you love and things that you want to make more of". If nobody hates it, "it's not you enough".

### Make the work you want to be paid for

- **Provenance:** [D] "inevitably you're going to get paid for the kind of work you're already making so make the work you want to make" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)

### What counts

- **Provenance:** [D] "passion projects aren't branded at all they show your craft your thinking things you care about maybe your diverse background" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience.md`)

| Rule | His words |

## Outreach and hiring

### How reviewers read a portfolio

- **Provenance:** [D] "there is that routine of like you you quickly glance oftentimes people will jump to the about me page" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Signal:** Front page, about page, then back to the work. [D] "I just want to quickly know who this person is I want to put a face to the person behind the work and then I jump back and I like dive deep into the work". So put a photo on the about page.

### Go to the people who post the role

- **Provenance:** [D] "finding those sort of posts on LinkedIn and netork with those people I think has been helpful because often the times the recruiters get just like bombarded with tons of in inbound messages" (`video/why-i-quit-my-art-director-job-at-amazon-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-4.md`)
- **Signal:** Search LinkedIn posts for the role keywords. The poster is often a copywriter, art director or producer on the team who wants to refer someone.
- **His own record:** first job through school, the rest from people seeing his work on LinkedIn, and Huge by messaging the creative directors directly.

### What to say in a message

- **Provenance:** [D] "Become a student of their work! Read what they've already shared." (`linkedin/056-2026-03-20.md`)
- **Signal:** Put more thought in than "would love to pick your brain".
- **Cited steps:** [X:Aisha Hakim] decide what work you want to make, find who makes it, study their portfolios, build spec work, then message.
- **Not found:** [M] any message template.

### Where to apply

- **Provenance:** [D] "a lot of you could probably just go straight for junior art director or even mid-level art director jobs especially if you're a mid-career senior designer" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Places:** ad agencies, design studios modelled on agencies, social-first agencies, in-house creative departments, and "creator focused companies that are starting to hire our directors".

### Tailor the book to the industry

- **Provenance:** [D] "some Industries especially like Entertainment Sports you know luxury fashion cars there are a lot of creative directors that will only hire creatives with that experience all already" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **His method:** steer what you pitch toward what you want more of. For Claritin he pitched an animated campaign about getting kids outside.
