# People and reading

Every person named in the Jason Murray corpus, one heading each, then the reading order and a table of books, courses and schools. Search by name. Each section also carries the spelling the captions heard, so a search on "Asa Hikim" or "Edward Son" finds the right person.

How to read a section:

- **Group** is one of five: source he learned from, guest, colleague or friend, student or reviewed portfolio, creator he cites.
- Markers: [D] Jason's words, with the file. [D:vis] text read off a frame of his video. [D:guest:name] a guest's words on his podcast. [X:name] a cited person's own framework, from their own source. [G] a fact from trade press or a stated number. [M] NotebookLM prose, not confirmed. [I] a pattern he never states. [A] a named gap.
- Quotes are printed as captioned, so spoken ones carry no punctuation and keep the caption's mis-hearings.
- File paths are relative to `knowledge/corpus/jason-murray/.raw/` unless they start with `analysis/`.
- A guest's ideas stay the guest's. The sabbatical and "prison of your own success" are Jason Gold's. The six-month runway and the Pentagram versus Smith & Diction comparison are Edward Sun's. Most photography direction detail is Garrett King's.
- Sections with short single-line fields were filled from `analysis/pass-1/inventory-people.tsv`. In those, the second field ends with the marker and check note that `inventory-people.tsv` gives for the row.

## Jason Bagley

- **Group:** source he learned from
- **Who:** Former creative leader at Wieden+Kennedy Portland (Old Spice, Nike). He founded The Audacious School of Astonishing Pursuits and teaches Creative Megamachine.   - The portfolio rule, relayed from the course: fill it "with just things you love and things that you want to make more of", and if nobody hates it, "it's not like polarizing enough or it's not you enough".   - Idea volume. Jason's own practice after reading Bagley: "just try not to come up with great ideas pour your subconscious out just come up with lots of ideas and a quantity of ideas and uh that's what I do I I really focus on volume" [D] `video/answering-every-aspiring-art-director-question-ye-concert-reaction.md`
- **Their own framework, in their words:** [X:Jason Bagley]
  - "Stop trying to come up with a mind-blowing idea. Instead, you better come up with tons of ideas." (LinkedIn, 28 January 2024)

## Sir John Hegarty

- **Group:** source he learned from
- **Who:** British art director, co-founder of Bartle Bogle Hegarty. The header on his post reads "Co-founder & Creative Director at The Business of Creativity". Jason states no personal tie. [D] video; [D:vis] the post on screen. Captions print "Sir John Hegerty".
- **What Jason takes from them:**
  - One LinkedIn post, read aloud to back his own split between designer and art director. He introduces Hegarty as "the H in BBH". [D] `video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`
  - Jason's own claim, which the post supports: "a designer not always but often is more focused on the function the utility of what they're creating while an art director honestly function goes out the window and they're just so much more focused on attention" [D] same video.
  - Jason's gloss in speech is maximum effect versus maximum impact. The words "architect of clarity" and "creative anarchist" are Hegarty's and stay tagged as his.
- **Their own framework, in their words:** [X:Sir John Hegarty], LinkedIn post of 14 March 2025.
  - "Designer vs Art Director. Two roles. One industry. Polar opposites."
  - The designer: "You're the architect of clarity. Your job? Make chaos orderly." and "Your task is maximum effect." and "Designers distil and refine."
  - The art director: "You're the creative anarchist. Your job? Make order chaotic." and "Everything you touch is spontaneous, irreverent, raw." and "Your task is maximum impact." and "Art Directors amplify and provoke."
  - "One is Swiss precision – measured, considered, exact." and "The other is Jazz – improvised, experimental, bold."
  - The wording is singular. "Architects of clarity" and "creative anarchists" are paraphrase.
  - From the TIE Leadership podcast, September 2020: "when everybody's going in one direction, you should go in the other direction."
- **Where to start:** The 14 March 2025 LinkedIn post, free. Then "Hegarty on Creativity: There Are No Rules" (Thames & Hudson, 2014), paid.

## Stefan Sagmeister

- **Group:** source he learned from
- **Who:** Austrian designer in New York. Sagmeister Inc., then Sagmeister & Walsh. Jason states no personal tie. [D] podcast for the name.
- **What Jason takes from them:**
  - The sabbatical comes up on The Toolbox Podcast episode 3, and guest Jason Gold raises it: "every seven years he literally shut down his entire studio for a full year" [D:guest:Jason Gold] `video/becoming-a-freelance-creative-director-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-3.md`
  - Gold uses it to put a hopeful frame on leaving a job. The idea is Gold's use of Sagmeister's practice. Jason does not teach it as his own.
  - The follow-up line, "make sure every seven years you do take a beat to step off get off the ladder before you get too high", has no clear speaker in the captions. The inventory marks the whole exchange "speaker unlabelled".
- **Their own framework, in their words:** [X:Stefan Sagmeister], TED talk "The power of time off" (2009).
  - "I run a design studio in New York. Every seven years, I close it for one year to pursue some little experiments, things that are always difficult to accomplish during the regular working year."
  - "In that year, we are not available for any of our clients."
  - Why: "I adapt to it. And I get, over time, bored by them. And for sure, in our case, our work started to look the same."
  - "I thought it might be helpful to basically cut off five of those retirement years and intersperse them in between those working years."
  - What came out: "basically everything we've done in the seven years following the first sabbatical came out of thinking of that one single year."
  - He told many people in advance "so that there was no way that I could chicken out later on."
- **Where to start:** The TED talk "The power of time off", about 18 minutes, free.
- **Source:** `mentioned/people/stefan-sagmeister.md`; `video/becoming-a-freelance-creative-director-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-3.md`; `analysis/pass-1/lanes-5-7-career-content-motion.md` ("The Sagmeister sabbatical")

## Reading order

The corpus builder wrote this order in `mentioned/people/_index.md` on 3 October 2026. Jason gives no numbered list. [I] What he does say is narrower: he names "Hey Whipple, Squeeze This", Mark Pollard and Julian Cole as the places to learn insights before big ideas (`mentioned/people/julian-cole.md`, citing `video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`). The index file states the logic of its order as: "Idea first, craft second, career last."

1. **Luke Sullivan, "Hey Whipple, Squeeze This".** Paid. Jason calls it one of the Bibles of creative advertising. Chapters 3, 6 and 7 give truth, simplicity and conflict.
2. **Mark Pollard, "How to do account planning - a simple approach".** Free, 4,000 words. It gives the insight definition Jason repeats and the "X is Y" daily exercise.
3. **Julian Cole, "What is an INSIGHT?" and the GET/WHO/TO/BY article.** Free, two short posts. They turn Pollard's insight into a brief you can hand to a creative.
4. **Sir John Hegarty, the 14 March 2025 post, then "Hegarty on Creativity".** The post is free and the book is paid. The index file says the post is the line between design and art direction that Jason's pitch rests on.
5. **Jason Bagley, the 28 January 2024 post and "The Creative Virtuous Cycle".** Free. Write many ideas before judging any. Jason took the full paid course; the index file calls these two the free core.
6. **Dave Dye's Pinterest boards and one "Stuff from the Loft" interview.** Free. The index file's reason: this is how to build a visual memory. Take one photographer board, then read how the work was made.
7. **Michel Gondry's videos, starting with "Sugar Water", "Let Forever Be" and "Come Into My World".** Free. One rule, done by hand, held to the end. They show what naming a look after a director means.
8. **Aisha Hakim, "The Art of Deck-Making".** Free. The index file's reason: an idea nobody buys is not made, and her seven rules cover how an art director sells one.

After those eight, the index file adds three: Rick Rubin, pages 77 to 78, for finishing (paid). Adam W. Morgan's essay for where the job leads (free). Stefan Sagmeister's TED talk for when to step away (free).
