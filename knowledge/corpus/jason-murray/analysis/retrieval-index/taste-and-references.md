## Why taste alone is not enough

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the metaphor of taste is actually a pretty passive one consuming things and as an art director that's never been enough for me we have to visually cook we have to use our taste" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Source:** `video/the-theory-of-taste.md`; `shorts/2026-04-03-the-theory-of-taste-SU7U2uT3Gj4.md`; `linkedin/055-2026-03-24.md`
- **Signal:** Taste, as people use the word, means consuming and liking. Jason wants taste you can "direct and execute and build on". He disputes the common line that taste is a moat: [D] "taste alone passive taste i truly don't think it's the moat everyone thinks it is". On LinkedIn, March 24, 2026: [D] "People are saying that 'taste' will be your only moat. I think that's a little naive. I think VISION is a lot more powerful".
- **Note:** He says "passive taste". He never says "active taste"; that pairing is NotebookLM's.

## Taste versus cooking

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "taste alone does not give you an ability to cook, and a chef who does specialize in a single lane can be world-class" (`shorts/2026-04-03-the-theory-of-taste-SU7U2uT3Gj4.md`)
- **Signal:** Taste is what you can judge. Cooking is what you can make. A critic or curator may need only taste. An art director has to make. LinkedIn, April 22, 2026: [D] "Taste ≠ visual literacy." (`linkedin/036-2026-04-22.md`)

## Milestone 1: broaden your inputs

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "nobody trusts the taste of someone who only eats fast food" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Signal:** Raise the volume and the variety of what you look at. [D] "To become a connoisseur you have to go outside your lane." [D] "The more you've tasted the more useful your taste becomes when it's time to actually make something." The Short's version: [D] "If you want to develop insatiable taste, you need to become a voracious eater."
- **Hard-to-reach sources he lists in the Short:** a Chef's Table experience in the Yucatan jungle, a virtual garden gatekept from the algorithms, an Internet Archive trick. The garden is probably Are.na; he does not name it there.
- **Capture rule:** [D] "you can't articulate taste you haven't actually experienced and stopped to collect". He calls saving "the first shift from passive scrolling to intentional saving".

## The three levels of expressing taste

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "this is the bridge between passively consuming and having taste you can actually use in your job in your career and there are levels to this" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Diagram:** [D:vis] an hourglass drawn on an iPad at 3:38. A funnel labelled INPUTS on top, a filter mark at the neck, then a pyramid of three tiers going down: LIKE?, FEELINGS, INGREDIENTS (`frames/the-theory-of-taste.md`).

| Level | What you do | His example words | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Like or dislike | Save what you like. Reject what you don't. | "why did I save that", "do I actually like this", "what about it do I like" | yourself |
| 2. Feeling | Say how it feels with a simile, metaphor or reference. | "uncomfy", "dreamlike", "tense", "this visual feels uncomfortable", "this maybe feels like a Michel Gondry film" | clients |
| 3. Ingredients | Name what made it feel that way. | "flash lighting", "a hand-drawn mixed media effect", "that note of toasted oak" | photographers, directors, vendors |

- **His recap:** [D] "That's how you go from a consumer to someone who can actually communicate their taste."

### Level 1: like, dislike, deliberate rejection

- **Provenance:** [D] "the first level is simply knowing what you like or dislike that sounds obvious but most people don't even get here intentionally" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Signal:** Most people scroll past and never ask how an image affects them. Rejecting on purpose counts as much as liking.

### Level 2: say how it feels

- **Provenance:** [D] "this level of language is actually really useful with clients because they don't need to know the technical words they just need a shared feeling" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Signal:** Describe the emotion and leave the technique out. Clients need to point at something and say "yes that". He titles collections after the feeling, and has "a whole collection titled" Michel Gondry.
- **Note:** The caption hears "michelle gandry". The director is Michel Gondry.

### Level 3: name the ingredients

- **Provenance:** [D] "flashlighting a handdrawn mixed media effect that note of toasted oak you're not just saying how it feels anymore you're naming what made it feel that way" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)
- **Signal:** Identify "the specific elements that created that flavor". That lets you "speak with the executors the partners or vendors who are going to help you create this work". [D] "You're filling a pantry."
- **Note:** "Visual pantry" is Gemini's phrase. He says "filling a pantry" and "filling my pantry". "Toasted oak", "caramel" and "horseradish" are tasting notes inside the food metaphor. They are not colours.

### Mashing two worlds together (a level 2 shortcut)

- **Provenance:** [D] "You don't need fancy jargon or technical terms to talk art direction. One of the easiest ways to express a visual idea is with metaphor. Just describe how it feels." (`shorts/2025-04-09-how-to-talk-art-direction-without-technical-knowledge-QvolURczV5k.md`)
- **Three formulas he gives:** "It's like a Wes Anderson film… but shot on a flip phone." "It should feel like a luxury ad but made in a garage." "Imagine a horror movie but in pastel tones with soft lighting."
- **Signal:** [D] "you can create a unique feeling by just combining existing visual worlds". He calls the Wes Anderson reference "cliche, I know".

## The five taste types

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "everything I just talked about is a nuanced matrix of flavor profiles. There is no single monolith of taste." (`shorts/2026-05-01-you-re-thinking-about-taste-wrong-bKjx0_m3zLM.md`)
- **Source:** that Short; `linkedin/034-2026-05-01.md`

| Type | His definition | What it is good for |
|---|---|---|
| Mainstream | [D] "Casts the widest net. It's a safe taste. Not a bad or good taste, but not always valued as taste even though understanding mainstream taste can unlock scale." | scale |
| Cultural | [D] "You can study cultural taste, you can serve it, amplify it, but you can't claim it if you didn't live it." | "Gatekept by lived experience." The hardest one for brands. |
| Counter | [D] "Counter taste, on the other hand, is developed through conviction, the willingness to reject what everyone else wants." | It "often creates a collective", and risks being absorbed by the mainstream or the elite. |
| Elite | [D] "any of those judgments in which the criteria is gatekept by pure access or privilege" | "mainstream culture does envy the elite" |
| Commercial | [D] "a little flavor of aspiration mixed with something familiar and inviting can be leveraged to deliver a message that resonates with a specific audience" | what a creative director uses: pieces of the other four that fit the business objective |

- **The question to ask before executing:** [D] "your taste doesn't matter if you can't make it palatable to others. And that depends on the audience. Whose taste?"
- **LinkedIn version:** [D] "Your superpower is not achieving some singular 'taste' milestone. Your superpower is understanding the matrix of tastes and discerning how to cater to them."

## How to trace an image to its maker (six steps)

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "you click the source link you find the actual photographer or director behind it you look at their full portfolio not just the one image the algorithm surface to you you read their notes" (`video/the-theory-of-taste.md`)

1. Find something you like in Cosmos.
2. Click the source link.
3. Find the photographer or director.
4. Look at the full portfolio.
5. Read their notes and project write-ups: "the way they describe it the way they articulate the art and medium".
6. Find their rep or management and open that site. Browse the rest of the roster.

- **What you get:** "not just what they made but how they think and how they speak", and a name to hire.
- **Why it matters for a mood board:** [D] "this is where your mood board stops being a sort of decorative reference and starts being something you can actually execute because you know who can execute each of those visuals"

## Visual lineage: the family tree of an image

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "Every visual idea carries baggage/associations, references, predecessors. And as art directors, we have a choice: we can utilize parts of that lineage intentionally, or we can accidentally plagiarize it." (`newsletter/2026-02-17-how-art-directors-should-understand-visual-lineage.md`)
- **Source:** that newsletter; `shorts/2026-02-06-how-art-directors-weaponize-visual-lineage-FcOKqjHmd0E.md`; `linkedin/065-2026-02-19.md`; `linkedin/068-2026-02-06.md`
- **Signal:** [D] "nothing exists in a vacuum". Lineage is sometimes an association you can use and sometimes baggage to steer around. His terms: "visual lineage", "visual DNA", "visual baggage", "visual shorthand", "visually incestuous".

### How to trace a lineage

- **Provenance:** [D] "when you find a visual that inspires you, DONT STOP THERE. Trace it back." (same newsletter)
- **Four questions to ask of any reference:**
  1. Where did each of these ingredients come from?
  2. Is there a previous inspiration that I'm unaware of?
  3. Can I take inspiration from parts of its "DNA" without stealing the whole thing?
  4. Can I branch out from a less incestuous generation of inspiration?
- **Three questions when you see a clever ad:**
  1. Who did this concept first?
  2. What photographer or artist established this visual language?
  3. How far back can you go before it is something unusable?
- **The payoff:** [D] "If you can branch out from further back on that tree (ie you reference Weston instead of the 2025 campaign everyone else saw) you'll arrive at something more unique."
- **The limit:** [D] "You can't avoid influence. But you can understand where your influences come from, and make conscious choices about how you incorporate them into your work."

### Worked example: macro texture as landscape

- **Provenance:** [D] "in the 1930s photographer Edward Weston was shooting macro photography of fruits and textures that looked like larger worlds or landscapes" (`shorts/2026-02-06-how-art-directors-weaponize-visual-lineage-FcOKqjHmd0E.md`)
- **The tree, newest first:**

| Generation | Work | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Decathlon print ads | athletic gear shot as macro landscapes |
| near the same time | a Colombian grocery campaign (Metro) | near-identical road metaphors |
| 2015 | a Roland-Garros tennis ad | the same macro road |
| undated | a Toyota campaign | "All roads lead to ______" over a macro texture |
| recent | Werner Bronkhorst paintings | thick texture plus miniature figures, read as an aerial view |
| undated | Lurpak by Wieden+Kennedy | cabbage, broccoli and flour as other worlds |
| 1930s | Edward Weston | peppers and shells as landscapes |
| early 1900s | F. Percy Smith | he calls him "the father of macro photography" |

- **Why the association works:** [D] "A close-up of fabric intuitively reads as terrain. A zipper becomes a road. Stitching becomes a path." Viewers make the leap themselves "if you present it clearly".
- **Where it fails:** [D] "there's a fine line between leveraging visual shorthand and simply repeating what's been done".
- **The student fix he praises:** one spec campaign that swaps a few ingredients, leaves others, and so does not read as a copy.
- **Note:** The newsletter writes "Rolland Garros". The caption hears Bronkhorst as "Werner Bron Horse". He credits the Social Juice newsletter for spotting the look-alike ads. Campaign facts are in `mentioned/campaigns/`.

### Worked example: the Hockney joiner

- **Provenance:** [D] "The technique is called panography, like panorama. But most call it a Hockney joiner because the credited father of joiners is David Hockney." (`shorts/2026-04-11-what-s-a-hockney-joiner-add-this-to-your-visual-toolbox-Z2Xeb2zhWAU.md`)
- **How he traced it:** he saw Nike ACG and Converse "Forever Chuck" ads built from overlapping photos, saved them to a Cosmos collection, and worked back to who did it first. [D] "I slowly found out that it was most likely David Hockney." (`video/visuals-you-can-t-find-on-pinterest.md`)
- **What the name buys you:** a word a photographer understands, and "a frame of reference that you can share with your client".
- **Uses he lists:** sports motion in a still image, wardrobe swapped between frames, time collapsed into one frame, a message about attention, accumulation "more like a time-lapse".

## The mood board workflow, step by step

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the biggest misconception about mood boards is that you're finished once you pull in reference images that's just gathering" (`video/how-to-art-direct-your-mood-board-in-photoshop.md`)
- **Source:** that video; `frames/how-to-art-direct-your-mood-board-in-photoshop.md`; `shorts/2026-03-16-art-direct-your-mood-boards-ZW_2yF_0WAQ.md`
- **Tools:** Adobe Firefly Boards for gathering and layout, Adobe Photoshop 2026 for edits. The video is Adobe-sponsored.
- **The board in the video:** [D:vis] titled "SWET ATHLETIC", six images in two rows, direction "earthy greens and browns athletes and athletic apparel".

### Step 1: gather in Firefly Boards

- **Provenance:** [D] "i'm collecting all these images into Firefly boards which is a super slick in browser and collaborative workspace for concepting and gathering"
- **Do:** Drop every candidate image onto the board. In the video that is about 15 loose sports photos. This is gathering, and the work has not started.

### Step 2: cut with the "why is this image here" filter

- **Provenance:** [D] "if I can't answer why an image is there it doesn't make the cut"
- **Three questions per image:** Does it contribute to the vision I'm building? Does its texture or lighting reinforce the tone I'm after? Does it have a specific ingredient worth showing?
- **Result in the video:** 15 images become six.

### Step 3: find the image that breaks the board

- **Provenance:** [D] "the image itself is right the feel the subject the lighting but the jacket is this fluorescent yellow it doesn't belong in this palette if I leave it everyone looking at this board is going to get stuck on it"
- **Do:** Keep the image and fix the one thing wrong with it. A reference can be corrected to fit.

### Step 4: recolour in Photoshop

- **Provenance:** [D] "turn that layer into a clipping mask command option G on a Mac now it only applies to the coat layer and nothing else"

| # | Action | Tool or shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open the image and duplicate the layer. Keep the original underneath. | Cmd+J (Mac), Ctrl+J (PC) |
| 2 | Pick the Object Selection tool. Hovering highlights elements. | W |
| 3 | For a person: in the options bar choose Select people, click the person, pick from the labelled parts (glasses, lips, eyebrows, hair, face). | options bar |
| 4 | Choose where the selection is processed: on device (fast) or cloud (better on complex edges). Select the coat. Apply. | Select Subject dropdown: Device or Cloud |
| 5 | Add a mask. The coat is now isolated on its own layer. | contextual taskbar, "add a mask" |
| 6 | Click Adjust colors. It makes a Hue/Saturation adjustment layer. | contextual taskbar, or the Adjustments panel |
| 7 | Clip the adjustment to the coat layer. | Cmd+Opt+G, or Alt-click the line between the two layers |
| 8 | Adjust colors has already found the colours in the image. Select yellow. Pull hue toward green or olive. Desaturate a little. | Hue/Saturation sliders |

- **Layer stack at the end:** [D:vis] top to bottom: Grain 1, Hue/Saturation 1 (clipped), the copy layer with a mask, the original.
- **Note:** The Short also mentions the "select subject button".

### Step 5: add one unifying layer

- **Provenance:** [D] "thinking of a sort of layer that you can add to not only this image but to all of the images to really bring them together and make them all feel like they are from a single source"
- **Do:** Add a Grain adjustment layer on top of the stack. It is non-destructive. [D] "Keep it subtle i'm just smoothing over differences in image quality."
- **Other options he names:** a colour adjustment, or what the caption hears as "a lot", almost certainly a LUT.

### Step 6: Copy Merged and paste into the board

- **Provenance:** [D] "I'm not the best art director in the world but I pride myself in being a fast art director when you're in a fastmoving environment being speedy can make you more valuable than sometimes talent alone"
- **Do:** Cmd+Shift+C copies everything visible, flattened, and leaves the layers intact. Paste straight into Firefly Boards or any slide software. No export. [D] "I don't need to fill my hard drive with dozens of versions of the same image".

### Step 7: lay the board out like a scene

- **Provenance:** [D] "each image is intentionally positioned to build that scene like a movie we have a close-up shot a wide and and textures we show a variety of activities and people"
- **Do:** Crop first: "what do you actually want the viewer to see". Mix close-up, wide and texture. Spread colour and texture evenly so no single ingredient makes the slide lopsided.

### Step 8: put the client hat on

- **Provenance:** [D] "put your client hat on and think is anything going to be misinterpreted if so do you need to add some text or prep some voice over so that you can keep everyone on the same page"
- **Three fixes for an image that could be misread:** add text, prepare what you will say over it, or remove or adjust the image.

### Step 9: add a short write-up

- **Provenance:** [D] "My creative directors would often push me to add a brief write up to every single mood board. And that simple step removes the subjectivity that comes from looking at references and articulates clear boundaries." (`shorts/2025-03-17-the-language-of-art-direction-5-tGRNcDsIk.md`)
- **Signal:** [D] "Without a clearly articulated vision, you get mismatch luggage."

### The test of a finished board

- **Provenance:** [D] "the goal is a through line something that makes the board feel like it was made by one person with one intention" (`video/how-to-art-direct-your-mood-board-in-photoshop.md`)
