# Content and hooks: the blueprint, the formulas, finishing

Jason Murray's short-form method, his hook teaching, his rules for finishing projects, and what his podcast guests say about building an audience. ## The content blueprint, step by step

- **Type:** [CT]
- **Provenance:** [D:vis] the Notion page "The Lazy Creator Blueprint": Introduction, Module 1: Ideation + Scripting, Module 2: Shooting + Setup, Module 3: Editing + Overcoming Mental Hurdles (`frames/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Who it is for:** [D:vis] "creative professionals who want to stop overthinking and start creating".
- **The result he cites:** [D] "going from 1,900 followers to now over 50,000 followers", in about six months from mid-2024 to February 2025.

| Step | Lesson | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Power of a Strong Idea | Pick one idea from the six prompts below. |
| 2 | Make It 30 Seconds | Aim for 30 to 45 seconds, 70 to 90 words. |
| 3 | Script Word for Word | Write how you talk, short sentences. |
| 4 | Crafting an Interesting Hook | Open with one of four hook formulas. Clear, not clever. |
| 5 | Talk to Camera | Face the lens. No b-roll needed. |
| 6 | A Repeatable Shooting Space | One spot, facing a window or a simple light. |
| 7 | Lazy Gear | iPhone, CapCut, a teleprompter app. |
| 8 | Clean, Simple Cuts | Cut dead air. No transitions, effects or templates. |
| 9 | Overcoming Procrastination | Lower the bar. One hour to script, shoot and edit. |
| 10 | Overcoming Embarrassment | Secret account, or a warning video. |

### Step 1: a strong idea carries the video

- **Provenance:** [D] "if the idea of the video is strong it's going to carry a lot of the weight for you it's going to do a lot of the heavy lifting for you" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** Spend the time on the idea and the edit gets cheap. He calls this "the Crux of the entire lazy Creator philosophy".
- **Note:** [M] "80% of the video" is not in the transcript.

### Step 1, continued: the six idea prompts

- **Provenance:** [D] "unpack a hidden Insight teach a skill in one sentence that's something that I I do a lot with my content expose a common mistake leverage career or creative insights um share a tool or shortcut" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`); examples are [D:vis] off the Notion page

| # | Prompt | His example on the page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Share a counterintuitive truth | "The secret to being consistent isn't discipline, it's lowering the bar." |
| 2 | Unpack a hidden insight | "Why simple typography often wins in branding (and how overdesigning kills trust)." |
| 3 | Teach a skill in one sentence | "The fastest way to get a clean composition? Blur your eyes to spot clutter." |
| 4 | Expose a common mistake | "Why overusing mockups can hurt your portfolio (show process, not just polish)." |
| 5 | Leverage career or creative insights | "Creative professionals overthink their content because they hold it to the same standard as their art. Content isn't art." |
| 6 | Share a tool or shortcut | "Why CapCut is the best app for creatives to edit short-form video (and how to use it in under 10 minutes)." |

- **Where to start:** prompts 5 and 6. Page tip: "Focus on tools, tips, and shortcuts you've personally used in your work."

### Step 2: thirty seconds, 70 to 90 words

- **Provenance:** [D] "aim for 70 to 90 words if you've got a a word counter" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** The reason is value density. Short videos force a clear idea. Page tip: ask "What's something I can explain in 30 seconds or less?" and break larger topics into pieces.

### Step 3: script word for word

- **Provenance:** [D] "if you don't want to use telepromter go sentence by sentence" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** A script removes hesitation, keeps the message clear and stops rambling. Read it from a teleprompter (CapCut has one), or record sentence by sentence with the iPhone camera's pause button.

### Step 4: the hook, clear and not clever

- **Provenance:** [D] "your hook should be clear not clever don't overthink it and I might tweak that slightly" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** The page says viewers decide in seconds. The four formulas are in the hook section below. He says he tries not to be clickbaity.

### Step 5: talk to camera

- **Provenance:** [D] "module two lesson five is why you should talk to camera this is another decision I'm just going to tell you right now if you want to do it the laziest way" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Page text:** [D:vis] "People trust and engage with creators who talk directly to them." "It's uncomfortable at first, but the more you do it, the easier it gets."

### Steps 6 and 7: one spot, cheap gear

- **Provenance:** [D] "you really don't need more than like a simple ring light um and it doesn't have to be a ring light" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** Stand by a window, or use a $15 to $30 light. Apple earbuds, or a wired Rode lavalier for iPhone at about $30. Background: bookshelves, art or your workspace. Page gear list: iPhone, CapCut, a teleprompter app. Optional: a small tripod, a ring light, a clip-on mic.

### Step 8: edit by removing

- **Provenance:** [D] "remove the dead air tighten the pacing really it's all about letting the strength of your idea carry the video" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Signal:** Delete any sentence that feels off. Add only the minimum captions.

### Step 9: lower the bar, set a time limit

- **Provenance:** [D] "secret to being consistent isn't discipline it's lowering the bar" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Page text:** [D:vis] "Focus on reps, not perfection." "Set a time limit: Give yourself 1 hour to script, shoot, and edit."
- **His record:** [D] "I had the script already but I've shot and edited a video in and then had it posted in 30 minutes". The script was written beforehand.

### Step 10: getting past embarrassment

- **Provenance:** [D] "hey I'm going to be making videos now unfollow if you don't want that because I'm doing this because I I like to do it" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)
- **Two routes:** a secret account, as he did on TikTok a year earlier. Or a warning video. His own is dated July 3, 2024, captioned "THE 2024 CONTENT STRATEGY FOR FULL-TIME CREATIVES" ([D:vis]).
- **Page text:** [D:vis] "Most people are supportive or don't care as much as you think."

### The mindset under all ten steps

- **Provenance:** [D] "your content is a tool not your Masterpiece I think that's huge for creatives and artists because your taste level is so high" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`)

### What to post when you have nothing: the FOMO test

- **Provenance:** [D] "what is something someone else would be missing out on that you could tell them about" (`video/building-my-2025-creative-empire-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-2.md`)
- **Signal:** People scroll so they do not miss a tool or shortcut. Share something you love that others would miss: a book, a tool, a person to follow, a mantra.

### The lazy series as a worked example

- **Provenance:** [D] "the insight that being lazy honestly isn't always a bad thing it's a motivator to be more efficient" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **Signal:** One insight carries a whole series. [D:vis] ten of his reel covers on one slide: "Tools for LAZY Creatives" (5.8M views), "3 Tools for LAZY Creatives" (1.5M), "LAZY CREATOR GEAR" (127K), and four "HOW TO BE A LAZY ART DIRECTOR" episodes.

## What a hook is

- **Type:** [DEF]
- **Provenance:** [D] "a hook is what grabs and holds your attention the the emphasis here is on grabbing and holding because just like an actual fishing hook it's not enough to just startle a fish you want to hold it" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **Slide:** [D:vis] "A hook is what grabs & holds your attention."
- **Earlier wording, September 2024:** [D] "hooks are simply the combination of elements designed to grab your attention amidst a sea of media". The "hold" half arrives by 2026.
- **Where he first heard it:** [D] "i remember one of my creative directors asking me about one of my ideas asking "what's the hook?" and at that point i didn't entirely know what they meant"

## Every hook formula, with an example

### Five attributes of a great hook

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the hook needs to be aligned with the story's idea otherwise you're going to grab someone's attention but there's nothing to hold on to" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`); the list is [D:vis] at 8:28

| # | Attribute | His example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sparks curiosity | *The Hunger Games* first line. His red note on the slide: "who is normally on the other side of the bed?" |
| 2 | Reframes something familiar | *1984*: "thirteen!? Clocks don't strike thirteen." Also The Dark Knight's bank heist, and a book cover that reuses the Japanese wave. |
| 3 | Instantly understandable | "Think small." |
| 4 | Hits a nerve: funny, curious or emotional | *Fahrenheit 451*, with "pleasure" and "burn" underlined. |
| 5 | Aligned with the story's idea | *Red Rising*. His note: "Sets the tone. Alludes to an idea of peace/war. Otherwise there won't be a payoff." |

- **Note:** He applies the same five to the idea behind a video. The example for attribute 3 is this file's pairing, [I]; he shows the Volkswagen ad in the run of hooks without tying it to one attribute.

### Four verbal hook formulas (the blueprint)

- **Type:** [CT]
- **Provenance:** [D:vis] "Lazy Hook Formulas for Creatives" on the Notion page, Lesson 4 (`frames/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`); spoken examples are [D] from `video/my-exact-content-blueprint.md`

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| Ask a surprising question | [A] the page has no example filled in |
| State a counterintuitive truth | "The secret to being consistent isn't discipline, it's lowering the bar." (his example for the matching idea prompt) |
| Expose a common mistake | "Why overusing mockups can hurt your portfolio" (his example for the matching idea prompt) |
| Make a bold claim | "this feels illegal", "stop doing this" |

- **Note:** The four formulas end in colons on the page with nothing after them. The examples for rows 2 and 3 come from Lesson 1, where the same phrasing is an idea prompt.

### Title, visual, first line: the three parts of a reel's hook

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D:vis] the diagram at 7:28: one of his reels with three red arrows (`frames/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **The three parts, with his own reel as the example:**
  1. Visual: title or text. "Tools for LAZY Creatives".
  2. Audio: the first spoken line. "This is the mockup website of my dreams..."
  3. Visual: "setting, props, situation, action". A screen and laptop tell designers it is for them.
- **The formula on the slides:** "{visual + audio}", then "{visual + audio} + insight".
- **Note:** "The 3-part stack" is Gemini's name for it.

### Music as a hook: three techniques

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "when the music fights what you're seeing your brain goes "wait what?" that creates tension and intrigue" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)

| Technique | How it works |
|---|---|
| The Mismatch | Calm footage over an aggressive track. The tension is the hook. |
| The Amplifier | Match the music to the emotion on screen and turn it up. |
| The Sonic Signature | Repeat motifs across videos. Caption on screen: "you're training them to associate a sound with the value you bring". |

- **His framing:** [D] "most people think of hooks as visuals but music can stop the scroll just as fast"
- **Note:** This is the Musicbed sponsor segment.

### The hook in music is the earworm

- **Type:** [DEF]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the hook is that earworm it's the part that gets stuck in your head" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **Signal:** The music industry has used the word for almost half a century. Slide: "Music: hook ≠ chorus". It can be a bass riff. Streaming-era artists put it first.

### The insight is the deepest hook

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "great ideas come from great insights and great ideas will in turn be great hooks" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)

- **Slides:** "Insights = unspoken human truths". "The best insights sound counterintuitive". "The best insights are startling." "Why?"

### Covers, thumbnails and headlines

### Film and TV openings

- **Type:** [EX]
- **Provenance:** [D] "why are their pants flying through the air what is this rv running from is that guy dead" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **Signal:** Breaking Bad's opening raises questions. The Dark Knight takes a familiar bank heist and reframes it. His favourite hooking visual is from the short film *Inside the Blind Iris* by Douglas Bernardt ([D:vis] title frame).

### Hooks are not a formula

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "creating great hooks is an art form it's not going to be a complete science you can't always steal the exact framework of others" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook.md`)
- **What kills a hook:** gimmicks that "startle you but not actually pull you in", with no payoff later.
- **Not found:** [M] "never start with hey guys". His own 2017 gimbal review opens "hey guys Jason here".

## Finishing projects

### The finish line is the end of your guaranteed time

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "make the end of your timeline the finish line no other criteria make the end of your guaranteed available time the finish line" (`video/how-to-finish-every-project-you-start.md`)
- **How to run it:** [D] "kids are asleep wife's in bed I have one hour to draw and post it to my Instagram tonight". If it is unfinished at minute 59: [D] "too bad time is up project is over better adjust next time".

### Three bad finish lines

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "you can't guarantee you can't promise me or yourself you'll actually get that done within your time frame" (same video)
- **The three:** finished when it is perfect. Finished when it meets my standards. Finished when it is 500 pages long.

### For longer projects, shrink the scope

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "you need to adjust your project Ambitions the scope of your project so it can fit more snugly into your guaranteed available timeline" (same video)
- **Signal:** If you cannot guarantee next Thursday at 10:00 pm, the project will not get finished.

### Seven projects you will finish

- **Type:** [CT]
- **Provenance:** [D] "a doodle not a painting a comic strip not a graphic novel a short GIF or GIF not an animated film a short story not a book" (same video; also `shorts/2023-09-22-7-project-ideas-you-ll-actually-finish-n2aVpsnveII.md`)

| Do this | Not this |
|---|---|
| a doodle | a painting |
| a comic strip | a graphic novel |
| a short GIF | an animated film |
| a short story | a book |
| episodes | movies |
| a series of interviews | a documentary |
| a collection of poems | one long novel |

### Repeat small pieces, then package them

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "most of these projects can be repeated over and over in order to then be packaged up as a larger more impressive looking portfolio piece" (same video)
- **His example, Beeple:** [D] "the Finish Line was never 5 000 every days or the 50 million dollar art piece the Finish Line was midnight every single day".
- **Fact check:** [G] the Christie's sale in March 2021 was $69.3 million. The caption hears Beeple as "people".

### Rick Rubin: lower the stakes

- **Type:** [RES]
- **Provenance:** [X:Rick Rubin] read aloud by Jason from *The Creative Act*: "one of the best strategies is to lower the stakes" (same video)
- **Signal:** Beginning, completing and sharing are where people stall. Treat the work as "a small work a beginning". Make many things and put them out.

### Timers, and a time limit built into the project

- **Type:** [CT]
- **Provenance:** [D] "set a little 30 minute timer or one hour timer and um just just give myself that time to like rush and get something done" (`video/building-my-2025-creative-empire-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-2.md`)
- **Built in:** [D] "say you only have 30 minutes at lunchtime every day to like doodle thing that's your project I'm doodling 30 minutes every day"

### Twenty small things beat one big unfinished thing

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "I'm a much more fulfilled creative making little looping gifts or putting out a 6sec animation" (`video/building-my-2025-creative-empire-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-2.md`)
- **Signal:** He calls the target "the minimum viable piece of content".

### His own hours: 10:00 pm to 1:00 am

- **Type:** [C]
- **Provenance:** [D] "I would do all of it from 10 p.m. to 1:00 a.m. I'm a night owl late at night that's when I would make stuff" (`video/building-my-2025-creative-empire-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-2.md`)
- **Signal:** Two years of content made at night while employed. Earlier he kept Thursday evenings, agreed with his wife. He often did one or two hours and went to bed.

### Lean into the weakness

- **Type:** [J]
- **Provenance:** [D] "I just lean into the weakness of like not a good illustrator so I'm just going to make this rough or whatever i'm going to animate and I'm going to make it the scribbliest animation ever" (`video/building-creative-influence-as-a-photography-creator.md`)

## Guest audience-building advice

Everything in this section belongs to the guest named. None of it is Jason's framework.

### Oren John, through Garrett King: content is its own skill

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] quoting [X:Oren John]: "It is a skill learning how to make content that moves well on the internet." (`video/building-creative-influence-as-a-photography-creator.md`)
- **Context:** King had seven or eight years of portfolio work and a small audience. He and Jason took the same Cut30 cohort in July 2024.

### Garrett King: break down what you like and build around it

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] "when you see something you like try to break it down why do you like it and like build around that"
- **Context:** His flash style came from taking apart a Men's Health editorial with Method Man and American Apparel's lighting.

### Garrett King: speed over perfection

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] "I'm like a speed over perfection guy"
- **Context:** Some photographers deliver 25 images from an 8 to 10 hour day. He sends 150.

### Garrett King: hundreds of free shoots first

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] "i shot hundreds of free shoots hundreds it took eight years before I made any money at all"

### Garrett King: keep the day job separate

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] "I like having those parts of my life and some level of separation because I found that if I have a crappy day at work it's really nice to have something else"
- **Context:** He has worked at Mars pet care for 8 and a half years.

### Garrett King: bring your style to another subject

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Garrett King] "one time he just got access to professional bull riding and shot the most beautiful rodeo short film that I've ever seen"
- **Context:** Said of photographer Jeff Vallee (caption: "Jeff Valet"; the match is likely and not proven).

### Edward Sun: you can start without showing your face

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "for the first 2 years I was only posting Graphics I never showed my face or talked to camera" (`video/how-content-became-this-designer-s-escape-plan.md`)
- **Context:** He passed 100,000 followers. His biggest brand deals came in 2024.

### Edward Sun: keep an outlet no client depends on

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "I realized that I needed something that was not tied to money uh that was a creative Outlet so something that no client was depending on me to be good at"

### Edward Sun: a creator who teaches has to keep practising

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "I have to always be in there I have to always be doing it"
- **Context:** His Dr. Mike point. NotebookLM folds it into Jason's flywheel.

### Edward Sun: say who you serve

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "if you go to my website it says worldclass design for Christian Le organizations that's kind of the niche"
- **Context:** The caption hears "Christian Le" for Christian-led.

### Edward Sun on digital products

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "there is a digital product route which is helping people um I should have done that earlier I look at what you're doing and I'm like very smart I should I haven't rolled out anything yet"

### Jason Gold: own one thing

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "having your thing specifically or if it's typography or if it's whatever I do think that's a great way to to differentiate" (`video/becoming-a-freelance-creative-director-the-toolbox-podcast-ep-3.md`)

### Jason Gold: get out from behind the deck

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "I would be the kind of creative who's really perfectionist likes to kind of get into all the details be at my desk working and making decks but hiding behind my work"

### Taylor Dahl: a plain review needs a point of view and a package

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Taylor Dahl] "if you make a thing out of it if you brand it or create a nice visual package and give a point of view then it becomes interesting" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
