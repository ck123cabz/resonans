## The no-ad-school path, step by step

- **Type:** [F]
- **Provenance:** [D] "the different sort of things you'd have to learn from visual craft to Big Ideas to then making campaigns make some passion projects and then put them all into a portfolio" (`video/turn-your-passion-projects-into-an-art-director-portfolio.md`)
- **Where it lives:** the free Art Director Roadmap FigJam (https://jsnmrry.gumroad.com/l/agitc), walked through in `video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md` and `video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`.
- **Why he built it:** [D] "you just don't want you to have to spend thousands and thousands of dollars on schools and portfolio schools that might not be useful" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md`)
- **Note:** He does not number the stages. The five-step split is how he walks the board.

### Step 0: know what the job is

- **Provenance:** [D] "creates and develops concepts and defines the look and feel of those concepts across channels and mediums they are responsible for visual execution" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Signal:** An art director is not a senior designer. [D] "a designer not always but often is more focused on the function the utility of what they're creating while an art director honestly function goes out the window and they're just so much more focused on attention". The copywriter is a partner of equal rank. The creative director is above both.
- **Cited backing:** [X:Sir John Hegarty] the designer is "the architect of clarity"; the art director is "the creative anarchist". Jason reads it aloud.
- **Do not start as a creative director:** [D] "if you're a creative director at the beginning of your career I would consider rethinking your title and rethinking your path"

### Step 1: learn the craft

- **Provenance:** [D] "one big thing that I would emphasize about an art director is you need to be multilingual" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Do:** Learn design first: "The most important one of these craft skills in terms of being pretty Universal is design." Then add languages by the work you want: photography, cinematography, editing, animation, styling, casting, colour, typography, illustration. Learn to make decks.
- **Where:** YouTube. He names five Adobe Digital Academy courses in `newsletter/2026-02-25-art-direction-craft-becoming-multilingual.md`: Design Fundamentals with AI, Image Editing, Graphic Design, Presentation Design with Adobe Express, Multichannel Marketing. Adobe partnership.
- **His admission:** "I was a terrible designer at first".

### Step 2: learn to think conceptually

- **Provenance:** [D] "an insight is a revelation or it's an unspoken human truth it's one of those things that when you say it it changes the way you see the world or it changes the way you see yourself" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Do:** Learn insights, then big ideas, then idea sizes. [D] "A concept or an idea needs to have an insight and an idea is just what you do with that insight."
- **Reading he gives:** Luke Sullivan, *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*. Mark Pollard and Sweathead. Julian Cole on insights. Jason Bagley's Creative Mega Machine. Aisha Hakim's The Art of Deckmaking. Details in `people-and-reading.md`.
- **Platform tests:** [X:Luke Sullivan] it fits on a Post-it; it "starts talking and won't shut up". Jason credits Sullivan.

### Step 3: make spec campaigns and passion projects

- **Provenance:** [D] "your portfolio can be completely fake and that's okay everyone understands that" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Do:** Pick a globally known brand. Find an insight, write the idea, mock up four or more touchpoints. Study The One Show archive, D&AD New Blood and student portfolios: [D] "these are your competition".

### Step 4: build the portfolio

- **Do:** Four to six strong projects on a site of your own. The rubric and the page layout are in `portfolio-playbook.md`.

### Step 5: apply

- **Provenance:** [D] "a lot of you could probably just go straight for junior art director or even mid-level art director jobs especially if you're a mid-career senior designer" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Do:** Internships and junior roles for students. A sideways move for senior designers. Search LinkedIn posts for the role and message the person who posted.

### Free programs he lists

- **Provenance:** [D] "there's the one school which is specifically for black creatives um and its mission is to get more black creatives into the industry and it's completely free" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025.md`)
- **List:** ONE School. The Work Shop. Goodby Silverstein & Partners' free portfolio program. "Make Ads With Me", a Facebook group he is not sure is active. The One Club's portfolio reviews, Young Ones, One Creator Lab (`shorts/2025-05-16-how-to-break-into-the-ad-industry-w57L8NdLolY.md`).

### How long it takes

- **Provenance:** [D] "if you want to become an art director in 2026 you are literally 30 days away from having a portfolio that is an art director portfolio" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026.md`)
- **Signal:** The 30 days is the bootcamp, which yields one finished 360 campaign. [A] He gives no duration for the free route alone.

## Agency, in-house, freelance

### Agency

- **Provenance:** [D] "give you raises by certain increments of like 10K time you start at like 55k as a junior art director" (ep 4)
- **Pay:** $55,000 to start in Chicago in 2018. Raises of about $10,000, so title outruns salary and people change agencies to catch up. $110,000 as a senior art director at Huge in 2021.
- **Life:** hours run "9 to 9". Retention is low, so you sample several places.
- **Guest view:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] on a year at Grey: "that's better than any master's degree". He took a pay cut to join Droga5.

### In-house

- **Provenance:** [D] "they can be hard to get into but they pay so much better than they did at ad agencies" (ep 4)
- **Who:** Amazon, Facebook, Google, Microsoft, Airbnb, Spotify.
- **Pay:** [D] "I went I doubled my salary just going to Amazon and got promoted even from there". And: "I'm already making at Amazon money that is more than most creative directors making".
- **What you learn:** the language of sales and media buying. You are not siloed on creative.
- **What a CD does there:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "a large chunk of the job was also things like uh performance reviews and helping with hiring and kind of dealing with process weekly business reviews spreadsheets Revenue goals"

### Freelance inside agencies

- **Provenance:** [D] "there's a pretty common practice of bring contractors in to fill spots to kind of like fill headcount" (ep 4)
- **Signal:** The same job with an end date, one week to two months. He treats it as plan B at an ACD-level rate. [A] He never states a day rate.

### The order he took

- **Provenance:** [D] "if circumstances were different I would continue working full-time probably for another couple years to get a little closer to like 10 years in the industry" (ep 4)
- **Signal:** Agencies, then a tech company, then solo. He describes his own path and does not lay it down as a rule. [M] as a three-step rule.

### What he weighs

- **Provenance:** [D] "the most important thing as a creative is creative control creative freedom" (`video/i-found-the-dream-creative-career-path.md`)
- **The five:** creative control, creative freedom, time and lifestyle control, variety, money. He asks viewers to rank them.

### Six months solo

- **Provenance:** [D] "this year working for myself I think there's a possibility that I could make the exact same amount" (`video/i-found-the-dream-creative-career-path.md`, August 3, 2025)
- **His day:** family until 10:00 or 10:30 am, work from 11:00 am, lunch, work through his son's nap until dinner, sometimes an hour after bedtime.
- **Hard parts:** health insurance, housing and tax. A month and a half on the road with little income.

## Freelance creative director advice

Guest ideas are tagged. They are not Jason's framework.

### Jason: title follows the rate you can charge

- **Provenance:** [D] "I would honestly look more at the rate that you can comfortably charge and then draw a line over what would the title be that would get me that rate" (ep 4)
- **Signal:** If the title climbs faster than the value you bring, jobs get hard to win. 2026 Short: [D] "Don't try to become a creative director. You need to try to work for a creative director." (`shorts/2026-04-14-how-to-start-as-a-creative-director-for-brands-bJ9141nuask.md`)

### Jason: price so you can subcontract

- **Provenance:** [D] "price it at a level and price it at a price where you could potentially subcontract it out and hire someone else to work under you" (ep 2)
- **Signal:** He priced the Logitech job so an editor friend could cut it. May 2025 Short: "Raise your prices, double them two times", because "If you charge more, you can do less but better."

### Jason: use your maker skills in the pitch

- **Provenance:** [D] "i use my maker skills quite a bit to mock things up create presentations and style frames" (`video/ai-will-steal-my-job-as-an-art-director.md`)

### Jason: introverts are not top of mind

- **Provenance:** [D] glossary entries "Top of mind" and "Digitally extroverted" (`glossary.md`); `video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live.md`
- **Signal:** "the best opportunities come to people that are top of mind". He used Slack to be visible at agencies.

### Jason Gold: a prison of your own success

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "it's kind of you get stuck in a in a prison of your own success in in some ways" (ep 3)
- **Signal:** The higher you climb, the scarier it gets to step back. Zoom out before chasing the next title.

### Jason Gold: the sabbatical

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] citing [X:Stefan Sagmeister]: "every seven years he literally shut down his entire studio for a full year" (ep 3)
- **Signal:** Gold uses it to reframe leaving a job.

### Jason Gold: the cushion

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "you need to be able to survive for a couple months if you don't have a steady paycheck coming in" (ep 3)
- **Note:** [M] "3 to 6 months" is not in the transcript.

### Jason Gold: other freelancers are the workforce

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "it seems almost like counterintuitive uh that like oh this is all competition but in reality like this is just kind of like the new Workforce" (ep 3)

### Jason Gold: take the pay cut for the right place

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "I remember also taking a pay cut to go to droga because I was like not going to negotiate with them if I had the chance to to to go work there" (ep 3)

### Jason Gold: own one thing, and get out from behind the deck

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "having your thing specifically or if it's typography or if it's whatever I do think that's a great way to to differentiate" (ep 3)
- **Also:** "hiding behind my work" is his description of his earlier self.

### Jason Gold: study the Droga5 archive

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Jason Gold] "younger folks should definitely go back into the droga 5 archives and see some of those early campaigns" (ep 3)

### Edward Sun: how he prepared to quit

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "I did 6 months saved up of personal and business expenses added up together" (`video/how-content-became-this-designer-s-escape-plan.md`)
- **Steps:** used two required weeks off as a trial run. Saved six months of expenses. Gave 90 days' notice. Lined up deals into the new year. Wrote his proposal process and pricing sheet before leaving.

### Edward Sun: proposals as a web page

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "making a proposal that way has made them like made me able to close like 90% of them" (same episode)
- **Signal:** One Framer page per proposal, filled from a CMS form.

### Edward Sun: Pentagram versus Smith & Diction

- **Provenance:** [D:guest:Edward Sun] "pentagram releases a mid Journey driven Rebrand of like a US Government website or something and like pentagram gets completely flamed" (same episode)
- **His two tests for AI in client work:** is the training data licensed, and does it feel like cheaply replacing creatives. A four-person studio gets more benefit of the doubt than Pentagram.
- **Jason's part:** he had not seen the Smith & Diction work. His view of the Pentagram icons is under "AI position by date".

## His business model, with numbers

### The Creative Empire: three parts

- **Type:** [F]

### Swet Studio

- **Provenance:** [D] "taking out the a makes it more ambiguous is just a dumb word companies can be anything you want them to be" (ep 2)
- **Name:** from his hyperhidrosis. "sweat is to me what bats are to Batman". Spelled Swet. Instagram is @jason_swet.
- **What it sells:** 1. Freelance art direction and creative direction, direct to brand or as an ACD-level contractor. 2. Consulting on brand content, ideation and strategy. 3. One-to-one creator coaching, priced weekly or monthly. 4. Experiments: social-first production, products, collaborations.
