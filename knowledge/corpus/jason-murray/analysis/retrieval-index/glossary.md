## Concept: insight, idea, platform

### Insight
A human truth that explains why people behave the way they do. It should feel like a revelation, not a fact.
- Quote: "what an Insight is in the simplest way that I could find to Define it is it's a human truth that explains some sort of human behavior" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- On the Roadmap, signed Jason: "The Insight is a revelatory or unspoken truth." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D], [D:vis]

### Observation
What an insight is not. An observation states what people do; an insight says why.
- Quote: "an Insight is deeper than an observation" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Roadmap note: "Bad insights sound more like observations. Obvious. Not very profound. This is a spectrum though." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D], [D:vis]

### Human truth
His working synonym for insight: the emotional motivation behind a behaviour.
- Quote: "the Insight is a human truth it's like the emotional human motivation that um that you can you you might learn or find out about your customer" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Tension
The gap, struggle or contradiction inside an insight. One of his four traits of a strong insight is "Tension-Driven".
- Roadmap note: "Tension-Driven – It highlights a gap, struggle, or contradiction in people's lives." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

### Big idea
The one-sentence formula for what a campaign will do, rooted in the insight. Internal, not the tagline.
- Quote: "that's what a big idea is It's your overarching creative formula that tells you what this project or this campaign is going to do" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- 2025 wording: "it's a this rallying cry is what a lot of people will say that brings the brand solution to life and fuels more ideas" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Big Idea Pyramid
His drawing of how a project stacks, a triangle in three bands: Insight at the point, Idea in the middle, Execution at the wide base. He explains it before he talks about ingredients.
- Quote: "that's thinking big the big idea I visualize it as a big idea pyramid" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D] for the name. [D:vis] for the three bands, top to bottom "Insight / Idea / Execution" (`frames/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use.md`, 2:16).

### Creative formula
What a big idea gives the art director: a rule every execution follows.
- Quote: "you're using that same creative formula from your big idea and you're executing it across as many touch points as possible" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Organizing idea
The idea that turns loose thoughts into a campaign. He uses it as another name for the big idea.
- Quote: "you need, especially in art direction, you need an organizing idea. And ideally, that's going to be rooted in an insight." (`video/answering-every-aspiring-art-director-question-ye-concert-reaction`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Platform
One level above a campaign: a single line that can produce campaigns for years.
- Quote: "what a platform is is it's just one level higher than a campaign where all it really is is a single uh message a single line" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Roadmap note: "A platform is a type of 'big idea'" (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D], [D:vis]

### Fits on a Post-it
The first test of a platform or a big idea: it has to be short enough to write on a Post-it note.
- Quote: "it only qualifies as a platform if it fits on a Post-It note it's got to be like super succinct" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [X:Luke Sullivan]. He reads it from Sullivan ("like he says here"). The Post-it test comes up again in `shorts/2026-02-05-how-to-think-like-an-art-director-JSZhWIf_dcE`.

### Starts talking and won't shut up
The second test of a platform: say it, and executions start coming.
- Quote: "the other sign that it's a platform is it starts talking and won't shut up as soon as we say the idea winning isn't for everyone you can start to think of all the the executions" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [X:Luke Sullivan], read aloud by Jason.

### Truth + Conflict = Platform
The formula for building a brand world, on the Roadmap beside the two tests.
- Roadmap note: "How to create brand worlds: Truth + Conflict = Platform." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [X:Luke Sullivan] through [D:vis]

### Has legs
An idea has legs when many smaller ideas can hang under it.
- Quote: "the idea has legs if that big idea could could have you know legs like a centipede have hundreds of mini ideas underneath it then it's a big idea" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Campaignable
Able to be said to customers and stretched across executions. An insight on its own is not.
- Quote: "the insight isn't the idea because the insight might not be articulated in a way that is customerf facing or is is something that can be campaignable" (`video/how-to-pivot-to-art-direction-with-no-experience`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Idea Sizing Chart
His ladder of idea sizes, biggest to smallest: Business Strategy & Positioning, Branding, then **Big Ideas** (Platform, Campaign), Execution, then **Small Ideas** (One-off, Tactics), then **No ideas** (Crap).
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`, table read off the FigJam)

### One-off, Tactics, Crap
The bottom three rows of the Idea Sizing Chart. A one-off is a single execution with no idea above it. "Crap" is his label for work with no idea.
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)

### 360 campaign
One idea shown across many channels. The first project type in his portfolio table.
- Roadmap table: "A 360 campaign means multiple touch-points, so show the breadth of your idea." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]; also said in `video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio`

### Touchpoint
Any place the idea shows up: social, out of home, TV, in real life, a stunt, an activation, print.
- Roadmap table: "Try for 4-10 different touch-points (social, OOH, TV, IRL, stunt, activation, print, non-traditional/unexpected, etc.)" (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

### Key visual
The single image that tells the whole campaign idea at a glance. He asks for it on portfolio thumbnails.
- Quote: "you need some sort of key visual to communicate what the entire campaign is about and right now the Billboards and out of home stuff feels like extensions of the idea" (`video/art-director-reacts-to-portfolios-live`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Spec work
Fake work for real brands, made to prove you can do the job.
- Quote: "You make spec work (fake work). It's the number one way creatives get into this industry." (`newsletter/2026-08-14-how-to-prove-you-re-an-art-director-mad-010`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Passion project
Self-started work that is real, not spec, and shows who you are outside advertising.
- Roadmap note: "Do the thing for real. Not spec. Not fake." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

### Projectize
Give a passion a concept, a story and a finish line so it can sit in a portfolio.
- Quote: "You need to projectize that passion project. Not not every passion needs to be turned into a project" (`video/answering-every-aspiring-art-director-question-ye-concert-reaction`)
- Provenance: [D]. Spelled "Project-ize" on the Roadmap.

### Master of the hook
What the Social / Content project in a portfolio should prove.
- Roadmap table: "Show that you are a master of the hook." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

## The role

### Art director
- Slide: "An Art Director creates and develops concepts, and defines the look & feel of those concepts across channels and mediums." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

### Concepts + Craft
His two-word summary of the job, on a slide of its own. The corpus profile records the rule as "50/50 craft and concept".
- Slide: "CONCEPTS + CRAFT" (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis] for the slide. [M] for "50/50": no transcript, Short, newsletter or post contains "50/50" or "fifty-fifty".

### Art Direction ≠ Design; Attention vs Function
Two slides in a row. Design serves function; art direction earns attention.
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)

### Creative anarchist
The art director, as against the designer as "architect of clarity".
- Quote: "the art director you're the creative anarchist your job make order chaotic everything you touch is spontaneous irreverent raw" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [X:Sir John Hegarty]. Jason reads Hegarty's LinkedIn post on screen. It is not his coinage.

### Multilingual
An art director speaks several visual languages (photography, styling, type, illustration, editing, casting, colour) without being a specialist in each.
- Quote: "I like to say art directors are multilingual I could never have a full career Maybe I could but I'm I'm not going to have a career as an illustrator" (`video/how-to-craft-an-art-director-portfolio`)
- Provenance: [D]; the slide "Art Directors are Multilingual" is in `frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`

### Creative Pipeline
His timeline of a project: Strategy, Brief, Ideation, Concepts Sold, Execution. Art directors join at the brief; designers, producers, photographers and directors join after concepts are sold.
- Slide text: "Art Directors are not more senior by default ... But they are usually involved earlier in the pipeline." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

### Matching luggage
Brand consistency: many pieces that all look like one set.
- Quote: "there's kind of a term of like matching luggage there's all these different pieces but you want to make sure they all all look cohesive" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2025`)
- Provenance: [D]. He calls it "a term", so it is trade slang he passes on.

### Look and feel
The vibe the visual choices create around an idea. The left end of his spectrum.
- Quote: "how different ingredients might support in terms of creating the feeling Sometimes we call this the look and feel and create a vibe that helps us understand this idea" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]

### People with the vision, people who execute
His two halves of creative work. AI, he argues, takes the executing half.
- Quote: "creative jobs can be categorized in two ways there's the people with the vision and there's the people who execute the vision" (`video/ai-will-steal-my-job-as-an-art-director`)
- Provenance: [D]

## Craft: ingredients and the spectrum

### Ingredients (visual ingredients, art direction ingredients)
Every visual decision that brings an idea to life: medium, type, colour, casting, set, edit, music. He treats them as a cook treats a pantry.
- Quote: "This is where art direction ingredients come in It's my way of comparing the different conceptual mechanisms the visual devices the different creatively visual decisions we are making" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Art Direction Menu
The on-screen list of ingredient categories he works from.
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/art-direction-menu`). The category names are in that file and in lanes 1 to 2.

### Standout ingredients
The two or three choices a viewer notices. The rule is restraint.
- Quote: "there might be a lot of ingredients to make a dish but there's usually like two to three standout flavors standout ingredients" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visual feeling
The left end of his spectrum. The words carry the message and the visuals support it.
- Quote: "On the left side of the spectrum I write visual feeling Oftentimes concepts they just need supporting visual ingredients" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]; the chart is `frames/shorts-visual-tools` (Short nYl9UnPoPv4)

### Visual solution
The right end of the spectrum. The image itself delivers the idea.
- Quote: "in other ways we could communicate the idea through a visual device through a visual solution" (`video/art-direction-techniques-you-can-use`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visual device
A mechanism in the image that carries the concept. He uses it as a near synonym for visual solution, and for a director's one simple trick.
- Quote: "He takes a simple sort of visual device ... it's just like what if you could do this and that's how he that's the concept for the entire film" (`video/the-art-direction-of-zendaya-x-spike-jonze`, on Spike Jonze)
- Provenance: [D]

### "It's how you say it"
His line for why art direction matters when the message is not new.
- Quote: "You can say a thing, but it's how you say it that really slices into your psyche." (`shorts/2026-07-07-polaroid-s-anti-ai-art-direction-I95qQ7t8mns`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Through line
What holds a mood board together. Usually colour.
- Quote: "that through line of between your images in your mood board is usually color. Uh not always, but it's often color." (`video/visuals-you-can-t-find-on-pinterest`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Comp
A mockup of an execution good enough to sell the idea.
- Course page: "How to make clean, convincing comps using the tools you already know." (`frames/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`, Week 3 of the curriculum)
- Provenance: [D:vis]

## Techniques he names

Names in this section are the labels he puts on screen in 2026 and the node names on Iris, his technique map.

### Visual literalism
Turn the phrase or key message into an image word for word.
- Quote: "Word for word, what does the phrase or key message itself look like when translated to images?" (`shorts/2026-03-06-visual-literalism-art-direction-techniques-001-pj2Iy8RjPdc`)
- Written: "when the image reflects the message so literally that it creates a surprising visual solution!" (`linkedin/039-2026-04-21`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visual lineage (visual DNA)
The line of earlier work a look descends from. Trace it back and you can branch from nearer the original.
- Quote: "All of these ads have a shared visual lineage. They use a shared technique. And when you trace that visual DNA back, you can branch out from a more original perspective." (`shorts/2026-04-11-what-s-a-hockney-joiner-add-this-to-your-visual-toolbox-Z2Xeb2zhWAU`)
- Provenance: [D]; long form in `newsletter/2026-02-17-how-art-directors-should-understand-visual-lineage`

### Hockney joiner (panography)
A picture built from many overlapping photos. His worked example of tracing lineage.
- Quote: "The technique is called panography, like panorama. But most call it a Hockney joiner because the credited father of joiners is David Hockney." (`shorts/2026-04-11-what-s-a-hockney-joiner-add-this-to-your-visual-toolbox-Z2Xeb2zhWAU`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visual quotation
Borrow the visual codes of a known genre, period, format or single work.
- Quote: "Visual quotation references known genres, periods, or pieces of work. It allows the image to stand on the shoulders of its predecessors, as long as that reference is known (at least subconsciously) by the audience." (`newsletter/2026-09-28-the-art-direction-of-szilveszter-mako-mad-011`)
- Provenance: [D]. On Iris its four variables are "genre code", "period", "format", "work" ([D:vis], `frames/shorts-visual-tools`).

### Genre quotation
Visual quotation aimed at a genre. He also calls it pastiche.
- Quote: "It's a form of pastiche or genre quotation. It allows you to predict what comes next because the media is adopting a known genre, era, or format." (`shorts/2026-09-02-why-are-these-4x4-visuals-everywhere-8Mfvf0YRGZ4`)
- Prescription: "If your visuals don't mean anything, try genre quotation. Reference something." (`shorts/2026-09-30-art-direction-techniques-that-draw-you-in-pI8_WejU9sQ`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visible facture
Leave the making in view: paint strokes, grain, built scenery.
- Quote: "Visible facture is when the process can be seen in the final work ie when the hand of the maker stays present through painted surfaces, grain, brushwork, and constructed scenery." (`newsletter/2026-09-28-the-art-direction-of-szilveszter-mako-mad-011`)
- Spoken: "Facture is a new word I learned recently. It's the process of how something was made." (`shorts/2026-09-24-these-photos-look-like-paintings-2BYgOxYTE-0`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Frame within a frame
- Quote: "Frame within a frame turns the portrait into a small stage. Scenes within scenes." (`newsletter/2026-09-28-the-art-direction-of-szilveszter-mako-mad-011`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Scale disparity
- Quote: "Scale disparity utilizes the contrast of sizes in order to accentuate a message." (`newsletter/2026-09-28-the-art-direction-of-szilveszter-mako-mad-011`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Figure-ground reversal
Make the empty shape the subject.
- Quote: "Too obvious? Try a figure ground reversal. Turn the subtraction into the subject." (`shorts/2026-09-30-art-direction-techniques-that-draw-you-in-pI8_WejU9sQ`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Form substitution
- Quote: "Too literal? Try form substitution. Use the shape of one thing to mean something else." (`shorts/2026-09-30-art-direction-techniques-that-draw-you-in-pI8_WejU9sQ`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Reframe the familiar
Take what people already recognise and change one thing. He files it under visual hooks.
- Quote: "Reframe the familiar. Take something people recognize and break up the pattern. You can borrow the association of those images." (`shorts/2026-09-02-why-are-these-4x4-visuals-everywhere-8Mfvf0YRGZ4`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Anthropomorphism
Giving a product human traits. Topic of his Apple Mac Mini breakdown and a node on Iris.
- Written: "it sent me down a rabbit hole of visual metaphors, humanization and anthropomorphism" (`linkedin/008-2026-08-31`)
- Provenance: [D]; [D:vis] for the Iris node

### Iris ("A Taxonomy of Visuals")
His map of 39 techniques joined to example images. Filter chips: Differentiate, Group, Sequence, Predict. A second view adds "reference families" (historical portraiture, theatrical sets, painted backdrops, religious iconography). Not public as of 2026-10-03.
- Written: "I've been building something new... I call it, Iris™" (`newsletter/2026-10-02-i-m-building-the-art-direction-school-i-wish-existed`)
- Provenance: [D], [D:vis] (`frames/shorts-visual-tools`, `frames/iris-taxonomy-of-visuals`)

### Other Iris technique names
Read off the map, with no definition from him yet: Symmetry, Face schema, Visual rhyme, Center placement, Leading lines, Gaze cue, Closure, Forced perspective, Caricatural exaggeration, Semantic compounding, Size hierarchy, Body vector, Reading path, Pattern break, Occlusion, Crop at the edge. 23 of the 39 names are legible.
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/shorts-visual-tools`)

## Taste

### Taste (and "visually cook")
Taste is consumption until you use it. His series The Theory of Taste is about making it usable.
- Quote: "the metaphor of taste is actually a pretty passive one consuming things and as an art director that's never been enough for me we have to visually cook we have to use our taste" (`video/the-theory-of-taste`)
- Provenance: [D]

### The three levels of expressing taste
Level 1, know what you like or dislike. Level 2, say how it feels. Level 3, name the ingredients that made it feel that way. Drawn as INPUTS (a funnel), then LIKE?, FEELINGS, INGREDIENTS.
- Level 1: "the first level is simply knowing what you like or dislike that sounds obvious but most people don't even get here intentionally"
- Level 2: "the second level is expressing how it feels and how it makes you feel not technically just emotionally"
- Level 3: "the third level is where you start to identify the actual ingredients the specific elements that created that flavor"
- All three: `video/the-theory-of-taste`. Drawing: `frames/shorts-visual-tools` (Short 4BL7iKgmPUQ).
- Provenance: [D], [D:vis]

### Inputs
What goes into the funnel at the top of the taste drawing: everything you see and save.
- Provenance: [D:vis] (`frames/shorts-visual-tools`)

### Flavor profiles
Taste is not good against bad. He names five: mainstream, cultural, counter, elite, commercial.
- Quote: "everything I just talked about is a nuanced matrix of flavor profiles. There is no single monolith of taste." (`shorts/2026-05-01-you-re-thinking-about-taste-wrong-bKjx0_m3zLM`)
- Provenance: [D]. Only "MAINSTREAM TASTE" and "ELITE TASTE" appear as on-screen labels.

### Curators
The shortcut to taste: find the people or sources whose picks always suit you.
- Quote: "Find the curators, the people or sources who bring you the stuff that always tastes good to you, sound good to you, look good to you." (`shorts/2026-05-08-the-shortcut-to-taste-13bLkEtwfsk`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Visual literacy, visual fluency
Being able to read, understand and express in a visual language. It comes from immersion.
- Quote: "Literacy is being able to critically read, comprehend, and express." (`shorts/2026-04-22-turn-your-taste-into-visual-fluency-R8ML5nlW2f8`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Go to the source
Do not stop at the image. Find who made it, then who represents them.
- Quote: "you don't become fluent in a language by skimming the surface of it you go straight to the source you move to another country the best chefs don't just like sushi they move to japan" (`video/the-theory-of-taste`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Surface (of the algorithm)
Whatever the feed floats past you. Good for chance finds, useless for fluency.
- Quote: "When I say surface level, I'm thinking like algorithm and like whatever is just floating on by you as you're scrolling, that's that's only going to give you like the happenstance inspiration" (`video/visuals-you-can-t-find-on-pinterest`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Rolodex
His answer to "taste is your moat". The moat is knowing the photographers, directors, DPs and their reps.
- Quote: "A lot of people talk about taste as your moat. I really think it's your Rolodex. It's the the sources where you're finding information." (`video/visuals-you-can-t-find-on-pinterest`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Student of the work
How he describes himself: curious, still learning the names.
- Quote: "I feel like I'm proud of being a curious person and being a student of the work" (`video/visuals-you-can-t-find-on-pinterest`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Swipe
Saved reference: images, tools, sites. Advertising slang he uses without defining.
- Quote: "as an art director I'm constantly Gathering visual inspiration or swipe tools websites" (`shorts/2025-02-17-collecting-art-director-tools-inspo-sU1RPbLx81c`)
- Provenance: [D]

## Content and career

### Hook
Whatever catches attention and holds it to the end. Older than social media.
- Quote: "the hook is whatever is going to catch people's attention and hold their attention all the way through a piece of content" (`video/my-exact-content-blueprint`)
- Provenance: [D]; the full argument is `video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook`

### Lazy creative (lazy creator, lazy art director)
His series name and his content method. Lazy means spending no effort where it does not count.
- Quote: "the insight that being lazy honestly isn't always a bad thing it's a motivator to be more efficient" (`video/the-lost-art-of-the-hook`)
- Written: "The lazy creative finds shortcuts to become more efficient" (`shorts/2024-07-08-3-free-websites-for-lazy-creatives-JC2JUlsHTj4`)
- Provenance: [D]

### Toolbox ("add this to your toolbox", "happy toolsday")
His brand since 2022 and his sign-off for any tool, site or technique. A technique is something to "add to your visual toolbox".
- Written: "just another tool to add to the toolbox" (`shorts/2024-10-11-a-designer-s-dream-mockup-website-fb0Aq5RgjSo`); "// happy toolsday" (`linkedin/146-2022-09-07`)
- Provenance: [D]

### No gatekeeping
Why the Roadmap and the Toolbox are free.
- Quote: "I don't want to gatekeep the art direction career at all so I'm trying to put out as much as possible so that you could do it on your own" (`video/how-to-become-an-art-director-in-2026`)

### Swet (Swet Studio, "swettiest")
His handle (@jason_swet) and his company name. A rebrand of his own sweaty palms.

### Creative empire

### Chronic dabbler
His self-description. His Gumroad bio reads "art director / animator / chronic dabbler".
