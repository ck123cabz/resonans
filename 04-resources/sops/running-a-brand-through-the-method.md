# Running a brand through the method

**Use this when:** a brand engagement starts, or an existing brand needs the method applied.
Client work and your own brands both.

**You're done when:** a gap list exists with an owner against each line, every inferred
document is marked as inferred, and the owner has said yes or no to each one.

> [!quote]+ Source
> **April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019)** for positioning: competitive alternative,
> unique attributes, the value they create, who cares most. **Jennifer Aaker, *Journal of
> Marketing Research* (1997)** for the five measurable personality dimensions. **Nielsen
> Norman Group** for the four tone dimensions. **Mark and Pearson, *The Hero and the
> Outlaw* (1999)** for the 12 archetypes.
>
> The run order below is my own rule and it has one run behind it, Pickle Blinders on
> 17 Sep 2026. The frameworks are borrowed; the sequence is not yet tested twice.

> [!todo]+ Steps
> 1. **Search everything you already hold before asking the client anything.** `grep -ril`
>    the brand name over the repo and the vault, then look on disk. Pass: you can name the
>    venue, the people and the last event without being told.
> 2. **Ask the owner one question: what is the current asset, and what is dead.** Nothing
>    else. Pass: one path or link you have been told is authoritative.
> 3. **Read the whole existing kit, whatever format it is in.** A design-tool export is
>    readable: strip the tags and read the text. Pass: you can quote the kit's own voice
>    rules back.
> 4. **Audit against the seven artifacts and write the gap list.** Access · audience ·
>    positioning · voice and tone · visual kit · channels · copy bank. Pass: every artifact
>    marked complete, partial or absent, with the file that proves it.
> 5. **Harvest the brand's own language from where it already speaks.** Group chats, posted
>    announcements, reviews, support threads. Pass: 5 or more verbatim quotes with a name and
>    a timestamp on each, and the two questions the audience asks most.
> 6. **Grade the last real posts against the kit's own rules.** Pass: every rule the client
>    is already breaking is written down, with the post that breaks it.
> 7. **Write only the gaps.** Never restate what the kit already carries; reference its
>    section. Pass: no sentence duplicated between your files and theirs.
> 8. **Snapshot the source of truth into version control, stamped with the time.** Pass: the
>    repo copy names itself a snapshot and says what was excluded.
> 9. **Mark every inferred document and send it for a yes.** Pass: the owner's answer per
>    line, or the file stays marked inferred.

> [!example]+ Worked example
> **Pickle Blinders, 17 Sep 2026, 35 minutes.** The audit found 1 artifact of 7 complete:
> the visual kit, and it was thorough enough that nothing in it needed touching. Step 5 pulled
> the two questions every announcement gets read for, from the member group: "Nsa pa vacant?"
> (Joy Ismil Rendal, 7:44 pm, 15 Sep) and "kinsa imo partner" (Kyle, 3:16 pm). Step 6 found
> the kit's Never list banning emoji and exclamation marks while the club's own admin posted
> "WINNER TAKES ALL!" with two emoji on the main channel, a day before the kit was delivered.
> Six files written, zero questions asked of the client.
> Run log: `scratchpads/pickle-blinders-brand-run/log.md`.

> [!warning]+ Where it goes wrong
> - **Asking the intake questions first.** Six hours of interview buys what an hour on disk
>   and in the chat history already holds. The client corrects one thing (step 2) and that is
>   the only question worth spending.
> - **Trusting a search you ran before the owner spoke.** Everything found for Pickle
>   Blinders in step 1 was three days stale. Keep what it taught you about the people and
>   the events; drop what it claimed about the assets.
> - **Writing a rule with no channel named.** A Never list that does not say where it applies
>   gets broken by the people posting, and they were right to break it. Name the surface.
> - **Copying a live folder without a timestamp.** The Pickle Blinders kit changed twice in
>   16 minutes while it was being read.
> - **Rewriting what exists.** The temptation is strongest where the existing work is good.
